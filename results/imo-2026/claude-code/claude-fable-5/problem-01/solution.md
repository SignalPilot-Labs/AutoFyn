# IMO Problem 1 — Solution

## Problem

There are $2026$ integers greater than $1$ written on a blackboard, not necessarily distinct. In one move, Confucius chooses two integers $m>1$ and $n>1$ written in different positions and replaces them by
$$\gcd(m,n) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \frac{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}{\gcd(m,n)}.$$
He continues making moves for as long as a move is possible.

**(a)** Prove that, regardless of his choices, the process terminates after finitely many moves with exactly one integer $M>1$ on the board.

**(b)** Prove that the value of $M$ is independent of all choices made during the process.

---

## Setup and notation

Throughout, the board always carries exactly $2026$ positive integers (a move replaces two entries by two entries). A move is possible if and only if **at least two** entries of the board are greater than $1$; hence the process stops exactly when **at most one** entry exceeds $1$.

For a prime $p$ and a positive integer $x$, let $v_p(x)$ denote the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorization of $x$, so $x=\prod_p p^{v_p(x)}$. We use the standard facts, valid for all $m,n\ge 1$:
$$v_p\big(\gcd(m,n)\big)=\min\big(v_p(m),v_p(n)\big), \qquad v_p\big(\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\big)=\max\big(v_p(m),v_p(n)\big). \tag{1}$$

**The move is well defined.** By $(1)$, $v_p(\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)) \ge v_p(\gcd(m,n))$ for every prime $p$, so $\gcd(m,n)\mid \operatorname{lcm}(m,n)$ and $\dfrac{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}{\gcd(m,n)}$ is a positive integer. Thus the board always consists of positive integers.

**Local description of a move in exponents.** If a move is applied to entries $m,n$ and $p$ is any prime, write $a=v_p(m)$, $b=v_p(n)$. By $(1)$, the two new entries have $p$-exponents
$$\min(a,b) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \max(a,b)-\min(a,b)=|a-b|. \tag{2}$$
(All other entries, and hence their exponents, are untouched.)

---

## Part (a): the process terminates, with exactly one entry $>1$

For a board state $B=(x_1,\dots,x_{2026})$ define
$$P(B)=\prod_{i=1}^{2026}x_i \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 1}, \qquad k(B)=\#\{\,i : x_i>1\,\}\in\{0,1,\dots,2026\}.$$

**Claim 1.** *Each move strictly decreases the pair $\big(P(B),k(B)\big)$ in lexicographic order.*

*Proof.* Suppose the move is applied to entries $m,n>1$, and let $g=\gcd(m,n)$, $\ell=\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)$. Since $g\ell=mn$, the product of the two new entries is $g\cdot \dfrac{\ell}{g}=\ell=\dfrac{mn}{g}$. All other entries are unchanged, so
$$P \longmapsto \frac{P}{g}. \tag{3}$$

- **Case $g>1$:** by $(3)$, $P$ strictly decreases. (The pair decreases lexicographically no matter what $k$ does.)
- **Case $g=1$:** by $(3)$, $P$ is unchanged. The two chosen entries $m,n$ (both $>1$) are replaced by $g=1$ and $\dfrac{\ell}{g}=mn>1$: among the two positions involved, the number of entries exceeding $1$ drops from $2$ to $1$, and all other positions are untouched. Hence $k$ decreases by exactly $1$, so the pair decreases lexicographically. $\blacksquare$

**Termination.** The values $\big(P(B),k(B)\big)$ lie in $\mathbb{Z}_{\ge1}\times\{0,1,\dots,2026\}$, which is well-ordered under the lexicographic order (there is no infinite strictly decreasing sequence: the first coordinate, a positive integer, can strictly decrease only finitely many times, and between consecutive decreases of the first coordinate the second coordinate, an integer in a fixed finite range, must strictly decrease at every step). By Claim 1 every move strictly decreases this pair, so only finitely many moves can occur. Thus the process terminates, regardless of choices.

**Claim 2.** *At every moment, at least one entry of the board is greater than $1$; i.e. $k(B)\ge 1$ always.*

*Proof.* Initially all $2026$ entries exceed $1$, so $k=2026\ge1$. Consider any move, applied to $m,n>1$ with $g=\gcd(m,n)$. If $g>1$, the new entry $g$ exceeds $1$. If $g=1$, the new entry $\dfrac{\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)}{g}=mn>1$. In either case, at least one of the two new entries exceeds $1$, so after the move the board still contains an entry greater than $1$. By induction, $k\ge1$ holds at all times. $\blacksquare$

**Conclusion of (a).** The process terminates after finitely many moves. At termination no move is possible, which means $k\le 1$; combined with Claim 2 this gives $k=1$. Hence the final board consists of exactly one integer $M>1$ together with $2025$ ones. $\blacksquare$

---

## Part (b): the final value $M$ is independent of all choices

Fix a prime $p$. For a board state $B=(x_1,\dots,x_{2026})$ define
$$G_p(B)=\gcd\big(v_p(x_1),\,v_p(x_2),\,\dots,\,v_p(x_{2026})\big)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\ge0},$$
with the standard conventions $\gcd(t,0)=t$ for $t \ge 0$ and $\gcd(0,0,\dots,0)=0$. (Equivalently, $G_p(B)$ is the nonnegative generator of the subgroup of $\mathbb{Z}$ generated by $v_p(x_1),\dots,v_p(x_{2026})$.)

**Claim 3.** *For every prime $p$, the quantity $G_p(B)$ is invariant under every move.*

*Proof.* A move changes only two entries; by $(2)$ their $p$-exponents change from $\{a,b\}$ to $\{\min(a,b),\,|a-b|\}$, while all other exponents are unchanged. Assume WLOG $a\ge b\ge 0$. Then the new pair of exponents is $(b,\,a-b)$, and
$$\gcd(b,\,a-b)=\gcd(a,b),$$
because any integer divides both $b$ and $a-b$ if and only if it divides both $a$ and $b$ (if $d\mid b$ and $d\mid a-b$ then $d\mid b+(a-b)=a$; conversely if $d\mid a$ and $d\mid b$ then $d\mid a-b$). This identity also covers the edge cases $a=b$ (where $\gcd(a,0)=a=\gcd(a,a)$) and $b=0$ (where $\gcd(0,a)=a=\gcd(a,0)$).

Since $\gcd$ is commutative and associative, for any multiset $S$ of nonnegative integers,
$$G_p(B)=\gcd\Big(\gcd(a,b),\ \gcd\big(\text{exponents of the untouched entries}\big)\Big),$$
and the inner value $\gcd(a,b)$ is unchanged by the move while the untouched exponents are literally unchanged. Hence $G_p$ is unchanged. $\blacksquare$

**Computation of $M$.** Let $a_1,\dots,a_{2026}>1$ be the initial numbers and set, for each prime $p$,
$$g_p:=\gcd\big(v_p(a_1),\dots,v_p(a_{2026})\big).$$
Note $g_p=0$ for all primes $p$ not dividing $a_1a_2\cdots a_{2026}$, and $g_p\ge 1$ for the (finitely many, and at least one) primes that divide some $a_i$.

By part (a), any run of the process ends in a state $B_{\mathrm{fin}}$ consisting of one integer $M>1$ and $2025$ ones. For each prime $p$, the multiset of $p$-exponents of $B_{\mathrm{fin}}$ is $\{v_p(M),0,0,\dots,0\}$, so
$$G_p(B_{\mathrm{fin}})=\gcd\big(v_p(M),0,\dots,0\big)=v_p(M).$$
On the other hand, by Claim 3 applied to every move of the run,
$$G_p(B_{\mathrm{fin}})=G_p(B_{\mathrm{init}})=g_p.$$
Therefore $v_p(M)=g_p$ for **every** prime $p$, which forces
$$\boxed{\,M=\prod_{p \text{ prime}} p^{\,\gcd\left(v_p(a_1),\ \dots,\ v_p(a_{2026})\right)}\,}$$
(the product has finitely many factors $\ne 1$). This value depends only on the initial numbers $a_1,\dots,a_{2026}$ and not on any of the choices made during the process. Hence $M$ is independent of all choices. $\blacksquare$

*(Consistency check: since each $a_i>1$, some prime divides $a_1$, so some $g_p\ge1$ and the formula indeed yields $M>1$, in agreement with part (a).)*
