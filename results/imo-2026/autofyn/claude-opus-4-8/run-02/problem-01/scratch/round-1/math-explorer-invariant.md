## imo-2026-01

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Per-prime valuation-gcd invariant (this is the answer to part (b)).** Fix a prime `p`. Look at the vector of `p`-adic valuations `(v_p(a_1),...,v_p(a_n))` of the `n=2026` board entries. A move on entries `m,n` replaces `(v_p(m), v_p(n)) = (a,b)` by `(min(a,b), max(a,b)-min(a,b))` — this is exactly one step of the subtractive Euclidean algorithm on the pair `(a,b)`, and it is classical that `gcd(min(a,b), max(a,b)-min(a,b)) = gcd(a,b)`. All other entries of the vector are untouched. Hence the **integer** `g_p := gcd(v_p(a_1), ..., v_p(a_n))` (gcd of the whole valuation-multiset, with the convention `gcd(x,0)=x`, `gcd(0,...,0)=0`) is invariant under every move, for every prime `p` simultaneously (moves act coordinatewise identically across all primes since they come from the same pair choice `(m,n)`).
  2. **Read off the final value directly from the invariant.** At the terminal state exactly one entry is `>1` (part (a), granted) and all others equal 1, i.e. have valuation `0` at every prime. So for each prime `p`, the final valuation vector is `(0,...,0,g_p^{final},0,...,0)` (one nonzero slot), whose gcd is just that nonzero entry itself: `gcd(0,...,0,x,0,...,0) = x`. Since the gcd is invariant and equals `g_p` from the start, the final nonzero valuation at `p` **must equal** `g_p`. This holds independent of which pair-choices were made, and independent of which slot ends up holding `M` — giving choice-independence of `M` directly, prime by prime.
  3. **Closed form for M.** Combining primes: `M = ∏_p p^{g_p} = ∏_p p^{gcd(v_p(a_1),...,v_p(a_n))}`, product over primes dividing at least one initial entry. This is NOT the ordinary gcd of the board (that uses `min` of valuations, not `gcd` of valuations) — it is a genuinely different, larger quantity in general (e.g. `gcd(4,2)=2` in the usual sense at a prime with valuations `2,1`, but here we'd use `gcd(2,1)=1`... actually re-verify per-prime it's the gcd of the *exponents themselves*, an integer combination, not a min).
  4. **Termination angle (part a) for the same lens**: a natural monovariant for termination is `Σ_i (number of prime factors of a_i, with multiplicity) = Σ_i Ω(a_i)`, or equivalently `Σ_p Σ_i v_p(a_i)`. Since replacing `(a,b)` by `(min(a,b), max(a,b)-min(a,b))` changes the *sum* `a+b` to `max(a,b)`, which is `≤ a+b` with equality iff `min(a,b)=0`, the total sum of valuations at `p` strictly decreases whenever both entries have positive valuation at `p`, and is unchanged (that prime coordinate) if one of the two is already zero there. Total `Σ v_p` over all primes is a natural-number monovariant, non-increasing, and it strictly decreases on any move where the chosen `m,n` share a common prime factor (i.e., `gcd(m,n) > 1`). This is the seed of a part (a) termination argument (not attempted further — that's the outliner's job) but is closely coupled to this lens's invariant.

- Dead ends (do not retry): The naive guess that "the overall board GCD (min of valuations) is invariant" is **false** — once other entries hit 1 (valuation 0 everywhere), the board's ordinary gcd collapses to 1 regardless of `M`, so ordinary-gcd-of-the-board is not a useful invariant post-move; the correct invariant is the **gcd of the valuation values themselves** (an arithmetic combination across the exponent multiset per prime), not a divisibility/min relation among the integers. Do not confuse these two.
