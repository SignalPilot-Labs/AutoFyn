## imo-2026-06

- Distinct openings (finite-state / covering-system lens):
  1. **Persistent-prime-set framing.** Define a prime `p` "persistent" if it divides infinitely many terms `a_n`. Conjecture/target lemma: the set `S` of persistent primes is finite, and (deep fact, the real crux) *every* term `a_n` (not just large ones) is divisible by some prime of `S` — i.e. `S` is not just "what recurs" but "what's structurally forced to recur to satisfy the gcd-with-everyone condition." Once this is established, let `M = ∏_{p∈S} p`. The claim is that for `n` large, `a_{n+1}` is determined purely by `a_n mod M` (all older constraints against `a_1,...,a_{n-1}` are automatically discharged because each `a_i` already shares an `S`-prime, and the candidate `a_{n+1}` will be built from `S`-primes too) — giving a **finite-state transition function** `f: Z/MZ → Z/MZ` (partial/eventual). A trajectory of a function on a finite set is eventually periodic (pigeonhole: only `M` possible states, so among `a_n mod M` for `n=1,...,M+1` two coincide), giving `T` with `a_{n+T} ≡ a_n (mod M)` eventually, hence (once one shows the "amount added" over one full period is *constant*, not just the residue) `a_{n+T} = a_n + L` with `M | L`.
  2. **Covering-system / density framing.** Instead of "persistent primes," phrase it as: the primes 2,3,5,7,... have density `1/p`; once enough small primes are "in play" as shared factors, the union of their residue classes covers a positive-density (eventually full, on the relevant window) subset of integers, which is what keeps the *gap* `a_{n+1}-a_n` bounded (this is really the same fact as opening 1 but framed via covering systems / Chinese Remainder rather than "persistence").
  3. **Gap-boundedness-first framing.** Attack the bound on `a_{n+1}-a_n` directly and treat "finite prime set" as a corollary rather than the primary target: show inductively that the gap can never grow past some absolute (or `a_1`-dependent) constant, by exhibiting, at every step, a specific small-multiple-of-an-already-used-prime candidate within bounded distance that is guaranteed (by an argument on why old constraints are already satisfied) to work. This is the hardest-but-most-direct route and is likely what the outliner needs to actually nail down; treat it as THE crux lemma no matter which framing wins.
  4. **Reverse/inverse-map framing (borrowed from aimo-0577 template).** In the ISL/Croatia problem `x_{k+1} = x_k+d` or `x_k/a`, periodicity was proved by showing the update map is a *bijection* on an explicit finite invariant set `S = {0<x<ad, gcd(x,d)=1}`, with an explicit inverse. Investigate whether an analogous explicit finite invariant set and an invertible transition map can be built here (state = residue mod `M`, or mod `M` together with a bounded "carry" from the most recent few gaps) — if the map is shown injective on a finite set, it's a bijection, giving periodicity for free without extra pigeonhole bookkeeping. This is a *technique* transplant, not a claim the same set works — the mechanism producing candidates here (global smallest-integer-search, not a fixed 2-branch recurrence) is structurally harder, so this may not directly transplant; flag as a "try, but expect friction" idea.

- Candidate technique(s): finite-state pigeonhole on a periodic dynamical system (KB: "Linear recurrences ... sequences are eventually periodic mod m", "Order of an element, Fermat/Euler: periodicity of a_n mod m"); CRT / modular arithmetic (KB "Modular arithmetic, CRT"); Dirichlet is NOT needed (we want existing small primes, not new primes in an AP); pigeonhole/extremal principle (KB "Pigeonhole / extremal principle"). The genuinely hard, non-off-the-shelf part is proving (a) finiteness of the persistent-prime set and (b) that the transition is eventually a well-defined function of the residue alone — these are NOT directly knowledge-base entries, they need a bespoke argument (likely: minimality of `a_{n+1}` forces reuse of small primes because searching for a "genuinely new" prime factor to satisfy one specific old constraint costs more than reusing an existing structural prime, by an explicit counting/size comparison).

- Cheap-kill candidates:
  - Parity is NOT a universal killer: verified numerically that the eventual period can contain terms not divisible by 2 (e.g. start=105, T=58,L=210, the term `a_58 = 315` is odd) — so "eventually all terms even" is FALSE in general; only true when 2 actually is one of the persistent primes AND is present in literally every residue-class visited, which is not automatic. Do not let the outliner assume 2 dominates.
  - A genuine cheap structural fact: **`a_{n+1}` must share a prime factor with `a_n` specifically**, and `a_n` has only finitely many (in fact ≤ log2(a_n)) prime factors — so `a_{n+1}` lies in a finite union of arithmetic progressions determined solely by `a_n`'s own factorization. This bounds the search "locally" but does not by itself bound the gap uniformly (need also to know one of those progressions clears the OLDER constraints quickly) — flag as a real but partial pruning tool.
  - Size/multiplicity count: within any window of `M` consecutive integers (`M` = the eventual persistent-prime modulus), by CRT there exist several integers hit by at least one persistent prime's residue class relevant at that state — this is the mechanism for the gap bound once `M` is fixed, but circular until finiteness of `S` is proved first.

- Small-case / intuition notes (all conjectural, numerically verified for the seeds tried, not proved):
  - `a_1=2,3,4,5,6,7,9,25` (any n with very few / a single small prime factor with no room to "diversify"): sequence is just the straight-up multiples of the smallest prime factor of `a_1` forever; `T=1`, `L=`(that prime).
  - `a_1=15=3·5`: `T=8, L=30=2·3·5`. `a_1=45=3²·5`: same `T=8, L=30` (same eventual modulus, matches `a_1`'s squarefree radical).
  - `a_1=105=3·5·7`: `T=58, L=210=2·3·5·7`. `a_1=35=5·7` (missing the factor 3!): ALSO settles to `T=34, L=210=2·3·5·7` — i.e. prime 3 gets pulled into the persistent set even though absent from `a_1`, presumably because 3 is small/dense enough that the minimal-successor search recruits it quickly. This is strong evidence that `S` is not simply "prime factors of `a_1`" but a genuinely emergent finite set (likely: prime factors of `a_1` unioned with the smallest primes needed to keep gaps small) — the outliner needs a real argument for *how* `S` stabilizes, not just "whatever divides `a_1`."
  - Measured gaps for the `a_1=105` run over 600 terms: gaps ∈ {2,3,4,6} only, max gap 6 — consistent with a modulus `M=210=2·3·5·7` (all gaps are among small multiples of persistent primes). This is direct (numerical, not proved) support for gap-boundedness once `S` stabilizes.
  - `L` always came out as an exact multiple of the apparent persistent-prime modulus `M` (`L=M` in every case tested) — conjecture `L` is always exactly `M` (or a small multiple of it), a fact the outliner should try to nail down and could simplify the endgame (avoid needing a separate "L is a multiple of M" lemma if L=M can be shown directly).
