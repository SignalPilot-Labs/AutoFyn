## imo-2026-02 — LENS: orientation/directed-angle gap, analytic/coordinate route

### Framing of the gap (precise, in coordinate-identity's own notation)
With B=(−p,0), C=(q,0), A=(a,h), h>0, K=B+u·e^{−iθ}(A−B), L=C+v·e^{iθ}(A−C), u,v>0, θ=∠KBA=∠ACL:
- Condition A (∠LBK=∠LNC) reduces to EA=Im[(L−B)(C−N)·conj((K−B)(L−N))]=0, i.e. **arg((L−B)/(K−B)) = arg((L−N)/(C−N)) (mod π)** — this is exactly the classical "directed line-angle mod π" statement (I checked algebraically: EA ∝ Im[W1·conj(W2)] with W1=(L−B)/(K−B), W2=(L−N)/(C−N), so EA=0 ⟺ arg W1 ≡ arg W2 (mod π)). So the coordinate route's gap and the synthetic-sigma-spiral route's gap (its Step-3 bullet "∠(CL,CK)=∠(MB,MK)") are the **same underlying fact**, just packaged differently.
- Condition B (∠LCK=∠BMK) is the mirror: FK(u)=0 ⟺ arg((K−C)/(L−C)) ≡ arg((K−M)/(B−M)) (mod π).
- Crucially: unsigned angle equality only gives the mod-π statement automatically(**up to sign**, since arg(ratio)∈(−π,0) or (0,π) and "unsigned angle" is |arg|). Interiority must pin the sign.

### Numerically-confirmed exact facts (strong evidence, not yet proof)
I built a fresh, from-scratch numerical model (independent script, `/tmp/probe_universal.py`, `/tmp/probe_filter.py`), solved the two unsigned-angle equations via `fsolve`, and **explicitly filtered to configurations that pass ALL FOUR interiority/betweenness constraints** (K∈int△BMC, L∈int△BNC via barycentric-sign test; K inside ∠LBA and L inside ∠ACK via the "ray between two rays" cross-product test). Over 97 genuinely-interior random configurations (random p,q,a,h,θ), the result is **exceptionless**:
- arg((L−B)/(K−B)) = arg((L−N)/(C−N)) **exactly** (not just mod π — full argument match, both always negative).
- arg((K−C)/(L−C)) = arg((K−M)/(B−M)) **exactly** (both always positive).
- Equivalently: sign(cross(BK,BL)) = sign(cross(NC,NL)) = −1 always; sign(cross(CL,CK)) = sign(cross(MB,MK)) = +1 always.

By contrast, when I do NOT filter for interiority (just solve the unsigned system from an arbitrary start), `fsolve` frequently lands on **spurious branches** where the signs mismatch (e.g. one probe found a solution satisfying both unsigned equations to 1e-14 with arg r1=+23.97°, arg r2=−23.97° — opposite signs, and separately a solution with arg r3=−3.35°, arg r4=+3.35° opposite signs) — confirming round 1's finding that unsigned equality alone is not enough; only the interior-filtered branch has matching signs.

### Route 1 (recommended): Elementary local half-plane / betweenness sign chain — fully coordinate-free
This is the most promising and *cheaply buildable* route; it needs only two elementary lemmas about triangles, applied four times, with no trigonometric identity and no continuity/limiting argument.

**Lemma H (half-plane containment).** If triangle PQR has one edge, say QR, contained in a line ℓ, then every point strictly interior to △PQR lies strictly on the same side of ℓ as the opposite vertex P is NOT what we want — restate correctly: if edge PQ of △PQR lies on line ℓ, every interior point of △PQR is strictly on the same side of ℓ as R. (Standard: interior points are positive combinations of the three vertices with positive barycentric weights; the R-weight being positive is exactly "same side as R".)

**Lemma R (ray-betweenness).** For any point X strictly interior to a triangle VYZ, the ray VX lies strictly between the rays VY, VZ; equivalently sign(cross(VY,VX)) = sign(cross(VY,VZ)) = sign(cross(VX,VZ)).

Apply these to get the four needed base/derived signs:
1. **Sign of cross(BA,BK):** Triangle BMC has edge BM ⊂ line AB (since B,M both on line AB — M is midpoint of AB), opposite vertex C. K ∈ int△BMC ⟹ by Lemma H, K is on the same side of line AB as C ⟹ sign(cross(BA,BK)) = sign(cross(BA,BC)). The latter is a FIXED sign depending only on the (A-above-BC) labeling convention — I verified cross(BA,BC) = −h(p+q) < 0 always (elementary, direct computation, no case split).
2. **Sign of cross(BK,BL):** Given hypothesis "K inside ∠LBA" ⟹ ray BK strictly between rays BA, BL. This is literally Lemma R applied to the (degenerate, angle-only) configuration at B with the two bounding rays BA, BL — or can be stated directly as a betweenness hypothesis giving sign(cross(BA,BK)) = sign(cross(BA,BL)) = sign(cross(BK,BL)). Combined with step 1: sign(cross(BK,BL)) = sign(cross(BA,BC)) = negative. ✓ matches numerics.
3. **Sign of cross(NC,NL):** By Lemma R directly at vertex N of triangle BNC (L interior ⟹ ray NL strictly between rays NB, NC): sign(cross(NC,NL)) = −sign(cross(NB,NC)) [since going from NC to NB one passes through NL — i.e. cross(NC,NL) and cross(NC,NB) share sign]. So sign(cross(NC,NL)) = sign(cross(NC,NB)) = −sign(cross(NB,NC)).
4. **Sign of cross(NB,NC):** N lies strictly on segment AC (N is the midpoint), so triangle N,B,C has the SAME orientation as triangle A,B,C (sliding a vertex along a cevian/side that does not pass through the fixed vertex B never crosses zero area, since line AC does not pass through B in a non-degenerate triangle — an explicit continuity-on-a-segment argument, NOT the same as the disputed global continuity argument since here the "moving point" ranges over a single segment with a single, uncrossable endpoint exclusion). Hence sign(cross(NB,NC)) = sign(cross(AB,AC)) [with matching vertex order], a FIXED universal sign for the given labeling. I verified numerically cross(NB,NC) > 0 always, matching sign(cross(MB,MC)) exactly (also checked: cross(NB,NC) ≡ cross(MB,MC) ≡ −½cross(BA,BC), an exact algebraic identity worth deriving symbolically for the final writeup).

Combining 3+4: sign(cross(NC,NL)) = −(positive) = negative, matching step 2's negative. **This closes Condition A's sign**, purely from Lemma H + Lemma R + one fixed-sign coordinate computation — no numerics needed in the final writeup, no continuity/connectedness argument, no global-orientation subtlety beyond the initial coordinate convention (A above BC).

The mirror argument (swap B↔C, M↔N, K↔L, θ sense flips) closes Condition B's sign (cross(CL,CK), cross(MB,MK) both positive) by the identical two lemmas applied at C and M.
