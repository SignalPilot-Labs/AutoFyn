## imo-2026-02 (synthetic lens)

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Hidden combinatorial symmetry.** Verified symbolically: the swap σ: B↔C, M↔N, K↔L (A, O fixed) maps the *whole* hypothesis set onto itself — condition (1) ("K inside angle LBA, L inside angle ACK") is invariant under σ; condition (2) (∠KBA=∠ACL) is invariant under σ; and conditions (3),(4) (∠LBK=∠LNC, ∠LCK=∠BMK) are **swapped by σ into each other** (σ sends (3)→(4) and (4)→(3)). So the data (A;B,M,K) and (A;C,N,L) play formally symmetric roles even though ABC is scalene (this is a *relabeling* symmetry, not a geometric isometry of the plane — do not conflate the two). This symmetry is the natural reason to expect OM=ON and should organize any proof: whatever quantity/relation is derived for the (B,M,K) side should have a mirror statement for (C,N,L) "for free" by re-running the same argument with the swap — an outline built to exploit this can halve the casework.
  2. **Midpoint-doubling (parallelogram) trick.** M, N are midpoints of AB, AC — the classical move for an angle condition anchored at a midpoint (∠BMK, ∠LNC) is to reflect a point through the midpoint to build a parallelogram: let K' = reflection of K over M (so ABKK'... i.e. BK'AK is a parallelogram since M bisects both AB and KK'); this turns ∠BMK into an angle of triangle AK'K or a angle at A/B under the translation K↦K' (vector K'−K = 2(M−K), and AK' ∥ BK, AK'=BK). Symmetrically reflect L over N to get L'. This is a concrete way to eliminate the "angle at a midpoint" conditions (3),(4) in favor of angles at A, converting the whole problem into one purely about angles/lengths anchored at A, B, C, K, L, K', L' — worth flagging as the natural synthetic key idea distinct from spiral-similarity framing.
  3. **Spiral similarity at A sending B→C, M→N.** Because AM = AB/2 and AN = AC/2, the spiral similarity ρ centered at A with ratio AC/AB and rotation angle ∠BAC (oriented B→C) sends B↦C and, automatically (by the fixed ratio), M↦N exactly. I verified this algebraically and numerically (ρ(M)=N to machine precision). **This is a real, provable geometric fact, not conjecture** — it should be usable as a lemma regardless of which approach is chosen. However, see Dead ends below: ρ(K) ≠ L in general, so do not expect ρ to carry the whole configuration.
  4. **Direct trig/law-of-cosines target.** OM²=OA²+AM²−2·OA·AM·cos∠OAM and ON²=OA²+AN²−2·OA·AN·cos∠OAN (with OA known common to both since O is circumcenter of AKL, giving OA=OK=OL trivially — not by itself informative). Since AM≠AN and I numerically found angle OAM ≠ angle OAN and no simple congruence OMK≅ONL, matching these two expressions requires a genuine trig identity coming from the three angle hypotheses — flagging this as the crux computation the synthetic proof must ultimately supply (whether reached via pure angle chase or via law of sines in the small triangles ABK, ACL, BKL/NLC, BMK).
  5. **Radical axis / concyclicity search** — I searched numerically for any concyclic 4-subset of {A,B,C,M,N,K,L} (aside from the trivial OA=OK=OL) and found **none** for a generic scalene triangle (cross-ratio test on all C(7,4)=35 subsets gave no real cross-ratios). This rules out "K,L,M,N concyclic" or "B,K,L,C concyclic" style shortcuts as the mechanism — the intended synthetic proof likely does NOT go through a single clean auxiliary circle through the given points; expect it to need a genuinely constructed auxiliary point (e.g. K', L' from the doubling trick, or an isogonal/spiral-similarity image) before concyclicity appears.

- Dead ends (do not retry):
  - **ρ(K)=L is FALSE.** I tested numerically (scalene triangle A=(0,0),B=(5,0),C=(1.3,4.1), solving the 3 angle equations for K,L with one free parameter): the spiral similarity ρ centered A sending B→C, M→N does **not** send K to L (numeric gap ≈0.89, far from the ~1e-16 noise floor that ρ(M)=N achieved). Any approach assuming a single spiral similarity at A carries (B,M,K)↦(C,N,L) wholesale is wrong; ρ only legitimately carries the B,M pair.
  - **No 4-point concyclicity among {A,B,C,M,N,K,L}** in the generic case (see opening 5) — don't waste a round hunting for "K,L,M,N concyclic" or similar without first constructing an auxiliary point.
  - **OB=OC does NOT hold** (numerically OB≈3.58, OC≈3.09 in my test triangle) — O is NOT the circumcenter of ABC and is NOT on the perpendicular bisector of BC, even though MN∥BC. So the perpendicular bisector of MN (through O) is a genuinely different line from the perpendicular bisector of BC — don't conflate them.
  - **KM≠LN and angle KOM≠angle LON** numerically (KM≈1.002 vs LN≈1.039; angle KOM≈31.6° vs angle LON≈33.2°) — so there is no simple SAS congruence ΔOMK≅ΔONL to shortcut the proof; the equality OM=ON is a genuinely "balanced" (not termwise-congruent) identity, consistent with needing the full σ-symmetry / trig identity rather than a local congruence.

- Small-case / intuition notes (all labeled conjecture/numeric evidence only, scalene triangle A=(0,0), B=(5,0), C=(1.3,4.1)):
  - The hypothesis system is 3 angle-equations in 4 unknowns (2 dof for K, 2 for L) ⇒ generically a **1-parameter family** of valid (K,L) configurations satisfying the equalities (before imposing the "inside" containment constraints, which just cut this family to an open sub-arc). I solved this family for 7 values of the free parameter p=∠KBA ∈ [0.15,0.5] rad and in every case: (a) a valid solution exists with K inside △BMC and L inside △BNC (containment constraints are satisfiable, not vacuous), and (b) **OM=ON held to ~1e-10 relative precision in every case**, confirming the claim is a genuine identity holding along the whole family, not a coincidence of one configuration.
  - AK≠AL, AM≠AN in general (scalene) — the circle AKL is not isosceles-symmetric in an obvious way; whatever produces OM=ON must be a non-trivial relation, not "K,L symmetric about the A-angle-bisector."
