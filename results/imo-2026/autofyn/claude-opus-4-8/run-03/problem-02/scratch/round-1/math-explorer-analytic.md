## imo-2026-02

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Coordinate/complex bash on the whole 1-parameter family.** Numerically (see below) the conditions do NOT pin down a unique (K,L): they define a genuine 1-parameter family, naturally parametrized by θ := ∠KBA = ∠ACL. The correct analytic target is therefore *not* "solve for K, L, plug into O, check OM=ON" but rather "show OM(θ) = ON(θ) identically as θ ranges over the admissible interval" — i.e. an identity in one free parameter, not a single numeric check. Concretely: place B, C on the x-axis (using MN ∥ BC, midline theorem, so this also makes MN horizontal); rK(θ), rL(θ) are then the two real roots of the 2x2 system coming from equations 4–5 (∠LBK=∠LNC, ∠LCK=∠BMK); express O = circumcenter(A,K(θ),L(θ)) via the standard determinant formula; show the x-coordinate of O is the constant (M+N)_x/2, independent of θ. This is a finite, well-defined algebraic identity to chase (candidate for symbolic/Gröbner verification with sympy before hand-proof).
  2. **Power-of-a-point / radical-axis framing.** OM=ON ⟺ pow(M, ⊙AKL) = pow(N, ⊙AKL) (tautologically, same circle & radius). Numerically confirmed equal (see data). A synthetic route: find an auxiliary circle ω through both M and N whose center lies on line MN's own perpendicular bisector automatically (e.g. circle with diameter MN, or a circle related to B, C, M, N), then show O lies on the radical axis of ⊙(AKL) and ω — if ω's center is on the perp bisector of MN, and O is shown to lie on that same radical axis intersected appropriately, OM=ON follows. (Seed idea only — not developed.)
  3. **Continuity/monovariant argument over the family.** Since the configuration is a genuine continuum (θ free), one could avoid solving for K, L in coordinates altogether and instead argue synthetically that OM−ON, viewed as a function of θ, is forced to vanish identically by some angle-chase invariant that holds at every θ (not just an endpoint) — i.e. look for a *pointwise* synthetic reason (e.g., a spiral similarity or a fixed auxiliary point) that reproduces OM=ON for the general θ, rather than pinning a specific configuration.

- Dead ends (do not retry, from my own numerical probing):
  - **B,C,K,L concyclic** — false (concyclic-determinant nonzero and varies substantially with θ, e.g. 21.9, 40.6, 53.2 across samples — not even close to zero).
  - **M,N,K,L concyclic** — false but numerically small-ish (det ≈ 0.45–0.56 in units where other dets are ~20–130); tempting but confirmed NOT zero across the family (varies smoothly with θ, never hits 0 in the sampled range) — do not assume this concyclicity in the proof.
  - **B,N,K,L, C,M,K,L, N,C,K,L, M,B,K,L concyclic** — all false (large nonzero determinants).
  - **"Spiral similarity between triangle BKM and triangle CLN"** — checked angles; only ONE angle coincides (∠KBM = ∠LCN), but this is a *tautology*: M lies on ray BA so ∠KBM=∠KBA=θ automatically, and similarly N on ray CA gives ∠LCN=∠LCA=θ=∠KBA by hypothesis (eq. 3) — so this "match" carries zero new information. The other two angles of BKM vs CLN are NOT equal (e.g. 146.2° vs 133.0°, 14.1° vs 27.3° in one sample) — the triangles are NOT similar. Do not pursue this "spiral similarity" framing.

- Small-case / intuition notes (all **conjectural / numerical**, precision ~1e-14, two independent scalene non-special triangles tested):
  - The conditions define a genuine **1-parameter family** of valid (K,L), not a unique pair: continuation in θ = ∠KBA=∠ACL over an interval (bounded by where K stays inside △BMC and L inside △BNC) produced 16–40 valid solutions per sweep, all satisfying the containment constraints (K inside BMC, L inside BNC checked via barycentric sign test — always True on the interior of the range tested).
  - **OM = ON holds to ~1e-15 for every θ in the family**, in both test triangles (A=(0,4),B=(-3,0),C=(5,0) and the non-axis-aligned A=(1.3,5.2),B=(-2.7,0.4),C=(4.1,-0.9)).
  - **Stronger discovered invariant**: O does not merely satisfy OM=ON — its **projection onto the direction of MN is constant** (equal to the projection of the midpoint of MN), to ~1e-10, across the whole θ-family, in both triangles. Equivalently: *O always lies exactly on the perpendicular bisector of segment MN*, not just at one configuration but along the entire admissible range of θ. This is the strongest and most useful hidden structural fact found — it upgrades the target from a single-point coordinate check to a clean algebraic identity (one real function of θ is constant), which is a natural target for a symbolic (sympy) or careful synthetic proof.
  - power(M, ⊙AKL) = power(N, ⊙AKL) exactly (confirmed, e.g. both ≈ −4.087078760708... in one sample) — both M and N lie strictly **inside** circle (AKL) in the sampled configurations (negative power). This is the direct restatement of OM=ON via a common circle/radius and is not independent new information, but flags that an "M, N both inside ⊙AKL, with equal power" framing is consistent and could support a power-of-a-point proof strategy if a second circle through M, N can be constructed.
  - Sanity-checked classical fact: MN ∥ BC and |MN| = |BC|/2 (midline theorem) in both triangles — safe building block for any coordinate setup.
