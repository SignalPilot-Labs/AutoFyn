## imo-2026-02

- Distinct openings (this route's contribution — spiral similarity / special-point / moving-points lens):
  1. **Moving-points reduction (strongest lead).** The three angle equalities (∠KBA=∠ACL, ∠LBK=∠LNC, ∠LCK=∠BMK) are only 3 real equations in the 4 real unknowns (coordinates of K, L), so for a fixed triangle ABC the valid configurations (K,L) form a genuine 1-parameter family, not a single point. I solved this numerically (fsolve/least_squares, scipy) for two different triangles and swept the free parameter. **Result: OM=ON holds to machine precision (residuals ~1e-14) at every point along the whole family**, and O sweeps a continuous locus that is exactly the perpendicular bisector of MN (verified: in a frame where MN is horizontal, O's x-coordinate is numerically constant and equals the x-coordinate of the midpoint of MN, to 1e-14 relative precision, for a full range of the free parameter). This is a much stronger fact than "OM=ON for the specific configuration": it says **O always lies on the fixed line perp-bisector(MN), independent of which point on the 1-parameter family (K,L) is chosen**. This suggests the intended proof does NOT need to pin down K, L exactly — a valid strategy is to prove directly that O lies on perp-bisector(MN) using only a subset of the hypotheses, or to show the defining conditions force A, K, L to lie on a fixed circle/locus whose center's locus is that bisector. This is consistent with a "moving point" / identity-on-a-family style argument (per lens (b)): if one shows the claim OM=ON is (i) true for one member of the family and (ii) an algebraic identity of bounded degree in the free parameter, a finite check would suffice — but note this is a continuous real-parameter family, not the usual finite/projective moving-points setup (which needs a variety over ℂ or a rational parametrization); converting it to a genuine "moving points" degree argument would require first exhibiting an algebraic (not just angle-chase) parametrization of the family, which I did not attempt (out of scope for exploration).
  2. **Spiral similarity at A: ruled out (checked and rejected).** Tested whether triangle ABK ~ triangle ACL via a spiral similarity centered at A (i.e., BK/BA = CL/CA together with the given ∠KBA=∠ACL, which would be an SAS similarity). Numerically false: BK/BA ≈ 0.288 vs CL/CA ≈ 0.253, and ∠BAK ≈ 8.15° vs ∠CAL ≈ 6.87° — not equal. So K, L are NOT simply images of each other under a spiral similarity fixing A that also fixes B↔C. This specific clean hypothesis is dead; don't retry it as stated.
  3. **MKNL concyclic: ruled out.** Tested whether M, K, N, L are concyclic (would explain the ∠LNC / ∠BMK conditions as inscribed-angle conditions in a shared circle through M, N). The 4-point concyclicity determinant is small in absolute terms but demonstrably nonzero and varies smoothly (not converging to 0) across the family — not an identity, just numerical smallness from the specific coordinate scale. Ruled out as an exact structural fact.
  4. **Special-point identification: negative result.** O is NOT the circumcenter of ABC, NOT the orthocenter, and NOT a fixed point at all (it moves along the family) — it does coincide in x-coordinate with the nine-point center of ABC in the first test triangle, but that is exactly restating "O lies on perp-bisector(MN)" (the nine-point center also lies on that same perpendicular bisector, as a known fact: the nine-point center is the circumcenter of the medial triangle MNP, which is equidistant from M and N). This is likely not a coincidence and points to a genuinely promising idea: **relate O to the medial-triangle's own circumcenter (the nine-point center of ABC)** — e.g. show OM=ON via the same argument that shows the nine-point center is equidistant from M, N (both being on the circle through the three midpoints), perhaps by relating the circle (AKL) to the medial-triangle's circumcircle (nine-point circle) via inversion or a projective correspondence. This is a concrete, distinct idea from lenses (a)/(c) requested by the dispatch and worth flagging to the outliner as a promising "different top-level target": instead of computing O directly, show that A, K, L project onto (or are inversive images of) points related to the nine-point circle.
  5. **Angle-chase "disguised center" check (lens a/c):** the conditions ∠LBK=∠LNC and ∠LCK=∠BMK pair an angle at B (resp. C) with an angle at N (resp. M) — i.e., cross the two midpoints. This "crossed" pairing (K's defining angle at C references M, the midpoint on the OTHER side from where K lives near B; similarly L's defining angle at B references N) is a strong hint that the construction is designed to force a spiral similarity or Miquel-type incidence between the two midpoint-triangles (BMC-family and BNC-family) rather than a naive local symmetry. I did not find a single clean cyclic quadrilateral or fixed spiral center reproducing this pairing directly (see dead-ends above), so if the outliner pursues this, expect to build a two-step spiral similarity (one at B, one at C) rather than one global one.
