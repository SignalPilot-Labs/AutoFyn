## imo-2026-04

### Setup / notation used below
Triangle angles (X,Y,Z), X+Y+Z=180°. A move on this triangle: pick one angle, say X (the "split" angle — geometrically Mulan puts P on the side opposite that vertex), choose p1+p2=X with p1,p2 ∈ (0,X) freely (continuum choice), producing two candidate triangles
  child1 = (Y, p1, Z+p2),  child2 = (Z, p2, Y+p1)
(verified in sympy: both sum to X+Y+Z=180). Shan-Yu keeps exactly one; the "check T has angle θ" happens only when the loop restarts, so Shan-Yu will always discard whichever child contains θ if only one of them does, and is FORCED (zero real choice) whenever Mulan arranges that exactly one child contains θ.

### Distinct openings found from this lens

1. **One-move win exists in two disjoint regimes** (proved directly, not conjectural):
   - **θ = 90° is universal**: for ANY starting triangle, split the largest angle X (≥60°, and the unique angle ≥90° if the triangle is right/obtuse) into p1=90−Y, p2=90−Z where Y,Z are the other two angles. Then p1+p2 = 180−Y−Z = X automatically, and BOTH children get an angle of exactly 90° (child1's third angle Z+p2=90, child2's third angle Y+p1=90). Verified numerically on 5 random triangles (all valid splits, both children hit 90.0 exactly). Since Shan-Yu literally has no non-losing child to keep, θ=90° is won in exactly one move, no matter what triangle Shan-Yu builds. This identity 2θ=180 (equivalently θ=90) is the ONLY θ for which "put θ into both children of a single split" is achievable in general (shown algebraically: forcing θ into both child1 and child2 simultaneously via one split of X requires either the special triangle condition X=2θ, or the universal identity X+Y+Z=2θ, i.e. θ=90).
   - **Any θ for which some current angle equals exactly 2θ**: split that angle into θ+θ; both children get θ, forced win. This is a "special-triangle" win, not universal — Shan-Yu simply avoids ever having an angle equal to 2θ (easy, it's just one forbidden real value at each step) as long as θ≠90.

2. **Forced-transfer / residue-mod-θ reduction (the pure-shave sub-game).** If Mulan splits an angle X>θ into (θ, X−θ) — pairing the θ-piece with Y (say) — the resulting child1=(Y,θ,Z+X−θ) contains θ so Shan-Yu is FORCED (no real choice) to keep child2=(Z, X−θ, Y+θ). This is exactly "subtract θ from lineage X, add θ to lineage Y, leave Z untouched" and Mulan can choose which of the other two angles receives the θ. Doing this repeatedly (whenever some current angle >θ) is entirely deterministic — Shan-Yu has zero agency in any round where Mulan places θ into exactly one child. Because every move only ever changes a lineage's value by ±θ, **each of the three lineages' values mod θ is invariant under this restricted strategy**. Consequence: pure-shave play can force an EXACT hit of θ (or of 2θ, which mod θ is the same residue class, feeding case 1's second bullet) **if and only if at least one of the original triangle's three angles is an exact positive integer multiple of θ**. In that case Mulan wins by shaving that lineage down by θ repeatedly (k−1 shaves if the angle is kθ), with Shan-Yu never having a real decision at any step — a clean, fully constructive sufficient condition.

3. **Mirror move producing 180−θ unconditionally.** A slightly different split of the same triangle (target Z+p2=θ in child1, i.e. p2=θ−Z, p1=X−θ+Z) again forces Shan-Yu into child2 = (Z, θ−Z, 180−θ) — verified in sympy — which contains the FIXED value 180−θ regardless of the rest of the triangle, whenever Z<θ<Z+X (i.e. Z<θ and Y<180−θ). By symmetry (swapping the roles of θ and 180−θ in the same construction) there is a matching move that instead forces θ itself into the survivor whenever the triangle has an angle Z<180−θ and another angle Y<θ: survivor becomes (Z, 180−θ−Z, θ) — an immediate win. This gives a *second*, more permissive one-move win condition (not requiring an exact multiple, just an inequality on two of the three angles), worth checking against case 1/2 for overlap and against what a defensive Shan-Yu triangle must avoid.

4. **Open/harder question — necessity.** For a triangle where NO angle is an integer multiple of θ (Shan-Yu's easy generic defense against opening 2), and where the case-3 inequality condition also fails (Shan-Yu can likely arrange this too — needs checking), Mulan must use "impure" splits that don't place θ in exactly one child, which reintroduces genuine choice for Shan-Yu. Whether Mulan can, in finitely many further steps, force a lineage to become ≡0 (mod θ) despite Shan-Yu's real choices, or whether Shan-Yu can maintain "no lineage ≡ 0 mod θ" as a permanent invariant against ALL strategies (not just pure-shave ones), is the crux still open from this lens. Candidate dividing line to test next: θ commensurable with 180° (θ = 180°·p/q, rationals) vs θ an irrational multiple of 180°. Since the invariant total is always exactly 180°, and 180 mod θ = 0 exactly when θ = 180/n for a positive integer n, the values θ=180/n (n=2,3,4,5,...: 90,60,45,36,30,...) look structurally special (residues of the three angles must mod-θ-sum to 0, a nontrivial constraint on Shan-Yu), but this needs care — θ=90 already wins by a DIFFERENT mechanism (case 1) unrelated to residues, so the residue argument alone should not be assumed to be the deciding invariant; it is only proven to be sufficient (opening 2), not shown necessary.

### Candidate technique(s)
- Reduction to a discrete "chip-transfer"/Euclidean-algorithm-style combinatorial game on the triple of angles once Mulan restricts to θ-exact splits (a genuinely new framing, not standard KB material, but structurally similar to `invariants-and-monovariants` write-ups in the KB).
- Residue-class invariant mod θ (a monovariant/invariant argument) as the natural tool for proving a *defense* for Shan-Yu when no angle is a multiple of θ — this is the natural place to look for an impossibility proof for some θ.
- Density / continuity argument: since Mulan has continuum control over each split, "reachability of exact θ" arguments should be paired with an argument about what real-valued relations Shan-Yu can enforce to block her (e.g., choosing the initial triangle's angles to be algebraically independent from θ, or from 180° in a suitable sense) — this points toward needing a rigorous algebraic-independence / commensurability argument, not just small-case pattern matching.

### Cheap-kill candidates
- θ=90°: settled in one move for every triangle (see above) — no need for heavy machinery on this instance, it's a base case / sanity check for the general framework, and its identity 2θ=180° should show up naturally as a boundary case in whatever the final characterization turns out to be.
- Parity/degenerate check: θ must strictly be in (0°,180°); an angle can approach but never equal 0° or 180° (P strictly interior to a side), so no boundary degeneracy issues to special-case beyond that.
- Any θ ≥ 180°/... not applicable since domain restricts 0<θ<180 already.
