## imo-2026-03 (lens: Claim (B) for not-fully-paired F, c<n cuts)

- **Distinct openings surfaced:**
  1. **Single-Residual Generalization of Cross-Term Vanishing.** `greedy-halving-adversary`'s Lemma 18 only handles $A(F)=0$ (F exactly pairs up). By the certified `leftover-formula`/`odd-run-reduction-lemma`, the next-simplest non-fully-paired shape is $F=\{v\}\cup(\text{t exact pairs})$ — exactly ONE unpaired residual (odd-run length $\ell(F)=1$), so $u_F(x)\equiv\mathbb1[x<v]$ identically (clean indicator, same mechanism as Lemma 3's proof). Applying the certified general cross-term identity (`cross-term-identity-threshold`) with this $u_F$ gives, after using `safe-window-lemma` ($G'$'s pieces all $\le p_2$) exactly as in the proof of `half-window-vanishing-lemma`/Lemma 18: split the interaction integral at $\min(v,p_2)$ and at $p_2$. If $v\ge p_2$ (as in the numeric witness below, where $v=p_2$ exactly), the integral $\int_0^v u_F v_{G'}$ reduces to $\int_0^{p_2} v_{G'}$ only (since $v_{G'}\equiv0$ on $[p_2,r)$ by Safe-Window). This is algebraically the *same reduced target* the Half-Window Vanishing Lemma closed for $c_1=1$ — i.e. this route reduces "single-residual $F$, arbitrary $c<n$" to a *window-bound on $[0,p_2)$*, structurally parallel to (not identical to — $F$ now has an arbitrary number of fragments $<p_2$ below the residual, not just two) the already-closed $c_1=1$ case. This looks like the single most promising extension: it targets exactly the case that the numeric probe below shows is where equality with $a_n$ actually occurs.
  2. **Direct exchange/perturbation argument on $F^*$-with-one-fewer-cut.** Since $F^*=\{p_2,\dots,p_n,p_{n+1},p_{n+1}\}$ (using all $n$ cuts, certified in `claim-a-achievability-construction`, with the round-8-corrected cut count) is the actual Claim-(A) optimum but leaves zero tail budget, the natural analog with $c=n-1$ cuts is to *merge two adjacent fragments of $F^*$ back together* (freeing exactly one cut for tail refinement) and ask: for every choice of which two fragments to re-merge, and every use of the freed cut on the tail, does $A$ stay $\ge a_n$? This is a small, finite (one real parameter for the freed cut's split point, finitely many merge choices) family — tractable for a direct case-by-case `single-cut-perturbation-identity` (Lemma 14, already certified) computation rather than a general theorem. It would not close the general claim but would test whether the "near-$F^*$" analog is actually competitive for Xiang Yu (the numeric probe below suggests the true worst case at $c=2,n=3$ is $F=\{p_2,p_3,p_3\}$, which is *not* this merge-family — it merges $p_4,p_4$ into $p_3$ conceptually rather than un-merging $F^*$ — worth checking whether the general worst-case shape is "shift the split threshold up by one ladder level" rather than "un-merge $F^*$").
  3. **Case-split by $\ell(F)$ (odd-run length of $F$), induct upward from $\ell(F)=1$.** `odd-run-reduction-lemma` already gives closed-form $A$ for any vertex with several simultaneous ties, so the not-fully-paired family decomposes by $\ell(F)\in\{1,2,3,\dots\}$ (parity forced by `parity-coincidence-and-zero-iff-dead-end`'s $\ell(S)\equiv|S|\bmod2$ fact, though that lemma is about the *whole* multiset $S=F\cup G'$, not $F$ alone — re-derive for $F$ alone before reusing). Close $\ell(F)=1$ first via opening (1) above; this is very likely where the Vertex-Minimum Theorem's extremal configurations actually live (see numeric evidence below — every hard witness on file so far, going back to round 7's $F=\{4,2,2\}/15$, has $\ell(F)=1$), so closing just this layer may already capture the true worst case, leaving $\ell(F)\ge2$ as a residual that might turn out to never bind (worth an explicit domination check: does $\ell(F)\ge2$ ever attain a value close to $a_n$, or is it always strictly larger — this is checkable numerically before investing in a general-$\ell$ proof).
