## imo-2026-03

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Reduce the claiming phase to a pure sorting fact.** Since all piece lengths are fixed and public before claiming begins, and there is no interaction beyond "which pieces remain," the alternating-claim sub-game has a *forced* optimal line: greedily take the largest remaining piece is dominant for both players (standard exchange argument — deviating only lets the opponent grab the best remaining item, weakly hurting you). Hence, given the final multiset of piece lengths, Liu Bang's guaranteed total is **exactly** the sum of the odd-ranked pieces (1st, 3rd, 5th, ... largest) in descending sorted order. This collapses the whole problem to a two-stage *continuous partition* minimax: Liu Bang picks ≤n cut points to define a partition, Xiang Yu picks ≤n more cuts (subdividing Liu Bang's pieces) to minimize the resulting odd-rank sum, Liu Bang wants to maximize the guaranteed worst case. This reduction should be the outline's first lemma — it is elementary and provable from scratch, not a citation.
  2. **The "pairing/tie exploit" — Xiang Yu's structural weapon.** If the final piece multiset can be partitioned into pairs of *equal* values (adjacent after sorting), each pair splits exactly 50/50 between the players regardless of where it falls in the alternation (a block of `2t` equal-valued items always splits `t`–`t`). Consequence: an **equal partition by Liu Bang is bad** — Xiang Yu can always drive Liu Bang down to exactly 1/2 by bisecting just enough of Liu Bang's equal pieces to make the leftover "untouched" block even in count (he needs only 0 or 1 extra cut to fix parity: if `n+1` is even, don't cut at all; if `n+1` is odd, bisect exactly one piece). This kills the naive "Liu Bang uses n equal pieces" idea outright.
  3. **A second, subtler pairing exploit that also kills the natural non-equal "n copies of the same big piece" construction.** I initially conjectured Liu Bang's optimal construction is 1 piece of size `1/(2n+1)` and `n` pieces of size `2/(2n+1)` (so that Xiang Yu bisecting all `n` big pieces yields `2n+1` equal pieces, giving Liu Bang `(n+1)/(2n+1)`). This is **refuted**: because the `n` big pieces are *equal to each other*, Xiang Yu can instead leave them all untouched (an even-count block when `n` is even, contributing net 0 advantage) and merely bisect the one small leftover piece into two more equal pieces — again forcing an even/paired multiset and dropping Liu Bang all the way to exactly `1/2` for `n=2`. Verified numerically (see below). So **any Liu Bang construction with repeated piece sizes is vulnerable**; the winning construction needs *all distinct* piece sizes, arranged so no sub-collection's sum/half matches another piece.
  4. **Geometric/binary doubling construction — the strongest opening found.** Take Liu Bang's `n` points so the `n+1` initial pieces have lengths `1, 2, 4, ..., 2^n` (scaled to sum 1), i.e. lengths `2^i/(2^{n+1}-1)` for `i=0..n`. Because of the binary/Zeckendorf-style uniqueness of subset sums in a doubling sequence, this partition resists both pairing exploits above (this is exactly the `n=1` case generalized: `n=1` gives pieces `1/3, 2/3`, which I prove by hand below gives Liu Bang exactly `2/3`). Numerically, for `n=2` (pieces `1/7, 2/7, 4/7`), the best response found for Xiang Yu across both 1-point and 2-point strategies (random search + local refinement, ~10^5 trials) always gives Liu Bang exactly `4/7`, matching the top piece value `2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)`.
  - **Conjecture:** `c(n) = 2^n / (2^{n+1} - 1)`. Verified rigorously for `n=1` (full case analysis, see below); numerically supported for `n=2` (value `4/7`, robust to Xiang Yu using 1 or 2 points, random+local-search optimization, no counterexample found beating it). NOT proven for general `n`, and I did not verify Liu Bang cannot do strictly better than the doubling construction for `n=2` (only that Xiang Yu cannot beat 4/7 against it) — an outer max-min search over Liu Bang's own point placement was noisy and did not cleanly settle above/below 4/7 (best found ~0.56–0.58 with weaker inner solvers, consistent with 4/7≈0.571 once the inner solver was strengthened). This conjecture is the strongest lead but needs an actual proof, not just simulation.

- Small-case / intuition notes (all labeled conjecture except where marked PROVEN):

Full case analysis (median-maximization over Xiang Yu's single split of the larger piece) shows Xiang Yu's best response gives Liu Bang `(1+a)/2` for `a ≤ 1/3` and `1-a` for `a ≥ 1/3`; this is maximized at `a=1/3`, giving `c(1) = 2/3`, attained by the partition `(1/3, 2/3)` — Xiang Yu's optimal reply there is to bisect the `2/3` piece into two `1/3`'s, producing three equal `1/3` pieces; Liu Bang (first mover, 3 pieces) takes 2 of the 3, total `2/3`.
  - **Numerical conjecture, n=2:** the doubling partition `(1/7, 2/7, 4/7)` (points at `1/7, 3/7`) guarantees Liu Bang `4/7`, robust against random+local-search optimization over Xiang Yu's 1- or 2-point responses (best found is exactly `4/7`, matching the top piece `2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)` with `n=2`). This is evidence, not proof — no exhaustive/analytic verification that `4/7` is truly optimal for Xiang Yu, nor that Liu Bang cannot beat `4/7` with a non-doubling construction (outer search over Liu Bang's placement was inconclusive with the compute budget available).
  - **Working conjecture for general n:** `c(n) = 2^n / (2^{n+1} - 1)`, via the binary-doubling partition `2^i/(2^{n+1}-1)` for `i = 0, ..., n`. Sanity: `c(n) → 1/2^+` as `n → ∞`; `c(1) = 2/3` (matches proof); `c(2) = 4/7` (matches numerics).
