- Distinct openings:
  1. **Exact small-n computation → guess-and-prove.** Compute c(1), c(2), c(3) exactly (analytically for n=1, numerically/near-exactly for n=2,3), spot the closed form, then split the proof into (a) a **construction** for Liu Bang showing he can guarantee ≥ c(n), and (b) an **adversary strategy** for Xiang Yu showing she can hold Liu Bang to ≤ c(n). This is the standard extremal-problem shape and is what the numerics below support.
  2. **Superincreasing-partition framing.** The extremal Liu Bang construction found numerically is: cut the stick into n+1 pieces of lengths proportional to 1, 2, 4, …, 2^n (geometric ratio 2), i.e. mark points at cumulative fractions (2^k−1)/(2^{n+1}−1) for k=1,…,n. This is a **superincreasing sequence**: each piece is strictly larger than the sum of all smaller pieces (2^k > 2^{k-1}+…+1 = 2^k − 1). That structural property is likely the key inductive invariant for the lower-bound proof: no matter how Xiang Yu subdivides pieces further, the piece descended from the current-largest block can be shown to remain (weakly) the largest available block at Liu Bang's turn, or a clean accounting argument tracks how much of each geometric "tier" each player can secure.
  3. **Piece-claiming subgame is provably "always take the largest remaining piece."** This reduces the whole problem to a **combinatorial game about interval lengths only** (not geometry) once the final multiset of piece lengths is fixed — the claiming phase has a closed-form optimal policy (see Cheap-kill / small-case verification below), so the real content of the problem is entirely in the **point-placement minimax**, not the claiming phase. This suggests attacking the problem as: (i) prove the claiming-subgame value formula (greedy-alternating-sum of sorted pieces) as a clean standalone lemma, (ii) reduce to optimizing this piecewise-linear function of the cut positions under Liu-then-Xiang alternation.
  4. **Scaling/self-similarity opening.** Because the found optimal configuration is geometric-ratio-2 in the piece sizes, there may be a self-similar / recursive relation c(n) in terms of c(n−1): note 2^n/(2^{n+1}−1) satisfies c(n) = 1/(2 − 2^{-n}), and 1 − c(n) = (2^n − 1)/(2^{n+1} − 1) = c(n−1)·(2^n−1)/2^{n-1}... (not a clean recursion yet, but the structure 2^{n+1}−1 = 2·(2^n−1)+1 suggests an inductive "peel off the smallest piece" argument could carry an induction on n.)

- Dead ends (do not retry):
  - **Equal-spacing constructions** (Liu Bang divides into n+1 *equal* pieces) are suboptimal and should not be pursued as the extremal configuration. Verified numerically: for n=2, Liu points at {1/3, 2/3} (equal thirds) only guarantee Liu Bang **1/2**, strictly worse than the 4/7 achievable via geometric-ratio spacing — Xiang Yu can drive Liu Bang's total down toward 1/2 by cutting just inside one of Liu's existing points, creating an (almost-)degenerate tiny sliver that "uses up" one of Liu Bang's picks cheaply. Do not conjecture c(n) via equal partition into 2n+1 pieces.
  - **Guess (n+1)/(2n+1)** (matches n=1 = 2/3 but predicts c(2) = 3/5 = 0.6): refuted numerically — best achievable for n=2 is 4/7 ≈ 0.5714 < 0.6, confirmed by three independent numeric methods (differential evolution nested minimax, fine deterministic grid search, and random-restart search), all capping out at 4/7 and never exceeding it.

- Small-case / intuition notes (labeled by confidence):
  - **n=1: PROVED analytically** (not just numerically). Liu Bang marks x=1/3 (WLOG x≤1/2 by symmetry). Case analysis on where Xiang Yu's single point y falls relative to x shows: if y splits the length-x piece, Liu Bang's total → (1−x)+min(a,x−a) which is minimized (over y) approaching 1−x as a→0 (infimum, not attained since points must be distinct); if y splits the length-(1−x) piece into b, (1−x)−b, the median of {x, b, (1−x)−b} — hence Xiang Yu's own claimed amount — is exactly max(x, (1-x)/2) rather, worked out precisely: for x ≤ 1/3 Xiang Yu's best gives Liu Bang exactly (1+x)/2 (increasing in x); for x ≥ 1/3 Xiang Yu's best gives Liu Bang exactly 1−x (decreasing in x, attained exactly by choosing y so the split piece exactly equals x, producing pieces {x, x, 1−2x}). These two branches meet and Liu Bang's guarantee is maximized at x = 1/3, giving **c(1) = 2/3**, attained by the extremal 3-piece configuration {1/3, 1/3, 1/3} (all equal — a coincidence of n=1 that does NOT generalize, see dead end above).
  - **n=2: CONJECTURED, strong numerical evidence.** Liu Bang marks points at {1/7, 3/7} (pieces of length 1/7, 2/7, 4/7 — geometric ratio 2). Three independent search methods (nested differential evolution with many restarts, a fine deterministic 2D grid search over Xiang Yu's response with resolution up to 800×800, and 60 random-restart local searches) all agree: Xiang Yu's best response drives Liu Bang's total down to exactly **4/7 ≈ 0.571429**, and no Liu Bang configuration found beats 4/7 (best found alternatives cap out at ≈0.558–0.575, all either equal to or below 4/7 once inner minimization is run to full convergence — apparent "beats" in coarse/under-converged searches evaporated on re-verification with tighter tolerances, i.e. those were optimizer noise, not real improvements). So **c(2) = 4/7** (conjectured, numerically very well supported but not yet proven).
  - **n=3: CONJECTURED, moderate numerical evidence.** Liu Bang marks points at {1/15, 3/15, 7/15} (pieces 1/15, 2/15, 4/15, 8/15 — geometric ratio 2 again). Nested differential-evolution search (6+ restarts, high iteration count) finds Xiang Yu's best response gives Liu Bang exactly **8/15 ≈ 0.533333**. A Nelder–Mead local search from this point initially suggested a slightly higher value (~0.535) at a perturbed configuration, but re-checking that perturbed point with a much more thorough (10-restart, 1000-iteration) inner minimization showed its true guaranteed value is actually ≈0.532, i.e. *below* 8/15 — confirming the earlier apparent improvement was again just under-converged-inner-solver noise. So **c(3) = 8/15** (conjectured).
  - **Closed-form pattern**: the three data points (2/3, 4/7, 8/15) fit exactly **c(n) = 2^n / (2^{n+1} − 1)**, equivalently c(n) = 1/(2 − 2^{-n}). This is the strongest single candidate closed form to hand the outliner: n=1 gives 2/3 ✓ (proved), n=2 gives 4/7 ✓ (strong numerics), n=3 gives 8/15 ✓ (numerics). As n→∞, c(n)→1/2 monotonically decreasing, which makes sense (more marking power for both sides erodes Liu Bang's first-move edge but he never loses it since he always moves first with strictly more information... note Xiang moves *after* seeing Liu Bang's points, so this is a real minimax, and the fact that Liu Bang still keeps a strict edge > 1/2 for all finite n, shrinking geometrically, matches the "each extra marking pair roughly halves Liu Bang's remaining edge over the fair split" story implicit in the 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1) formula.
  - **Claiming-subgame optimal-play verification**: confirmed by exhaustive game-tree minimax (bitmask DP, `diff(mask) = max over available pieces of (piece − diff(mask minus piece))`) against the naive "always take the largest remaining piece" greedy strategy on 2000 random instances of 2–7 pieces: **exact match every time** (max discrepancy ~1e-15, floating-point noise only). So it is safe to assert (and should be provable by a one-line exchange argument) that once the final piece multiset is fixed, both players' optimal policy is "always take the currently-largest unclaimed piece," and the first mover's guaranteed total is the sum of odd-ranked pieces (1st, 3rd, 5th, … largest).
