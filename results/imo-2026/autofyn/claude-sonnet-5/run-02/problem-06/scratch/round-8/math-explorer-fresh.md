### Distinct openings (whole-claim targets NOT going through "build S₀ then CRT")

**Opening 1 — Strong induction on |Q| (or on Ω(a_1)) via a seed-reduction/coupling
lemma, bypassing recruitment entirely.**
Instead of fixing a_1, analyzing its own recruitment process, and trying to show it
halts, prove the WHOLE claim by strong induction on k = |Q| (number of distinct
primes dividing a_1), using a *coupling* between the given sequence and a
"reduced" sequence with strictly smaller Q, rather than any prime-recruitment
machinery internal to the fixed instance. Base case k=1 is already fully solved
(certified in the workspace: a_1 with |Q|=1 gives a_{n+1}=a_n+q for all n). The
needed new ingredient — NOT in the certified stack — is a **Seed-Coupling Lemma**:
given a_1 with Q = {p_1,...,p_k}, relate its greedy sequence to the greedy sequence
generated by some seed a_1' with a strictly smaller prime set Q' ⊊ Q (e.g.
Q' = Q \ {p_k}, or a_1' = a_1 / p_k^{v_{p_k}(a_1)}) such that the two sequences'
type-partitions are provably "isomorphic modulo one extra coordinate" for all
sufficiently large n (i.e., τ(n) in the a_1-sequence determines and is determined
by τ'(n) in the a_1'-sequence together with a single extra bit "is a_n divisible by
p_k"). If such a coupling exists and is provably eventually stable, periodicity of
the smaller instance (by the inductive hypothesis) plus a bounded-correction
argument for the extra coordinate would directly give periodicity of the full
instance — with NO need to ever exhibit S₀ or run CRT on the full prime set. This is
genuinely different in top-level target: the induction variable is the SEED's prime
count, not a monovariant internal to one fixed sequence's recruitment history (the
latter is exactly what round 3's |A'|+|B'| descent and round 5's witness-index
descent both tried and both hit the "refinement manufactures new small-index
classes" wall on — see Dead ends). This opening has not been tried by anyone in the
population; it should be checked first for whether the coupling claim is even true
on small examples (e.g. compare a_1=15 (Q={3,5}) against a "reduced" a_1'=5 or
a_1'=3 and see whether the type-transition structure is genuinely related) before
committing further.

**Opening 2 — Minimal-counterexample on the WHOLE claim, ordered by a_1 itself (not
by an internal recruitment measure), with the contradiction object being a SMALLER
seed produced by "shrinking" a witnessed violation.**
Assume the theorem is false; let a_1 be the SMALLEST positive integer (in the
standard integer order, not a derived combinatorial measure) whose greedy sequence
is not eventually periodic. The earlier proof attempts (rounds 3, 5) tried
minimality on an in-sequence combinatorial quantity (|A'|+|B'|, witness index) and
both failed because refining the core prime set can manufacture new, smaller
witnesses — an artifact of measuring complexity *within* one fixed sequence's
recruitment process. Ordering by a_1 directly sidesteps that: the failure mode
those attempts hit (a refinement operation increasing rather than decreasing the
measure) cannot occur here because there is no refinement step in this framing at
all — the object under induction is literally which STARTING INTEGER produces
non-periodic behavior. The remaining work (not attempted anywhere in the workspace)
is to find a legitimate reduction taking a genuine "bad" a_1 to some structurally
related, strictly smaller integer a_1'' that must ALSO be bad (contradicting
minimality) — for instance via the observation that eventually every large term of
the sequence lies in a bounded-size persistent-type class, and the sub-sequence
restricted to one persistent type A, considered as its own sequence with its own
"first term," might itself violate periodicity if the whole sequence does, while
being generated by a strictly smaller effective seed (fewer required primes, or a
provably smaller integer) — this needs a precise "does non-periodicity propagate
down to a sub-persistent-type sequence with smaller data" lemma, which is untried.
This is speculative and its main open sub-question (does such a reduction exist)
should be probed by direct hand construction before an outliner commits to it, but
it is a genuinely different well-ordering target from the two the workspace has
already exhausted.

**Opening 3 — Direct dynamical-systems / rotation-number argument treating (a_n mod
L) as an orbit under an affine interval-exchange-like map for a GUESSED L, verified
a posteriori, instead of constructing L combinatorially via S₀.**
The Bounded Gap Lemma (a_{n+1} ≤ a_n + a_1) together with the fact that the sequence
is a subset of the integers missing exactly the "illegal" candidates gives the gap
sequence g_n = a_{n+1}-a_n a value in the bounded alphabet {1,...,a_1}. Rather than
building S₀ from witnesses, directly conjecture the value of L as an EXPLICIT
closed-form function of a_1 (e.g. L := the radical of a_1 times a bounded correction,
or — per my numeric check above — a_1=15/45 (Q={3,5}) both gave L=30=2·3·5, and
a_1=35/105 both gave L=210=2·3·5·7, suggesting L might depend only on the SET of
primes ever recruited, consistent with, but possibly provable independently of, the
S₀-construction) and prove a_{n+L-shift}=a_n+L by DIRECT VERIFICATION that "n and
n+T have the same set of legal successors mod L" using only the Bounded Gap Lemma
and Free Facts (no recruitment bookkeeping) — i.e., treat the problem as a
finite-alphabet symbolic dynamics question and prove eventual periodicity via a
counting/return-time argument (Poincaré recurrence flavor: the "state" is which
residues mod a large but FIXED, explicitly-computed modulus have been used, and one
shows this process is a finite rotation). NOTE: per `reversible-transition-map`'s
certified finding (round 5), "S-sufficiency" (this kind of forward-well-defined
automaton at a fixed core S) is PROVABLY EQUIVALENT to "V=∅ at level S" — so this
opening, if it uses S=S₀, is NOT independent (it is the same FAH question in
different language, already flagged as a dead bypass). The only way this remains
genuinely different is if L is guessed EXPLICITLY (closed-form in a_1, not
constructed via the witness/recruitment machinery) and periodicity is verified by a
counting argument at that explicit L directly — untried, likely hard, flagged here
only as a stub in case the outliner sees a concrete closed form worth checking.

**Opening 4 — A density/counting argument over intervals [1,N], independent of
per-type witness bookkeeping.**
Instead of tracking individual "types" and specific witness primes, use a global
counting argument: for each prime p, let d_p(N) := density of multiples of p in
[1,N] "used" by the sequence. Since Free Facts forces every two terms to share a
prime, and Bounded Gap Lemma bounds how sparse the sequence can be, a
Legendre/inclusion-exclusion sieve-style count of how many integers in [1,N] can
simultaneously avoid sharing a prime with ALL prior terms might directly force the
sequence's density to stabilize to a RATIONAL number ρ = L/T via an averaging
argument (Cesàro/Fekete subadditivity: a_{n+1} ≤ a_n + a_1 gives a_n/n → some limit
ρ by a version of Fekete's subadditive lemma applied to a_n - a_1·n or similar).
This gives the AVERAGE gap converges, a weaker fact than exact eventual periodicity
of the gap sequence — but it is a genuinely different technique (subadditivity /
Fekete, KB entry "Linear recurrences"/asymptotic density flavor, not in the KB
verbatim but a standard general tool) that no approach in the population has tried.
It would only be a stepping stone (average periodicity, not exact term-by-term
periodicity), so on its own it does NOT close the problem, but it is untried and
might supply an independent handle on WHY the recruitment process must terminate
(the average gap can only take finitely many "achievable" rational values below a_1,
by a pigeonhole on possible (L,T) pairs bounded by a_1 — this could give an
independent finiteness argument for something like the core-size question without
ever building S₀ explicitly). Flagged as a stub, not developed further (per lens
scope: report the opening, do not chase it to completion).
