## imo-2026-02

All approaches below share one cheap, fully-rigorous **preliminary reduction** (Lemma 0), proved once and reused; each approach file restates it briefly, then diverges.

**Lemma 0 (fixed-target reduction).** Let N9 be the nine-point center of ABC (midpoint of the segment from the circumcenter to the orthocenter, equivalently the circumcenter of the medial triangle). Since M, N both lie on the nine-point circle (midpoints of AB, AC), N9M = N9N. For any point O,
`OM² − ON² = (O−M+O−N)·(N−M) = 2(O−N9)·(N−M) + [(N9−M+N9−N)·(N−M)]`, and the bracket is `2(N9−M)·(N−M) − |N−M|²`... — cleanest derivation: `OM²−ON² = (2O−M−N)·(N−M)` and `0 = N9M²−N9N² = (2N9−M−N)·(N−M)`; subtracting, `OM²−ON² = 2(O−N9)·(N−M)`. Since MN is the midline of ABC, N−M is parallel to C−B (midline theorem). Hence
**OM=ON ⟺ (O−N9)·(C−B) = 0 ⟺ O−N9 ⊥ BC.**
This is an unconditional algebraic identity (no use of the K,L conditions yet) — it converts the two-distance target into a single perpendicularity target against the *fixed* point N9. All approaches adopt this as their literal target unless noted.

nine-point-locus-two-position: new
Target: OM = ON, via Lemma 0's equivalent target `(O − N9) ⊥ BC`.
Technique: Moving-point / two-special-positions argument, modeled on the aimo-1007 (IMO SL 2023 G5) second solution — prove a circumcenter lies on a fixed line by showing (a) the circumcenter's locus, as the internal parameter varies, is itself a straight line, and (b) that line meets the target line at two points, hence coincides with it.
Skeleton:
  1. Fix triangle ABC. Parametrize the admissible family by θ = ∠KBA = ∠ACL (confirmed by the coordinate-explorer to give a genuine 1-parameter family once conditions (ii),(iii) and the containments are imposed); write K(θ), L(θ) for the (locally unique, continuous) solution branch, θ ranging over an open interval I forced by the containment conditions.
  2. Key Lemma A: the map θ ↦ (K(θ), L(θ)) is, in a suitable sense, "affine/projective" — concretely, express K(θ)−B and L(θ)−C via the sine rule in triangles ABK and ACL (each has one known angle θ and one known side, AB or AC) as `K(θ) = B + ρ_K(θ)·(cos θ, sin θ)`-type expressions with ρ_K(θ) determined by intersecting the ray from B at angle θ with the locus cut out by conditions (ii)/(iii); then show, via the sine-rule/angle-chase, that ρ_K(θ) and ρ_L(θ) are ratios of linear (degree-1) trigonometric expressions in θ — by tool: Law of Sines in the four triangles ABK, ACL, LBK/LNC, LCK/BMK (kb: Synthetic toolkit / trig identities entry, `knowledge_base.md` line ~131,140).
  3. Key Lemma B: the circumcenter O = O(θ) of A, K(θ), L(θ) — using the standard circumcenter-as-a-rational-function-of-vertices formula — collapses, under the parametrization of Lemma A, to an *affine* function of a single auxiliary parameter t=t(θ) (this is exactly the mechanism in aimo-1007: the circumcenter of a triangle with one fixed vertex and two vertices moving via a shared angle parameter, each on a ray subject to a linear constraint, is affine in that parameter) — by direct computation (builder fills in the algebra) or by exhibiting a spiral similarity centered at some fixed point sending the parameter-line to the O-locus.
  4. Two special positions: evaluate at the two endpoints of I (or two easily-computable interior values, e.g. the limiting/degenerate configuration where containment becomes equality — K on the boundary of angle LBA, or the isosceles sub-case AB=AC where θ has a symmetric solution) and verify `(O−N9)⊥BC` directly there by an explicit (finite, checkable) computation.
  5. Since O(θ) is affine in θ (Lemma B) and satisfies the *linear* condition `(O−N9)·(C−B)=0` at two distinct values of θ, it satisfies it identically on I — by tool: a linear function vanishing at 2 points on a line is either identically zero or the two points coincide; rule out the degenerate case by checking the two special θ are genuinely distinct.
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - Lemma 0 (above) — because M,N ∈ nine-point circle centered N9, midline MN ∥ BC.
  - Lemma A: K(θ), L(θ) admit a common rational/affine reparametrization — because each is cut out by one angle condition (fixing a ray from a fixed vertex) intersected with a linear-in-cotangent condition from the paired angle equality (ii)/(iii), a standard trig-Ceva-style mechanism.
  - Lemma B: O(θ) is affine in the reparametrized variable — because the circumcenter formula, restricted to two vertices each moving affinely/rationally along fixed rays, is (after correct choice of the auxiliary parameter, as in aimo-1007) itself affine — this is the crux borrowed from aimo-1007 and must be re-derived from scratch here (cite aimo-1007 as inspiration only, not as a citation).
Open gaps: Lemma A's exact closed form for ρ_K(θ), ρ_L(θ) (needs sine-rule algebra through conditions (ii),(iii)); Lemma B's affineness (the hardest step — must be verified by explicit computation, not assumed); identification of two genuinely tractable special positions with distinct θ.
Cases to cover: the two special positions must be shown non-degenerate (distinct θ, and both inside the valid containment interval I); if the isosceles case AB=AC is used as one special position, must confirm it is an admissible interior point of I for the given (possibly non-isosceles) ABC — if not, replace with a genuine boundary/limit computation.
Watch out for: assuming O(θ) is affine without proof (the explorer confirmed this only numerically for OM=ON, not for O(θ)'s functional form) — this is the load-bearing unproven step; if Lemma B fails to be literally affine, the weaker "degree ≤1 in θ after the SAME algebraic substitution that linearizes the target line" may still suffice — the builder should not force affineness if a different auxiliary parameter makes the target condition polynomial of higher degree matched by enough sample points instead.

complex-number-argument-bash: new
Target: OM = ON, established as a direct symbolic identity via Lemma 0's target `(O−N9)·(C−B)=0`, computed by full complex-number coordinates (no appeal to a "locus" argument — this approach commits to closing the *entire* family in one symbolic sweep, treating the residual freedom explicitly as a free real parameter).
Technique: Complex-number bash with directed angles: place A,B,C as free complex numbers, encode each angle equality as an equality of arguments of complex ratios (`arg((K−B)/(A−B)) = arg((L−C)/(A−C))` for ∠KBA=∠ACL, etc.), introduce the genuine 1 free real parameter explicitly (say s = the modulus/position of K along its determined ray), solve the resulting system for K(s), L(s) as explicit complex expressions in A,B,C,s, then substitute into the standard complex circumcenter formula for O(s) and verify `(O(s)−N9)·conj(C−B)` (real part of the product, the standard complex perpendicularity test) is identically 0 in s by symbolic simplification (sympy).
Skeleton:
  1. Set up complex coordinates: A,B,C ∈ ℂ free (generic), M=(A+B)/2, N=(A+C)/2, N9 = (A+B+C+H)/4 where H=A+B+C−2·(circumcenter) — or more simply N9 = centroid-of-(O_ABC,H)/1 — by tool: standard formula N9 = (A+B+C)/2 − O_ABC/1... use the clean identity **N9 = (M + N + midpoint(BC))/3 + ...**; simplest: N9 is circumcenter of the medial triangle MN·midpoint(BC), so compute it as the circumcenter of those three known points directly (avoids needing H or O_ABC at all) — by tool: circumcenter-of-three-points complex formula (kb: Coordinates/complex — `knowledge_base.md` line 137).
  2. Translate condition (i) ∠KBA=∠ACL into `arg((K−B)/(A−B)) = arg((A−C)/(L−C))` (mod appropriate sign/orientation convention fixed once and used consistently) — by tool: directed angle ↔ complex argument dictionary.
  3. Translate conditions (ii),(iii) similarly: `arg((K−B)/(L−B))=arg((C−N)/... )` etc. — this yields, after clearing arguments (writing each as `(X−Y) = λ·|X−Y|/|Z−W|·(Z−W)` for a positive real λ, since equal arguments means equal-argument complex ratios, i.e. the ratio (K−B)/(L−B) ÷ (C−N)/(L−N)-type expression is a POSITIVE REAL number) three equations that are each "ratio is real and positive," i.e. 3 real equations on 4 real unknowns Re(K),Im(K),Re(L),Im(L).
  4. Introduce the free parameter explicitly: solve two of the three real equations for L in terms of K (or vice versa) symbolically, substitute into the third to get one residual real equation F(K,K̄)=0 (a curve in K-space, matching the confirmed 1-parameter family) — parametrize that curve however is algebraically cleanest (e.g. by Re(K) or by the angle θ=arg(K−B)−arg(A−B)).
  5. Compute O(θ) via the complex circumcenter formula for A,K(θ),L(θ) and check `Re[(O(θ)−N9)·conj(C−B)] ≡ 0` as a rational/trigonometric identity in θ — by tool: sympy symbolic simplification (`trigsimp`/`simplify`), executed by the builder as a verifiable computation, not hand-waved.
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - "Equal directed angle at a vertex ⟺ ratio of two complex differences is a positive real multiple of a third ratio" — because arg(z)=arg(w) ⟺ z/w ∈ ℝ_{>0}, the standard complex encoding of angle equality.
  - N9 as circumcenter of the medial triangle — because the medial triangle's circumcircle IS the nine-point circle (standard fact, re-derivable: medial triangle is homothetic to ABC via h(A,1/2) centered at centroid... more directly, cite "nine-point circle passes through the 3 midpoints" as the defining property, re-proved if the builder does not want to assume it: nine-point circle is definitionally the circle through the 3 side-midpoints, so its center IS the circumcenter of the medial triangle by definition — no extra proof needed).
  - The final identity `Re[(O(θ)−N9)conj(C−B)] ≡ 0` — because it is exactly the algebraic content of the theorem; this is THE gap the builder's symbolic computation must close.
Open gaps: steps 2-3's precise sign/orientation conventions (must fix one consistent directed-angle convention and verify containments pick the correct branch — a real risk of sign errors); step 4's explicit solve (may be a high-degree system — sympy `solve`/`groebner` may be needed, verify it terminates); step 5's final simplification is the crux and must literally reduce to 0, not merely "looks like it based on numerics" (the coordinate-explorer's numerics support this exists, but symbolic proof is required for `solved` status).
Cases to cover: none extra (this is a single symbolic computation valid for generic ABC); must separately confirm the computation doesn't silently divide by zero for degenerate sub-cases (isosceles AB=AC, or θ at the boundary) — flag any denominator that could vanish and treat by continuity/limiting argument.
Watch out for: sign errors in the directed-angle-to-complex-ratio translation (very easy to get ∠XYZ vs ∠ZYX backwards, which flips positive real to negative real) — the builder should sanity check each translated equation numerically against the coordinate-explorer's `fsolve` solutions before trusting the symbolic algebra.

symmetric-vector-decomposition-sigma: revise-free new (uses the σ-symmetry structural finding directly as the proof engine, not just for organization)
Target: OM = ON via Lemma 0's target `(O−N9)⊥BC`, proved by exhibiting O−N9 as an explicit sum of two vector terms that are swapped (not fixed) by the combinatorial involution σ: B↔C, M↔N, K↔L, A fixed — so that the B↔C swap of the *whole configuration* forces `O−N9` to lie along a σ-antisymmetric direction, and separately showing that direction is forced to be along BC-perpendicular... 

Actually restated cleanly: **exploit σ by constructing the reflection map ρ = reflection through the perpendicular bisector of MN composed appropriately with a symmetry of the *defining conditions* (not of the geometric triangle, which is scalene) to show ρ(O) is also a valid circumcenter for the "mirrored" data, and that ρ(O) and O are forced to coincide (or be related) enough to pin O−N9 ⊥ BC.**
Technique: Structural/combinatorial symmetry argument (kb: exploit symmetry — problem-solving heuristic in `knowledge_base.md`), using σ as an *algebraic* involution on the solution space of the defining system (K,L,ABC) rather than a geometric reflection of the plane (since ABC is scalene, σ is not realized by an isometry — but as the synthetic explorer noted, it IS realized by an actual reflection isometry in the special case AB=AC).
Skeleton:
  1. Establish (already done by synthetic explorer): the system defining (K,L) from (A,B,C) is exactly σ-invariant, where σ swaps B↔C, M↔N, K↔L and fixes A: i.e., if (K,L) is a valid solution for (A,B,C), then (L,K) is a valid solution for (A,C,B) — proved by direct relabeling of all 3 equalities + 2 containments (already checked structurally by the synthetic explorer; builder must write this out as an actual verification, one line per clause).
  2. Consider the auxiliary triangle A,C,B (i.e. ABC with B,C swapped — same triangle, opposite orientation/labeling) and its own instance of the construction, giving (K*,L*) = the valid pair for (A,C,B). By step 1, K* corresponds to L under the correspondence, i.e. **(K*,L*) = (L,K)** for the SAME original K,L (not a new solution) PROVIDED the construction is deterministic given a parameter choice matched consistently — this needs the 1-parameter family to be handled by fixing "the same θ" under the swap, i.e. use θ*=θ (∠K*BA... wait B,C swapped, so ∠K*CA = θ) — by tool: direct substitution into step 1's relabeled system.
  3. Since swapping B,C labels doesn't change the actual geometric points A,B,C (only how we name them), and (K*,L*)=(L,K) literally as points, the circumcenter of A,K*,L* is the SAME circle as the circumcenter of A,L,K = circumcenter of A,K,L = O. So **this symmetry alone doesn't produce a NEW point** — it's a consistency check, not new information; the real content must come from comparing O to a genuinely different point, namely N9 (which manifestly satisfies N9(B)=N9(C) under the swap, i.e. N9 is σ-invariant as a point of the plane, since the nine-point center doesn't depend on vertex labeling).
  4. Key computational step: write O − N9 in the fixed A,B,C coordinate frame and show its component along BC (equivalently along N−M) is a σ-ANTIsymmetric algebraic expression in the "θ-data" (angles/lengths at B vs at C), while the component perpendicular to BC (along the direction from N9 towards A, roughly) is σ-symmetric — then argue via the σ-invariance of the *defining system* that the antisymmetric expression must be built purely from quantities that themselves vanish under antisymmetrization (e.g. differences of two expressions forced equal by the σ-swapped pair of conditions (ii)/(iii): ∠LBK=∠LNC and ∠LCK=∠BMK are themselves a σ-swapped PAIR, so subtracting a computation done via (ii) from the analogous computation via (iii) should telescope to zero for the BC-component of O−N9).
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - σ-invariance of the defining system — because direct relabeling shows each clause maps to another true clause (established structurally by the explorer; builder must verify explicitly, clause by clause).
  - N9 is independent of the B↔C vertex-labeling — because the nine-point center is an intrinsic point of the (unlabeled) triangle, defined via the 3 side-midpoints symmetrically.
  - The BC-component of O−N9 is a σ-antisymmetric combination that vanishes because it is literally the DIFFERENCE of two quantities forced equal by the paired conditions (ii)/(iii) — this is the load-bearing claim and is currently only a heuristic; the builder must make the "component along BC" computation explicit (e.g. via a dot product with C−B expressed through the sine rule in the relevant triangles) to see the cancellation concretely.
Open gaps: this whole approach hinges on making step 4 an actual computation rather than a plausibility argument — it is the riskiest approach of the field (most "outline, not yet a proof path") but offers the most direct route to a SHORT synthetic proof if the antisymmetric cancellation can be exhibited cleanly; if the builder cannot make step 4 concrete within reasonable effort, this approach should be marked stuck and not pursued further (report back to outliner rather than forcing it).
Cases to cover: none beyond the generic scalene case; note the isosceles case AB=AC serves as a sanity check where σ becomes a literal reflection and O−N9⊥BC should be visible by symmetry alone (worth the builder verifying this limiting case first, cheaply, before the general antisymmetric computation).
Watch out for: conflating "σ maps solutions to solutions" (true, step 1-3) with "σ gives new information about a FIXED solution" (false without the extra work in step 4) — the synthetic explorer's finding is necessary scaffolding but NOT by itself a proof; do not let the builder stop at step 3 believing the symmetry alone finishes the problem.

homothety-doubling-target: new
Target: OM = ON, reformulated (per the synthetic explorer's opening 2) as: letting O' = 2O − A (image of O under the homothety h centered at A with ratio 2, which sends M↦B, N↦C), prove **O' is equidistant from B and C**, i.e. O' lies on the perpendicular bisector of BC (the line through the circumcenter of ABC, perpendicular to BC through its midpoint) — this is provably equivalent to OM=ON since h is an affine bijection sending the perpendicular bisector of MN exactly onto the perpendicular bisector of BC.
Technique: Direct computation with the "doubled" triangle A,K',L' (K'=2K−A, L'=2L−A, the images of K,L under h) — since O is circumcenter of AKL, O'=2O−A is (by direct verification, |O'−A|=2|O−A|=2·(AKL circumradius), and |O'−K'|=2|O−K|=2·(AKL circumradius) since h scales all distances from A by 2 and K'−A=2(K−A)) the circumcenter of A,K',L'. The advantage: the target now directly involves B and C (not just their midpoints), and the angle conditions (i)-(iii), read through the lens of the ORIGINAL triangle BMC/BNC where K,L live, may translate more transparently onto K',L' since K'=2K−A relates K to B via K'−B = 2K−A−B = 2K−2M = 2(K−M), i.e. **K' − B = 2(K − M)**, similarly **L' − C = 2(L − N)** — meaning K' is the reflection-scaled image of K "as seen from M" translated to sit relative to B, and the angle conditions (which reference M, N directly in clauses (ii),(iii): ∠LBK=∠LNC, ∠LCK=∠BMK) may become cleaner once phrased via K'−B, L'−C.
Skeleton:
  1. Verify the homothety-transfer lemma rigorously: O'=2O−A is the circumcenter of AK'L' — by tool: homothety scales all lengths from the center A by factor 2, hence preserves the "equidistant from A,K,L" defining property of a circumcenter under the map P ↦ 2P−A.
  2. Re-target: OM=ON ⟺ (by applying the SAME Lemma-0-style algebra to h, using that h(M)=B,h(N)=C, and h is an affine map so preserves ratios/perpendicular-bisector correspondence up to the linear part of h being a scalar multiple of the identity, i.e. purely a dilation — so it preserves perpendicularity and the perpendicular-bisector-of-MN maps EXACTLY onto the perpendicular-bisector-of-BC) O'B = O'C.
  3. Express O'B² − O'C² directly via the standard circumcenter identity applied to O' (circumcenter of AK'L'): `O'B² − O'C² = |O'−B|² − |O'−C|²`, expand using O'−B=... and relate to known equal-distance facts `O'A = O'K' = O'L'` (circumradius of AK'L', doubled) plus the specific positions of B,C relative to A,K',L' — by tool: direct vector algebra, `2(O'−X)·(C−B)=0` reduction (X = circumcenter of ABC, since X is the unique point equidistant from B,C at the "natural" fixed reference — analogous role to N9 in Lemma 0 but now for the ORIGINAL triangle's circumcenter).
  4. Key Lemma: relate `(O' − O_ABC)` (O_ABC = circumcenter of ABC) to the angle data (i)-(iii) using the identities K'−B=2(K−M), L'−C=2(L−N) from above, converting conditions (ii) ∠LBK=∠LNC and (iii) ∠LCK=∠BMK (which literally reference M,N as vertices) into statements about L,K directly at B,C once doubled — this is the crux computation the builder must carry out (likely another sine-rule/coordinate step, structurally similar to the other approaches' final algebraic step, but organized around B,C,O_ABC instead of M,N,N9).
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - O'=2O−A is circumcenter of AK'L' — because homothety centered at A scales distances from A by the ratio, preserving the equidistance-from-3-points defining property.
  - Perpendicular bisector of MN maps to perpendicular bisector of BC under h — because h(M)=B, h(N)=C exactly (M,N are midpoints of AB,AC, so h with ratio 2 centered at A is literally the map "double the vector from A," which sends the midpoint of AB to B).
  - K'−B = 2(K−M), L'−C=2(L−N) — direct algebra: K'−B = (2K−A)−B = 2K−(A+B) = 2K−2M = 2(K−M) since M=(A+B)/2.

Target: OM = ON, attacked via a genuinely synthetic (non-computational) route: locate two circles ω_B, ω_C (built from the angle conditions at B and C respectively, using the σ-symmetric pairing (ii)↔(iii)) such that O lies on the radical axis of ω_B, ω_C, and separately show that radical axis IS the perpendicular bisector of MN (e.g. because ω_B, ω_C are congruent circles centered on a line parallel to... or because M, N have equal power with respect to both) — the classic synthetic mechanism for "X is equidistant from two points" is "X lies on the radical axis of two circles of EQUAL RADIUS through/related to those points," which directly gives equidistance without any circumcenter-formula computation.
Technique: Concyclicity + radical axis (kb: Synthetic toolkit — spiral similarity, power of a point — `knowledge_base.md` line ~131), building on the two paired conditions (ii) ∠LBK=∠LNC and (iii) ∠LCK=∠BMK, each of which is the classic "inscribed angle" signature of 4 points being concyclic (∠LBK=∠LNC as angles subtending the same segment LK — or LN, respectively — from B and N is exactly the condition for B,N and the relevant points to lie on a common circle through L,K under directed-angle bookkeeping, PROVIDED the angles are measured on the correct arcs — must be checked, not assumed).
Skeleton:
  1. From condition (ii) ∠LBK = ∠LNC: test (numerically first, then synthetically) whether this is exactly the concyclicity condition for **B, N, K, L**-type or **K,L,B,N** configuration (i.e., ∠LBK and ∠LNC subtend the same segment, giving B,N,K,C or similar 4 points concyclic) — by tool: inscribed angle theorem / converse (kb: Synthetic toolkit, power of a point).
  2. Symmetrically from condition (iii) ∠LCK=∠BMK, test whether **C,M,K,L** (or the analogous quadruple) are concyclic.
  3. If both concyclicities hold (this is the FIRST thing the builder must verify numerically before committing further effort — flag as the load-bearing existence check), let ω1 = circle(B,N,K,...) and ω2 = circle(C,M,K,...) [exact point sets TBD by the builder's numeric check]; K (and possibly L) is then a natural common point / radical-axis-adjacent point of ω1, ω2, and the goal becomes showing O (circumcenter of AKL) has equal power with respect to two circles of equal radius, or lies on a line that is provably the perpendicular bisector of MN by an explicit radical-axis-of-two-equal-circles argument.
  4.
