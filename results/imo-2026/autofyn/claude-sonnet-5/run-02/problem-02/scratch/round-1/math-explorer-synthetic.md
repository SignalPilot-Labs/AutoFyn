## imo-2026-02

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Combinatorial swap symmetry σ: (B,M,K) ↔ (C,N,L), A fixed.** I verified algebraically that the defining system is exactly invariant under this relabeling. Write σ for "swap B↔C, M↔N, K↔L, fix A." Then:
     - σ(condition i: ∠KBA=∠ACL) = itself (∠ABK=∠LCA is the same statement re-lettered) — condition (i) is self-symmetric.
     - σ(condition ii: ∠LBK=∠LNC) = ∠KCL=∠KMB, which is *exactly* condition (iii) ∠LCK=∠BMK (same angles, different ray order). So σ swaps (ii)↔(iii).
     - σ("K inside ∠LBA") = "L inside ∠ACK" and vice versa — the two containment conditions swap into each other.
     So the *entire* system (3 equalities + 2 containments) is σ-invariant: it says K is produced from (A,B,C) by the same "recipe" Φ that produces L from (A,C,B), i.e. K=Φ(A,B,C), L=Φ(A,C,B) for a single construction Φ. This is the strongest structural fact I found and should drive the outline: any proof strategy that treats K and L asymmetrically is fighting the grain of the problem; the natural target is a proof that is manifestly σ-symmetric (e.g., builds a single symmetric object and reads off both OM and ON from it), or that reduces to the isosceles case (AB=AC) by continuity/algebraic identity, since in the isosceles case σ becomes an actual reflection isometry of the plane, K and L become literal mirror images, and OM=ON is then immediate by symmetry.
  2. **OM=ON as "doubled" circumcenter equidistant from B,C.** OM=ON ⟺ O on perp. bisector of MN. Since the homothety h centered at A with ratio 2 sends M↦B, N↦C, and h is affine, h maps the perpendicular bisector of MN to the perpendicular bisector of BC. Concretely: let O'=2O−A (image of O under h). Since h(A)=A, h(K)=2K−A=:K′, h(L)=2L−A=:L′, and O is circumcenter of AKL, O' is exactly the circumcenter of the homothetic triangle A K′ L′ (checked: |O'−A|=2|O−A|=2·OA=2·OK=|O'−K'|, etc.). So **OM=ON ⟺ O' is equidistant from B and C**, i.e. O' lies on the perpendicular bisector of BC (the line through the circumcenter of ABC). This reformulation turns the goal into "the circumcenter of the doubled triangle AK'L' lies on the perpendicular bisector of BC" — potentially easier to attack directly with the angle conditions since B, C (not just their midpoints) now appear.
  3. **Moving-points / 1-parameter-family technique (à la ISL-style problems).** Dof count: K,L have 4 real degrees of freedom; the three angle equalities are 3 independent scalar constraints ⇒ generically a **1-parameter family (curve) of valid (K,L) pairs** satisfying the equalities, with the containment conditions cutting out a sub-arc. I confirmed this numerically (see below): for a fixed scalene triangle, parametrizing by t=∠KBA=∠ACL and solving the other two equations for K,L via a nonlinear solve, a whole interval of t gives valid (containment-respecting) configurations, and OM−ON is ~0 (1e-10 to 1e-13, solver precision) across the *entire* interval, not just at one point. This is exactly the shape of ISL problem aimo-1007 (IMO SL G5, ana below): a family of configurations parametrized by one point moving on a line, where the target equality is proved for two special/degenerate positions of the parameter and then shown to persist along the whole family via a spiral similarity (or other similarity map) sending the parameter-line to the locus of the constructed circumcenters, which is therefore also a line — so agreeing with the target line at 2 points forces agreement everywhere. This suggests: find two special/degenerate configurations (e.g. K→B or K→M boundary limits, or the isosceles-triangle case) where OM=ON is easy, then show the map t ↦ O(t) traces a line (via a spiral similarity or an affine argument), which would finish the general case.
  4. **Direct angle-chase toward concyclicity / spiral similarity at a fixed center.** ∠LCK=∠BMK and ∠LBK=∠LNC each equate an angle at C (resp. B) with an angle at M (resp. N) — classic shape for "these four points are concyclic" or "there's a spiral similarity taking one segment to another" once the angles are shown to subtend a common chord under directed-angle bookkeeping. Worth having a builder push directed-angle chasing (∠(XY,XZ) mod π) from these two conditions to see if K,L,B,N or K,L,C,M-type concyclicities fall out; I did not push this to completion (out of scope for exploration), but it's a natural knowledge-base match (spiral similarity, Miquel point machinery).

- Small-case / intuition notes (numerical, i.e. conjectural but strongly evidenced):
  - Set up A=(0,4), B=(-3,0), C=(2.5,0) (scalene), M,N midpoints. Parametrized valid (K,L) pairs by t=∠KBA=∠ACL∈[0.35,0.9] rad (solving the other two equalities + containments via `scipy.optimize.fsolve`, then checked K∈int(BMC), L∈int(BNC), K inside ∠LBA, L inside ∠ACK via signed-angle sum tests). At an out-of-range t=0.3 where the containment L∈int(BNC) failed (invalid configuration), OM−ON was macroscopically nonzero (≈1.1), confirming the containment conditions are essential (not vacuous) and that the equality genuinely depends on the configuration being valid.
