## imo-2026-01

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Exponent-pair transform + gcd invariance (per-prime Euclidean view).** For fixed prime p, a move on positions (i,j) with exponents (a,b) sends them to (min(a,b), |a-b|). Elementary identity `gcd(min(a,b), |a-b|) = gcd(a,b)` (both equal `gcd(a,b)`, standard subtractive-Euclidean fact) shows the GCD of the *entire* multiset of p-exponents across the whole board is an exact invariant of every move, for every prime p simultaneously. This is a route to **part (b)** that never needs a full "majorization/sorted-exponent-multiset" argument — only this one identity, applied prime-by-prime.
  2. **Omega + active-count potential for termination (part (a)).** Same identity, viewed additively: `Omega(m)+Omega(n) - [Omega(gcd)+Omega(lcm/gcd)] = Omega(gcd(m,n)) ≥ 0`. So total `Omega` over the board is non-increasing, and strictly decreases whenever `gcd(m,n) > 1` and `m ≠ n` (the only case where both outputs stay `>1`). Whenever `gcd(m,n)=1` or `m=n`, one output becomes exactly `1`, so the count `k` of board entries `>1` strictly decreases by exactly 1. Hence the pair `(Omega_total, k)`, ordered lexicographically, strictly decreases every move and is bounded below by `(0,0)`/`(0,1)` — giving finite termination with an explicit move-count bound `moves ≤ Omega_total(initial) + (n-1)` where n=2026. This is a **self-contained elementary termination proof**, no need for prime-factorization "majorization of vectors" machinery, no need for a well-ordering on multisets — just two integers combined lexicographically.
  3. **Direct answer formula.** Combining opening 1 with the halting condition (board ends with ≤1 entry >1, so per prime at most one nonzero exponent remains, which — by gcd-invariance — must equal the gcd across all initial numbers of that prime's exponent, or 0 if all initial exponents were 0): `M = ∏_p p^(gcd_i v_p(x_i))`, product over all primes p dividing at least one initial number, of p raised to the GCD (not max, not sum) of the p-adic valuations of the initial 2026 numbers. This single formula proves both (a)'s uniqueness of the surviving position pattern and gives the exact value for (b) in one shot.

- Candidate technique(s): elementary p-adic valuation bookkeeping (`gcd(min(a,b),|a-b|)=gcd(a,b)`), a two-part lexicographic monovariant (Omega, active-count) for termination, invariance-of-GCD-under-Euclidean-pair-reduction for the uniqueness of M. No heavy machinery needed (no LTE, no majorization theory, no advanced number theory) — this is a "small trick, big payoff" problem.

  - **M = gcd(x_1,...,x_2026) (plain number-theoretic gcd of the initial numbers).** Tested against 300 random simulated trials (2–7 numbers, values 2–80, 10 repetitions each with random move order): **0/300 matched** in general — e.g. initial `[4,14,16]` gives simulated M=14 in every trial, but `gcd(4,14,16)=2 ≠ 14`.
