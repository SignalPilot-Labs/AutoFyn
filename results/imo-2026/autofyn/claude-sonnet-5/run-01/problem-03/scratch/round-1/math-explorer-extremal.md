## imo-2026-03

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Greedy-take reduction (foundational, needed by every approach).** Once all cuts are made, the "claim pieces alternately, each maximizing own total" phase is *equivalent* to: sort the final pieces descending and give odd ranks (1st, 3rd, 5th, ...) to Liu Bang (LB), even ranks to Xiang Yu (XY). I verified this by exact minimax recursion (brute force over all play orders) against the greedy formula on 200 random piece-multisets of size ≤7 — exact match every time. This is a standard exchange-argument fact ("maximize your own sum in an alternating pick where any item is choosable by anyone") but should still be *proved* (short exchange/induction argument), not just cited. This turns the whole problem into a pure *optimization* problem: LB picks initial pieces `a_1≥...≥a_k` (`k≤n+1`, `Σa_i=1`) to maximize `min` over XY's refinement (≤n further cuts distributed anywhere) of `Σ (odd-ranked pieces of the final multiset)`.
  2. **Weighting/potential-function opening.** Since the payoff is "sum of odd ranks of a sorted multiset," a natural dual object is a weight `w` on pieces with `Σ w(claimed by LB) ≥ c` provable by a rank/parity argument (e.g., pairing up consecutive ranks `(2i-1,2i)` and bounding each pair's LB-share). This is the natural route to the **upper bound direction** (XY's strategy showing LB can't do better) and possibly the lower bound too, via an inductive potential that tracks "current guaranteed share of the top `2j+1` pieces."
  3. **Self-similar/geometric-construction opening** (this is what the numerics point to — see below): try `a_i` in geometric ratio `r`, and look for the `r` that is a fixed point of the adversary's best "matching" attack.
  4. **Small-`n` closed-form-guessing opening**: solve `n=1,2,3,4` essentially exactly (by hand / exact rational arithmetic) and pattern-match a formula in `n`, then aim to prove it by induction on `n` (peel off the largest piece / smallest piece each step).

- **Key numerical finding — the "pairing attack," which invalidates two natural first guesses.** I set up the reduced optimization numerically (Python, scipy Nelder-Mead with many random restarts + exact-fraction verification) and found:
  - The **equal-partition** (`n+1` equal pieces) and the **"harmonic" decreasing partition** `a_i ∝ (n+2-i)` (which I initially got by *restricting* XY to equal-subpiece splits — this restriction is WRONG, see below) both cap out at value `1/2` for `n≥2`, i.e. they give LB essentially no advantage over `1/2` as `n` grows.
  - The reason: XY has a much sharper move than "split into equal parts" — he can split a large piece into two parts **one of which exactly matches (ties) the length of an existing smaller piece**. A tie between two same-length pieces splits their two consecutive ranks 50/50 between the two players (order doesn't matter since lengths are equal), which *destroys* the "LB gets the odd rank" advantage that piece used to carry. This pairing/matching move beats naive "equal split" or "no split" in general, so any approach that restricts XY to equal splits is testing the wrong game and will get a bogus (too favorable to LB) bound.
  - **The partition that neutralizes this attack: geometric ratio `1/2`.** Take `a_i = 2^{n+1-i} / (2^{n+1}-1)` for `i=1,...,n+1`, i.e. piece sizes proportional to `2^n, 2^{n-1}, ..., 2, 1`. This sequence is special because *bisecting any piece exactly reproduces the size of the next piece in the sequence* — so XY's best "cascading pairing" attack (bisect pieces `1,...,n` — i.e. all but the smallest, using exactly the pieces that need it, sometimes fewer than `n` cuts suffice) turns the multiset into runs of doubled equal-length pieces that telescope down. Computing the resulting LB total exactly (rational arithmetic) for this partition and this attack gives, for `n=1,2,3,4`: `2/3, 4/7, 8/15, 16/31` — matching the closed form `2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)` to full numerical precision (confirmed both by the exact-fraction hand/`Fraction`-arithmetic replay of the attack and by unrestricted Nelder-Mead search over ALL of XY's allocations/split-points, not just the cascading one).
  - I also swept other geometric ratios `r∈[0.3,0.7]` (both coarse and fine grids) for `n=2,3` and consistently found `r=1/2` at or very near the peak (the true bilevel value as a function of `r` appears to be maximized at `r=1/2`), and separately confirmed `k=n+1` pieces (using all `n` allowed cut points) beats using fewer.

- **Conjectured answer**: `c(n) = 2^n / (2^{n+1} - 1) = 1/(2 - 2^{-n})`, achieved by LB's geometric partition `a_i = 2^{n+1-i}/(2^{n+1}-1)`. As `n → ∞`, `c(n) → 1/2` from above, which matches the intuition that with more cuts XY can always dilute LB's edge down toward (but never quite to) an even split. **This is a numerically strong but UNPROVEN conjecture** — small cases only (`n=1..4`), and I have not proven (a) that this LB partition beats every possible XY response (only checked the cascading-pairing response is optimal for XY against it, via search, not proof), nor (b) that no other LB partition does better (checked several families — equal, harmonic, various geometric ratios — none beat `r=1/2`, but this is not exhaustive).

- **Candidate technique(s):**
  - Exchange argument for the greedy-take reduction (elementary, should be proved rigorously first — it's the linchpin every approach needs).
  - Induction on `n`, likely peeling the **largest** piece: `c(n)` vs `c(n-1)` relation. The recursive structure `2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)` strongly suggests a clean induction (note `1 - c(n) = (2^n-1)/(2^{n+1}-1)`, and `c(n) = 1 - \frac{1}{2}(1-c(n-1))\cdot(\ldots)` type recursions may exist — worth checking `2^{n+1}-1 = 2(2^n-1)+1`).
  - Weighting/potential function keyed to rank-parity of a sorted multiset (dual/LP-style argument) for the upper bound (XY's guarantee that LB gets ≤ `c(n)`), generalizing the pairing-attack idea to an arbitrary LB partition, not just the geometric one.
  - Self-similar / dyadic-sequence construction for the lower bound (LB's guarantee), matching the "largest term exceeds sum of the rest" dyadic-sequence motif common in adversarial-game olympiad problems.

- **Cheap-kill candidates:** none that immediately dispose of the problem, but two useful structural prunings for the outliner:
  - **Prune "restrict XY to equal splits."** Any approach assuming XY's best move is always to bisect (or equally divide) pieces is provably suboptimal — the pairing/matching attack strictly beats it in general (see `a=[0.6,0.4]`, budget 2: equal-split value is `0.5`, but that's actually tied here; the *clean counterexample* is `a=[0.4,0.3,0.2,0.1]`, `n=3`: equal/harmonic-restricted search says `0.55`, but the true value (found by unrestricted search) is `0.5`, via XY bisecting the `0.4` piece into `0.3+0.1` to tie the existing `0.3` piece, and bisecting the `0.2` piece into `0.1+0.1` to triple up with the existing `0.1` piece).
  - **Prune "fewer than `n+1` initial pieces."** Numerically, using the full `n` allowed cut points (`k=n+1` pieces) always weakly dominates using fewer, in every case checked.

- **Analogous past problems (cruxes):**
  - `aimo-0117` (games-and-strategy, Dutch olympiad "stones in boxes" game) — crux: *"Assign the played values as a two-sided geometric (dyadic) sequence so that the single largest value strictly exceeds the sum of all the others."* This is the same dyadic/geometric-sequence motif (though ratio there is presumably different) that shows up as the winning construction in our numerics (ratio-`1/2` geometric partition). Worth reading for how a dyadic sequence is used to defeat an adversary who can move/rearrange values — genuinely analogous at the *technique* level, not at the problem-statement level.
  - `aimo-0019` ("painful game" — adversary blackens dyadic-length intervals) — crux: *"Bound a family of dyadic-length pieces of pairwise distinct sizes by twice the largest, via the geometric sum of distinct negative powers of two."* Same dyadic-bounding flavor (`Σ 2^{-i}` telescoping), relevant to bounding sums over our geometric partition `2^n,...,1`.
  - `aimo-0198` (liar's guessing game) — crux: *"sum a geometric weight `λ^(run)` over all candidates into a single potential the adversary greedily minimizes"* — a genuinely useful pattern for the **weighting/potential-function opening**: build a single scalar potential with geometric weights that the adversary (XY) can only decrease by a bounded amount per move, then bound the number of moves (`≤n`) to cap total damage. This is possibly the cleanest route to a rigorous upper-bound proof for our problem (generalizing the pairing-attack insight into a formal potential argument).
  - None of these are the same *problem* (different mechanics — no stick-cutting analog was found in the corpus after searching `games-and-strategy` and `extremal-principle` under `combinatorics` for "stick/interval/split/cut/segment/piece" keywords), so treat all three as *technique* hints only, not template solutions.

  - As `n→∞`, `c(n) → 1/2⁺`: with unlimited cuts XY can dilute LB's structural first-mover edge down to (but not below/at) half the stick.
