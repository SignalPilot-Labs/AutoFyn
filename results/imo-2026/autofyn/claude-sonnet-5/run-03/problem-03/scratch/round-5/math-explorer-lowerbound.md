## imo-2026-03 (lens: the three residual lower-bound gaps — greedy-reduction-geometric's TOP-ONLY d=1,2 gap; self-similar-induction's middle regime + Case-B(m,k); whether they're the same obstruction)

### Headline finding (part c, answered concretely — read this first)

**The two "open regions" are not just parallel, they are literally the *same* target once you specialize `self-similar-induction-on-n`'s general trichotomy to the TOP-ONLY sub-case (tail completely untouched, `c=0`), and moreover `self-similar-induction-on-n`'s Reduction B — already proved in full — closes `greedy-reduction-geometric`'s *entire* residual region `2^(m-3)≤a1<2^(m-1)` (not just part of it) in one step, reducing it to a single clean target that is NOT the same computation `greedy-reduction-geometric`'s Theorem-6 machinery tried and failed at.**

Details:
- `self-similar-induction-on-n`'s trichotomy compares `b1` (top fragment of the split of `2^m`) to `μ:=max(S)` (tail's actual running max) and to `2^{m-1}`. In TOP-ONLY, the tail is exactly `Γ_{m-1}` untouched, so `μ=2^{m-1}` *exactly* (not merely `≤2^{m-1}`). Substituting `μ=2^{m-1}` into the trichotomy collapses it from three cases to two: Case A (`b1≥2^{m-1}`, = Dominant-Chain's boundary, already fully closed) and Case B (`b1<μ=2^{m-1}`) — **the "middle regime" `μ≤b1<2^{m-1}` is vacuous under TOP-ONLY** (it only exists when the tail itself has also been cut, so `μ<2^{m-1}` strictly — that's a genuinely different, more general sub-problem than TOP-ONLY, see caveat below).
- So for TOP-ONLY, Reduction B applies to *all* `a1<2^{m-1}` at once (violation depths `d=1,2,3,...` uniformly — Reduction B does one single peel of `μ=2^{m-1}`, it never depends on `d`). This reduces the *whole* complementary regime to one target:
  $$\textbf{Case-B}(m,k):\qquad \mathrm{OddSum}(B\cup\Gamma_{m-2})\ \le\ 2^m-1\quad\text{for every partition }B\text{ of }2^m\text{ into }\le m\text{ positive parts, with }\max(B)<2^{m-1}.$$
- **I verified algebraically that this is exactly the same statement as `greedy-reduction-geometric`'s own Section 8.4 "`d=1` residual bound"** (`EvenSum(A∪Γ_{m-2})≥2^{m-1}` ⟺ `OddSum(A∪Γ_{m-2})≤2^m-1`, since `sum(A∪Γ_{m-2})=2^m+2^{m-1}-1`) — the two approaches independently arrived at the *identical* reduced inequality. `greedy-reduction-geometric` proved (Section 8.4) that its specific technique — Lemma 7′ (EvenSum floor via max) plus EvenSum-superadditivity — **fails** this target by a margin growing like `-Θ(2^{m-1})` (a large, non-shrinking-as-fraction margin). But that failure is a failure of *that specific weak technique* (which throws away all information about `A` except `{a1, sum(A)}`), not evidence the target itself is false.
- **This is the single most useful redirection I can offer:** stop trying to extend Theorem 6's `d`-indexed Prefix-Run-Decomposition-plus-superadditivity machinery to `d=1,2` (it is provably too weak there, by design — it only uses `a1` and `sum(A)`, discarding all of `A`'s internal structure, exactly where the internal structure matters most). Instead, **directly attack `Case-B(m,k)`** as its own self-contained combinatorial-inequality lemma. It is a clean statement (no dependence on `n`/game framing at all — purely "maximize a rank-weighted sum of a sorted sequence with a max-cap and sum constraint"), it is provably enough to close the *entire* residual gap in one shot (not case-by-case in `d`), and my numerics below say it is essentially tight, hence tractable by an exchange/rearrangement argument rather than a crude additive bound.

**Caveat (do not overclaim to the outliner):** this only closes TOP-ONLY's residual region. `self-similar-induction-on-n`'s middle regime `μ≤b1<2^{m-1}` remains a genuinely separate open problem — it only arises when the tail itself is also cut (`c≥1`, so `μ<2^{m-1}` strictly), which is exactly the fully-general (non-TOP-ONLY) Case 2 that neither approach has closed. So: **the TOP-ONLY-restricted gap is now reduced to one clean lemma (`Case-B(m,k)`); the fully-general Case 2 (tail also cut) still has its own separate, unclosed middle-regime gap.** These are related (same peeling mechanism) but not literally the same open problem.

### Part (a): numerics on d=2 (and d=1) — is the technique's failure margin small?

Ran a heuristic global optimization (coordinate descent + `scipy.optimize.differential_evolution`) directly on the true quantity `OddSum(A∪Γ_{m-1}) − 2^m` (not the proof-technique's derived bound) at `m=8,9,10,11`, restricted to the actual violation-depth regions, respecting the real cut budget (`j≤m+1` fragments):

- **`d=2` region (`2^{m-3}≤a1<2^{m-2}`):** worst (minimum) margin found ≈ `0.129·2^m`, essentially constant as a *fraction* of `2^m` across `m=8..10` (33.0/256, 66.0/512, 133.1/1024 — all ≈0.129). **Not tight — comfortable, non-shrinking slack.** This means: a sharper *direct* argument for `d=2` does not need to be razor-thin; a generous bound with ~13% slack suffices, which is much easier to prove than a tight one (matches the spirit of the `m=2` hand computation in `self-similar-induction-on-n`, which used the full order-statistics profile, not a scalar bound).
- **`d=1` region (`2^{m-2}≤a1<2^{m-1}`):** worst margin found ≈ `0.008–0.01·2^m` — an order of magnitude tighter, and the extremal configuration sits at `a1→2^{m-1}⁻` (the boundary with the already-closed Dominant-Chain regime, where the known equality-attaining self-similar construction lives). This is consistent with `d=1` being genuinely close to the true equality case, so **any proof for `d=1` (equivalently, any proof of `Case-B(m,k)` near its own tight boundary) must be essentially sharp there** — no slack to exploit close to `a1=2^{m-1}`.
- **Reconciling this with `Case-B(m,k)`'s tightness (below):** the worst-case configurations for `Case-B(m,k)` (found independently, see part (b)) have `b1→2^{m-1}⁻`, i.e. they live in the `d=1`-adjacent boundary, not deep in `d=2`. This is fully consistent: `Case-B(m,k)` is one single target covering all `d≥1` uniformly, and its tightness is entirely concentrated near the `d=0/d=1` boundary (continuity with the known-tight Dominant-Chain equality case), while its value deep in the `d≥2` interior has real slack — matching the `d=2` numerics above exactly. **This is a coherent, cross-validated picture, not a coincidence.**

### Part (b): Case-B(m,k) closed form / extremal structure

Numerically searched (differential evolution + hand-constructed candidate family) for the maximizer of `OddSum(B∪Γ_{m-2})` subject to `sum(B)=2^m`, `max(B)<2^{m-1}`, `≤m` parts, for `m=4..12`.

- **Found an explicit near-extremal family, exact to `10^{-6}`, for every `m=4,...,12` tested:**
  $$B_\varepsilon = \bigl(2^{m-1}-\varepsilon,\ 2^{m-2},\ 2^{m-3},\ \ldots,\ 2^1,\ 1+\varepsilon\bigr)\qquad(m\text{ parts}),$$
  i.e. **`B` is literally `Γ_{m-1}` itself, with its top element nudged down by `ε` (to satisfy `max(B)<2^{m-1}` strictly) and one unit of mass parked on the bottom element to keep the sum at `2^m`.** As `ε→0⁺`, `OddSum(B_\varepsilon∪Γ_{m-2}) → 2^m-1` from below, for every `m` tested — i.e. the bound `≤2^m-1` is **tight, approached but not attained** (a supremum, not a maximum — consistent with the hypothesis `b1<2^{m-1}` being a strict/open condition).
- **General differential-evolution search (not seeded with this candidate) independently converged to the same value** for `m=4..8` (exact match to numerical precision) and close to it for `m=9,10,11` (small residual gap, plausibly just optimizer non-convergence in higher dimension rather than a real excess — the hand-built candidate above is exact and un-ambiguous evidence for the closed form).
- **Suggested clean closed form / conjecture to hand the outliner:** `Case-B(m,k)`'s extremal instance is (in the limit) *exactly the geometric self-similar shape one level down* — i.e. the same shape that makes the Dominant-Chain / original equality case tight, shifted by one peel. This is a strong hint that `Case-B(m,k)` should be provable by an **exchange/smoothing (majorization) argument**: show that among all valid `B` (fixed sum `2^m`, capped max, ≤m parts), `OddSum(B∪Γ_{m-2})` is maximized by pushing `B` toward the geometric shape — i.e. a rearrangement/smoothing lemma "OddSum of a rank-weighted sum is maximized by the most geometrically-decaying admissible profile," analogous to (and possibly provable via) the certified Lemma S (OddSum-subadditivity) plus an exchange argument on adjacent unequal coordinates, rather than via superadditivity at all. This sidesteps the *proved-impossible* superadditivity route entirely — a genuinely different mechanism, not a disguised retry.
- This avoids the circularity of Proposition C: `Case-B(m,k)` is stated for a *fixed*, already fully-known tail (`Γ_{m-2}`, not an arbitrary `S`), so there is no risk of recursing into another instance of the same-shape-but-harder problem — the only free variable is `B`.
