## imo-2026-03 — upper-bound gap: balanced/near-uniform region `p1<1/2, p_{n+1}>1/(2^{n+1}-1)`

- **Distinct openings:**
  1. **Push the "top-only allocation is optimal" aggregate claim (dyadic-potential-invariant's surviving conjecture) specifically into the balanced region**, not just small-n spot checks elsewhere. New numeric evidence below (n=2,3, at both the *numerically-found worst partitions in the balanced region* and at the *exactly-uniform* partition) shows top-only ties the true minimum every time tested — this is the strongest evidence yet for that conjecture and would, if provable, collapse the whole balanced-region gap to a 1-piece splitting sub-problem: "how should XY optimally split p1 alone against the fixed tail R, and is that value always ≤ c(n) when p1<1/2, p_{n+1}>1/(2^{n+1}-1)?"
  2. **A clean "parity-fix" mechanism at the extreme (exactly uniform) end of the region**: at p=(1/(n+1),...,1/(n+1)), XY needs only a *fraction* of its budget (0 or 1 effective cut, the rest wasted/degenerate) to hit the absolute floor OddSum=1/2 exactly — via forcing an even-length tied block (parity argument, same mechanism as the certified Doubling Lemma, but applied to *equal-value* ties rather than duplicated distinct values). Since 1/2<c(n) always, uniform partitions are comfortably easy for XY, not the hard sub-case — the actual stress point in the balanced region is more moderate imbalance (p1 close to but below 1/2), yet even there the margin found is real (~0.02-0.03 below target, not razor-thin), suggesting T(n) is likely TRUE with slack in this region, not a knife-edge case.
  3. **A generalized "match/duplicate the tail from the smallest up" mechanism for splitting p1 alone**: optimal top-only splits found numerically (see below) don't bisect p1 uniformly — they produce fragments that partially *match exact values of R* (e.g. at n=3 worst case, one fragment of the optimal p1-split equals p4 exactly, and two fragments duplicate p3), strongly suggestive of the same even-length-tied-block mechanism used in the certified Doubling Lemma/General Insertion Lemma (Theorems 1,4 in `universal-halving-adversary.md`), but applied in the regime p1<S (where those theorems' hypotheses fail) by matching only a *suffix* of R rather than all of R. This is a genuinely new mechanism candidate, not yet formalized — worth outlining as its own lemma target ("Partial-Match Insertion").

- **Candidate technique(s):** parity/tie-block counting (as in the certified Doubling Lemma and General Insertion Lemma — reusable machinery), applied to a *single-piece* splitting sub-problem rather than the whole multiset; Lemma B (first-mover-half floor, `lemmas/tie-neutrality-and-first-mover-half.md`) as a sanity/floor check.

- **Cheap-kill candidates:** none found that close the gap outright; but two easy prunings worth stating to the outliner: (a) the previously-dead "bisect all but the smallest" heuristic reconfirmed to fail badly at near-uniform (0.667 vs target 0.571 at n=2, worsening with n) — do not retry; (b) exactly-uniform partitions are *not* the hard sub-case (they hit the 1/2 floor trivially) — the outliner should not spend effort there; focus should be on p1 moderately below 1/2 with a comparably large p_{n+1}.
