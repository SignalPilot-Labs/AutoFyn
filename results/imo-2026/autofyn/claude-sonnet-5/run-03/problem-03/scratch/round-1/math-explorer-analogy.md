## imo-2026-03

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Greedy-reduction opening**: Once all 2n (or fewer) marked points are fixed and the stick is cut into pieces, the alternating "claim any unclaimed piece" endgame has a *fixed value independent of strategic subtlety* — this is a folklore lemma: in a finite perfect-information game where players alternately take any item from a fixed-value multiset (no adjacency/order constraint) to maximize their own sum, greedy ("always take the currently-largest remaining item") is optimal for both players, and the outcome is exactly: sort pieces descending x_1≥x_2≥...≥x_m; the first mover (Liu Bang) gets the odd-ranked pieces x_1+x_3+x_5+..., the second mover gets the even-ranked ones. This reduces the whole problem to a *pure combinatorial-geometry minimax*: Liu Bang picks ≤n points to maximize, Xiang Yu (seeing them) picks ≤n more points to minimize, the sum of odd-ranked pieces in the resulting multiset. This lemma is not yet in knowledge_base.md and should be stated/proved explicitly (short exchange-argument induction) as a first lemma in any approach.
  2. **Mirror/pairing-defense opening**: If Liu Bang's point set is symmetric under x ↦ 1−x, Xiang Yu can defeat him down to exactly 1/2 by playing a matching symmetric (mirroring) response, splitting the resulting piece-multiset into mirrored equal-length pairs (plus possibly one unpaired near-zero piece); equal-valued pairs always split 1–1 between the two players because tied values occupy *consecutive* sort-ranks, which always straddle a Liu/Xiang boundary. This is a genuine "cheap-kill" ruling out all symmetric constructions for Liu Bang and points to the true optimal construction being deliberately *asymmetric*. (Verified numerically: n=1 with a=1/2, and n=2 with any Liu point pair {a,1−a}, both cap at exactly 1/2 regardless of a.)
  3. **Geometric/dyadic-piece opening**: numerically the optimal Liu Bang construction is *not* equal subdivision but a geometric (doubling) subdivision — pieces of length proportional to 1,2,4,...,2^n (i.e. Liu Bang's n points split [0,1] into n+1 pieces of length 2^i/(2^{n+1}−1), i=0..n). This is structurally the same device as the crux "assign values as a two-sided geometric/dyadic sequence so the single largest value strictly exceeds the sum of all the others" (see aimo-0117 below): a doubling sequence guarantees a strict dominance hierarchy that resists any adversary's attempt to "equalize" enough pieces to steal parity.
