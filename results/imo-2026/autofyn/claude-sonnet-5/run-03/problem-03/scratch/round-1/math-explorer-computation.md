- Small-case / intuition notes (all CONJECTURED from numerics, not proved):
  - **Claiming subgame is greedy-optimal**: verified by exhaustive minimax DP against the "take the largest remaining piece" heuristic on 3000 random instances (1–7 pieces) — exact match every time (`/tmp/claim_game.py`). This is a *strong* candidate for a clean provable lemma (classic exchange argument: given m pieces sorted descending p1≥p2≥...≥pm, the first player's optimal total under alternating turns equals p1+p3+p5+...). Treat this as a lemma to prove by induction on m, not as an assumption.
  - Consequently, if LB marks a points and XY marks b points (a≤n, b≤n) giving a+b+1 total pieces, LB's payoff is the sum of odd-ranked pieces (1st, 3rd, 5th, ...) when pieces are sorted descending. Both players will use their *full* budget of n points in the equilibrium (using fewer points only helps the opponent) — confirmed for n=1,2,3 numerically (the max in every search used all n points for both sides).
  - **n=1**: exact numeric optimum c(1) = 2/3, achieved by LB marking a single point at x = 1/3. XY's best replies are exactly at the boundary between two tied strategies: bisect the larger piece (2/3 → 1/3,1/3) or epsilon-shave near LB's point — both give XY exactly 1/3 when x=1/3 (verified analytically: g(x) = 1 − max(x, (1−x)/2), maximized at x=1/3 where the two branches x and (1−x)/2 are equal, giving g=2/3). For x < 1/3, XY prefers bisecting; for x > 1/3, XY prefers epsilon-shaving off a sliver of size x.
  - **n=2**: numeric optimum (vectorized fine-grid search, resolution up to 400×400 pairs, refined by zooming) converges cleanly to c(2) = 4/7 ≈ 0.571428..., achieved by LB marking points at **1/7 and 5/7** (pieces 1/7, 4/7, 2/7). XY's optimal response (inferred from the resulting equilibrium piece multiset, reconstructed exactly in fractions) is to bisect the 4/7 piece into two 2/7's, then bisect one of the 2/7's into two 1/7's — giving a final 5-piece multiset {2/7, 2/7, 1/7, 1/7, 1/7}; LB (odd ranks) gets 2/7+1/7+1/7 = 4/7, matching the search value exactly.
  - **n=3**: candidate construction LB points at 1/15, 5/15, 7/15 (pieces 1/15, 4/15, 2/15, 8/15 — a permutation of {1,2,4,8}/15) gives, under best-found XY response (grid search up to resolution 50, robust/stable across resolutions), value **exactly 8/15** ≈ 0.5333. This matches the conjectured closed form below to the digit; I did NOT fully verify this is the true n=3 optimum over *all* LB configurations (only spot-checked two alternative configs, both scoring lower), so treat n=3 as a strong but not exhaustively confirmed data point.
  - **Conjectured closed form**: **c(n) = 2ⁿ / (2ⁿ⁺¹ − 1)**. Matches n=1 (2/3), n=2 (4/7 — solid numeric confirmation), n=3 (8/15 — good but less exhaustive confirmation). Equivalently XY's guaranteed share is (2ⁿ−1)/(2ⁿ⁺¹−1) = c(n) is complementary to (2^n - 1)/(2^{n+1}-1). Sanity check: c(n) → 1/2 as n→∞ (extra marking power washes out the first-move advantage) — plausible. NOTE: at n=1,2 the alternative naive guess c(n) = (n+1)/(2n+1) also matches n=1 (2/3) but diverges at n=2 (predicts 3/5=0.6, but true numeric value is 4/7≈0.571, strictly less) — ruled out by the n=2 computation. Also the guess c(n)=2n/(2^{n+1}-1) coincides with 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1) at n=1,2 (2^1=2·1, 2^2=2·2) but diverges at n=3 (predicts 6/15=0.4 vs. computed 8/15) — ruled out by n=3 data. So **2ⁿ/(2ⁿ⁺¹−1)** is currently the only formula consistent with all three data points.
  - **Structural pattern for LB's optimal marks** (conjectured from n=1,2,3): LB's n points split the stick into n+1 pieces whose lengths (in left-to-right order) are a specific permutation of {1, 2, 4, ..., 2ⁿ} scaled by 1/(2ⁿ⁺¹−1); the permutation looks like a **bit-reversal permutation** — e.g. n=2 order is (1,4,2) = (2⁰,2²,2¹) which is the bit-reversal of exponent order (0,1,2) in 2-bit indexing; n=3 candidate order (1,4,2,8) = (2⁰,2²,2¹,2³), consistent with the same bit-reversal-style rule extended to 2 bits with a leading/trailing extra slot for the top power. This pattern needs to be nailed down precisely and proved (by induction on n, inserting the new largest piece 2ⁿ at the position dictated by bit-reversal) — flagging as the key structural conjecture for the outliner to formalize or refute.
  - XY's optimal response in the equilibrium is a mix of "bisect the currently largest piece" and "epsilon-shave a sliver equal to some existing smaller piece," not a single uniform rule — the n=1 case shows the crossover at x=1/3 exactly, and the n=2/n=3 reconstructions are consistent with repeated bisection of the largest piece at each of XY's turns (but note the caveat above: blind repeated-bisection is NOT always optimal, e.g. it fails to reproduce 8/15 when applied naively without care in the choice — actually for n=3 the *bisect-largest-each-turn* heuristic gave 0.6, worse (better for LB) than the true adversarial optimum 8/15, meaning XY needs a smarter, not purely greedy-bisection, strategy at n≥3. This is a real gap: the exact description of XY's general optimal response (n≥3) is still open and is probably the crux of the upper-bound proof.
