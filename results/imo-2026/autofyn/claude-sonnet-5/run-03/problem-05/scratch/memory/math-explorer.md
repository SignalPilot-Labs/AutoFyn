ALWAYS: For chained-inequality FEs (like QM(x,f(y)) >= (f(x)+y)/2 >= GM(x,f(y))), try the substitution that makes the two outer bounds coincide (e.g. x=f(y) makes QM=GM=f(y)) — it forces the middle to equal both, upgrading a pair of inequalities into an exact identity/FE for free. Very high value, cheap to find (round 1, imo-2026-05).
ALWAYS: When testing candidate solution families for a two-sided AM/QM/GM-type FE inequality, check whether the family reduces the whole chain to a *tautological* power-mean inequality (QM>=AM>=GM on some pair) — if so the family is valid for a whole range of parameters, not just one function; don't assume the answer is a single rigid function just because x=identity works (round 1, imo-2026-05: f(x)=x+c works for ALL c>=0, not just c=0).
NEVER: Trust that single-orbit / same-orbit asymptotic substitutions (x,y both drawn from iterates of one starting point under f) will produce a contradiction to rule out non-uniform solutions — they tend to collapse to trivial true statements (e.g. c^2>=0) since the orbit is self-consistent by construction; look for cross-orbit or genuinely global substitutions instead (round 1, imo-2026-05).
