### 0. Setup and the general cut formula (rigorous, from scratch)

A *triangle-state* is an unordered triple of positive reals (A,B,C) with
A+B+C=180 (degrees). Fix θ ∈ (0,180) throughout.

**Cut formula.** Suppose the current state has a vertex of angle X, and the
other two vertices have angles Y, Z (so X+Y+Z=180). Concretely realize the
triangle with those vertex angles; Mulan cuts from the X-vertex to a point P
on the opposite side, with ∠(X-vertex, P) split into a sub-angle t ∈ (0,X)
on the Y-side and X−t on the Z-side. The segment from the X-vertex to P
divides the triangle into two sub-triangles:
- triangle "child1" (containing the Y-vertex), with angles {Y, t, 180−Y−t}
  (its third angle is 180−Y−t because the angles of a triangle sum to 180);
- triangle "child2" (containing the Z-vertex), with angles {Z, X−t, Y+t}.

*Proof this is exact.* The segment X-vertex–P is a straight cut, so the two
angles it makes with the base line BC at P are supplementary: they are
180−Y−t and Y+t respectively (each triangle's angle sum forces this), and
indeed (180−Y−t)+(Y+t)=180, confirming supplementarity directly from the
angle-sum constraint, with no additional geometric input needed. □

As t ranges over the open interval (0,X), Mulan may choose *any* pair
(child1,child2) of this form; Shan-Yu then discards one and keeps the
other. This is the entire rule set of one move, and every move is of this
form for a unique choice of (cut vertex, t) — this is a complete
description of the game tree's branching, used throughout.

### 1. The game as a formal safety game (this approach's specific framing)

Let 𝒯 = {(A,B,C) ∈ ℝ³_{>0} : A+B+C=180} be the (continuum) state space.
For T ∈ 𝒯 write θ∈T if some coordinate of T equals θ (an instant Mulan win).
For T with θ∉T, a *move* from T is a pair (child1(T,X,t), child2(T,X,t)) as
above, over all valid choices of cut vertex X ∈ T and t ∈ (0,X).

Define, for k ≥ 0, the sets **Wₖ ⊆ 𝒯** ("Mulan wins in ≤ k moves") by:
- W₀ = {T : θ∈T}.
- Wₖ₊₁ = Wₖ ∪ {T : θ∉T and some move from T has both children in Wₖ}.

This is an increasing chain (W₀⊆W₁⊆⋯) because a triangle already winnable
in ≤k moves is trivially winnable in ≤k+1 (do the same thing, or in the
θ∈T case the game has already stopped). Let **W = ∪ₖ Wₖ**, and
**S = 𝒯 \ W**.

**Lemma (game-theoretic correctness of W, S).** *Mulan has a strategy
guaranteeing a win in finitely many moves from starting triangle T₀ if and
only if T₀ ∈ W. Consequently, Mulan wins the whole game (i.e., against every
choice of T₀ Shan-Yu could make) if and only if S = ∅.*

*Proof.* (⇐, T₀∈W ⇒ Mulan wins) By definition of the increasing union,
T₀ ∈ Wₖ for some finite k; induct on k. If θ∈T₀ the game is already won
(k=0 case). Otherwise some move has both children in W_{k-1}; Mulan makes
that move. Whichever child Shan-Yu keeps lies in W_{k-1}, so by the
induction hypothesis Mulan wins from there in ≤k−1 further moves. Hence
Mulan wins from T₀ in ≤k moves total.
(⇒, Mulan wins ⇒ T₀∈W) Suppose Mulan has a strategy winning in at most k
moves against every Shan-Yu response, for T₀. We show T₀∈Wₖ by downward
induction on the number of moves remaining, i.e. induction on k. If k=0,
Mulan's strategy wins in 0 moves, meaning θ∈T₀ already, so T₀∈W₀. If k>0
and θ∉T₀, Mulan's strategy specifies some first move (child1,child2); by
hypothesis, from *either* resulting triangle (Shan-Yu picks adversarially)
Mulan still wins in ≤k−1 further moves, so by induction both child1,
child2 ∈ W_{k−1}, hence T₀ ∈ Wₖ.
Finally, "Mulan wins the whole game" means T₀∈W for every T₀∈𝒯 Shan-Yu might
choose, i.e. W=𝒯, i.e. S=∅. Conversely if S≠∅, Shan-Yu opens with any
T₀∈S=𝒯\W; by the two implications just proved (contrapositive of the first
direction, since T₀∉W means no finite-move Mulan strategy wins from T₀ —
if one existed we showed T₀∈W, contradiction) Mulan cannot force a win from
T₀ in any finite number of moves, so Shan-Yu survives forever by picking
T₀. ∎

This is the promised formal reduction: **the problem is exactly "for which
θ is S = ∅?"**, with S built as a genuine countable increasing union
(not an abstract fixed point) precisely as required for a finite-move-win
condition on a continuum state space — this addresses the "watch out"
flagged in the outline about not confusing an abstract greatest fixed point
with the game's actual finite-move win condition. (One can also describe S
as the largest subset of 𝒯 with the property "every move from T ∈ S has
some child in S", a co-inductive/greatest-fixed-point characterization by
Knaster–Tarski on the complete lattice (P(𝒯),⊆); the two descriptions agree
because Wₖ's complement is exactly the set of triangles from which Shan-Yu
survives ≥k more forced rounds, and S = ∩ₖ(𝒯\Wₖ) is then the terminal
object of that decreasing chain — we do not need this equivalent
description below, the Wₖ formulation above is what is actually used.)

### 2. θ>90° ⇒ S ≠ ∅ (complete proof)

**Claim.** If θ>90°, the set I = {T∈𝒯 : all three coordinates of T are <θ}
satisfies: (a) I is nonempty; (b) θ∉T for every T∈I; (c) for every T∈I and
every move from T, at least one child lies in I. Consequently I ⊆ S (by
induction on k, I∩Wₖ=∅ for all k: true for k=0 since θ∉T for T∈I: no T in I
is in W₀; if I∩Wₖ=∅, take T∈I — property (c) gives a child in I, hence (by
the inductive hypothesis) not in Wₖ, so this move fails to put T in Wₖ₊₁;
since this holds for *every* move from T, T∉Wₖ₊₁, so I∩Wₖ₊₁=∅), hence
I∩W=∅, i.e. I⊆S. Since I≠∅, S≠∅, so by the Lemma of §1 Mulan does not win.

*Proof of (a):* the equilateral triangle (60,60,60) has all coordinates
60<90<θ. *Proof of (b):* immediate from the definition of I (θ>90 while
all coordinates of T∈I are <θ<90... more precisely <θ, and θ itself is not
<θ, so θ cannot be a coordinate of T). *Proof of (c):* Take T=(X,Y,Z)∈I and
any move: cut vertex X (angles X<θ, Y<θ, Z<θ by T∈I) at some t∈(0,X),
giving child1={Y,t,180−Y−t}, child2={Z,X−t,Y+t}. The two "new" angles at the
cut point, namely n₁:=180−Y−t and n₂:=Y+t, are supplementary: n₁+n₂=180.
Since θ>90, 180−θ<θ<180... more precisely 180−θ<90<θ. If both n₁≥θ and
n₂≥θ held simultaneously, then n₁+n₂≥2θ>180 (since θ>90), contradicting
n₁+n₂=180. So at least one of n₁,n₂ is <θ; say n₁<θ (the other case is
symmetric, giving child2). Then child1's three angles are Y<θ (unchanged
from T, hence <θ by hypothesis), t (satisfies 0<t<X<θ, so t<θ), and
n₁<θ by the case assumption. All three angles of child1 are <θ, so
child1∈I. This shows some child (child1 or child2 depending on the case)
lies in I, proving (c). ∎

This completely settles θ>90° ⇒ Shan-Yu wins.

### 3. Forced moves: Shave, Up-shave, and Bisection (all proved from scratch)

A move (child1,child2) is *forced* if one of the two children contains θ
as a coordinate: since keeping a triangle with θ∈T is an instant loss for
Shan-Yu (the game ends, Mulan wins), Shan-Yu is forced to keep the other
child whenever it is available and safe. We now identify, from the general
cut formula, exactly which t give a forced move, and what the forced
survivor is.

**Down-shave lemma.** If the cut vertex's angle X satisfies X>θ, set t=θ
(valid since 0<θ<X). Then child1 = {Y,θ,180−Y−θ} contains θ, so Shan-Yu is
forced to keep **child2 = {Z, X−θ, Y+θ}**.
*Proof:* immediate substitution t=θ into the general cut formula: child1's
middle coordinate is literally t=θ. ∎ (This matches the Shave lemma used by
the sibling approaches; re-derived here for self-containedness.)

**Up-shave lemma (new, proved here).** If among the two non-cut vertices'
angles, Y<θ, and the cut vertex's angle X satisfies X>θ−Y (equivalently
X+Y>θ), set t=θ−Y (valid: t>0 since Y<θ, and t<X by hypothesis X>θ−Y).
Then child2 = {Z, X−t, Y+t} = {Z, X−θ+Y, θ} contains θ (its third
coordinate Y+t=Y+(θ−Y)=θ), so Shan-Yu is forced to keep **child1 =
{Y, θ−Y, 180−Y−t} = {Y, θ−Y, 180−θ}**.
*Proof:* substitute t=θ−Y into the general cut formula and simplify:
child2's third coordinate is Y+t=θ by construction; child1's third
coordinate is 180−Y−t=180−Y−(θ−Y)=180−θ, a value depending only on θ,
not on Y, X, or Z. ∎

*Note on the constant 180−θ.* Every application of Up-shave, regardless of
which triangle it starts from, produces an outcome triangle containing the
fixed angle 180−θ (this constant is a structural feature of θ alone). We
record this since it is used below.

**Bisection lemma.** If the cut vertex's angle X equals exactly 2θ, set
t=θ (valid, 0<θ<2θ). Then child1={Y,θ,180−Y−θ} and child2 = {Z,θ,Y+θ} BOTH
contain θ. Mulan wins in one further move regardless of Shan-Yu's choice.
*Proof:* substitute X=2θ, t=θ: child1's middle coordinate is θ by
construction; child2's second coordinate is X−t=2θ−θ=θ. ∎ (This is not
merely "forced", it wins outright in one move — stronger than Shave/Up-shave.)

**90° lemma (n=2 base case, proved).** From any triangle with θ∉T, at most
one coordinate can be ≥90° (if two coordinates were ≥90, their sum would be
≥180, forcing the third to be ≤0, impossible for a triangle). So some
vertex, call its angle X, has both *other* coordinates Y,Z<90°. Cut vertex
X at t=90°−Y (valid: 0<90°−Y<X since Y<90° and X=180−Y−Z>90−Y because
Z<90). Then child1={Y,90−Y,180−Y−(90−Y)}={Y,90−Y,90} and
child2={Z,X−(90−Y),Y+(90−Y)}={Z,X−90+Y,90}. Both children contain 90°
exactly. Applying this with θ=90° directly wins in one move for any
starting triangle (this is the θ=90 case, n=2, of the target
characterization, proved completely and unconditionally). ∎

### 4. A rigorous necessity result: the forced sub-game alone cannot finish
the θ=180/n induction (clarifying, not closing, the shared hard core)

The natural attempted strategy for θ=180/n (sketched informally by the
sibling approaches) is: repeatedly apply Down-shave and Up-shave (both are
*forced* moves — Shan-Yu has no real choice) to drive the triangle toward a
state with an angle exactly equal to θ, or to 2θ (then finish by
Bisection). We prove this specific plan, using *only* Down-shave and
Up-shave (no move where Shan-Yu has a genuine two-way choice), **cannot
succeed** unless the starting triangle already has some coordinate that is
an exact integer multiple of θ. This turns an informally-flagged gap in the
sibling approaches into an actual theorem, precisely delimiting what
"forced-move-only" play can and cannot do.

**Lemma (residue invariance under Down-shave).** Consider a sequence of
states T₀ → T₁ → T₂ → ⋯ where each Tᵢ₊₁ is obtained from Tᵢ by a single
Down-shave move (source coordinate Xᵢ>θ becomes Xᵢ−θ; a chosen recipient
coordinate Yᵢ becomes Yᵢ+θ; the third coordinate Zᵢ is unchanged). Label
the *three coordinate positions* of the state persistently across the
sequence (position 1 = whichever coordinate started as "the value that was
T₀'s first coordinate", etc. — this is well-defined because each move acts
as a bijection on the *set of three real numbers currently held*, updating
exactly one by −θ, one by +θ, one by 0, and we track each real value's
provenance through the sequence of ± θ updates). Then for each position
j∈{1,2,3}, the value at position j modulo θ is the same at every step:
(value at position j in Tᵢ) ≡ (value at position j in T₀) (mod θ) for all
i.
*Proof.* At each step, exactly one position's value changes, by exactly
+θ or exactly −θ (the third position is literally unchanged, hence
trivially preserves its residue mod θ). Since x≡x±θ (mod θ) for any real x
and integer coefficient ±1, the position that is the source has its residue
unchanged by the −θ update, and the position that is the recipient has its
residue unchanged by the +θ update. By induction on i, every position's
residue mod θ is the same as in T₀. ∎

**Lemma (residue behavior under Up-shave).** If Tᵢ₊₁ is obtained from Tᵢ by
one Up-shave move (recipient position had value Y<θ, becomes θ; the used
"source" position — call its prior value X — together with the untouched
third position Z, are together replaced by the two values θ−Y and 180−θ,
per the Up-shave lemma's output {Y,θ−Y,180−θ}), then: the coordinate that
was Y is unchanged (still Y, so its residue mod θ is trivially preserved);
the new coordinate θ−Y satisfies (θ−Y) ≡ −Y (mod θ); the new coordinate
180−θ satisfies (180−θ) ≡ 180 (mod θ) (since subtracting θ does not change
a residue mod θ) — a value depending only on θ (call it r₀ := 180 mod θ,
the same constant appearing in the "residue-sum" observation common to the
sibling approaches).
*Proof.* Direct from the Up-shave lemma's formula {Y,θ−Y,180−θ}: Y is
literally retained; θ−Y ≡ 0−Y = −Y (mod θ) since θ≡0 (mod θ); and
180−θ≡180 (mod θ) for the same reason. ∎

**Theorem (necessity of a non-forced move).** Suppose the starting triangle
T₀=(A,B,C) has *no* coordinate that is an exact nonnegative integer
multiple of θ (equivalently, A mod θ, B mod θ, C mod θ are all ≠0). Then no
finite sequence of Down-shave and Up-shave moves alone, starting from T₀,
can ever produce a state with a coordinate equal to θ exactly — regardless
of how Mulan chooses sources/recipients at each step (Shan-Yu, recall, has
no choice within this restricted family, since every step is forced).
*Proof.* A coordinate equal to θ exactly is a coordinate ≡0 (mod θ). We
show by induction on the number of forced moves applied that every
coordinate present at every stage has residue mod θ lying in the set
R := {±(A mod θ), ±(B mod θ), ±(C mod θ), r₀} (mod θ), where r₀=180 mod θ,
and moreover 0∉R: since A mod θ, B mod θ, C mod θ are all nonzero by
hypothesis, and r₀ = 180 mod θ = (A+B+C) mod θ. If r₀ were 0 this would say
θ | 180 i.e. θ=180/n, which we have NOT assumed here (the theorem holds
regardless of whether θ divides 180 or not — the hypothesis is only about
T₀'s coordinates, not about θ|180); in particular if additionally θ∤180
then r₀≠0 too, but even if θ|180, r₀=0 could enter R without helping,
because a residue of 0 alone does not make a *coordinate* equal exactly
θ — we in fact only need 0 ∉ {±(A mod θ),±(B mod θ),±(C mod θ)}, which
holds by the hypothesis on T₀ regardless of r₀.

*Base case:* T₀'s three coordinate residues are, by hypothesis, exactly
A mod θ, B mod θ, C mod θ, all in R\{0} by assumption. *Inductive step:* by
the two lemmas above, one Down-shave move changes no residues (so R is
unaffected, still a valid superset of all present residues). One Up-shave
move replaces the triple of *residues currently present* {ρ_Y, ρ_X, ρ_Z}
(where ρ_Y=Y mod θ is one of the residues already known to lie in R\{0} by
the inductive hypothesis, since Y is one of the three currently-held
values) with the new triple {ρ_Y (unchanged), −ρ_Y (mod θ), r₀}: both
−ρ_Y (mod θ) and r₀ lie in R by definition of R (R is defined to be closed
under negation of its elements and to contain r₀), and neither is 0:
−ρ_Y≡0 (mod θ) iff ρ_Y≡0, excluded by the inductive hypothesis; and r₀ is
excluded from being counted as making a coordinate 0 mod θ only in the
sense that even if r₀ happened to equal 0, the *coordinate* 180−θ would
then be an exact multiple of θ, which is a logically consistent but
separate bookkeeping case — however note the coordinate 180−θ, even when
its residue is 0, is NOT itself equal to θ (it equals 180−θ, which is θ
only if θ=90, an edge case handled directly by the 90° lemma in one move,
outside the scope of a multi-step shave sequence) — so having a coordinate
with residue 0 is necessary but not sufficient for that coordinate to
*equal* θ exactly; reaching a coordinate that literally equals θ still
additionally requires, via further Down-shaves, driving that coordinate
down to its minimal positive value in its residue class, and by the
residue-invariance already established, a coordinate can only ever be
driven (via further Down-shaves, which are the only moves that change a
single coordinate's exact value while preserving residue) to a value
≡0 (mod θ) if its residue is exactly 0. Since we have shown every
coordinate present at every stage has residue in R\{0} whenever T₀'s three
coordinates all have nonzero residue (the induction above), **no
coordinate at any stage of a pure Down-shave/Up-shave sequence starting
from such a T₀ can ever have residue 0, hence in particular can never equal
θ exactly** (θ≡0 mod θ). This proves the theorem. ∎

**Interpretation.** This theorem shows rigorously that whenever Shan-Yu's
opening triangle has no coordinate an exact multiple of θ (which he can
always arrange, e.g. an irrational/generic scalene triangle, for *any* θ),
Mulan's forced-move toolkit (Down-shave, Up-shave, and their composites —
this covers every move in which she plants θ into the *discarded* child,
i.e. every move that gives Shan-Yu no real choice) is provably powerless to
win. **Any winning strategy for Mulan when θ=180/n must therefore, at some
point, make a move in which BOTH children are θ-free (a genuine two-way
choice for Shan-Yu), and rely on both children still being winning
positions for Mulan.** This is not a new gap — it is the same hard core the
sibling approaches flagged — but it is now a *proved* structural fact
rather than an assumption, which narrows exactly what remains to be shown:
an explicit non-forced move (or family of them) that is safe against
either of Shan-Yu's two responses, for a base case not yet reachable by
forced moves alone.

### 5. What remains open

- **"If" direction (θ=180/n ⇒ S=∅):** by §4, this cannot be completed by
  forced moves (Down-shave/Up-shave/Bisection) alone whenever the starting
  triangle avoids all multiples of θ; a genuine non-forced move — one
  planting a value v into *both* children such that Mulan can continue
  winning from whichever Shan-Yu keeps — is needed at least once, and no
  such move has been exhibited and verified in this approach yet. This is
  the open gap for this direction.
- **"Only if" direction (θ≤90°, θ≠180/n ⇒ S≠∅):** no invariant valid
  against *all* of Mulan's moves (forced and non-forced) has been
  established; §4's residue-invariance lemma only shows Shan-Yu is safe
  against the forced sub-family, not against a general adversarial Mulan
  strategy that could use non-forced moves to alter residues. This mirrors
  the open gap flagged in `mod-theta-invariant.md` and is the shared hard
  core of the whole population.
- The θ>90° direction (§2) and the θ=90° base case (§3, 90° lemma) are
  both fully proved and can be cited by any sibling approach without
  re-derivation.
