## imo-2026-02

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Underdetermined system → 1-parameter family, prove invariant along it.** The three angle equations (∠KBA=∠ACL, ∠LBK=∠LNC, ∠LCK=∠BMK) are only 3 scalar equations in 4 unknowns (K,L each have 2 coordinates). Numerically this gives a genuine 1-parameter family of valid (K,L) pairs (parametrize e.g. by θ=∠KBA). The "K inside triangle BMC / L inside triangle BNC / K inside ∠LBA / L inside ∠ACK" clauses are NOT extra equations — they are positional/branch-selection conditions that cut the ambiguous arccos branches down to the geometrically valid sub-interval of θ. The actual theorem to prove is an identity that holds along this whole locus, not at an isolated point. This reframes the problem as: "show OM=ON is invariant under the residual 1-parameter freedom," which is more tractable than expecting a rigid, unique configuration.
  2. **O lies on the perpendicular bisector of MN, and in fact traces that fixed line as the free parameter varies.** This is much stronger than a pointwise OM=ON check (see numeric finding below: O's coordinate along the direction of MN is *exactly constant* = the MN-midpoint's coordinate, across the whole valid range of θ). This suggests looking for a synthetic/algebraic reason O always lands on that specific fixed line — e.g., showing OA² − (fixed quantity) has a clean form, or that O is the intersection of two loci each individually known to be perpendicular-bisector-of-MN-adjacent.
  3. **Spiral-similarity framing at B and C.** The equalities ∠KBA=∠ACL and (∠LBK=∠LNC, ∠LCK=∠BMK) have the shape of two spiral-similarity-style angle conditions: one links rays from B and C directly (∠KBA=∠ACL), and the other two link B,C-based angles to angles at the midpoint triangle vertices M,N (∠LNC, ∠BMK). This suggests hunting for two spiral similarities (centered perhaps at K and at L, or at B and C) that carry the medial triangle data (M,N) onto (B,C) or onto (K,L), and then relating O to a spiral-similarity center of the medial triangle. I checked numerically whether this gives an easy similar-triangle read (△BLK ∼ △NLC or △CKL ∼ △MKB via the shared-vertex spiral criterion) — the angle equality ∠LBK=∠LNC alone is NOT enough to force those two triangles similar (the *included* angles at L, resp. K, are NOT numerically equal — see below), so a naive one-line spiral-similarity claim is a dead end; a genuine argument needs an extra relation (likely from the cross condition ∠KBA=∠ACL, or from the "inside angle" positional data) to close the similarity.
  4. **Coordinate/algebraic brute force.** Set B=(-1,0), C=(1,0) (WLOG, or leave A=(a,b) symbolic), express the 3 angle conditions as tan-based or slope equations for K=(kx,ky), L=(lx,ly), and try to eliminate down to a 1-parameter algebraic curve; then compute the circumcenter O of A,K,L symbolically and check O_x·(N_x−M_x) + O_y·(N_y−M_y) = const (perpendicular-bisector condition), aiming for a fully symbolic (sympy) verification. This is the "hard slog" route but is guaranteed to work in principle since we now know exactly what identity to target (O on perp-bisector of MN) rather than searching blind.

- Small-case / intuition notes (all labeled conjecture, verified numerically only):
  - Set up 3 different scalene triangles (coordinates given in transcript, e.g. A=(0,3), B=(-2,0), C=(3.5,0)), solved the 3 angle equations plus one extra "θ=∠KBA" constraint via `scipy.optimize.fsolve` for many θ values, then filtered to those where K is genuinely inside △BMC and L inside △BNC (checked via barycentric-sign point-in-triangle test).
  - **On the valid sub-interval of θ (where both interior conditions hold), OM=ON numerically to ~1e-10 to 1e-13 precision, consistently, across all 3 triangles tested.** Outside that interval (wrong branch), OM≠ON by macroscopic amounts (differences of 0.03 to 1.9 in these unit-scale triangles) — so the "K inside BMC, L inside BNC" hypotheses are load-bearing, not decorative.
  - **Stronger numeric finding:** in the coordinate frame with BC on the x-axis, O's x-coordinate stayed *exactly* constant (0.375, matching the x-coordinate of the midpoint of MN) across the entire swept range θ=19°…39° (residuals ~1e-9 or better) while O's y-coordinate varied freely. Since MN is horizontal in this frame (M, N both midpoints, MN ∥ BC), this says **O traces exactly the perpendicular bisector of segment MN** as the residual free parameter varies — a much sharper and more useful target than a pointwise distance check, and a natural guide for what algebraic identity a coordinate proof should isolate (an expression for O's projection onto the MN-direction that is manifestly independent of the free parameter).
  - This also confirms the configuration is well-posed and non-vacuous: valid (K,L) pairs satisfying all stated conditions do exist for generic triangles, and form a continuum, not an isolated point.
