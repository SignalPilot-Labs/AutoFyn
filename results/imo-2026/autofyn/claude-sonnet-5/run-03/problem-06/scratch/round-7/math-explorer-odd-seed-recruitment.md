## imo-2026-06 (lens: odd-seed extension of renormalization-induction-on-seed — non-parity fresh-prime-recruitment tracking)

- Distinct openings:
  1. **"Small-p near-total self-sufficiency" fact (new, sharply confirmed this round).** For $p=\min R(a_1)=3$: among 107 primes $q$ tested ($5\le q<600$), $p=3$ locks forever (Nec$\setminus R(a_1)=\emptyset$, $T=1,L=3$) in **every single case except $a_1=15$ ($q=5$)**. This is a much sharper empirical statement than anything currently in `renormalization-induction-on-seed.md` (§4.3/§7.4 only diagnose *why* $p=2$ works and $p\ge3$ doesn't in general; they don't note that $p=3$ is *itself* almost self-sufficient). A genuinely different top-level target for a builder: prove "$p=3$ locks forever whenever $q\ge7$" directly (a fully explicit, checkable, near-total sub-case, structurally analogous to the already-solved even case but for the *next* smallest prime), then treat $a_1=15$ (and its only relatives, seeds with $\{3,5\}\subseteq R(a_1)$) as the base exceptional family to handle separately. This could give a second *fully solved infinite sub-case* (all $a_1$ with $\min R(a_1)=3$ and $5\notin R(a_1)$) parallel to the even-seed theorem, without needing the full general induction.
  2. **"2 is always the (or a) fresh recruit when locking fails" (new, 100% correlation, 60/60 lock-failure instances in the two-prime sweep).** Combined with the already-certified General-$a_2$-Formula / $2p\mid a_2$ Corollary (odd-anchor-lemma.md's companion facts), this suggests a genuinely different mechanism from the already-refuted "parity as a free covering agent": instead of trying to use evenness *directly* as a certificate (dead, per Odd-Anchor Lemma), track **2's own escape race** as a *second instance* of the same Third-Term-Dichotomy-style machinery, now with $p'=2$ playing the role of the locked prime for the *tail* starting at $a_2$ (once $2\mid a_2$). I.e., don't ask "is 2 free," ask "does 2, once introduced at $a_2$, itself win its own escape race" — this is a *non-parity* question (an arithmetic divisibility race, exactly like the $p=3$ vs $q$ race in Opening 1) about whether 2 becomes self-sufficient for the sub-sequence of even terms, structurally recursive rather than assumed free.
  3. **Escape-race state-growth induction (extends current best, not new architecture but a concretely different induction target than $\omega(a_1)$).** The population's current diagnosis (§4.3/§5 of `renormalization-induction-on-seed.md`) is that no bounded lookahead works because the state needed to decide the race grows with $n$. A genuinely different framing: induct not on $\omega(a_1)$ (seed's own prime count) but on $|Q_{\min}\setminus R(a_1)|$ — the number of fresh recruits *already realized* — and try to show that whichever fresh prime is recruited **last** must, at the moment of its own recruitment, satisfy a small-prime-style self-sufficiency criterion relative to the *already-accumulated* active set (echoing Opening 1's "$p=3$ almost always locks" finding, generalized: a newly recruited prime $r$ that is small relative to the current accumulated $L$ tends to lock immediately). This is untried by any approach in the population; it targets a genuinely different monovariant (the *recruited set's own arithmetic*, not a function of $a_1$ alone) than the already-dead $\Lambda^{(K)}$/covering-density mechanisms.

- Candidate technique(s): (a) direct algebraic extension of the already-certified Third-Term Dichotomy Lemma (`lemmas/third-term-dichotomy-lemma.md`) to characterize *when* the $p=3$ race is won forever (Opening 1) — should be tractable since $p=3$ leaves only ONE "in-between" candidate class beyond the free Minimum-Gap exclusion ($a_n+2$, the only candidate $2\le j\le p-1=2$), i.e. essentially the same proof shape as the even-seed theorem but with one extra candidate to rule out instead of zero; (b) recursive/self-similar application of the escape-race formula to the *recruited* prime 2 (Opening 2), reusing `general-a2-formula.md` and `third-term-dichotomy-lemma.md`'s proof technique rather than its literal statement; (c) NOT recommended as a fresh target: closed-form covering-density thresholds ($\Lambda^{(K)}$, jacobsthal-covering-bound) — already deprioritized 2+ rounds, no new lever found this round either.
