## imo-2026-06 — odd-seed extension route (lens: renormalization-induction-on-seed's open Step 2 for p=min R(a_1) odd)

- Distinct openings:
  1. **New free general lemma (verified, not in `lemmas/` yet): a_2 = a_1 + p unconditionally, for EVERY a_1>1**, where p:=min R(a_1) — not just for prime-power or squarefree-two-prime seeds as currently proved in §3/§4.1 of renormalization-induction-on-seed.md. Proof sketch (3 lines, cheap): for 1≤j<p, j is not ≡0 mod any prime factor q of a_1 (since q≥p>j>0), so none of a_1+1,...,a_1+p-1 shares a factor with a_1; a_1+p is a multiple of p, hence valid; minimality gives a_2=a_1+p. This is a genuinely new, more general lever than what's currently certified, and it directly explains the "odd p" obstruction structurally: **if a_1 is odd, p is odd, so a_2=a_1+p is automatically EVEN** — the fresh prime 2 is recruited *unconditionally* at index 2 whenever a_1 is odd. This reframes "odd seed ⟹ fresh-prime recruitment" not as a possible failure mode but as a forced, provable fact about a_2 specifically (distinct from later, harder-to-control recruitments at a_3, a_4, ...).
  2. **Two-phase covering picture**: after step 2, the sequence has (at least) two "covering agents" available for future candidates: p (covers multiples of p) and 2 (covers even numbers, via a_2). An in-between candidate a_n+j (2≤j≤p-1, for a_n≡0 mod p) is validated for free if it is even (shares factor 2 with a_2, PROVIDED 2 still needs to be checked only against a_2, not the whole prefix — this still needs an argument that 2 stays "safe" against all earlier odd terms too, which is not free). This halves the "unaccounted" p-2 candidates roughly in half (odd, non-multiple-of-p ones remain the genuine problem) — a concrete narrowing of the gap, not a solve.
  3. **Lock success vs failure is a real, non-monotonic seed-dependent dichotomy**, not resolvable by any single small feature (q mod p, q mod 6, size of q) tried. Two disjoint outcomes are numerically observed for every odd two-prime seed a1=pq: either (a) T=1, L=p forever (permanent lock, "trivial" case — majority of cases, ~75% in the sweep below) or (b) the lock breaks at some finite step and eventual L is a genuinely richer product including at least one extra prime — and in every resolved case tested, **2 | L whenever the lock fails** (26/26 resolved fail-cases, zero counterexamples). This "2 always joins L on failure" conjecture, if provable, would be a clean structural fact worth targeting as an intermediate lemma — it is consistent with observation 1 (2 is always recruited at a_2, so it is a natural first candidate to become periodically active when the lock breaks) but is NOT proved here — only checked numerically.
  4. **Transients/periods can be very large relative to a_1** — e.g. a_1=187=11·17 needs T=484, L=7854 (found only after extending the search window/simulation length substantially — an initial pass with fewer terms returned "unresolved"); a_1=209 needs T=528; a_1=221 needs T=334; a_1=385 remains unresolved even at 40000 simulated terms with max_T search up to 1500 (period, if it exists — and it must, per the problem statement — is even larger). This is important negative evidence reinforcing the already-certified `bounded-lookahead-insufficiency.md`: not only can a fixed small lookahead fail to certify locking, the transient before periodicity sets in can be enormous and does not appear bounded by any simple function of a_1 alone (rules out hoping for "small polynomial in a_1" bounds on T or the recruitment step count).
