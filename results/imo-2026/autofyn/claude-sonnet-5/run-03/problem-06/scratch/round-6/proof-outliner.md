renormalization-induction-on-seed: revise
Target: For every positive integer sequence a_1,a_2,... generated by the greedy rule, exhibit T,L with a_{n+T}=a_n+L for every n (the full IMO claim).
Technique: strong induction on a well-founded measure of the seed a_1 (currently omega(a_1)-style), NOT the shared Q/Good_Q machinery. This round's mechanism: a fully general a_2=a_1+min R(a_1) lemma plus a "two covering agents" (p and 2) argument for odd seeds.
Skeleton:
  1. Certify the general Lemma: a_2 = a_1 + p where p:=min R(a_1), for EVERY a_1>1 — by the same 3-line minimality argument as the existing squarefree-two-prime Lemma 4.1, generalized (no prime-factorization restriction on a_1 needed).
  2. Corollary: if a_1 is odd, p is odd, so a_2 is unconditionally even — the prime 2 is forced into the active set at index 2 for every odd seed (not a possible failure mode, a proven fact).
  3. Attempt: prove 2 remains a valid covering agent for every even in-between candidate a_n+j against the ENTIRE prefix a_1,...,a_n (not just a_1,a_2) — the open technical step.
  4. If (3) succeeds, restate the open gap as: only the ceil((p-2)/2) ODD non-multiple-of-p in-between candidates remain unaccounted, for the "lock succeeds" branch; separately attack the numerically-supported conjecture "lock fails ⟹ 2 | L" as a standalone, smaller deliverable.
  5. Cover BOTH branches (lock succeeds forever vs. lock eventually fails) — do not treat covering-agent mechanism as only needing to explain failures.
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - a_2 = a_1 + p, for every a_1>1 — because for 1≤t<p no prime factor of a_1 (all ≥p) can divide t, so gcd(a_1+t,a_1)=1, while a_1+p is a genuine multiple of p.
  - Corollary: odd a_1 ⟹ a_2 even — immediate parity of a_1+p with p odd.
  - Conjecture (not yet proved): lock-on-p fails ⟹ 2 | eventual L — numerically 26/26 resolved odd-seed failures, no counterexample; mechanism unclear, flagged as conjecture only.
Open gaps: step 3 (2's safety against the whole growing prefix, not just a_1,a_2) is the crux unresolved step; the "lock succeeds forever" branch has no argument at all yet; the general induction over arbitrary omega(a_1) remains open beyond this.
Cases to cover: odd a_1 with p=min R(a_1) — both the lock-succeeds and lock-fails sub-branches, for every odd prime p, not just p=3,5 spot-checked.
Watch out for: do not silently reuse naive permanent-locking (refuted a_1=35) or bounded-lookahead certification (refuted a_1=65); do not assume a single congruence (q mod p, q mod 6, size of q) predicts lock success — explorer checked and found none.

scalar-difference-pigeonhole: revise
Target: same full IMO claim, via the prime-free scalar route — does Y_{T*} become syndetic/cofinite for some T*?
Technique: Morse-Hedlund subword-(factor-)complexity reformulation of the entire central gap in terms of the finite-alphabet gap sequence (d_n), citing Morse-Hedlund by name (add to knowledge_base.md).
Skeleton:
  1. Restate the central gap exactly as: p(k) (number of distinct length-k factors of (d_n)) is bounded as k→∞ (Morse-Hedlund: eventually periodic over a finite alphabet iff p(k)≤k for some k, equiv. p bounded since non-decreasing).
  2. Prove the free Complexity Bound Lemma: each length-k factor of (d_n) determines g_n(k) (its sum) exactly, and g_n(k)∈[k,kR] (Lemma 1, already certified) — hence at most kR-k+1 distinct SUMS occur; carefully verify/fix whether this actually bounds p(k) itself (distinct factors could share a sum) before claiming p(k)=O(k).
  3. Sanity-check against the known-solved even-a_1 sub-case: (d_n) is constant (=2), so p(k)=1 for all k — the machinery must reproduce this trivially.
  4. Attempt to sharpen the O(k) bound toward the needed ≤k, or connect boundedness of p(k) to the Q_min/Nec machinery as a cross-check (likely reduces back to the same central gap — state this honestly if so).
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - Morse-Hedlund equivalence (classical, cite by name) — a one-sided finite-alphabet sequence is eventually periodic iff its factor complexity is bounded.
  - Complexity Bound Lemma (new, to be proved carefully) — because Lemma 1's g_n(k) bound directly limits the number of distinct partial sums realizable by any length-k window.
Open gaps: whether p(k) is actually bounded remains fully open; step 2's argument needs care (sum-collisions can make it invalid as a bound on p(k) itself, only valid on distinct-sum count).
Cases to cover: none; the reformulation is case-free.
Watch out for: don't conflate "Morse-Hedlund gives eventual periodicity" with "periodicity holds from n=1 with zero transient" (a separate, stronger, though numerically supported, claim) — the transient-free finish still needs the certified transient-free-finishing-theorem.md-style argument on top.
