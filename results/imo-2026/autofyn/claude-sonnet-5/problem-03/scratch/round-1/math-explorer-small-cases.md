## imo-2026-03

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Closed-form conjecture from exact small-case computation**: c(n) = 2^n / (2^{n+1} − 1), derived and checked *exactly* (via `fractions.Fraction`, not just floats) for n=1,2,3,4. This is the strongest lead — the outliner should treat this as the target answer and build proof machinery (bound + construction) around it, not re-derive from scratch.
  2. **Claiming-subgame as a clean recursive game value**: for a fixed multiset of piece lengths S, the value to the player-to-move under optimal play is `g(S) = sum(S) − min_{x∈S} g(S\{x})`, `g(∅)=0`. This is just the minimax recursion (no assumption needed — it IS the definition of optimal play in a finite perfect-information sequential game with a fixed total payoff). A rigorous solution needs to formalize this recursion (or an equivalent direct argument) and then prove greedy-take-largest realizes it — see below.
  3. **"Equal-pairs ⇒ exactly half" lemma as XY's weapon**: if the piece multiset can be partitioned into pairs of equal lengths (values can differ *across* pairs), the second player can mirror ("whenever the mover takes one piece of a pair, immediately take its partner") and thereby guarantee exactly half the total to himself, capping the first mover at exactly 1/2. Verified computationally (e.g. pieces {0.2,0.2,0.3,0.3} give g = 0.5 exactly, matching sum/2). This explains *why* naïve LB strategies (e.g. n+1 equal pieces) fail, and is the mechanism XY exploits.
  4. **Dyadic/geometric construction opening**: LB's apparent optimal move is to place his n points so the n+1 resulting pieces have lengths proportional to `2^n, 2^{n-1}, …, 2, 1` (sum `2^{n+1}−1`), i.e. piece i (in decreasing order) has length `2^{n+1-i}/(2^{n+1}-1)`. Note the **largest piece alone already has length exactly c(n) = 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)** — the conjectured answer. This matches KB entry on dyadic/geometric sequences (see below) and is a strong candidate for the "construction" half of the proof.
  5. **XY's matching response = "halve the top pieces"**: computationally, XY's best response to the dyadic LB construction is to split the *largest* few pieces each exactly in half using his n points (e.g. for n=3: split the 8/15-piece into two 4/15's and the 4/15-piece into two 2/15's, using 2 of his 3 points; the 3rd point doesn't help further). This produces a mix of equal-pairs (which XY neutralizes to exactly half each) plus untouched small "reserve" pieces, and the arithmetic collapses to exactly the big-piece value. This gives a candidate *general* XY strategy (not just against the dyadic construction) worth formalizing for the upper-bound half of the proof: given ANY LB configuration, XY should try to create maximal equal-pairing among the largest pieces.

- Candidate technique(s):
  - Recursive/inductive game-value analysis of the claiming subgame (prove greedy-largest optimal, or work directly with the `g(S) = sum(S) − min_x g(S\{x})` recursion).
  - "Equal pairs force exactly half" pairing/mirroring argument (needs a clean proof — likely an easy induction on pairs, comparing to KB's pairing/blocking-strategy techniques).
  - Dyadic/geometric sequence construction ("largest term exceeds sum of the rest," related to KB idea from crux aimo-0117 — see below) for the lower-bound (LB's guarantee) construction.
  - Induction on n (build the n-piece optimal configuration from the (n−1)-piece one; note 2^{n+1}−1 = 2(2^n−1)+1 suggests a natural doubling recursion).

- Cheap-kill candidates:
  - **Refute the naive "(n+1)/(2n+1)" answer immediately.** This is the most tempting first guess (LB splits into n+1 equal pieces so that, if XY splits the rest into n+1 more equal pieces, we get 2n+1 equal pieces and LB — moving first — gets ⌈(2n+1)/2⌉/(2n+1) = (n+1)/(2n+1) of the total). **This is WRONG for n≥2**: explicit counterexample computed — LB points at (1/5, 2/5) [pieces 1/5,1/5,3/5], XY responds with a *single* point at 0.7 splitting the 3/5-piece into two 3/10's, giving pieces {1/5,1/5,3/10,3/10} = two equal pairs, forcing g = exactly 1/2 < 3/5. (Verified both by exact recursion and by hand via the pairing lemma.) The flaw: LB's own construction created two equal pieces (1/5 and 1/5), which is a self-inflicted vulnerability XY exploits with fewer points than the "obvious" n+1-piece split would suggest. **Do not let the outliner default to this formula without checking it against this counterexample.**
  - Parity/pairing check: before any deep casework, check whether a candidate LB configuration allows XY to create an equal-pairing partition with ≤n points — if so, that configuration is capped at ≤1/2 (or the appropriate corrected bound) and is suboptimal.
  - The dyadic construction structurally prevents "cheap" equal pairs among LB's own original pieces (all distinct, ratio 2 apart), which is presumably part of why it resists XY's attacks.

- Small-case / intuition notes (all labeled conjecture except where marked "exact by construction/verification"):
  - **n=1: c(1) = 2/3, exact** (proved by hand casework — see reasoning below — and confirmed by fine grid search). LB places his one point at 1/3 (pieces 1/3, 2/3). If XY does nothing, LB takes 2/3 outright (greedy on 2 pieces). If XY splits the 2/3-piece into x, (2/3−x): the best XY can do is the balanced split x=1/3, giving three equal pieces of 1/3 each, and LB (moving first on an odd count of equal items) still nets ⌈3/2⌉·(1/3) = 2/3. Both XY options tie at exactly 2/3; any other choice of LB's single point (a ≠ 1/3) gives XY a strictly better response for himself, confirmed by full case analysis (split into `a<1/3` regime where XY's optimal is a balanced split of the big piece giving LB `(1+a)/2 < 2/3`, and `a≥1/3` regime where XY's optimal is to do nothing giving LB `1−a ≤ 2/3`, continuous and maximized exactly at a=1/3).
  - **n=2: c(2) = 4/7, strong numerical conjecture (exact-fraction verified)**. LB's construction: points splitting the stick into pieces (4/7, 2/7, 1/7) — a dyadic sequence. Verified via exact `Fraction` arithmetic that XY's best response (searched over single points, pairs of points with denominator up to 140, i.e. >9000 exact configurations) never beats 4/7, and that XY splitting the big 4/7-piece exactly in half (→ 2/7,2/7, tying with the existing 2/7-piece to form an equal pair) achieves exactly 4/7. Outer grid search over LB's own point choices (14×14 grid combined with local-search adversary) found 4/7 as the unique top value, achieved exactly at dyadic-type configurations (points at multiples of 1/7 matching {4,2,1}/7 in various orders/reflections).
  - **n=3: c(3) = 8/15, strong numerical conjecture (exact-fraction verified)**. Dyadic construction (8,4,2,1)/15. Random search over 3-point XY responses (20000 trials, exact fractions with denominator 225) found minimum exactly 8/15, matching target; XY's near-optimal strategy is "halve the top two pieces" (8/15→2×4/15, matching the existing 4/15 piece into a pair; 4/15... — i.e., using 2 of his 3 points to create equal pairs among the top pieces, leaving the smallest pieces as reserve untouched).
  - **n=4: c(4) = 16/31, strong numerical conjecture (exact-fraction verified)**. Dyadic construction (16,8,4,2,1)/31. Random search (15000 trials, exact fractions denom 310) and explicit "halve top k pieces" constructions (k=3 or k=4) all hit exactly 16/31 as the minimum XY can force, none lower.
  - **Pattern: c(n) = 2^n / (2^{n+1} − 1)**, i.e. numerators 2,4,8,16 and denominators 3,7,15,31 for n=1,2,3,4 — a very clean, IMO-plausible closed form. As n→∞, c(n) → 1/2⁺, consistent with the "equal-pairing caps the mover at exactly half" mechanism becoming maximally exploitable as XY's point budget grows relative to LB's pieces.
  - **Claiming-subgame fact (verified, not yet proven on paper)**: greedy-take-largest is always among the optimal moves in the claiming phase (checked against the full minimax recursion on 2000 random exact-rational instances, 0 mismatches). This should be provable by a short exchange/induction argument and is likely needed as a lemma regardless of which top-level approach is chosen.
  - **Open gap for the outliner**: the *upper bound* half (proving NO LB configuration beats 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1), i.e. that XY always has a response capping LB at ≤ this value, for arbitrary — not just dyadic — LB configurations) is the harder direction and is only checked numerically here for the specific dyadic LB construction, not proven for all possible LB configurations. This is the main remaining gap: a general argument for "whatever LB does with n points, XY can respond to force ≤ 2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)" is needed, likely via an inductive/greedy pairing strategy for XY (pair up the two largest available pieces by splitting the larger down to match the smaller, recursively) — this needs to be worked out and proven, not just spot-checked.
