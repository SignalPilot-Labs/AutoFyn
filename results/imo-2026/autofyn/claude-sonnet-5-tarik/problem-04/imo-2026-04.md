## Full proof

### 0. Setup: the angle-triple model

Throughout, a triangle is identified with its unordered angle data, but for
bookkeeping we always work with an *ordered* triple `(α,β,γ)` of positive
reals with `α+β+γ=180`, where the labels record which vertex holds which
angle. All angles are measured in degrees.

**The move, in coordinates.** Suppose Mulan attacks the vertex `A` holding
angle `α` (the argument is identical for `β` or `γ`; see the Symmetry Remark
in §2 for why we may always assume the attacked angle is named `α`). She
chooses a point `P` on side `BC` (the side opposite `A`), distinct from `B`
and `C`, and cuts along `AP`. This splits `α` into two parts: let `t=∠BAP`,
so `0<t<α` and the other part is `α−t=∠CAP`.

* Triangle `ABP` has angles `∠ABP=β` (unchanged, since `B` is untouched), `∠BAP=t`,
  and `∠APB = 180−t−β`. Since `α+β+γ=180`, we have `180−β=α+γ`, so
  `∠APB=α+γ−t`. Call this triangle's angle-triple
  `L(t) = (t, β, α+γ−t)`.
* Triangle `ACP` has angles `∠ACP=γ` (unchanged), `∠CAP=α−t`, and
  `∠APC=180−(α−t)−γ`. Since `∠APB+∠APC=180` (P lies on the straight line BC),
  `∠APC = 180−(α+γ−t) = β+t`. Call this triangle's angle-triple
  `R(t) = (α−t, γ, β+t)`.

Both are valid angle-triples: `t+β+(α+γ−t)=α+β+γ=180` and
`(α−t)+γ+(β+t)=α+β+γ=180`, and each of the six listed quantities is strictly
positive for `t∈(0,α)` (since `β,γ>0` already, and `t,α−t>0` by choice of `P`,
and the two `P`-angles `α+γ−t=180−t−β<180` and `β+t<180` are automatically in
`(0,180)` because they are triangle angles summing correctly with two other
positive angles). Mulan chooses `t∈(0,α)` freely (a continuous real
parameter); Shan-Yu then discards one of `L(t)`, `R(t)` and the survivor
becomes the new `T`. If the game has not already stopped (no angle exactly
`θ`), it repeats on the new `T`. This is the complete and exact translation of
the geometric rules into the angle-triple pursuit game; every argument below
is phrased purely in terms of `(α,β,γ)`, `L(t)`, `R(t)`.

**Convention.** For a real `x` and the fixed angle `θ`, write `x ≡ 0 (mod θ)`
to mean `x/θ ∈ ℤ`, and `x≡y (mod θ)` to mean `(x−y)/θ∈ℤ`. Note `x≡0 (mod θ)`
with `x>0` is exactly "`x` is a positive integer multiple of `θ`," and in
particular `x=θ` is the case `x≡0 (mod θ)` with `x/θ=1`.

**Answer.** Mulan can force a win in finitely many moves **iff θ=180°/n for
some integer n≥2.**

### 1. The winning direction: θ = 180°/n, n≥2 an integer

Fix such an n and write θ=180/n; note θ≤90 (equality iff n=2).

#### 1.1 Move 1 (transfer lemma)

**Lemma 1.** Suppose the current triangle has an angle `α>θ` (strict). Then
Mulan can force the deterministic transition
`(α,β,γ) → (α−θ, γ, β+θ)` (or, symmetrically, `(α,β,γ)→(α−θ,β,γ+θ)`, her
choice of which of the other two angles absorbs `+θ`), in exactly one move,
regardless of Shan-Yu's decision.

*Proof.* Since `α>θ>0`, `t=θ` lies in the valid range `(0,α)`. Set `t=θ`.
Then `L(θ)=(θ,β,α+γ−θ)` — its first coordinate is *exactly* `θ`. If Shan-Yu
keeps `L(θ)`, the very next check of the game ("does `T` have an angle
`=θ`?") succeeds and Mulan wins immediately. Since Shan-Yu plays to avoid
losing, and keeping `L(θ)` loses at once, Shan-Yu is forced to keep
`R(θ)=(α−θ,γ,β+θ)`. (If keeping `L(θ)` were somehow also fine for Shan-Yu the
game would already be over in Mulan's favor, so there is nothing to check —
either way Mulan is at least as well off.) This proves the first form.

For the symmetric form, set instead `t=α−θ` (also in `(0,α)` since `α>θ`).
Then `R(α−θ)=(θ,γ,β+α−θ)` has first coordinate `θ`, so by the identical
argument Shan-Yu is forced to keep `L(α−θ)=(α−θ,β,α+γ−(α−θ))=(α−θ,β,γ+θ)`. ∎

**Remark (immateriality of the label).** Both forms of Lemma 1 produce the
same *first* coordinate `α−θ`; only the labels attached to the other two
slots differ (whether `β` or `γ` receives the `+θ`). Since none of our
subsequent arguments ever refer to a "named" vertex — only to the *value*
`α−θ` and to the *value* of whichever angle is used as a helper in Move 2
below — this choice is immaterial throughout, and we do not re-derive it at
each stage: we simply write the post-Move-1 triangle as `(α−θ, X, Y+θ)` with
`{X,Y}` an unordered pair equal to `{β,γ}`, Mulan's choice of which is which.

**Corollary (iteration).** If `α = kθ` for an integer `k≥2`, iterating Lemma
1 on the shrinking first coordinate (always re-attacking the surviving
`α−θ`-slot) forces, after `k−1` further applications, the first coordinate to
equal exactly `θ` — an outright win. More generally, for *any* `α>θ`,
repeated application of Lemma 1 to the shrinking lineage is well-defined: at
each step the current lineage value is positive (if it was `>θ` before the
step, subtracting `θ` leaves it `>0`), and it strictly decreases by exactly
`θ` per step, so after finitely many steps (at most `⌈α/θ⌉` steps) the
lineage value is `≤θ`; if it lands exactly on `θ` the game is already won,
otherwise it lands in the open interval `(0,θ)`.

#### 1.2 Move 2 (helper-reset lemma)

**Lemma 2.** Suppose the current triangle `(α,β,γ)` has some angle `h<θ`
(the "helper"), and the remaining two angles are `A` and `k` (so
`{β,γ}={h,k}` up to which of `α,β,γ` plays the role of `h`; relabel so that
`α` is the angle *not* equal to `h`, i.e. one of the "attacked" candidates).
If `A` — one of the two angles other than `h` — satisfies `A+h>θ`, then
Mulan can force, in one move, the deterministic transition to the triangle
`(θ−h, h, 180−θ)` (independent of which specific starting triangle produced
`A,h`; the third original angle plays no role in the result beyond being
absorbed into the constant `180−θ`).

*Proof.* Write the current triangle as `(A, h, k)` with `A` the angle to be
attacked (so `α:=A`, `β:=h`, `γ:=k` in the notation of §0), where `A,h,k>0`,
`A+h+k=180`, `h<θ`, and `A+h>θ` (given). Set `t=θ−h`. Since `h<θ`, `t>0`; since
`A+h>θ`, `t=θ−h<A`; so `t∈(0,A)` is a valid choice. Compute, using
`A+k=180−h` (from `A+h+k=180`):
```
L(t) = (t, h, A+k−t) = (θ−h, h, (180−h)−(θ−h)) = (θ−h, h, 180−θ).
R(t) = (A−t, k, h+t) = (A−(θ−h), k, h+(θ−h)) = (A+h−θ, k, θ).
```
`R(t)`'s third coordinate is exactly `θ`, so by the identical forced-move
argument as in Lemma 1 (keeping `R(t)` ends the game at once in Mulan's
favor, so Shan-Yu is forced to avoid it), Shan-Yu must keep
`L(t)=(θ−h,h,180−θ)`. This is a valid triangle: all three of `θ−h>0` (since
`h<θ`), `h>0`, `180−θ>0` (since `θ<180`) hold, and they sum to `180`. ∎

**Symmetric restatement (which of β, γ is the helper does not matter).** In
the derivation above, we called the helper `h` (playing the "β-slot" of
§0) and the un-attacked leftover angle `k` (the "γ-slot"). If instead we want
the *other* remaining angle to play the helper role, the identical
computation with `β,γ` swapped in the §0 formulas (equivalently, replacing
`t` by `u=A−t`, under which `R(t)` as a function of `u` is
`(u,k,A+h−u)` — literally the same shape as `L` with `h,k` swapped) gives:
setting `u=θ−k` (valid exactly when `k<θ` and `A+k>θ`) forces the kept
triangle to be `(θ−k,k,180−θ)`. So Lemma 2 applies verbatim with either of
the two non-attacked angles serving as the helper, as long as that one is
`<θ` and its sum with the attacked angle exceeds `θ`; we use whichever
satisfies these two conditions (shown to always exist below), and we state
this once here rather than re-deriving it at each occurrence.

#### 1.3 The room-condition lemma (when is Move 2 available?)

**Lemma 3 (room condition).** Let `(a,b,c)` be any triangle (`a,b,c>0`,
`a+b+c=180`) with `θ≤90`. If some angle `h ∈ {a,b,c}` satisfies `h<θ`, then at
least one of the other two angles `A` (the two candidates being the two
elements of `{a,b,c}` other than the chosen instance of `h`) satisfies
`A+h>θ`. Hence Lemma 2 is always applicable whenever some current angle is
`<θ`.

*Proof.* Fix an instance of an angle `h<θ`, and let `A_1,A_2` be the other
two angles, so `A_1+A_2=180−h`. Suppose, for contradiction, that
**both** `A_1+h≤θ` and `A_2+h≤θ`. Adding these two inequalities:
`A_1+A_2+2h ≤ 2θ`, i.e. `(180−h)+2h ≤ 2θ`, i.e. `180+h≤2θ`. Since `θ≤90`
(this is where `n≥2` is used), `2θ≤180`, so `180+h≤180`, giving `h≤0`. This
contradicts `h>0`. Hence at least one of `A_1+h>θ`, `A_2+h>θ` must hold. ∎

**Lemma 4 (the complementary case).** If a triangle `(a,b,c)` has *no* angle
`<θ` — equivalently all three angles are `≥θ`, and since (by assumption, else
the game already ended) none equals `θ` exactly, all three are `>θ` strictly
— then `a+b+c>3θ`, so `180>3θ`, i.e. `θ<60`, i.e. `n>3`, i.e. `n≥4`. (So this
case is vacuous for `n=2,3`; for `n≥4` it is genuinely possible, e.g. the
equilateral triangle `(60,60,60)` when `θ<60`.) In this case Lemma 1 is
directly applicable, since every angle exceeds `θ`.

*Proof.* Immediate from `a,b,c>θ ⟹ a+b+c>3θ=180 ⟹ θ<60`. ∎

Lemmas 3 and 4 together show: **every** triangle with no angle equal to `θ`
falls into exactly one of two cases — (Case I) some angle `<θ`, in which case
Lemma 2 fires directly; or (Case II) all angles `>θ` (only possible if
`n≥4`), in which case Lemma 1 fires directly. These two cases are exhaustive
(their defining conditions, "∃ angle `<θ`" vs. "no angle `<θ`," are logical
negations of each other) and mutually exclusive by construction.

#### 1.4 The winning algorithm

We now describe Mulan's full strategy from an **arbitrary** starting
triangle `(a,b,c)` (Shan-Yu's adversarial choice) and prove it terminates in
her favor in a number of moves bounded by `2n−2` (a bound depending only on
`n`, not on the starting triangle).

**Step 0.** If some current angle already equals `θ`, the game is already
over (Mulan wins). Otherwise proceed.

**Step 1 (dispatch via Lemmas 3/4).** Apply the exhaustive case split of
§1.3 to the *current* triangle.

- **Case I** (some angle `h<θ`): go directly to Step 2.
- **Case II** (all angles `>θ`, only possible if `n≥4`): apply Lemma 1's
  iteration Corollary to a fixed lineage (e.g. starting from the maximum
  angle `A_0=max(a,b,c)>θ`, always directing the `+θ` bump in Lemma 1 onto
  the *same* one of the other two angles, leaving the third completely
  untouched throughout this loop). Each application is licensed because the
  current lineage value is `>θ` (checked before applying); by the Corollary,
  after at most `⌈A_0/θ⌉ ≤ n−1` applications (since `A_0<180=nθ`, the lineage
  value strictly decreases by `θ` each time and remains positive throughout,
  as shown in §1.1's Corollary) the lineage value either lands exactly on
  `θ` (Mulan already wins — stop) or lands in the open interval `(0,θ)`. In
  the latter outcome, we now have an explicit current triangle with an angle
  `h<θ` (the terminal lineage value): **we re-apply the case split of §1.3 to
  this new (post-loop) triangle**, exactly as in Step 1 — it is a bona fide
  triangle with three positive angles summing to `180`, so Lemma 3 applies to
  it verbatim (this is the reapplication the outline review required: the
  room-condition lemma is a statement about *any* valid angle-triple, so it
  is licensed again here with no new argument needed beyond invoking Lemma 3
  a second time, now on the new triple). Since this new triangle by
  construction has an angle `<θ`, it lands in Case I. Proceed to Step 2.

**Step 2 (fire Move 2 once).** By Lemma 3 applied to the current triangle
(whether it is the original one, in the direct Case I branch, or the
post-loop one from Case II), there is a valid helper `h<θ` and partner `A`
with `A+h>θ`. Apply Lemma 2: the triangle deterministically becomes
`(θ−h, h, 180−θ)`.

**Step 3 (finish via the universal constant).** Since `θ=180/n`, we compute
`180−θ = 180 − 180/n = 180(n−1)/n = (n−1)θ` — an **exact** positive integer
multiple of `θ`. Two sub-cases:

- **If n=2:** `(n−1)θ=θ` exactly, so the triangle produced in Step 2 already
  contains the angle `θ` literally (as its third coordinate) — the game is
  already won at the end of Step 2; no further move is needed. (Concretely,
  for `n=2` one checks directly from the formulas in Lemma 2's proof that
  `R(t)`'s third coordinate is always `θ` *and* `L(t)`'s third coordinate
  `180−θ` also equals `θ`, so **both** children of the Step-2 move contain
  `θ` — Shan-Yu's choice is moot.)
- **If n≥3:** the coordinate `(n−1)θ` satisfies `(n−1)θ>θ` (since `n−1≥2`),
  so Lemma 1 applies directly to it (it is `>θ` at this exact moment, no
  further case-dispatch needed — we are targeting this *specific, known*
  coordinate, not re-running the generic room-condition split). Apply
  Lemma 1's iteration to this coordinate's lineage: each application
  subtracts exactly `θ`, and since the lineage starts at an *exact* multiple
  of `θ`, every intermediate value `(n−1−j)θ` (`j=0,1,2,...`) is again an
  exact multiple of `θ`, so it is always either `>θ` (continue) or exactly
  `θ` (stop, win) — it can never land in a "remainder" sub-interval `(0,θ)`
  the way the Step-1 loop could, precisely because we started from an exact
  multiple. After exactly `n−2` further applications the lineage reaches
  `θ` exactly. Win.

**Termination bound.** Step 1's loop uses at most `n−1` applications of
Lemma 1; Step 2 uses exactly 1 application of Lemma 2; Step 3 uses at most
`n−2` further applications of Lemma 1 (0 if `n=2`). Total: at most
`(n−1)+1+(n−2) = 2n−2` moves, a bound depending only on `n`, valid from
**any** starting triangle. This proves Mulan has a winning strategy,
terminating in finitely (and boundedly) many moves, whenever `θ=180°/n`.

*Why the two-case dispatch in Step 1 is exhaustive and the hand-off to Step 2
is justified at every stage (addressing the outline-review's Gap 1
explicitly):* Step 1 is invoked exactly twice in the worst case — once on
the original triangle, and (only if Case II fired) once more on the triangle
produced at the end of the Step-1 loop. In both invocations, the object being
tested is an honest angle-triple `(x,y,z)` with `x,y,z>0`, `x+y+z=180`, and no
coordinate `=θ` (checked in Step 0 / re-checked implicitly since the loop
stops the instant a coordinate hits `θ`) — exactly the hypotheses of Lemmas
3–4. There is no third invocation: by construction, the second invocation
(if it happens) always lands in Case I (an angle `<θ` was just manufactured
by the loop), so it flows straight into Step 2 without looping back into
Case II again. This is a *linear* three-phase pipeline (Step 1 → Step 2 →
Step 3), not a recursive or open-ended reapplication, so termination is
manifest from the explicit bound above.

#### 1.5 Worked examples

**Example A: θ=90°=180/2 (n=2).** Take the adversarial-looking start
`(100°,50°,30°)` (no angle is `90°`). Since `50<90` and `30<90`, we are in
Case I immediately (Step 1 skips its loop). Take helper `h=50` — by Lemma 3,
check partners: `100+50=150>90` ✓ (also `30+50=80<90`, not valid alone, but
one valid partner suffices). Attack `A=100` with helper `h=50` (leftover
`k=30`), `t=θ−h=40`:
```
L(40) = (40, 50, 100+30−40) = (40,50,90)
R(40) = (100−40, 30, 50+40) = (60,30,90)
```
Both `L` and `R` already contain `90°=θ` (this is the `n=2` collapse noted
in Step 3): whichever Shan-Yu keeps, the game ends immediately with an angle
of exactly `90°`. **Mulan wins in 1 move.**

**Example B: θ=60°=180/3 (n=3).** Take the same numbers `(100°,50°,30°)`
(no angle is `60°`) but now `θ=60`. Since `50<60`, Case I. Take helper
`h=30` (leftover `k=50`), partner `A=100`: `100+30=130>60` ✓. `t=θ−h=30`:
```
L(30) = (30, 30, 100+50−30) = (30,30,120)
R(30) = (100−30, 50, 30+30) = (70,50,60)
```
`R` contains `60°=θ`, so Shan-Yu is forced to keep `L=(30°,30°,120°)`. Now

branch) applies: attack the `120°` angle with `t=θ=60` (the other two angles
are both `30°`, so `β=γ=30` here):
```
L(60) = (60, 30, 120+30−60) = (60,30,90)
R(60) = (120−60, 30, 30+60) = (60,30,90)
```
`L` and `R` coincide exactly (since the two non-attacked angles were equal,
`30=30`) and both equal `(60°,30°,90°)`, which contains `60°=θ`. **Mulan wins
in exactly 2 moves**, matching the bound `2n−2=4` (an over-estimate, as
expected — the bound is worst-case).

### 2. The survival direction: θ with 180/θ ∉ ℤ

Now suppose `180/θ` is **not** an integer (this includes every `θ>90°` other
than degenerate values, every θ with `θ∤180` among rationals, and every
irrational θ). We exhibit an explicit strategy by which Shan-Yu survives
forever, regardless of Mulan's play.

#### 2.1 Shan-Yu's explicit initial triangle

**Construction.** Shan-Yu starts with the triangle
```
T_0 = ( θ/2, θ/2, 180−θ ).
```

**Validity.** `θ/2>0` and `180−θ>0` (since `0<θ<180`); the three angles sum
to `θ/2+θ/2+(180−θ)=180`. So `T_0` is a genuine triangle for **every**
`θ∈(0°,180°)` — this is a single closed-form family, not a case-by-case or
"generic" choice.

**No angle is a multiple of θ.** We check each of the three angles of `T_0`
against the definition `x≡0 (mod θ) ⟺ x/θ∈ℤ`:
- `(θ/2)/θ = 1/2 ∉ ℤ` — true for **every** real `θ>0`, unconditionally. Both
  copies of `θ/2` are safe.
- `(180−θ)/θ = 180/θ − 1`. By hypothesis `180/θ∉ℤ`; subtracting the integer
  `1` from a non-integer real number yields another non-integer real number
  (if `180/θ−1=m∈ℤ` then `180/θ=m+1∈ℤ`, contradiction). So `(180−θ)/θ∉ℤ`.

Hence none of the three angles of `T_0` is `≡0 (mod θ)`, in particular none
equals `θ` — the game has not already ended, and (as shown below) never will.

**Explicit numeric instances (for the worked examples of §2.4):**
- `θ=50°`: `T_0=(25°,25°,130°)`.
- `θ=180/√2 = 90√2° ≈ 127.279°`: `T_0=(45√2°, 45√2°, (180−90√2)°) ≈ (63.640°,63.640°,52.721°)`.

#### 2.2 The invariant

**Invariant (I):** at every point in the game, no angle of the current
triangle is `≡0 (mod θ)`.

We show `(I)` holds for `T_0` (done above) and is preserved by every possible
move Mulan can make, for as long as `(I)` held before the move. Since `(I)`
in particular excludes any angle equal to `θ` itself (the `k=1` case of
`x≡0`), maintaining `(I)` forever means the game never stops — Mulan never
wins, i.e. Shan-Yu survives indefinitely.

#### 2.3 The congruence lemma (arbitrary attack, arbitrary split)

**Lemma 5.** Suppose the current triangle `(α,β,γ)` satisfies `(I)`
(none of `α,β,γ` is `≡0 (mod θ)`), and Mulan attacks the vertex holding angle `α`
with an **arbitrary** real `t∈(0,α)` (any vertex, any split — not restricted
to the special values `t=θ` or `t=θ−h` used in §1). Then it is **impossible**
for both children `L(t)=(t,β,α+γ−t)` and `R(t)=(α−t,γ,β+t)` to violate
`(I)`. Hence Shan-Yu can always choose a child (discard the other, or discard
either if both are fine) that preserves `(I)`.

*Proof.* Call a triple "bad" if at least one of its three coordinates is
`≡0 (mod θ)`.

`L(t)=(t,β,α+γ−t)` is bad iff `t≡0 (mod θ)`, or `β≡0 (mod θ)`, or
`α+γ−t≡0 (mod θ)` i.e. `t≡α+γ (mod θ)`. Since `(I)` holds for the *current*
triangle, `β≢0 (mod θ)`, so this middle possibility is excluded; hence
```
L(t) bad  ⟺  t≡0 (mod θ)  or  t≡α+γ (mod θ).      (*)
```
`R(t)=(α−t,γ,β+t)` is bad iff `α−t≡0 (mod θ)` (i.e. `t≡α (mod θ)`), or
`γ≡0 (mod θ)` (excluded by `(I)`), or `β+t≡0 (mod θ)` (i.e. `t≡−β (mod θ)`).
Hence
```
R(t) bad  ⟺  t≡α (mod θ)  or  t≡−β (mod θ).       (**)
```

Suppose, for contradiction, both `L(t)` and `R(t)` are bad. By `(*)` and
`(**)`, one of the following four congruences (mod θ) must hold:

1. `t≡0` and `t≡α` ⟹ `α≡0 (mod θ)`. This contradicts `(I)` (α is an angle
   of the current triangle).
2. `t≡0` and `t≡−β` ⟹ `β≡0 (mod θ)`. Contradicts `(I)`.
3. `t≡α+γ` and `t≡α` ⟹ `γ≡0 (mod θ)`. Contradicts `(I)`.
4. `t≡α+γ` and `t≡−β` ⟹ `α+β+γ≡0 (mod θ)` ⟹ `180≡0 (mod θ)` ⟹ `180/θ∈ℤ`.
   This contradicts the hypothesis of this section (`180/θ∉ℤ`).

All four cases lead to a contradiction — three against the invariant `(I)`
on the *current* triangle (the induction hypothesis), one against the
defining hypothesis on `θ`. So the assumption "both bad" is impossible: at
least one of `L(t)`, `R(t)` preserves `(I)`. ∎

**Symmetry Remark (attacking β or γ instead of α).** Lemma 5 was stated for
an attack on the angle named `α`, with `β,γ` the other two. But nothing in
the game distinguishes a canonical order among the three vertices — "attack
the angle called α" is just a name for "attack whichever vertex Mulan
chooses"; if she instead attacks the angle we called `β` (respectively `γ`),
relabel the triple by swapping which entry is called `α` (i.e. apply the
transposition α↔β, or α↔γ, to the ordered
triple before invoking §0's formulas). The hypothesis of Lemma 5 — `(I)` on
the current triangle — is a symmetric condition (it just says "no coordinate
of the unordered triple is `≡0 (mod θ)`"), so it is preserved under this
relabeling, and the *proof* of Lemma 5 goes through verbatim after
relabeling (it never used any asymmetry between `β` and `γ`, or any
property of `α` beyond "the attacked angle," in its four-case
case-analysis). Hence Lemma 5 holds for an attack on **any** of the three
vertices, not just the one named `α`.

#### 2.4 Conclusion of the survival argument, with worked examples

**Theorem (survival).** If `180/θ∉ℤ`, then starting from `T_0=(θ/2,θ/2,180−θ)`,
Shan-Yu can maintain invariant `(I)` forever (regardless of Mulan's choices
of vertex and split ratio at each move), and therefore the game never
terminates in Mulan's favor.

*Proof.* Induction on the number of moves played. Base case: `T_0` satisfies
`(I)`, shown in §2.1. Inductive step: suppose the current triangle satisfies
`(I)`. Mulan attacks some vertex with some real `t` in the appropriate open
interval; by Lemma 5 (using the Symmetry Remark to handle whichever vertex
she picked), at least one of the two children preserves `(I)`; Shan-Yu
discards the other (if both preserve `(I)`, he discards either). The new
current triangle again satisfies `(I)`. Since `(I)` excludes any angle
`=θ` exactly (as `θ=1·θ ≡0 (mod θ)`), the game's win-check ("does `T`
have an angle `=θ`?") never succeeds, so the game runs forever — Mulan never
wins. ∎

**Worked example 1: θ=50° (180/50=3.6∉ℤ).** `T_0=(25°,25°,130°)`, all safe
(`25/50=0.5∉ℤ`, `130/50=2.6∉ℤ`). Suppose Mulan attacks the `130°` vertex
(`α=130,β=25,γ=25`) with an adversarially-chosen `t=5` (chosen to try to hit
a bad residue: note `α+γ=155`, and `155 mod 50 = 5`, so `t=5` satisfies the
"`t≡α+γ (mod 50)`" bad-condition for `L`):
```
L(5) = (5,25,130+25−5) = (5,25,150).   150/50=3∈ℤ → L is bad (contains 3θ).
R(5) = (130−5,25,25+5) = (125,25,30).  125/50=2.5, 25/50=0.5, 30/50=0.6 → all ∉ℤ, R is safe.
```
Exactly as Lemma 5 guarantees, `R` is safe — Shan-Yu keeps `R=(125°,25°,30°)`,
and `(I)` is preserved (no angle is `50°`, `100°`, or any other multiple of
`50°`). One checks directly that no choice of `t` by Mulan can ever make
*both* `L(t)` and `R(t)` bad here: the four "both-bad" congruences from
Lemma 5's proof would require `130≡0`, `25≡0`, `25≡0`, or `180≡0 (mod 50)` —
and indeed `130 mod 50=30≠0`, `25 mod 50=25≠0`, and `180 mod 50=30≠0` — none
holds, consistent with the lemma.

**Worked example 2: θ=180/√2 = 90√2° (irrational, `180/θ=√2∉ℤ`).**
`T_0 = (45√2°, 45√2°, (180−90√2)°)`. Check `(I)`:
`(45√2)/(90√2)=1/2∉ℤ` for both copies of `45√2°`; and
`(180−90√2)/(90√2) = 180/(90√2) − 1 = √2 − 1`, which is irrational (since
`√2` is irrational and subtracting the integer 1 from an irrational number
keeps it irrational), hence certainly not in `ℤ`. So `(I)` holds at `T_0`.
By Lemma 5 (which places no restriction on `θ` being rational — the
four-case argument is pure algebra valid for any real `θ` and any real `t`),
whatever vertex Mulan attacks and whatever real `t` she chooses, at least
one child again satisfies `(I)` (the only possible failure mode, case 4 of
Lemma 5's proof, would require `180/θ=√2∈ℤ`, which is false since `√2` is
irrational). So Shan-Yu survives forever from this start as well, by the
identical induction.

### 3. Verification of the final answer

**Claim:** the set of θ for which Mulan wins is exactly
`{ 180°/n : n an integer, n≥2 }`.

**Exhaustiveness of the two cases.** For any θ in the problem's range
`0°<θ<180°`, we have `180/θ>1` strictly. So "`180/θ` is an integer `≥2`" and
"`180/θ` is an integer" are the *same* condition on this range: if `180/θ`
is an integer and `180/θ>1`, then automatically `180/θ≥2` (the smallest
integer exceeding 1 is 2). Hence: `θ = 180°/n` for some integer `n≥2`
`⟺` `180/θ ∈ ℤ`. So §1 (which handles exactly `180/θ∈ℤ`, i.e. `θ=180/n`,
`n≥2`) and §2 (which handles exactly `180/θ∉ℤ`) are logical complements
covering *every* `θ∈(0°,180°)` with no overlap and no gap.

- **Members verified:** `θ=90°=180/2` and `θ=60°=180/3` were each traced to
  an explicit, complete win in §1.5 (Examples A, B), from concrete adversarial
  starting triangles, using only the general algorithm of §1.4 (no
  special-casing beyond what the algorithm itself prescribes). The
  algorithm of §1.4 is proved, in general, to terminate in Mulan's favor
  within `2n−2` moves from *any* starting triangle, for *every* integer
  `n≥2` — so every member of the claimed set is a genuine win for Mulan, not
  just the two hand-traced instances.
- **Non-members verified:** `θ=50°` (`180/50=3.6∉ℤ`) and `θ=90√2°`
  (`180/θ=√2∉ℤ`) were each traced in §2.4 to an explicit survival
  strategy from an explicit starting triangle `T_0`, using the general
  Theorem of §2.4 (valid for *every* θ with `180/θ∉ℤ`, rational or
  irrational) — so every non-member of the claimed set is a genuine loss for
  Mulan (infinite survival for Shan-Yu), not just these two instances.

Both cases have now been fully settled with matching, mechanism-level proofs
(an explicit forcing algorithm with a proved termination bound on one side;
an explicit invariant-preserving construction on the other), and together
they cover the entire range `(0°,180°)`. This completes the proof of the
characterization.

**Final answer:** Mulan can guarantee victory in finitely many steps if and
only if
```
θ = 180°/n   for some integer n ≥ 2.
```

∎

### Problem mechanics (re-derived carefully, since this is the crux of the whole problem)
Mulan picks any point P on the perimeter (any of the 3 sides, any position,
not a vertex) and cuts to the vertex *not* on that side. This splits the
opposite vertex's angle into two parts; the two new triangles each retain
**exactly one** of the *other two* original angles unchanged, plus their share

ratio `t` (any real in the open interval); Shan-Yu, after seeing `t`, picks
which child survives. This is a real-valued (not just rational/dyadic) pursuit
game with one continuous move by Mulan and one binary adversarial choice by
Shan-Yu per round. Both must terminate the process in finitely many rounds for
Mulan to "win" — an infinite non-terminating game is a loss for Mulan (the
problem asks for guaranteed victory "in finitely many steps").

### Technique
Work in the angle-triple model (α,β,γ), α+β+γ=180, with `L(t)=(t,β,α+γ−t)`,
`R(t)=(α−t,γ,β+t)` (confirmed prior progress). Reduce everything to two
**forced** (non-adversarial) move-types, then settle existence/non-existence
of a finite forcing sequence via congruences mod θ.

- **Move 1 (transfer, needs attacked angle A > θ):** t=θ forces Shan-Yu to
  discard the branch containing θ, so the kept triangle is deterministically
  (A−θ, other-unchanged, other+θ) — proven already (forced-move lemma).
  Iterating Move 1 on the *same* lineage subtracts θ each time; if A = kθ
  exactly (k≥2 integer) this **wins outright** in k−1 more steps — this
  generalizes the bisection lemma from powers of 2 to **all** positive integer
  multiples of θ (cheap upgrade, worth stating explicitly).
- **Move 2 (helper-reset, needs an angle h<θ and another angle A with
  A+h>θ):** set t=θ−h (h the currently-unchanged retained angle in the
  branch Shan-Yu is forced into). Algebra: the forced kept triangle is
  **exactly** `(θ−h, h, 180−θ)` — independent of which third angle was
  discarded, because α+β+γ=180 is a fixed identity. This is the key new
  lemma: Move 2 always manufactures the *universal constant* 180−θ as one
  angle, regardless of the starting triangle.

### Skeleton — winning direction (θ = 180/n, n≥2 integer)
1. From any current triangle, if some angle already equals θ, done.
2. Else if some angle A>θ: repeatedly apply Move 1 to a fixed lineage
   (subtracting θ each time) until either it hits θ exactly (done) or drops
   below θ, producing a helper h<θ — this takes < n steps since A<180=nθ.
3. Apply Move 2 once (attacker = whichever current angle ≥θ, helper = h) to
   reach `(θ−h, h, 180−θ)`. Since θ=180/n, `180−θ=(n−1)θ`, an **exact**
   integer multiple of θ.
4. Apply Move 1 to the `(n−1)θ` coordinate n−2 more times to land exactly on
   θ. Win.
- **Room-condition lemma (needed before step 2/3 fire):** if all three angles
  are <θ, the three pairwise sums total `2×180=360`, so some pairwise sum
  ≥120>θ (since θ≤90 here) — giving a valid (attacker, helper<θ) pair for
  Move 2 directly, without needing step 2 at all. If instead some angle
  already >θ but not all three exceed θ (possible when θ>60, since 3θ>180
  forces not-all-three->θ), the other two include one <θ automatically,
  again giving a Move 2 pair. Only remaining case: θ≤60 and **all three**
  angles >θ (possible, e.g. equilateral start with θ<60) — there step 2's
  Move-1 loop is what produces the helper.

### Skeleton — survival direction (θ with 180/θ ∉ ℤ)
1. Shan-Yu picks any starting triangle with all three angles ≢ 0 (mod θ)

   of θ — has measure zero in the space of valid triangles).
2. **Invariant:** maintain "no current angle ≡ 0 (mod θ)" forever.
3. **Key congruence lemma (the crux, fully derived):** for *any* attacked
   angle α (any vertex Mulan picks) with remaining angles β,γ both ≢0 (mod
   θ), and *any* real split t Mulan chooses, L=(t,β,α+γ−t) and R=(α−t,γ,β+t)
   cannot **both** contain a multiple of θ. Proof sketch: L bad ⟺ t≡0 or
   t≡α+γ (mod θ); R bad ⟺ t≡α or t≡−β (mod θ). Both bad forces one of the
   four coincidences: α≡0, β≡0, γ≡0, or α+β+γ=180≡0 (mod θ). The first three
   are excluded by the invariant; the fourth is excluded exactly because
   **180/θ is not an integer**. So for every Mulan move, at least one child
   preserves the invariant — Shan-Yu picks it and never reaches θ.
4. Since θ never appears (residue 0 excluded forever), the game never
   terminates in Mulan's favor — she cannot force a win.
