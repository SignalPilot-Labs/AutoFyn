### `growing-modulus-descent` (revise) — CHANGES REQUESTED (load-bearing monotonicity is NOT a theorem as stated)
The `aimo-0678` min-of-a-set template is a legitimate framing to port, and the two-phase structure (freeze $\Sigma_n$ then descend on the frontier) is well-mirrored. Phase 1 ($P_1$-support stabilization, $\Sigma_n\to\Sigma_\infty$) is unconditional and free — correct.   - (i) The "covered stays covered" argument shows only that a *small transversal persists* (the covering survives); it does NOT show the *minimal-transversal prime set* is bounded by that covering. Empirical counterexample above ($\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\}\to+\{1,100\}$) shows adding a set with a new large prime makes that prime enter a minimal transversal even when the small covering persists. So $w_n$ CAN increase under set-addition in general. This is exactly the false MT-monotonicity the dispatch warned about, repackaged as "covered stays covered."
  - (ii) The monovariant is VACUOUS in the real sequence — $w_n\equiv+\infty$ (no large prime ever enters MT; checked for $a_1\in\{385,77,715,2085\}$). It only takes a finite value inside a proof-by-contradiction hypothesis (assume some $q>M_1$ enters), where its monotonicity is untestable and the proposed mechanism is broken.
  - (iii) The residual case $w_\infty=q^*<+\infty$ (Step 3e) explicitly "leans on the strip as a secondary mechanism" — i.e. falls back to `transversal-saturation`'s `aimo-0030` strip with the SAME open admissibility-transfer crux.

Net: as revised, `growing-modulus-descent` either chases a monotonicity that is false-in-general and unproven-for-the-greedy-sequence (with a broken mechanism), or collapses to the strip (duplicating `transversal-saturation`).
