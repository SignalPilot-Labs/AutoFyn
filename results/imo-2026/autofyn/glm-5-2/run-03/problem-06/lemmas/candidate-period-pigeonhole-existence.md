# Lemma (positive, elementary): candidate-period pigeonhole-existence

**Statement.** For the greedy increment-word $(d_n)_{n\ge1}$ of imo-2026-06 (where $d_n=a_{n+1}-a_n$), which by the certified `linchpin-and-gap-bound` takes values in the finite alphabet $\Sigma=\{1,\dots,M_1\}$ with $M_1=\operatorname{rad}(a_1)$, the following holds for every window-length $w\ge1$: there exist indices $i<j$ with
$$d_{i+k}=d_{j+k}\quad\text{for }k=0,1,\dots,w-1.$$
The offset $P_0:=j-i>0$ is a **candidate period** of the increment word. This existence is non-circular: it uses only (a) the finiteness of $\Sigma$ (certified by `linchpin-and-gap-bound`) and (b) the infiniteness of the word (the greedy never terminates, $a_n\to\infty$ since $d_n\ge1$); it presupposes no periodicity of $d$, no finiteness of the governing-prime set, no $\mathcal B_\infty$/$\operatorname{MT}$ structure, and it bounds $P_0$ a priori by nothing.

**Proof.** Fix $w\ge1$. Each $d_n\in\Sigma$ where $|\Sigma|=M_1<\infty$. The number of distinct length-$w$ blocks over $\Sigma$ is at most $M_1^w<\infty$. Consider the infinite sequence of length-$w$ windows $B_i:=(d_i,\dots,d_{i+w-1})$ for $i=1,2,3,\dots$. By the infinite pigeonhole principle, two of these windows coincide: $\exists\,i<j$ with $B_i=B_j$, i.e. $d_{i+k}=d_{j+k}$ for $k=0,\dots,w-1$. Set $P_0:=j-i>0$. $\square$

**Important caveat (the candidate is NOT necessarily a period).** The candidate $P_0(w)$ produced by pigeonhole need NOT be a period of $d$ for small $w$. Verified computationally ($a_1=35$, $M_1=35$, true period $T=34$, $L=210$): with $w=2$, the length-$2$ windows at $i=5$ and $j=10$ coincide ($(d_5,d_6)=(10,5)=(d_{10},d_{11})$), giving candidate $P_0=5$, but $d_{8}=4\ne10=d_{13}$ — a real deviation at $n_0=8$. With $w=8$, the smallest-offset coincidence is $i=0,j=34$, $P_0=34=T$, with no deviation. So $P_0(w)$ becomes a period only for $w\ge w_{\min}$ (the minimal window-length recovering the true period), and $w_{\min}$ is UNBOUNDED in $M_1$ (the round-4 `ergodic-window-state / increment-window-automaton` fence). A descent that concludes "$d$ is $P_0$-periodic" from this candidate must therefore either be non-uniform in $w$ (and pick the right $w$) or reduce to bounding $w_{\min}$ — see the negative lemma `deviation-descent-blocked-by-wmin-fence`.

**Status.** Reviewer-certified (round 5). The lemma is elementary (infinite pigeonhole on a finite alphabet) but usefully packages the "candidate period" half of any deviation-descent route; its non-circularity is explicit. Verified computationally on $a_1\in\{15,35,77,91,175\}$.
