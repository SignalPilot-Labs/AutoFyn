# Lemma (negative, structural fence): hole-patching / covering-termination framing of Gap A is obstructed

*Certified: round 7 (reviewer). Source: `approaches/parametric-recruitment-family.md`. Reviewer independently reproduced (corrected naive $O(N^2)$ gcd-greedy + MT-tracking, bit-exact vs `/tmp/round-6/mt_greedy.py`): (a) the MT-prime-set non-monotonicity for $a_1=9375$ ($115$ grow-events / $7$ shrink-events over $4000$ steps, single-step flush of $76$ transient primes at $n=222$, final MT-primes $=\{2,3,5,7,67\}$, $110$ distinct MT-primes ever seen of which $105$ transient); (b) the cofactor-collapse ($96.6\%$ of $a_1=9375$ terms carry a prime outside the governing skeleton $\{2,3,5,7,67\}$, exact $3864/4000$); (c) the $a_1=375$ MT-stabilization bedrock (MT stabilizes to $\{2,3,5,7,19\}$ from step 6, $5$ transversals, stable to $N=1500$).*

## Statement

The "hole-patching / covering-termination" framing of Gap A — in which one defines a skeleton $G_k$ of recruited primes, a skeleton period $L_k=\prod_{p\in G_k}p$, and a "hole-set" of residue classes mod $L_k$ not coverable by $G_k$-smooth admissible window-candidates, and attempts to prove finiteness of $G_\infty$ via "each recruitment strictly shrinks the (finite) hole-set" — is **structurally obstructed in two independent ways**:

(a) **Non-monotonicity of the MT-prime set.** The "skeleton" $G_k=\bigcup\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_k)$ is NOT monotone in $k$: transient primes both enter AND exit the MT-prime set at finite stages. Witness $a_1=9375=3\cdot5^5$ (reviewer-reproduced): over the first $4000$ steps, $115$ grow-events vs $7$ shrink-events; the MT-prime set reaches a peak of transient primes and a single step at $n=222$ flushes $76$ transient primes at once (MT-prime set drops by $76$ in one step); $110$ distinct primes ever appear in $\bigcup\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_n)$, of which $105$ are transient (only $5$ governing: $\{2,3,5,7,67\}$). Consequently the hole-set modulus $L_k$ changes at each event, and there is NO single fixed hole-set being shrunk across recruitments — the object the framing purports to shrink is re-defined at every event. (The clean sub-case $a_1=375=3\cdot5^3$ stabilizes immediately — MT-prime set fixed at $\{2,3,5,7,19\}$ from step 6 onward, $5$ transversals — but even there a non-monotone event occurs at steps 3–6: prime $7$ exits then re-enters. The framing is not even uniform within the $3\cdot5^e$ family: $e=3$ clean, $e=5$ deeply non-monotone.)

(b) **Cofactor-collapse of the termination half.** The "no-new-holes $\Rightarrow$ the greedy picks only $G_k$-smooth candidates thereafter" step requires proving that every admissible integer in the window $[a_n+1,a_n+M_1]$ is $G_k$-smooth — i.e. bounds the prime factorization of every admissible window-candidate. This is the certified-circular cofactor bound (`window-uniqueness-reduces-to-cofactor` clause (iii); `lemma-C-strip-no-go`; `schur-cofactor-premise-fails-in-periodic-regime`). The distinction "the admissibility check uses $m$'s $G$-prime-set, not the cofactor factorization" does NOT save the framing, because the framing ADDITIONALLY needs the chosen $m$ to BE $G_k$-smooth, and establishing THAT requires controlling $m$'s prime factors (the cofactor bound). Empirical witness (reviewer-reproduced): for $a_1=9375$, $96.6\%$ of the first $4000$ terms carry a prime outside the governing skeleton $\{2,3,5,7,67\}$ — the greedy does NOT stay within the governing skeleton; proving it EVENTUALLY does requires proving the term-factor prime set is eventually bounded, which is literally Gap A.

## Witness

(a) The $a_1=9375$ MT-prime-set evolution: $115$ grow / $7$ shrink events, single-step flush of $76$ primes at $n=222$, final MT-primes $=\{2,3,5,7,67\}$, $105$ transient primes ever seen. (b) The term-factor level of the same case: $96.6\%$ of terms carry a prime outside the governing set. The framing is empirically valid only for the clean sub-case $a_1=375$ (MT stabilizes immediately), but the framing is not uniform within the $3\cdot5^e$ family, and the general termination half collapses to the cofactor bound.

## Fence-conclusion

The hole-patching / covering-termination framing of Gap A is fenced by two independent obstructions: (a) non-monotonicity of the MT-prime set (transient primes enter and exit; no fixed hole-set is shrunk), and (b) cofactor-collapse of the termination half (the greedy does not stay within the governing skeleton; proving it does IS Gap A). Future rounds should NOT re-attempt a hole-patching / covering-termination framing of Gap A without first breaking one of these two obstructions.
