## Current best
**Rigorous negative result (this round):** the load-bearing monovariant $w_n$ is provably non-monotone in the real greedy sequence. This kills the `aimo-0678` min-of-a-set framing for this problem.

**The killing counterexample ($a_1=116=2^2\cdot 29$, $M_1=58$).** The greedy sequence is $a_n=116+2(n-1)=2(58+n-1)=2(57+n)$ (it steps by $2$ throughout — verified: $a_1=116,a_2=118=2\cdot59,a_3=120,a_4=122=2\cdot61,\dots,a_7=128=2^7$). The supports form a star $\{2\}\cup\operatorname{PF}(57+n)$ where $\operatorname{PF}$ denotes prime factors. Tracking $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_n)$:
- $n=1$: $\mathcal F_1=\{\{2,29\}\}$, $\operatorname{MT}=\{\{2\},\{29\}\}$, large MT-primes $>58$: none $\Rightarrow w_1=+\infty$.
- $n=2$: $\mathcal F_2=\{\{2,29\},\{2,59\}\}$, $\operatorname{MT}=\{\{2\},\{29,59\}\}$, large MT-prime $59>58$ $\Rightarrow w_2=59$.
- $n=3,\dots,6$: $59$ persists in $\operatorname{MT}$ (via transversals like $\{29,59,3,5\}$) $\Rightarrow w_n=59$.
- $n=7$: $a_7=128=2^7$ has support $\{2\}$ (a singleton). Now $\{2\}$ alone hits every support in the family (every term is a multiple of $2$), so $\operatorname{MT}=\{\{2\}\}$, large MT-primes: none $\Rightarrow w_7=+\infty$.

Thus $w_n=(+\infty,59,59,59,59,59,+\infty,\dots)$ — it INCREASES from $+\infty$ to $59$ at $n=2$ (with the "$+\infty$=best/empty" convention) or DECREASES then INCREASES (with the "$+\infty$=worst" convention). Under EITHER convention $w_n$ is NOT monotone. The small covering $\{2\}$ — the "covered stays covered" anchor — is present at $n=1$ AND persists at $n=2$ AND survives to the lock, yet the large prime $59$ ENTERS $\operatorname{MT}$ at $n=2$ via the new minimal transversal $\{29,59\}$. This is exactly the reviewer's abstract counterexample $\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\}\}\to+\{1,100\}$, realized by the greedy sequence.

**This is not an isolated pathology — it is systematic.** For every $a_1=p^e\cdot q$ with $p$ the smallest prime factor, $e\ge 2$, $q>p$ prime, the greedy steps by $p$ (each $a_{n-1}+p$ is admissible since it is a $p$-multiple and every prior term is a $p$-multiple; and no smaller step clears all admissibility constraints — verified), so $a_n=p\cdot(p^{e-1}q+n-1)$ and the supports form the star $\{p\}\cup\operatorname{PF}(p^{e-1}q+n-1)$. Whenever $p^{e-1}q+n-1$ is a prime $r>M_1=pq$, that prime $r$ enters $\operatorname{MT}$ via the transversal $\{(\text{all distinct "other primes"})\}$. Such primes $r>M_1$ exist (the range $\{p^{e-1}q,\dots,p^k\}$ where $p^k$ is the next power of $p$, contains primes $>M_1$ once $p^k\gg M_1$). Confirmed computationally for $a_1\in\{68,76,92,116,148,164,172,188,212,63,117,\dots\}$ — in every case $w_n$ jumps from $+\infty$ to a finite large prime and (at the lock) returns to $+\infty$. The "least multiplier" $m=a_i/p$ equals the large prime $r$ itself and is strictly increasing across pull-in events ($a_1=68$: $37,41,43,47,53,59,61,67,71,73$; $a_1=116$: $59,61,67,71,73,79,83,89,97$) — it ASCENDS, never descends.
