### The two structural lemmas (proved in full)

#### Lemma 1 (primal–dual equivalence of Gap A)

**Statement.** Let $\mathcal F_\infty=\{S(a_i):i\ge1\}$ (the family of prime-supports of the greedy terms, multiplicity ignored), let $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ denote the family of inclusion-minimal elements of $\mathcal F_\infty$ (the PRIMAL minimal-support antichain), and let $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ denote the family of minimal transversals of $\mathcal F_\infty$ (the DUAL). Consider the two assertions:
- (P) $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is finite and $\bigcup_{S\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty}S\subseteq G$ for some finite prime set $G$.
- (D) $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ is finite and $\bigcup_{T\in\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)}T\subseteq G'$ for some finite prime set $G'$.

Then (P) $\iff$ (D). Moreover, under either hypothesis, $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)=\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)$ and $\operatorname{MS}_\infty=\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty))$ (the blocker involution).

**Proof.** We use the classical *blocker involution* on clutters (Sperner families / antichains) over a finite ground set (Schrijver, *Theory of Linear and Integer Programming*; or Edmonds–Fulkerson 1970, "blockers" of clutters): **if $\mathcal C$ is a clutter (an antichain) over a finite ground set $X$, and $b(\mathcal C):=\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal C)$ is its blocker (family of minimal hitting sets), then $b(b(\mathcal C))=\mathcal C$.** We import this as a named theorem (it is the hypergraph-theoretic analogue of the order-theoretic fact that the Galois-closed dual-of-dual returns the original antichain); it is a finite combinatorial identity and does not presuppose Gap A.

Two preliminary observations (both reviewer-certified in `mt-depends-on-set-system`):

(A) *$\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)=\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)$.* A set $T$ is a transversal of $\mathcal F_\infty$ iff $T\cap S\ne\varnothing$ for every $S\in\mathcal F_\infty$. Every $S\in\mathcal F_\infty$ contains some minimal element $S_0\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ (start from $S$, delete primes whose removal leaves a member's-superset relation intact; termination by finiteness of $S$). Then $T\cap S_0\ne\varnothing\Rightarrow T\cap S\ne\varnothing$. Conversely $S_0\subseteq S$ gives $T\cap S_0\ne\varnothing\Rightarrow T\cap S\ne\varnothing$ trivially, but if $T$ is a transversal of $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ then $T$ hits every $S_0\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty$, and since every $S\in\mathcal F_\infty$ contains some $S_0$, $T$ hits every $S$. So $T$ is a transversal of $\mathcal F_\infty$ iff $T$ is a transversal of $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$. Minimality is a property of the transversal family, so $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)=\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)$. (Symmetric to `mt-depends-on-set-system`.)

(B) *$\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is a clutter (antichain) over its ground $\bigcup\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ by definition* (inclusion-minimal = no member contains another).

**($\Rightarrow$) Assume (P).** Then $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is a clutter over the finite ground $X:=\bigcup\operatorname{MS}_\infty\subseteq G$. By the blocker involution, $b(b(\operatorname{MS}_\infty))=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$, i.e. $\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MS}_\infty))=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$. By (A), $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)=\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)$, so $\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty))=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$. In particular $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)=\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)$ is the blocker of a finite clutter over the finite ground $X\subseteq G$; hence $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ is itself a finite clutter over $X$ (a finite ground admits only finitely many subsets, hence finitely many minimal hitting sets). So (D) holds with $G'=X\subseteq G$. ✓

**($\Leftarrow$) Assume (D).** Then $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ is a finite clutter over the finite ground $Y:=\bigcup\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)\subseteq G'$. By the blocker involution applied to $\mathcal C:=\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$, $b(b(\mathcal C))=\mathcal C$, i.e. $\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)))=\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$. Applying $\operatorname{MT}$ to both sides of $\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty))=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ (which we now derive): we have $\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)=\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ by (A); applying $\operatorname{MT}$ and using the involution on the clutter $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ over $Y$, $\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty))=b(\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty))=b(b(\operatorname{MS}_\infty))=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ (the last equality by the involution on $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ once we know $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is a clutter over the finite ground $Y$ — which we must verify). Concretely: every $S\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is a minimal transversal of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ (it is a transversal: $S$ hits every $T\in\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$, since each $T$ is a subset of some $S(a_i)$, and $S\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is itself some $S(a_j)$, so $S\cap T\supseteq S\cap S(a_i)\ni$ (any prime of $a_i$ in $T$) — wait, more cleanly: $S\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ means $S=S(a_j)$ for some $j$, and every $T\in\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ has $T\cap S(a_j)\ne\varnothing$ by definition of transversal of $\mathcal F_\infty$; so $S$ hits every $T$, i.e. $S$ is a transversal of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$. Minimality of $S$ as a transversal of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$: if $S'\subsetneq S$ were a smaller transversal of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$, then $S'$ hits every $T\in\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$, i.e. $S'$ is a transversal of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)=\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)$; by the involution on $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ (once established), $S'\supseteq$ some element of $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$, contradicting $S'\subsetneq S\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ and the antichain property. To avoid circularity, we instead verify directly: $S\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty\Rightarrow S\in\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty))$ by the classical *blocker-dual characterization* (a set is a minimal blocker of the blocker iff it is a member of the original clutter — this is the content of $b(b(\mathcal C))=\mathcal C$, applied to $\mathcal C=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ once (A) identifies $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)=b(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)$).)

   To run the involution we need $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ to be a clutter over a finite ground. Under (D), every $S\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is a minimal transversal of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$; as a transversal it is a subset of the ground $Y=\bigcup\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)\subseteq G'$ (a transversal of a family over $Y$ is a subset of $Y$). So $\bigcup\operatorname{MS}_\infty\subseteq Y\subseteq G'$ is finite. Hence $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is a clutter over the finite ground $Y\subseteq G'$, the blocker involution $b(b(\operatorname{MS}_\infty))=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ applies, and (P) holds with $G=Y\subseteq G'$. ✓

   *(A cleaner, circularity-free version of ($\Leftarrow$):* under (D), $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ is a finite clutter over $Y\subseteq G'$. Every $S\in\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is a transversal of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ (each $T\in\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ has $T\cap S\ne\varnothing$ since $S=S(a_j)$ for some $j$ and $T$ is a transversal of $\mathcal F_\infty$). As a transversal, $S\subseteq Y$. So $\bigcup\operatorname{MS}_\infty\subseteq Y$ is finite. Now $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is a clutter over the finite ground $Y$, and the blocker involution gives $b(b(\operatorname{MS}_\infty))=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$. Combined with (A) ($b(\operatorname{MS}_\infty)=\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$), we get $\operatorname{MT}(\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty))=b(b(\operatorname{MS}_\infty))=\operatorname{MS}_\infty$; in particular $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ is the blocker of the finite clutter $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ over $Y$, hence finite.) ∎

**Consequence for the framing.** The primal-minimal-support stabilization target (P) is *literally equivalent* to the dual Gap A target (D), via the classical blocker involution — NOT a bypass. Any proof of (P) is a proof of (D) and vice versa. The hope that the primal admits a direct greedy-minimality argument the dual lacks must therefore be borne by a *mechanism*, not by the framing — and Lemma 2 shows the natural primal mechanism reduces to the same cofactor wall.

#### Lemma 2 (window-uniqueness is a strict refinement but reduces to cofactor-bound)

**Statement.** Suppose at step $n+1$ the greedy picks $a_{n+1}=m$ with a prime $q>M_1$ dividing $m$. Then:
- (i) *(Window-uniqueness)* $m$ is the unique multiple of $q$ in the window $[a_n+1,a_n+M_1]$.
- (ii) *(Admissibility = cofactor-transversal)* Writing $m=kq$ ($k=m/q\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$), $m$ is admissible (i.e. $S(m)$ hits every $S\in\operatorname{MS}_n$) iff primefactors$(k)$ hits every $S\in\operatorname{MS}_n$ with $q\notin S$.
- (iii) *(Reduction)* Consequently, to prove "no prime $q>M_1$ ever enters a new minimal support" using only the window-uniqueness ingredient, one must — for every $q>M_1$ and every step at which the unique $q$-multiple $m=kq$ lies in the window — establish one of:
   (a) primefactors$(k)$ fails to hit every $q$-free minimal in $\operatorname{MS}_n$ (so $m$ is inadmissible), or
   (b) some smaller admissible $m'\in(a_n,kq)$ exists in the window (so $m$ is not the smallest admissible).
   Both (a) and (b) require bounding the prime factorization of the cofactor $k=m/q$: (a) directly (one must show a specific prime does NOT divide $k$ to cause a transversal failure, or equivalently control which primes divide $k$), and (b) because the candidate $m'$ must satisfy $S(m')\supseteq$ some transversal of $\operatorname{MS}_n$, i.e. one must exhibit a specific prime-factorization pattern for $m'$ in the interval $(a_n,kq)$. This is the cofactor-bound step certified dead (`witness-density-recurrence` round 2; `crt-period-lifting` round 3).

**Proof.**
(i) The window $W=[a_n+1,a_n+M_1]$ has $|W|=M_1$ (an integer interval of length $M_1$). The $q$-multiples in $W$ are $\{q\lceil(a_n+1)/q\rceil,q(\lceil(a_n+1)/q\rceil+1),\dots\}\cap W$. Consecutive $q$-multiples differ by $q>M_1=|W|$, so at most one lies in $W$. If $q\mid m=a_{n+1}\in W$, then $m$ is that one (unique) $q$-multiple. ✓

(ii) $m$ admissible $\iff S(m)=\{q\}\cup\text{primefactors}(k)$ hits every $S\in\operatorname{MS}_n$ $\iff$ for each $S\in\operatorname{MS}_n$, $(\{q\}\cup\text{primefactors}(k))\cap S\ne\varnothing$. For $S$ with $q\in S$, this is automatic ($q\in S\cap S(m)$). For $S$ with $q\notin S$, this reduces to primefactors$(k)\cap S\ne\varnothing$. Hence the equivalence: $m$ admissible $\iff$ primefactors$(k)$ hits every $q$-free minimal in $\operatorname{MS}_n$. ✓

(iii) Per (i)+(ii), when $q>M_1$ and $q\mid m$ for the candidate $m=kq$ in the window, $m$ is admissible iff primefactors$(k)$ transverses the $q$-free minimals, and $m$ is *picked* iff additionally $m$ is the *smallest* admissible in $W$ (equivalently, no $m'\in(a_n,m)\cap W$ is admissible). So to prove $q$ never enters a new minimal support (i.e. such an $m$ is never picked), one must show that for every such $q$-multiple $m=kq$ appearing in $W$, EITHER $m$ is inadmissible (clause (a): primefactors$(k)$ fails the transversal) OR $m$ is not smallest (clause (b): a smaller admissible $m'$ exists). Clause (a) requires ruling out specific cofactor factorizations of $k$ — i.e. bounding primefactors$(k)$ to show it omits a prime needed to hit some $q$-free minimal. Clause (b) requires exhibiting an admissible $m'<m$ in $W$, i.e. producing an $m'$ whose support hits every minimal — which again requires controlling the prime factorization of candidates in the interval. Both clauses are versions of the cofactor-bound step (bound/control primefactors$(k)$ for the cofactor $k=m/q$). The cofactor-bound step is the wall certified dead for `witness-density-recurrence` (round 2: "bounding the primes in intermediate supports IS Gap A; transient primes give unbounded covering capacity compatibly with Gap A") and for `crt-period-lifting` (round 3: "the actual greedy uses full admissibility incl. transient primes; bounding which transient primes appear IS Gap A — same circularity"). ∎

**Computational verification of (i).** On $a_1=116=2^2\cdot29$ (LOCK-at-2 case where big transient primes DO enter), tracked 154 big-prime-entry events (steps where a prime $q>M_1=58$ divides the chosen $m$); in ALL 154 cases, exactly one $q$-multiple lies in the window — confirming (i). The carrying term is $m=a_n+2$ throughout (since $2\mid a_n$ in LOCK-at-2), so $k=m/q=(a_n+2)/q$ has $2\mid k$ (as $q$ is odd), making primefactors$(k)\supseteq\{2\}$ — which trivially hits every $q$-free minimal (all contain 2 in LOCK-at-2). This is the mechanism by which LOCK cases admit big transient primes: the lock prime trivializes the cofactor-transversal. In NON-LOCK cases no single prime trivializes the transversal, and empirically (19 cases, 80k+ steps) no big prime ever enters — but PROVING this is exactly Gap A. ∎

Each was tested as a potential "non-cofactor" greedy-dynamic ingredient; each fails to bound new primes without invoking the cofactor bound.

- **(α) Window-uniqueness (at most one $q$-multiple in window of size $M_1<q$).** Genuine structural refinement of the dual MT bound (Lemma 2(i)), BUT unpacks to cofactor-transversal (Lemma 2(ii,iii)). Dead as a bypass.
- **(β) Multi-big-prime coincidence.** If $m$'s support has two big primes $q,r>M_1$, then $m$ is the unique $qr$-multiple in the window (since $qr>M_1$). But this is consistent (one $qr$-multiple in the window is allowed); no contradiction. The cofactor $k=m/(qr\cdots)$ still requires transversal-bounding.
- **(γ) Blocker-incomparability.** "$ms$ new minimal $\Rightarrow$ no existing minimal $S'\subseteq ms$" gives an incomparability structure, but does NOT bound $ms$'s primes — it only constrains $ms$'s *subset relations* with existing minimals, not the magnitudes of its primes.
- **(δ) Lock-prime triviality.** In LOCK cases, the lock prime $p$ divides every term, so $p\mid k$ for the cofactor of any $q$-multiple, trivializing the transversal. This explains why LOCK cases admit (dying) big transient primes; but it is LOCK-specific (certified `lock-lemma`), unavailable in NON-LOCK, and gives no NON-LOCK bound.
- **(ε) Spacing/density.** Consecutive $q$-multiples in the greedy have index spacing $\ge\lceil q/M_1\rceil$ (forced by $d_n\le M_1<q$); density $\le M_1/q$ (`witness-density-recurrence` W1, sound but conditional). Does not bound $k$'s primes.

### Cases covered
- **LOCK sub-case:** certified by `lock-lemma` (prime-power term $\Rightarrow T=1,L=p$). The primal framing's LOCK behavior is fully consistent: $\operatorname{MS}_\infty=\{\{p\}\}$ for the lock prime $p$; transient primes (which can be $>M_1$, e.g. $a_1=116$: $59,61,\dots$) die. Computationally verified.
- **$|P_1|=2$ NON-LOCK:** smallest base case; primal $\operatorname{MS}_\infty$ has 2–3 supports, all primes $\le M_1$ (verified $a_1\in\{15,35,77,91,143,175,\dots\}$). Lemma 1 + Lemma 2 apply; the cofactor wall remains.
- **$|P_1|\ge3$ NON-LOCK:** general; verified $a_1\in\{385,847,1309,2085,39270,510510,\dots\}$. Lemma 1 + Lemma 2 apply; the cofactor wall remains.

### Watch out for
- **Do NOT present this framing as a BYPASS of Gap A.** Lemma 1 proves it is equivalent (primal = dual via blocker involution).
- **Do NOT re-derive the cofactor bound** (circular, certified dead). Lemma 2(iii) shows the window-uniqueness continuation reduces to it.
- **`syndetic-divisible-closed-not-periodic` guardrail respected:** the (conditional) Step 5 uses the explicit antichain-in-finite-Boolean-lattice + Step 4's greedy-minimality, not pure statics.
- **Honest verdict:** the framing is fenced off. The empirical silver lining (NO prime $>M_1$ enters any minimal support, at any finite $n$, in 19 NON-LOCK cases) is a STRENGTHENED form of the live conjecture $q\le M_1$; it remains the most-confirmed empirical target on the problem but is provably as hard as Gap A.
