### The minimal-criminal reduction (Steps 1–6 — SOUND, fully proved)

**Step 1 (Assume Gap A is false; pick the minimal criminal).** Suppose Gap A fails. Then the set of governing primes is unbounded (a governing prime is in $\operatorname{rad}(T)$ for some $T\in\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$; if all governing primes were $\le M_1$, Gap A would hold). In particular there exists a governing prime $q > M_1$. By the well-ordering of $\mathbb N$, let $q$ be the **smallest** governing prime strictly above $M_1$. ∎_(by well-ordering + the definition of "governing.")

This is the standard minimal-criminal on the smallest element of a non-empty subset of $\mathbb N$. It is NOT the `witness-density-recurrence` rescue (round 2), which failed because its "induction on the order of governing primes $>M_1$" pushed UP and was not well-founded; here there is no upward induction — just a single contradiction to be derived on the smallest $q$.

**Step 2 (Primes in $(M_1,q)$ are MT-transient).** By minimality of $q$, no prime $p$ with $M_1 < p < q$ is governing. By the governing/transient dichotomy, every such $p$ is transient: $p$ may appear in $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_n)$ for finite $n$ but drops out of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$ eventually. ∎_(by definition of "smallest governing $>M_1$" + the dichotomy.)

**Step 3 ($q$ divides infinitely many $a_n$).** Since $q$ is governing, $q\in\operatorname{rad}(T)$ for some $T\in\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_\infty)$. By `binfinity-divisibility-progression-structure`, $\operatorname{rad}(T)\mathbb Z \subseteq \mathcal B_\infty$. The greedy sequence $(a_n)$ is the enumeration of $\mathcal B_\infty$ by the cyclic-successor map (conditional on Gap A — but the *infinitely many $a_n$ are $q$-multiples* fact is weaker and unconditional: if only finitely many $a_n$ were $q$-multiples, then beyond some index no $a_n$ is divisible by $q$; but $q\in\operatorname{rad}(T)$ means $q\mid\operatorname{rad}(T)$, and $\operatorname{rad}(T)$-multiples lie in $\mathcal B_\infty$, and the greedy realizes infinitely many of them since $a_n\to\infty$ and every sufficiently large $\operatorname{rad}(T)$-multiple exceeds $a_n$ and is admissible — contradiction). Hence $q\mid a_n$ for infinitely many $n$. ∎

**Step 4 (Local walk structure mod $q$).** Write $N_q := \{n\ge1 : q\mid a_n\}$ (infinite by Step 3). For each $n\in N_q$ with $n\ge2$ (so $a_n = a_{n-1}+d_{n-1}$), the gap bound (`linchpin-and-gap-bound`) gives $d_{n-1}\le M_1 < q$, and $q\mid a_n = a_{n-1}+d_{n-1}$ forces
$$a_{n-1} \equiv -d_{n-1} \pmod q, \qquad d_{n-1} = q - (a_{n-1}\bmod q).$$
Since $d_{n-1}\in[1,M_1]$, we have $a_{n-1}\bmod q \in \{q-M_1, q-M_1+1, \ldots, q-1\}$ (a "top window" of size $M_1$), and $d_{n-1}$ is **forced** by the residue. Between two consecutive $q$-multiples $a_n, a_m$ ($n < m$, both in $N_q$), every partial sum $\sum_{i=n}^{n+j-1} d_i$ for $1\le j \le m-n-1$ is nonzero mod $q$ (else it would give a $q$-multiple strictly between, contradicting consecutiveness in $N_q$), and the total $\sum_{i=n}^{m-1}d_i \equiv 0 \pmod q$. ∎_(by `linchpin-and-gap-bound` + modular arithmetic.)

**Step 5 (Cofactor-transversal structure).** Let $b_1 < b_2 < \cdots$ enumerate $\{a_n : n\in N_q\}$ (the $q$-multiple terms), and write $b_i = k_i q$ with $k_i\in\mathbb Z_{>0}$ (the **cofactor**). When $b_i = a_{n_i}$ is selected by the greedy rule, every prior term $a_j$ ($j < n_i$) must share a prime factor with $b_i = k_i q$. Split the prior terms into $q$-multiples (trivially sharing $q$) and non-$q$-multiples. For each prior **non-$q$-multiple** $a_j$ ($q\nmid a_j$), the shared prime cannot be $q$, so it lies in $\operatorname{primefactors}(k_i)$. Hence
$$\operatorname{primefactors}(k_i) \text{ is a transversal of } \{S(a_j)\setminus\{q\} : j < n_i,\ q\nmid a_j\}.$$
∎_(by the greedy admissibility condition $\gcd(b_i,a_j)>1$ and the split into $q$-/$q$-free factors.)

**Step 6 ($P_1$ always provides a small-prime transversal).** By `linchpin-and-gap-bound`, every $a_j$ is divisible by some $p\in P_1$. Since $q > M_1 \ge \max P_1$, we have $q\notin P_1$, so $P_1\cap\{q\}=\varnothing$ and $P_1 \subseteq \operatorname{primefactors}(a_j)\setminus\{q\}$-covering. Thus $P_1$ alone transverses $\{S(a_j)\setminus\{q\}\}$ for any collection of prior $a_j$; in particular, a $P_1$-smooth cofactor (a multiple of $M_1 = \prod P_1$) is **always admissible**. ∎_(by `linchpin-and-gap-bound` + Step 5.)

### Step 7 — the contradiction (NEGATIVE RESOLUTION)

The goal: derive a contradiction from (i) $q > M_1$ is the smallest governing prime, (ii) the cofactor sequence $(k_i)$ is strictly increasing and unbounded (since $b_i = a_{n_i}$ and $a_n\to\infty$, $k_i = b_i/q \to\infty$), (iii) $\operatorname{primefactors}(k_i)$ transverses the growing prior-non-$q$-multiple family (Step 5), (iv) the local walk mod $q$ with steps $\le M_1 < q$ (Step 4) — **without** the cofactor bound.

#### Candidate A (Schur / `aimo-0727`) — CERTIFIED DEAD

The outliner proposed: IF all $k_i$'s prime factors came from a fixed finite set, then by Schur/`aimo-0727`'s contrapositive $(k_i)$ would be "trivial" (bounded/eventually constant), contradicting unboundedness. This sub-route is **provably unwinnable** for three independent reasons.

**(A1) The `aimo-0727` engine is a multiplicative recurrence that is ABSENT here.** Read off the actual crux (`past_crux_moves_database.json`, `problem_id=aimo-0727`, `how_used`): the load-bearing mechanism is the recurrence
$$b_{k+1}a_{k+1} = (b_k+2)a_k, \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad a_{k+1} = a_k\cdot\frac{b_k+2}{b_{k+1}},$$
which gives $a_{k+1}\mid a_k(b_k+2)$. IF $(b_k)$ were bounded by $B$, then every prime factor of any $a_k$ would be $\le B+2$ or divide $a_1,a_2$ — a finite set — contradicting the hypothesis that infinitely many primes divide some $a_k$. The mechanism that **confines** the prime factors is the multiplicative divisibility $a_{k+1}\mid a_k\cdot f(b_k)$: a new prime entering $a_{k+1}$ must either already divide $a_k$ or divide $f(b_k)$, and $f(b_k) = b_k+2 \le B+2$ is bounded.

In our setting, consecutive cofactors $k_i, k_{i+1}$ are related **additively**, not multiplicatively:
$$k_{i+1} - k_i \;=\; \frac{b_{i+1}-b_i}{q} \;=\; \frac{1}{q}\sum_{n=n_i}^{n_{i+1}-1} d_n,$$
a sum of increments $d_n\le M_1$ (a bounded but *not recurrence-driven* sequence). There is **no** relation $k_{i+1}\mid k_i\cdot f(\text{bounded})$; the new prime factors of $k_{i+1}$ are unconstrained by $k_i$. Verified empirically ($a_1=15, q=3$): the ratios $k_{i+1}/k_i$ range over $[1.04, 1.33]$ with no confining pattern. **The `aimo-0727` confining mechanism does not port.** (Cf. the round-3 lesson: `aimo-0231`'s lift was a trivial identity; `aimo-0134`'s shrinking range was absent. Per CLAUDE.md, every borrowed crux step must be re-proven from scratch; here the engine itself is absent.)

**(A2) The contrapositive "fixed finite prime set $\Rightarrow$ $k_i$ bounded" is FALSE in general.** Schur's theorem (in the standard forward direction) states: a non-constant integer polynomial takes values with infinitely many prime divisors. Its contrapositive is: IF a sequence has only finitely many prime divisors, THEN the sequence is bounded — **but only for polynomial-driven sequences** (where the polynomial identity forces $a_{k+1}=P(a_k)$-style growth confined by the finite prime set). For an arbitrary unbounded integer sequence, "fixed finite prime set" does NOT imply "bounded." Counterexample: $k_i = 2^i$ has prime set $\{2\}$ (fixed finite) yet is unbounded. The greedy cofactor sequence $(k_i)$ is not polynomial-driven (no recurrence, per (A1)), so the contrapositive does not apply. **Even granting the Schur premise (which (A3) refutes), it would not yield the contradiction.**

**(A3) The Schur premise itself ("cofactors have eventually fixed-finite prime set") is PROVABLY FALSE in the periodic regime — the very regime the proof aims to establish.** This is the sharpest obstruction.

**Lemma (cofactor-prime-accumulation in the periodic regime).** Suppose Gap A holds, so the greedy is eventually periodic: $a_{n+T}=a_n+L$ for all $n\ge n_0$ (in fact from $n=1$ by `cyclic-successor-bijection`). Let $q$ be any governing prime (so $q\mid L$). Then for each residue class $r\pmod T$ (with $r\ge n_0$) such that $q\mid a_r$, the $q$-multiple terms at that residue are $a_{r + jT} = a_r + jL$ ($j=0,1,2,\ldots$), and their cofactors
$$k_j \;=\; \frac{a_{r+jT}}{q} \;=\; \frac{a_r}{q} + j\cdot\frac{L}{q}$$
form an arithmetic progression with common difference $L/q \ge 1$ (positive since $q\mid L$). An arithmetic progression $A + D j$ with $D\ge1$ has **infinitely many prime divisors**.

*Proof of the prime-divisor claim.* Suppose, for contradiction, that only finitely many primes $p_1,\ldots,p_s$ divide values of $A+Dj$ ($j\ge0$). Then every value $A+Dj$ is $P$-smooth for $P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_s\}$, i.e. of the form $p_1^{e_1}\cdots p_s^{e_s}$ with $e_i\ge0$. The number of $P$-smooth positive integers $\le X$ is $O((\log X)^s)$ (each exponent $e_i\le \log X/\log p_i$). But the AP $\{A+Dj : j\ge0\}$ contributes $\lfloor (X-A)/D\rfloor+1 = \Omega(X)$ values $\le X$ as $X\to\infty$. For $X$ sufficiently large, $\Omega(X) > C(\log X)^s$ for any constant $C$ — contradiction. Hence infinitely many primes divide values of the AP. ∎

So in the periodic regime, the cofactor sequence $(k_i)$ (a finite union of APs, one per residue class with $q\mid a_r$) has **infinitely many prime divisors** — the *exact opposite* of the Schur premise "fixed finite prime set." The premise is not merely unproved; it is **provably false** in the very regime the theorem establishes.

*Empirical confirmation ($a_1=15$, $T=8$, $L=30=2\cdot3\cdot5$, $M_1=15$, $q=3$ governing).*$\ $The $q=3$-multiples at residues $r\in\{0,1,3,4,5,7\}\pmod8$ have $a_r \in\{15,18,24,30,36,42\}$, cofactors $k = (5,6,8,10,12,14) + 10j$ (six APs with common difference $10 = L/q$). Over 6000 terms, the distinct primes $>M_1=15$ appearing in cofactors grow without bound: 59 (at 500 terms) $\to$ 108 (1000) $\to$ 198 (2000) $\to$ 361 (4000) $\to$ 516 (6000); the largest reaches 2477. This is the smooth-number density argument made concrete.

**The rescue fails too (MT-transient $\not\Rightarrow$ cofactor-transient).** The outliner's hoped rescue: separate TRANSIENT primes (each appears in finitely many $a_n$, hence finitely many $k_i$) from GOVERNING primes ($\le M_1$ by minimality of $q$); then for large $i$, $k_i$'s prime factors are governing ($\subseteq G\le M_1$) union finitely many still-active transients; if the active-transient set eventually empties, $k_i$'s prime set is eventually $\subseteq G$ (fixed-finite) and Schur applies. The outline-reviewer (round 4) sharpened the obstruction: there are TWO different notions of "transient" in play, and minimality of $q$ gives only one.

- **MT-transient** (what minimality of $q$ gives for primes $p\in(M_1,q)$): $p$ drops out of $\operatorname{MT}(\mathcal F_n)$ eventually — i.e. $p$ is redundant in the eventual hitting set.

In the $a_1=15$ periodic case, the MT has stabilized from $n=0$ (governing set $G=\{2,3,5\}\subseteq[2,M_1]$, every prime $>15$ is MT-transient from the start), yet **361 distinct primes $>15$ appear in cofactors AFTER stabilization** (i.e. after the MT has already reached its final form). Being redundant in the minimal transversal does NOT stop a prime from dividing terms of the (linearly growing, periodic) sequence — the terms $a_n$ range over infinitely many values and pick up unboundedly many prime factors regardless of MT-status. The rescue's "active-transient set eventually empties" is the negation of what the periodic-regime lemma proves.

**Conclusion of Step 7 (Candidate A).** The Schur/`aimo-0727` contradiction is **provably unwinnable** in this framing: (A1) the confining engine is absent, (A2) the contrapositive is false in general, (A3) the premise is provably false in the target regime, and the MT-transient $\Rightarrow$ cofactor-transient bridge is empirically false.
