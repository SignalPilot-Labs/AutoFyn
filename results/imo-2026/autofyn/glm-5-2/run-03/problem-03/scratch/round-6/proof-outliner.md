Skeleton:
  1. **Setup** (certified): the parity-XOR toggle lemma (`lemmas/parity-integral.md`) gives `j_final ≡ j_Liu ⊕ h (mod 2)`, `D_final = ∫[f ⊕ h] dt` where `f = [j_Liu odd]` and `h = h_{p_1} ⊕ … ⊕ h_{p_k}` (k ≤ n splits), each `h_{p_k} = 1_{[0,v_k)} + 1_{[u_k,p_k)}` (indicator of two intervals of equal length `v_k`, with `v_k ≤ p_k/2`, `u_k = p_k − v_k`).

So any representation of `f` MUST include a toggle covering `L_m` (the largest band can't be covered by combinations of smaller-interval toggles). That toggle's other interval (length `L_m`) creates a residual of at least `L_m − (sum of smaller bands)` > 0 — but this residual must itself be covered by further toggles, whose other intervals create further residuals. The recursion terminates with an uncovered residual ≥ the smallest band length `L_1 = 1` (in units of `1/D_n`), i.e. `D ≥ 1/D_n`.

The builder must check whether this constraint STRENGTHENS the obstruction (fewer reachable `f`-representations) or WEAKENS it (some dyadic-aligned toggles can cover bands more efficiently). The dyadic-config tightness (D = 1/D_n exactly at equal-halving) is the boundary: equal-halving each `2^k` creates toggles `h = 1_{[0, 2^{k−1})} + 1_{[2^{k−1}, 2^k)}` that cover the full piece `[0, 2^k)` — these are the "best-aligned" toggles, and they leave exactly the smallest band uncovered (the lone `1/D_n` piece). This is the tight case the obstruction must reproduce.
  7. **Generalization to rest-split / iii-a / iii-b**: the framing operates on `f = [j_Liu odd]` (fixed by the dyadic config) and `h = XOR of toggles` (Xiang's splits, any structure). It does NOT re-derive the rest-tiling or the discrepancy function `G`. So it applies uniformly to G1-i (rest unsplit), G1-ii (rest split), G1-iii-a (dominant from rest), G1-iii-b (flat) — the `f` and the toggle budget are the same; only the toggle ALLOCATION differs.
