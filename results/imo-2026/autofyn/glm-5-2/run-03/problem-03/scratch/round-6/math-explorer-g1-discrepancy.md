# imo-2026-03 — G1 discrepancy-bound scouting (lens: G1-i-HC `Alt_s` general bound + G1-iii-a third mechanism)

Scope: the general G1-i-HC discrepancy bound `Alt_s ≥ (D_{R_0}+1−M)/2` for n≥4, s≥3 (the G1-i-HC wall, Lemma 11 identity in `results/imo-2026-03/lemmas/splits-inequality.md`), AND G1-iii-a's third mechanism (peeling-pair + continuity both failed). Scout only — no proof attempted.

## (a) Closest superincreasing / Zeckendorf / alternating-sum analogue

The KB (`knowledge_base.md`) has NO entry for superincreasing-sequence, Zeckendorf-representation, or discrepancy inequalities. The closest KB entries are *Piecewise-concavity smoothing* (already the upper-bound engine, not a lower-bound tool), the *extremal-principle / pigeonhole* meta, and the dyadic-extremal "single largest strictly exceeds sum of all others" (the lower-bound construction itself). The *Three-gap / Steinhaus* and *Kronecker/Weyl* entries are about Kronecker-sequence gaps, not alternating-discrepancy bounds — not applicable.

Crux corpus (`past_crux_moves_database.json`) — three genuine analogues, none an exact match:

1. **`aimo-0530`** (Zeckendorf greedy positional representation, combinatorics `bijections-and-encoding` + `induction-and-construction`). Crux: *"Prove uniqueness/existence of a greedy positional representation by showing the largest allowed block must appear (bound the tail sum below the next block)."* The load-bearing inequality: `n ≥ F_k > F_{k−1}+F_{k−3}+F_{k−5}+…`, which **forces `F_k` into any representation**. This is the **exact superincreasing-prefix obstruction** dyadic-induction Lemma 10 establishes for `E_{R_0}`'s bands (`2^k > 2^k−1 = Σ_{j<k} 2^j`). It is the closest structural template: the largest band exceeds the sum of all smaller, so any representation must use it. BUT aimo-0530 is about representing an integer as a SUM of nonconsecutive blocks; our crux is an ALTERNATING SUM `Σ(−1)^{i+1}G(f_i)` at the breakpoints of a partition of a fixed total `W`. Different shape (sum-representation vs alternating-discrepancy), same superincreasing-prefix engine.

2. **`aimo-0493`** (dyadic-scale witness + super-geometric gap doubling, combinatorics `extremal-principle`). Crux: *"To cap the elements that escape a witnessing structure, show consecutive gap-sums from a fixed base grow super-geometrically: if a doubling inequality failed, the right dyadic scale would exhibit the very witnessing configuration that escape excludes."* Mechanism: for non-witness elements `u_1<…<u_p`, failure of `u_m−u_1 > 2(u_{m−1}−u_1)` lets one pick `k` with `2^k ≤ u_m−u_1 < 2^{k+1}` and exhibit a `k`-witness — contradiction. This is the **closest methodological analogue** to the superincreasing-overflow crux: it is a contradiction-via-dyadic-scale-bracketing argument where the dyadic tower's super-geometric growth forces misalignment. Adaptable framing: "if `Alt_s < target`, then F's breakpoints would have to align with G's extrema, but the superincreasing swing amplitudes force a bracketing dyadic scale where the alignment fails."

3. **`aimo-0558`** (bounded-gap max-excess selection, combinatorics `extremal-principle`). Crux: *"build the worst-case sequence as alternating same-sign blocks and bound any admissible selection by counting per block: the gap constraint forces at least one entry from each opposite-sign block."* Structurally the closest to our `G` (alternating-sign O/E blocks) and `Alt_s` (a "selection" of F's breakpoints). The block-counting bound is the analogue of the per-band misalignment bookkeeping.

**No known inequality of the exact form** "alternating sum `Σ(−1)^{i+1}G(f_i)` of a function `G` with superincreasing oscillation, evaluated at a partition `f_1≥…≥f_s` of a fixed total `W`, is bounded below by a function of `W` and the tower" exists in the KB or the crux corpus. The G1-i-HC discrepancy bound is a NEW inequality type. The three cruxes above are the closest hints to adapt (superincreasing-prefix obstruction + dyadic-scale-bracketing contradiction), not citations — every step would need proving from scratch.

## (b) Exact-rational sweep (n=4,5; s=3,4) — min-slack configs and worst-F structure

Script `/tmp/sweep_discrep.py` (fractions, ≤30s each, grid step 1/8; built `G`, `Alt_s`, `target=(D_{R_0}+1−M)/2` exactly). Recall (Lemma 10) `G`'s local extrema live at dyadic edges `2^k`; for n=4 the extrema are `G(1)=−1/2, G(2)=0, G(4)=−1, G(8)=1` (min at 4, max at 2 & 8, min at 1); for n=5 `G(1)=1/2, G(2)=0, G(4)=1, G(8)=−1, G(16)=3` (max at 1, min at 2... no: max at 4, min at 8, max at 16, etc.).

**Tight-config structure (n=4 s=3, 120 tight configs on grid):** EXACTLY the Lemma-6 family `F={4+ε_2, 2+ε_3, 1+ε_4}` with `ε_1+ε_2+ε_3+ε_4=1`, `W=8−ε_1`, `M=8+ε_1`. Confirmed: the tight configs are `{33/8, 2, 1}`, `{4, 17/8, 1}`, `{4, 2, 9/8}`, … — all near-dyadic-edge breakpoints with the `ε`-slack distributed arbitrarily among the breakpoints. So **the bound is tight iff s = n−1** (enough breakpoints to reach every G extremum), and for `s < n−1` there is positive slack (verified n=5 s=3: slack 1).

**Does the worst F have breakpoints chasing G's extrema?** YES, universally:
- n=4 s=3 worst {4,2,1}: `4`=local min of G, `2`=local max, `1`=local min — the alternating-sign extrema that MINIMIZE `Alt_s = G(min) − G(max) + G(min)`.
- n=5 s=4 worst {8,4,2,1}: `8`=min, `4`=max, `2`=min... (signs alternate correctly), `1`=max — full dyadic-tower prefix of extrema.
- n=5 s=3 worst {8,4,2}: three extrema (can't reach the 4th at `1`), so positive slack.

**Conclusion on the obstruction:** the worst F is ALWAYS the dyadic-tower prefix `{2^{n−2}, 2^{n−3}, …}` (s terms), breakpoints landing on G's alternating-sign extrema. The bound is tight iff `s = n−1` (Lemma-6 family). The obstruction is NOT "F's breakpoints cannot all align with G's extrema" (they CAN, when s = n−1, and that's exactly the tight case); it is "F's breakpoints aligning with G's extrema is the WORST case, and the W-sum constraint forces `Alt_s` there to equal exactly the target." The superincreasing-overflow crux is thus a **tight-at-the-tower-prefix** statement: `Alt_s` at the dyadic-tower-prefix breakpoints equals `(D_{R_0}+1−M)/2` exactly (arithmetic of the `ε`-slack: each `ε_i` contributes `±ε_i/2` to `Alt_s` and `−ε_1/2` to target, summing to equality via `Σε=1`). Any NON-tower-prefix F has `Alt_s > target` (slack ≥ 0). So the proof target is: **show the dyadic-tower-prefix is the unique minimizer of `Alt_s − target`** (a convexity/exchange argument: moving any breakpoint off a G-extremum increases `Alt_s`), then verify equality at the tower prefix arithmetically.

## (c) G1-iii-a third-mechanism candidates

G1-iii-a: all `2^n`-fragments `< 2^{n−1}`, rest's `2^{n−1}` UNSPLIT. `M = 2^{n−1}` (rest's, dominant). Config `= {M} ∪ {1,…,2^{n−2}} ∪ F'` where `F'` = `2^n`'s fragments (sum `2^n = 2M`, all `< M`). By Lemma 4, `D = M − D_R`, `R = {1,…,2^{n−2}} ∪ F'` (total `3M−1`); bound `D ≥ 1 ⟺ D_R ≤ M−1 = 2^{n−1}−1`.

Sweep (`/tmp/sweep_iii_a2.py`, fractions, step 1/2, ≤6 parts):
- n=4: max `D_R = 7 = M−1` → min `D = 1` (TIGHT). Worst `F' = {15/2, 9/2, 2, 1, 1/2, 1/2}` — largest fragment `15/2 = 8−1/2` just below `M=8`; remaining fragments near the dyadic tower `{4,2,1,½,½}`.
- n=5: max `D_R = 14 = M−2` → min `D = 2` (slack 1 on this coarse grid). Worst `F' = {31/2, 19/2, 4, 2, 1}` — largest `31/2 = 16−1/2` just below `M=16`; rest near dyadic tower. (Coarse grid may miss `D=1`; but the bound `D≥1` holds with at least slack 1 at n=5 on the coarse grid — suggests iii-a is EASIER than G1-i-HC for n≥5, with the min growing.)

**Candidate (a) — reduce iii-a to G1-i-HC with `M=2^{n−1}` dominant, `F'=2^n`'s fragments (sum `2M`): NOT FEASIBLE directly.** G1-i-HC's discrepancy machinery (Lemma 5: `D = M − D_{R_0} − D_F + 2C`; Lemma 11: `Alt_s = Σ(−1)^{i+1}G(f_i)`) is built for `F` summing to `W < U = 2^{n−1}` with a **top band `(M − U)`** on `(U, M]` (the dominant fragment `M` sits above the rest `R_0`'s max `2^{n−1}`). In iii-a, `M = U = 2^{n−1}` (the rest's piece IS the dominant), so **there is no top band** — `M` is at the `U` boundary, and `F'` sums to `2M > M`, not `< M`. The Lemma-5 decomposition and the `G` discrepancy function (which lives on `[0,U]` against `R_0={1,…,2^{n−1}}`) do NOT transfer: the "rigid background" would have to be the floor `{1,…,2^{n−2}}` (smaller), and the "fragment set" `F'` is LARGER than the background (roles swapped vs G1-i-HC). The discrepancy identity (Lemma 11) does not extend to this swapped configuration without a re-derivation. Confirmed: NOT a small-ε continuity limit (provenance-switch, already ruled out round 5) and NOT a direct reduction.

**Candidate (b) — DIRECT parity-integral: `D = M − D_R`, bound `D_R ≤ M−1`: FEASIBLE, requires a DUAL discrepancy problem.** `D_alt(floor {1,…,2^{n−2}}) = (2^{n−1}+(−1)^{n−2})/3` (3 at n=4, 5 at n=5); the worst `F'` raises `D_R` from `D_alt(floor)` to `M−1` (n=4: 3→7, i.e. F' contributes +4; n=5: 5→14, F' contributes +9). The bound `D_R ≤ M−1 = total(floor)` is the statement "`F'` raises the alternating sum of `floor ∪ F'` by at most `total(floor) − D_alt(floor)`." The worst `F'` has its largest fragment `M−ε` (just below `M`) and the rest a near-dyadic tower — the SAME superincreasing-overflow structure as G1-i-HC, but on the `F'` side with the floor's superincreasing `E`-bands (the floor `{1,…,2^{n−2}}` has its own `E`-band decomposition via Lemma 10's recursion at `n−1`) as the rigid background. So the **mechanism is the same superincreasing-overflow engine**, but the Lemma-5 decomposition must be re-derived for the swapped roles (`F'` large, floor small). 

**Promising sub-mechanism (b') — peeling recursion on F':** apply Lemma 4 to `R = floor ∪ F'`: `D_R = max(R) − D_{R''}` where `max(R) = max(F') = M − ε_1`. Then `D = M − (M−ε_1) + D_{R''} = ε_1 + D_{R''}` where `R'' = floor ∪ (F' \ {largest})`, total `2M − 1 + ε_1`. Repeating peels the largest fragment each time, giving `D = ε_1 + ε_2 + … + D_{final}` where `ε_i` are the "gaps below dyadic edges" of F''s sorted fragments. This REDUCES iii-a to a discrepancy bound on F' alone (the floor becomes a "rigid offset"). If `Σ ε_i + D_{final} ≥ 1` follows from the dyadic-tower structure of F' (each `ε_i ≥ 0`, `D_{final} ≥ 1` when the recursion bottoms out at the floor), iii-a closes. The n=5 slack (D=2) suggests this recursion has GROWING slack for larger n (iii-a is easier than G1-i-HC, not tight). **Most feasible candidate — but still requires generalizing the discrepancy identity.**

## (d) Genuinely-different G1 framing (far from discrepancy route + alternating-potential)

The dyadic-induction discrepancy route pins "shave 1" to `Alt_s = Σ(−1)^{i+1}G(f_i)` (breakpoints of F). The alternating-potential route is a re-lens of Lemma 4. A genuinely different framing:

**F_2-decomposition / parity-XOR reachability framing.** The certified parity-XOR toggle (`lemmas/parity-integral.md`) gives `j_final ≡ j_Liu ⊕ h (mod 2)`, `D_final = ∫[f ⊕ h] dt`, where `f = [j_Liu odd]` is the dyadic config's odd-region indicator and `h = h_{p_1} ⊕ … ⊕ h_{p_k}` is the XOR of per-split toggle indicators, each `h_{p_k} = 1_{[0,v_k)} + 1_{[u_k,p_k)}` (the indicator of TWO intervals of EQUAL length `v_k`). The framing:

> `D = 0` (the contradiction target) requires `f ⊕ h = 0` a.e., i.e. **`f` must be representable as a sum (mod 2) of `≤ n` "paired-equal-length-interval" indicators** `h_{p_k}`. Prove this representation is IMPOSSIBLE for the dyadic config's `f`, and the minimum "un-representable residual" is `≥ 1` (in units of `1/D_n`).

Why this is genuinely different: it works in the F_2-linear-algebra / XOR-measure space, NOT in the `Alt_s = Σ G(f_i)` alternating-discrepancy space (which is a refined decomposition specific to the rest-unsplit HC sub-case). The F_2-decomposition framing applies to the GENERAL G1 (rest-split, iii-a, iii-b) without re-derivation, because it operates on the raw `h`-toggle space.

**The cheap-kill structural observation:** `f = [j_Liu odd]` for the dyadic config is `1` on the union of dyadic bands `(2^{k}, 2^{k+1}]` where `n+1−k` is odd — these bands have **distinct, superincreasing lengths** (`1, 2, 4, …` in some order). Each toggle `h_{p_k}` is the indicator of **two intervals of EQUAL length** `v_k`. A single toggle can cover at most two of `f`'s bands, and only if those two bands have equal length — but `f`'s bands have DISTINCT lengths (superincreasing). So no single toggle covers two full bands of `f`; representation of `f` as `Σ h_k` requires at least one toggle per band of `f`, and `f` has `⌈n/2⌉` bands while Xiang has only `n` toggles — NOT obviously impossible, but the equal-length constraint + the superincreasing-band-length structure of `f` forces a minimum residual. The residual is `D`. Quantifying this residual via the superincreasing-band-length mismatch is the open step; it is the same superincreasing asset as Lemma 10's `E_{R_0}` bands, but attacked via an F_2-representation impossibility rather than an alternating-discrepancy bound. **This is the cleanest genuinely-different framing for the outliner to consider** — a parity/paired-interval-count argument that `D ≥ 1` because `f`'s superincreasing-band structure resists `≤ n` paired-equal-length toggles.

(Note: this is NOT the Lemma-7 union bound — that reformulates `D ≥ 1` as `|O_{R_0} ∪ O_F| ≤ …`, which is the same wall re-stated. The F_2-decomposition framing asks a different question: reachability of `f` in the toggle-algebra, not a union-measure bound. It may still collapse to the union bound at the bookkeeping level — the outliner should check this before building.)

## Summary for the outliner

- **G1-i-HC discrepancy bound:** the sweep CONFIRMS the tight case is the dyadic-tower-prefix F (breakpoints at G's alternating-sign extrema), tight iff `s = n−1`. The proof target is NOT "F can't align with G's extrema" (it can, that's the tight case) but "the dyadic-tower-prefix is the UNIQUE minimizer of `Alt_s − target`" (an exchange/convexity argument: moving any breakpoint off a G-extremum increases `Alt_s`), plus arithmetic equality at the tower prefix. The closest crux templates are aimo-0530 (superincreasing-prefix forces inclusion) and aimo-0493 (dyadic-scale-bracketing contradiction). No known inequality of this exact type — it is a new inequality to prove from scratch.
- **G1-iii-a:** candidate (a) [reduce to G1-i-HC] is INFEASIBLE (F' sums to `2M > M`; no top band; machinery doesn't transfer). Candidate (b) [direct `D_R ≤ M−1`] is FEASIBLE but requires a DUAL discrepancy problem (floor as rigid background, F' as the large fragment set); candidate (b') [peeling recursion `D = Σ ε_i + D_{final}`] is the most promising sub-mechanism — iii-a appears EASIER than G1-i-HC (n=5 shows slack, min D=2), so a looser argument may suffice.
- **Genuinely-different framing:** the F_2-decomposition / parity-XOR reachability framing (`D=0 ⟺ f` representable as `≤ n` paired-equal-length toggles; superincreasing-band-length mismatch forces residual `≥ 1`) is the cleanest far-from-discrepancy candidate for the outliner to build into a rival approach.
