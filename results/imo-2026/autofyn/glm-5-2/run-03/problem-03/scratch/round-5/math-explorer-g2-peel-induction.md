This is the literal generalization of the n=2 two-case contradiction (B+/B− on the sign of `2a−1`). Probed structure (n=3): when the simplest binding expression `p_3−p_4` (regime-1 `b-c` of peel `p_1→p_2`) exceeds `1/15`, the winner shifts to `{('p_1→p_4','b-c'), ('p_1→p_2','|2a−T|'), ('p_1→p_2','a-b'), ('p_1→p_4','a-b')}`, so the contradiction naturally branches. **This is the most direct route and the one the existing `pairing-charging` approach is already positioned to close** — the construction and subfamily are verified (0/30k); only the contradiction write-up is missing.

- **Opening B — Polyhedral-vertex reduction (the clean bookkeeping for Q2).** The construction value `v(p) = min` over peels × menu-members is a **minimum of piecewise-linear functions**, hence itself **piecewise-linear** (more precisely, piecewise-concave on each cell of the sort-regime arrangement). A piecewise-linear function on a compact convex polytope attains its **maximum at a vertex of the arrangement** (a point where two expressions cross, or where an expression meets a domain facet). The Case-C polytope's arrangement vertices are *finite* and *explicit* (intersections of the linear forms `p_i−p_j`, `2p_1−1`, `2(p_2+p_3)−1`, `p_4−(p_1−p_2)`, etc. with each other and the boundary facets `p_2=4/15`, `p_3=4/15`, `p_4=1/15`, `p_i=p_{i+1}`). **Verified numerically: the unique maximizer is the dyadic vertex `(8/15,4/15,2/15,1/15)` with `v=1/15`; every interior vertex has `v<1/15` strictly** (slack grows linearly, ≈ `2ε` along the ray `p_4=1/15+ε, p_2=4/15−ε`). So the casework *collapses to a finite vertex check*, not 12×3-regime expressions. This is the clean answer to dispatch Q2: the `min` over the 3-peel subfamily IS the right object, it IS tight only at dyadic, and the proof reduces to "evaluate `v` at each arrangement vertex, confirm `≤ 1/D_n`." The KB entry *Piecewise-concavity smoothing* (Algebra & Polynomials) is the structural template — max of a piecewise-concave function is at a breakpoint, and at a breakpoint some expression vanishes (becomes tight).

- **Opening C — Recursive minimax-tree (strengthened inductive hypothesis).** Define `f_n(config) := min_{peel p_1→p_j} f_{n−1}(rest)`, with `f_2(config) = min(c, |2a−T|, a−b, b−c)` (the certified n=2 menu). The claim `f_n(flat config) ≤ 1/D_n` is then an *induction on n* whose inductive step is precisely "min over peels of `f_{n−1}(rest) ≤ 1/D_n`" — i.e. the very-flat casework at level `n`. This frames the whole upper bound as a single recursive functional, NOT a per-level ad-hoc casework. The base `f_2 ≤ T/7` is CERTIFIED. The first non-trivial level is `n=3` Case C (Opening A or B closes it). Once `n=3` closes, the *same* inductive step (with `f_3` in place of `f_2`) closes `n=4`'s very-flat, etc. — provided the inductive step's casework is uniform in `n`. **Whether the step is uniform in `n` is the open question** (the arrangement-vertex count grows with `n`, but the *structure* — "min of PWL, max at dyadic vertex" — may persist).

### Answers to the dispatch's four questions

**Q1 — Can peel-once induction close Case C without the 12-expression casework?** **No — the induction cannot substitute for the casework; it IS the casework.** The obstruction is precise: the `(n−1)`-bound used as a black box gives `D_rest ≤ T/D_{n−1}` (loose scaling by total), and `T/D_{n−1} = (1−2p_j)/D_{n−1} ≤ 1/D_n ⟺ p_j ≥ g_{n−1}` — which is exactly the COMPLEMENT of the very-flat regime (`p_j < g_{n−1}`). In the very-flat regime the loose bound is STRICTLY TOO LOOSE. The only way to tighten is to use the `(n−1)`-bound's *tight structural form* (the full menu min, not the scalar `T/D_{n−1}`), and applying that to the derived rest IS the 12-expression casework. **The rest does NOT "fall out of the `(n−1)`-bound's hypothesis"** — the hypothesis is universal (any `n`-piece config) — it falls out of the bound's TIGHTNESS GAIN, which is rest-structure-dependent. Concretely for n=3: the n=2 menu is tight (= `T/7`) ONLY at the dyadic rest `(4T/7, 2T/7, T/7)`; for non-dyadic rests the menu is strictly below `T/7`. The derived rest `{p_1−p_2, p_3, p_4}` is near-dyadic precisely when the original config is near the dyadic boundary; away from it, the rest is non-dyadic and the menu gives slack. Quantifying "how non-dyadic is the rest as a function of how non-dyadic is the original" IS the casework — there is no scalar shortcut.

**Q2 — Cleaner bookkeeping for the 12 expressions? Structure of the verified 0/30k construction?** **Yes — see Opening B.** The construction value `v = min` over peels × menu-members is a **minimum of piecewise-linear functions**, hence piecewise-linear (piecewise-concave per cell). Its maximum over the Case-C polytope is at a **vertex of the linear-arrangement** (a crossing of two expressions or an expression meeting a domain facet). **The unique maximizer is the dyadic vertex** (`v=1/15`); all interior arrangement-vertices have `v<1/15` strictly (verified: 0 interior configs achieve equality, slack grows ≈ linearly `2ε` from dyadic). So the 12 expressions collapse to a **finite vertex check**, with the dyadic vertex as the unique tight point. Further pruning: only **5 of 12** (peel,member) pairs ever win (probed over 200k Case-C configs): `('p_1→p_2','b-c')` (= `p_3−p_4` in regime 1, the dominant tight pair), `('p_1→p_2','|2a−T|')`, `('p_1→p_2','a-b')`, `('p_1→p_4','b-c')`, `('p_1→p_4','a-b')`, plus `('p_2→p_3','b-c')` in a smaller region. The construction IS the `min` over the 3-peel subfamily, it IS tight only at dyadic, and the proof reduces to evaluating `v` at finitely many arrangement vertices — a clean contradiction-on-vertices, structurally identical to the n=2 two-case proof but on a finer arrangement.

**Q3 — Does peel-once + `(n−1)`-bound generalize to n≥4 very-flat?** **Not directly — it bottoms out at n=3 Case C.** At level `n` very-flat (all `p_j < g_{n−1}`), the peel-once induction needs the `(n−1)`-bound's TIGHT form (the full menu), which for `n−1=2` is certified (so n=3 Case C is doable) but for `n−1≥3` requires the `(n−1)` very-flat result — which is itself open until the prior level closes. So the induction is a **nested/chain induction**: n=3 Case C must close before n=4 very-flat is even attackable; n=4 before n=5; etc. The construction PATTERN (peel once + apply `(n−1)` full construction) is the right inductive shape, and the recursive functional `f_n` (Opening C) makes this literal, but each level's very-flat sub-case needs its own vertex/contradiction casework. **The arrangement-vertex count grows with `n`** (roughly multiplicatively per level: n=3 has ~5 effective expressions; n=4 would have ~4 peels × ~5 = ~20; etc.), so the casework DOES grow, but the *structure* (min of PWL, max at dyadic vertex) may stay uniform — an outliner could attempt a uniform inductive step on `f_n` rather than per-level ad-hoc casework.

**Q4 — The genuinely-hard sub-case at the bottom?** **n=3 Case C is the deepest base that must be proved directly.** n=2 is certified (the 5-member menu, 2-case contradiction, base of the peel-once induction). n=3 Case C is the FIRST level where the very-flat residual appears (because for n=2, the "very-flat" regime `p_2,p_3 < g_1 = 2/7` is vacuous — `g_1 = 2/7` and `p_2 ≥ p_3` with `p_3 > 1/7` forces `p_2 ≥ p_3 > 1/7`, but `p_2 < 2/7` is possible; however n=2 is closed by the direct menu, no peel needed). **No regime where the induction bottoms out "below" n=2 was found** — n=2 is a clean base. The hard direct case is n=3 Case C; once it closes, the induction can in principle climb to all `n` (each level adding a very-flat vertex-casework of the same structural flavor). The outliner/builder's exact hard step: **"prove `min` over the 5 effective (peel,menu) expressions `≤ 1/15` over the Case-C polytope, by evaluating the piecewise-linear min at each arrangement vertex (or by a 2–3 case contradiction mirroring n=2 §6.3), with tightness (= `1/15`) only at the dyadic vertex `(8/15,4/15,2/15,1/15)`."**

- **Intuition (not proof):** the dyadic config is the "hardest" point because it is the unique config where the rest after ANY peel is itself dyadic (self-similarity `g_k = 2 g_{k−1}`), so every peel's menu is tight simultaneously — a fixed point of the peel-then-menu operator. Away from dyadic, at least one peel breaks self-similarity and gives slack. This is the conceptual reason the cap is tight only at dyadic, and it is the right intuition to drive both the vertex-check (Opening B) and the recursive induction (Opening C).
