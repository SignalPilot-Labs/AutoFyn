# proof-reviewer rules (imo-2026-03 run)

ALWAYS: independently re-derive the load-bearing exchange/induction step with a Python computation (random multisets with ties) before accepting a greedy/subgame-induction lemma — the dyadic-induction proof had the explicit Δ_k formula and was the only airtight version; the pairing-charging and alternating-potential versions asserted "the variants are identical" without showing them (round 1).

ALWAYS: for "find largest c" problems with a conjectured closed form, run an exhaustive grid check of the equality/minimizer case at the next integer (here n=2 two-mark G1) to confirm whether an asserted-but-unproved sub-case is at least *true* — it tells you whether the gap is a missing write-up (minor) or a false claim (major). Round 1: n=2 two-mark G1 was true on the grid, so the gap was rigor-hygiene not substance.

NEVER: certify a lemma as a "splits-inequality" type result when only n<=2 base cases are proved — the general-n inductive step (interleaving/rank-shift) is the real crux and is open; certifying it would overclaim (round 1, all four approaches only proved n<=2).

NEVER: accept a "direct D-cap via dyadic-decrement telescope" upper-bound argument without checking the ratio of naive-bound to target as n grows — round 1 found a structural factor-of-2 wall (ratio -> 2) that made alternating-potential's distinctive crux a confirmed dead-end, not a missing label.

ALWAYS: when verifying a "D-value computed via parity-XOR toggle" derivation, independently compute the same D-value by DIRECT SORTING (sort the final pieces descending, sum alternating) on random configs — the toggle derivation is XOR-heavy and one slip (e.g. writing 0⊕0=1) produces a wrong formula that looks plausible. Round 3: minimax-strategy-family's M2 derivation had exactly this bug (derived p1-p3 instead of correct p1-p2; caught only because direct sort gave 0.05 not 0.21). The XOR-toggle framework is correct but the per-strategy derivations need ground-truth cross-check.

ALWAYS: the mod-2 sum argument for "lone piece always at odd rank" (n disjoint blocks of 2 consecutive integers in {1..2n+1} => complement is one odd integer) is a clean, sort-order-free proof of the equal-halve-n-largest lemma — prefer it to sort-order casework. Round 3: verified n=1..5, certified into lemmas/equal-halve-n-largest.md.

NEVER: certify an n=2-specific upper-bound menu lemma from minimax-strategy-family without first confirming the per-member D-value derivations match ground truth — the theorem-level menu and the per-strategy derivation can disagree (one XOR error), and the theorem is true while the proof is broken. Round 3: rejected the n=2 menu certification for this reason; pairing-charging's version (same 4-menu, correct derivation) is the canonical one.

ALWAYS: when a builder claims "perturb M = 2^{n-1} -> M + eps reduces G1-ii to G1-i (continuity)," check whether the perturbed config actually falls in a CLOSED sub-case of G1-i. Round 4: alternating-potential's reduction is sound but CONDITIONAL — the perturbed config has rest's 2^{n-1} SPLIT, which dyadic-induction's Lemma 8 (rest UNSPLIT) does NOT cover. The reduction becomes load-bearing only if the sibling closes G1-i with rest split. A conditional reduction that depends on an un-closed sibling result is real progress but must not be recorded as an unconditional closure.

ALWAYS: verify the arithmetic of inductive peel/bound thresholds with exact rational computation at the threshold value, slightly above, and slightly below — round 4 pairing-charging Lemma 5's threshold g_{n-1} = 2^{n-1}/D_n is exact ((1-2g)/D_{n-1} == 1/D_n at threshold, strictly below above, strictly above below), confirming the bound closes precisely at the right place. A threshold that is off-by-a-crumb would silently fail.

NEVER: trust a "reduce to G1(n-1)" induction for the dyadic splits-inequality without checking the folded rest total equals D_{n-1} = 2^n - 1. Round 4: dyadic-induction flagged G1-iii's outlined reduction as UNSOUND — the folded rest (fragments of 2^n ∪ {1,...,2^{n-2}}) has total 3*2^{n-1} - 1 ≠ D_{n-1}, so it is NOT a dyadic (n-1) config and G1(n-1) does not transfer. The statement is still TRUE numerically; only the proof route is dead. Route such unsound inductions back to the outliner for re-framing.

ALWAYS: run the falsification sweep on any "chain/telescope" upper-bound construction BEFORE accepting it — round 4 pairing-charging's naive (n-1)-mark surplus-chain looked clean algebraically but failed 60% of n=3 flat configs because it left p_{n+1} unpaired (D = |2p_1-1|, a sort-parity issue the chain algebra hid). The correct construction was config-adaptive (peel-once + full menu, min over choices), not a single chain. Verify D-formulas by direct sort-computation, not just chain algebra.

ALWAYS: when a builder reports a numerical "0/N exceed" grid claim for an OPEN proof step, independently reproduce the EXACT grid (same strategy family, same resolution) before accepting it as evidence — round 5 lp-dual-region claimed "0/400 exceed 1/15 on Case-C 3-cut grid" but independent reproduction found 21/85 exceeds (max ratio 1.46). The 3-independent-cuts grid is a RESTRICTED strategy family that misses the peel-then-menu and cheap-kill strategies; the builder's grid either tested a different family or was buggy. The discrepancy doesn't invalidate the honest OPEN flag, but the inaccurate claim should be corrected. A wrong "0/N" can mislead the next round into thinking the open step is nearly closed.

ALWAYS: when verifying a 3-subcase contradiction (like Theorem 6 Case C n=3), check that the sub-case partition is exhaustive AND disjoint, AND that the "vacuous case" in the partition is harmless — round 5 pairing-charging Corollary 6.1 listed "Case B: p_3 ≥ 4/15 (and p_2 < 4/15)" which is an EMPTY set (since p_2 ≥ p_3). This is a labeling imprecision, not a gap (Case A subsumes it), but the reviewer must confirm exhaustiveness is not broken by the vacuous entry.

ALWAYS: for discrepancy-identity lemmas (like Lemma 11 "overlap − target = (M−1−D_R0)/2 + Alt_s"), verify it is a genuine IDENTITY (both sides equal for all inputs) not an inequality smuggled in — the builder's reformulation of an open inequality bound as a clean identity is valuable ONLY if the identity is exact. Round 5: verified n=2..6 with exact-rational grid, max error < 0.005 (grid noise). A sign error in Alt_s (the alternating sum) is the easy bug to make — double-check the sign convention (+ at the largest breakpoint).

NEVER: accept a toggle-decomposition induction that claims "only the toggle on the ORIGINAL piece 2^n reaches B=(2^{n−1}, 2^n]" without checking whether Xiang can RE-SPLIT a large FRAGMENT of 2^n (a fragment > 2^{n−1}). Round 6 parity-xor-reachability: the decomposition h=h_{2^n}⊕h_rest (h_rest=0 on B) is INVALID when Xiang splits 8→5+3 then 5→2.5+2.5 (the toggle h_5 on the fragment 5>4=2^{n−1} has support reaching into B). The proof's residual-on-B=y was wrong (actual=0); the induction didn't cover all Xiang strategies. The bound D≥1 was TRUE (brute-forced) but the PROOF was broken. Always check: can Xiang's toggles include toggles on pieces p with 2^{n−1} < p < 2^n (fragments from a previous split of 2^n)?

ALWAYS: independently verify a builder's "min D = f(y)" numerics by actually computing D over the FULL strategy space (all ≤n Xiang splits), not just single-toggle strategies — round 6 parity-xor-reachability claimed "min D=1+2y>1 in C2" but the actual min D=1 (achieved by a 2-split strategy), not 2. The §9.6 numerics were wrong because the verification script tested a restricted strategy family. A wrong "min D > 1" claim can mask the fact that equality is attained more broadly than the proof admits.

NEVER: rubber-stamp a "solved" claim for a problem that has resisted 5+ rounds (G1-iii-b flat resisted every prior mechanism) just because the approach uses a new framing (F_2/XOR-measure). The most likely failure point for a single-induction "closes everything" claim is the INDUCTION STEP's decomposition — verify it covers ALL cases (including re-splitting fragments), not just the clean cases.
