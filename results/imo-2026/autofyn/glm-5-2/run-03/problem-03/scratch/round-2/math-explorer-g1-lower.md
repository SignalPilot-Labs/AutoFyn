## imo-2026-03 (G1-general — LOWER-BOUND wall)

### Distinct openings (routes to close G1: `D ≥ 1/D_n` for the dyadic config after ≤ n Xiang splits)

**Route A — convexity of `S_odd` + local-min verification at equal-halving (NEW; not in any prior approach).** `S_odd = a_1 + a_3 + a_5 + …` (Liu's payoff) is a SUM OF ORDER STATISTICS, hence CONVEX as a function of the multiset vector (the k-th largest element is a max of linear forms, sum-of-convex = convex). Since `D = 2·S_odd − T = 2·S_odd − 1`, `D` is convex in the multiset. The achievable refinement set (multisets from ≤ n cuts, 0-padded to a fixed length) is convex under multiset averaging (average of two refinements of piece `2^k` is a refinement of `2^k`). So the minimization of the convex `S_odd` over the achievable set is a convex problem. **Key verifiable claim:** at the equal-halving point, every feasible split-perturbation direction has directional derivative `≥ 0`. I verified: for `k ≥ 2`, perturbing the split of `2^k` to `(2^{k-1}+δ, 2^{k-1}−δ)` changes `S_odd` by `+|δ|` (the odd-rank half grows); for `k = 1` (piece 2), the perturbation is absorbed by the three trailing 1's and gives `ΔS_odd = 0`. So the directional derivative is `Σ_{k≥2} |δ_k| ≥ 0`. If `S_odd` is convex on a CONVEX achievable set and equal-halving has all directional derivatives `≥ 0`, it is the GLOBAL min, giving `S_odd ≥ 2^n/D_n` i.e. `D ≥ 1/D_n` for free.
- **Concrete next step:** formalize the achievable-set convexity (handle the unequal-cut-count case by 0-padding each piece's split-vector to a common length, making each piece's contribution a simplex → product of simplices = convex), then write the directional-derivative computation at equal-halving as a finite check (one per `k`). The obstacle: equal-halving has piece 1 UNSPLIT, so it sits on the boundary of the "split-all" polytope, not the interior — boundary directional derivatives (toward splitting piece 1, or toward multi-cutting a piece) must ALSO be checked `≥ 0`. I verified splitting piece 1 is `S_odd`-increasing (n=1: `S_odd = 2 + α`); the multi-cut boundary needs the same one-line check.
- **Promise:** HIGH. This is the cleanest route — convexity sidesteps the interleaving/rank-shift obstacle entirely (the sort is a convex operation, so interleaving is automatically handled).

**Route B — parity-integral with the ADD/SUBTRACT structure (NEW handle).** The parity-integral `D = ∫[j(t) odd] dt` (already proved) gives a cleaner handle on `j(t)` than on `D` directly. **NEW decomposition:** a split of piece `p` into `α + (p−α)` (α the smaller fragment) changes `j(t)` by `+1` on `[0, α]` and `−1` on `[p−α, p]` (NOT just `+1` on `[0,α]` as a naive reading suggests — the original piece `p` is removed and replaced by two sub-pieces). For the dyadic config, the SUBTRACT intervals `[2^k − α, 2^k]` are DISJOINT (one per dyadic band `(2^{k-1}, 2^k]`), while the ADD intervals `[0, α]` are NESTED at the bottom. The minimizer's parity profile is "all even except `[0, 1]` odd" (verified: equal-halving gives `j = 2n+1` on `[0,1]` odd, `2(n−k)` even on each `(2^{k-1}, 2^k]`).
- **Concrete next step:** prove that for any choice of `α_k ∈ [0, 2^{k-1}]` (one per piece `2^k`, `k ≥ 1`), the parity-integral `D ≥ 1`. The structure: adds nest inside `[0, 2^{n-1}]`, subtracts sit in disjoint dyadic bands. A direct parity-counting argument (track parity of `j` on each dyadic band as a function of the `α_k`) is the target. The base case `n = 1, 2` is a finite parity table; the inductive step uses that adds on `[0, 2^{n-1}]` only affect the lower `(n−1)` dyadic bands.
- **Promise:** HIGH. This is the route the dispatch prompt flagged; the add/subtract structure is the missing piece that makes the parity-integral actually computational. Combined with Route A (convexity) it is very strong: convexity gives the global-min statement, parity-integral gives the concrete handle.

**Route C — finite casework for `n = 2` two-mark, then induct on `n` with the parity-integral to beat interleaving.** The `n = 2` two-mark case (the open sub-case) IS closeable by finite casework — I brute-forced it exactly (rational grid, `N = 40`): min `D = 1` exactly, attained by "split piece 4 into `1+1+2`" (the `(n−1)`-dyadic tower plus extra 1) AND by equal-halving AND by barely-splitting. The equality set is a 1-parameter family, not isolated. For general `n`, the inductive obstacle (interleaving of `2^n`'s descendants with the smaller pieces in the SORTED order) does NOT arise in the `j(t)` picture, because `j(t) = j_small(t) + j_large(t)` is ADDITIVE over pieces — no sorting, no interleaving. The (n−1)-hypothesis on the smaller pieces (a scaled `(n−1)`-dyadic config) directly gives `j_small`'s parity profile; the add/subtract from `2^n`'s splits then layers on top.
- **Concrete next step:** write the `n = 2` two-mark casework explicitly (close the open sub-case, certify a small lemma). Then for general `n`: apply the `(n−1)`-hypothesis to `j_small` (the exceedance of the refined smaller pieces), and prove the layering of `j_large`'s add/subtract on top keeps `D ≥ 1`. The inductive variable is `n` (the tower height); the base `n = 1, 2` is fully settled.
- **Promise:** MEDIUM-HIGH. The `j`-additivity cleanly defeats the interleaving obstacle (the obstacle was an artifact of the sorted-order picture). Risk: the inductive step still needs the "layering preserves `D ≥ 1`" parity argument, which is Route B's core.

**Route D — charging / Hall-type matching (speculative).** The minimizer (equal-halving) has pieces pairing perfectly: `(2^{n-1}, 2^{n-1}), (2^{n-2}, 2^{n-2}), …, (2, 2), (1, 1),` lone `1`. `D = lone 1 = 1/D_n`. The lower bound `D ≥ 1/D_n` is equivalent to "no refinement pairs up better than leaving residual `1/D_n`." Since `D = Σ(a_{2k-1} − a_{2k})` (consecutive-pairing gaps) is the MINIMUM pairing surplus (consecutive pairing minimizes gaps for sorted multisets), the claim is: the consecutive-pairing surplus `≥ 1/D_n`. A Hall/matching lower bound on the surplus is conceivable but I did not find the right injection.
- **Promise:** LOW-MEDIUM. Speculative; no concrete handle found. Defer unless Routes A/B stall.
