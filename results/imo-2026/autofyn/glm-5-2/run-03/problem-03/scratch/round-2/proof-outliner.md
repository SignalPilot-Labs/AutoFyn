### dyadic-induction: advance
Target: the whole problem `c(n) = 2^n/D_n`; this round closes the LOWER bound (G1-general) end-to-end and certifies `lemmas/splits-inequality.md`. Technique: convexity of order statistics (spine, Route A) + parity-integral add/subtract (Route B, rigorous handle) + finite casework for the n=2 two-mark base.
Framing: the lower bound is a CONVEX minimization of `S_odd` (= 2·D + 1) over the convex achievable-refinement polytope; equal-halving is the verified local (hence global) min. The parity-integral gives the concrete finite check that defeats the sorted-order interleaving obstacle (j(t) is additive over pieces — no sorting).
Skeleton:
  1. Achievable-refinement polytope is convex — by showing each piece's split-vector lives in a simplex (after 0-padding to a common length); the product of simplices is convex, and multiset averaging of two refinements of piece `2^k` is a refinement of `2^k` — by convexity of simplices / definition of refinement.
  2. `S_odd` is convex in the multiset vector — because the k-th largest element is a max of linear forms over k-subsets (order-statistics convexity; sum-of-convex = convex). Hence `D = 2·S_odd − 1` is convex. (Prove this from scratch — not in KB.)
  3. Equal-halving is a local min of `S_odd` on the polytope — directional derivative in every split-perturbation direction is `Σ_{k≥2} |δ_k| ≥ 0` (for k≥2, perturbing the split of `2^k` to `(2^{k−1}+δ, 2^{k−1}−δ)` grows the odd-rank half by `|δ|`; for k=1 the perturbation is absorbed by the trailing triple of 1's, ΔS_odd = 0). Boundary directions (splitting piece 1; multi-cutting a piece) checked separately, each `≥ 0` (one-line per direction).
  4. Convex + local-min ⟹ global min — so `S_odd ≥ S_odd(equal-halving) = 2^n/D_n`, i.e. `D ≥ 1/D_n`. (This disposes of G1 for all n in one stroke.)
  5. Independent rigorous handle (Route B, parity-integral add/subtract): a split of `p` into `α + (p−α)` changes `j(t)` by `+1` on `[0,α]` AND `−1` on `[p−α, p]` (load-bearing: the subtract is NOT optional — missing it gives wrong j-profiles). For the dyadic config, subtracts land in DISJOINT dyadic bands `(2^{k−1}, 2^k]`, adds nest at the bottom. Prove `D = ∫[j odd] ≥ 1/D_n` by tracking parity per dyadic band as a function of the `α_k`. This is the concrete finite-verification companion to step 4 (and the fallback if convexity-of-order-statistics hits a snag).
  6. n=2 two-mark base case by finite rational casework — close the open sub-case explicitly (the equality family "split 4 into 1+1+2-variants plus equal-halving" is 1-parameter; min D = 1 exactly). Cheap, clears the base so the induction (if a backup inductive route is needed) stands.
  7. Promote `lemmas/splits-inequality.md` from step 4 (or 5) — statement: "for the dyadic multiset `{1,2,…,2^n}/D_n, after any ≤ n splits, D ≥ 1/D_n, with equality at equal-halving and its 1-parameter equality family." Certifiable; closes G1 for ALL approaches.
Key lemmas (claim + mechanism):
  - "S_odd is convex in the multiset vector" — because each order statistic a_(k) = max over k-subsets of the min, a maximum of linear forms, hence convex; S_odd is a sum of convex order statistics.
  - "Equal-halving is a local min" — because perturbing an equal split of 2^k (k≥2) grows the odd-rank fragment by |δ|; the trailing triple of 1's absorbs the k=1 perturbation; boundary perturbations (splitting piece 1) only add to S_odd (verified n=1: S_odd = 2+α).
  - "The achievable-refinement set is convex" — because each piece's split-vector is a simplex and refinement = product of simplices; multiset-average of two refinements of the same piece is a refinement.
  - "j(t) is additive over pieces" — because j(t) = Σ_i 1[a_i ≥ t] is a sum, so the sorted-order interleaving obstacle (which killed the naive induction) does not arise in the j-picture.
Open gaps: G2 upper bound for n ≥ 2 (this approach's peeling route is dead — peeling-smallest gives `D_rest = (1−2p_{n+1})/D_{n−1}`, loose at dyadic; piece-count does not close on n). Flag honestly; the upper bound is conceded to the sibling approaches this round.
Cases to cover: n=2 two-mark finite casework (step 6); boundary directional-derivative directions at equal-halving (step 3: split-piece-1, multi-cut each piece).
Watch out for: (a) equal-halving sits on the BOUNDARY of the "split-all" polytope (piece 1 is unsplit) — boundary directional derivatives must be checked separately, not just interior; (b) convexity of order statistics must be proved from scratch (not in KB) — the builder must invoke "k-th largest = max of linear forms"; (c) the parity-integral add/subtract must use the FULL decomposition (+1 on [0,α] AND −1 on [p−α,p]); the naive "splits only add to j" reading is FALSE (recorded dead end); (d) do NOT revive the sorted-order dyadic-induction (interleaving obstacle is fatal in that picture; the j-picture is its replacement).

### pairing-charging: advance
