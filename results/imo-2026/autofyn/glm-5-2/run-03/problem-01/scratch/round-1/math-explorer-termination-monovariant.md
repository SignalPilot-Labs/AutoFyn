## imo-2026-01 — route: global termination potential (P,C) + choice-independence invariant

- **Distinct openings (each a different attack the outliner could build into a rival approach):**
  1. **(P, C) lexicographic descent for part (a).** Board product P = product of all 2026 numbers; C = count of numbers >1. Under (m,n)→(gcd, lcm/gcd): write m=g·a, n=g·b, gcd(a,b)=1, g=gcd(m,n). The two numbers become (g, a·b). Board product of the pair changes g²·a·b → g·a·b, i.e. P is divided by g≥1. So P is non-increasing, **strictly decreases iff g>1** (integer division by ≥2). When g=1 the move is (m,n)→(1, mn): one of the pair becomes 1, so **C drops by 1 while P is fixed**. Crucial extra fact: C is *globally non-increasing* (case check below). Hence every move strictly decreases the lexicographic pair (P, C) — primary P (down), secondary C (down). Both P (positive integer ≥1) and C (integer in [0,2026]) are bounded below; lex order on ℕ_{≥1}×[0,2026] is well-founded (P can strictly drop only finitely many times, and between P-drops C drops only finitely many times). Termination follows. This is the cleanest termination argument and avoids per-prime Euclidean termination entirely.
  2. **Per-prime gcd-of-exponents invariant d_p for part (b) AND for ruling out C=0 in part (a).** For each prime p, track the multiset of p-adic valuations across the 2026 numbers. Under the move, the two chosen valuations (α,β) become (min(α,β), |α−β|) — the Euclidean subtraction step — and the other valuations are untouched. The identity gcd(α,β)=gcd(min(α,β), |α−β|) (Euclidean algorithm) gives: the gcd of the *whole multiset* of p-exponents is invariant. Call it d_p = gcd(v_p(a_1),…,v_p(a_2026)) computed once from the initial board. At any terminal stuck state with exactly one M>1 and the rest 1's, the multiset of p-exponents is (m_p, 0,…,0) with m_p=v_p(M), so d_p = gcd(m_p,0,…,0) = m_p. Therefore **M = ∏_p p^{d_p}**, a quantity fixed by the initial board — choice-independent.
  3. **Ruling out C=0 (the "all ones" terminus) via d_p.** Suppose the process terminates with C=0 (all numbers 1). Then every v_p=0 on the board, so d_p=0 for every p. By invariance the *initial* d_p=0 for every p, forcing every initial v_p(a_i)=0, i.e. every initial a_i=1 — contradicting the hypothesis that all 2026 numbers are >1. Hence the only reachable terminus is C=1, completing part (a) with "exactly one." (Equivalently: some initial a_i>1 ⇒ some prime p divides it ⇒ d_p≥1 since gcd(x,0,…,0)=x>0 ⇒ at the terminus m_p=d_p>0 ⇒ M>1 ⇒ C≥1; combine with C≤1.)
  4. **(Optional, not needed once 1–3 are in place) per-prime Euclidean termination as an alternative.** For a single prime p, the move is the subtractive Euclidean step on two exponents; iterating drives each pair toward (d_p, 0). But the moves are *coupled across primes* (the same pair of numbers is acted on for all p simultaneously), so a clean per-prime termination proof is non-trivial. The (P,C) lex descent in opening 1 sidesteps this coupling entirely — recommend it as the primary termination engine, with d_p as the invariant for both (a)-exactly-one and (b).

- **Small-case / intuition notes (CONJECTURE, verified by simulation, not a proof):** A Python simulation on initial boards {12,18,30,7,100,9,25}→M=210; {4,8,16,32}→M=2; {6,10,15}→M=30; {2,4,8,16,32,64}→M=2; {36,100,225}→M=900; {8,27,125,343}→M=9261000 confirms, over 20 random plays each, that (i) every move strictly lex-decreases (P,C), (ii) d_p is invariant throughout, (iii) the process always ends with exactly one number M>1 (never 0), and (iv) M = ∏_p p^{gcd(v_p(a_1),…,v_p(a_2026))} is choice-independent. - **Soundness of the termination argument:** SOUND. (P,C) strictly lex-decreases every move; both coordinates are bounded-below integers (P≥1, 0≤C≤2026); well-foundedness gives finiteness. The four cases for C's behavior under a move, all giving C non-increasing: (a) gcd=1 ⇒ (1,mn), C: 2→1 (drop); (b) gcd>1, m=n (so a=b=1) ⇒ (g,1), C: 2→1 (drop); (c) gcd>1, m≠n, ab>1 ⇒ (g,ab) both >1, C unchanged; (d) gcd=1, ab=1 impossible since m,n>1. P strictly drops in (b),(c); P fixed in (a). No move leaves both P and C fixed. Stuck ⟺ fewer than 2 numbers >1 ⟺ C≤1; the d_p invariant then forces C=1 (not 0).

- **Risks the outliner must settle:**
  1. **Lex-well-foundedness rigor.** Must state explicitly that lex on ℕ_{≥1} × [0,2026] is well-founded (no infinite descending chain): P drops only finitely often (positive integer, bounded below by 1); between consecutive P-drops, C drops only finitely often (bounded below by 0). The reviewer will reject hand-waved "obviously terminates."
  2. **The d_p invariance proof must name the Euclidean identity.** gcd(α,β)=gcd(min(α,β),|α−β|) — cite as the Euclidean algorithm / gcd(a,b)=gcd(a,b−a). Show the untouched exponents don't change the multiset gcd. Adding zeros (when a number becomes 1) preserves gcd: gcd(x,0)=x.
  3. **C=0 ruling must be airtight.** The chain is: terminus C=0 ⇒ all v_p=0 on board ⇒ d_p=0 ∀p (invariant) ⇒ initial d_p=0 ∀p ⇒ initial v_p(a_i)=0 ∀p,i ⇒ all a_i=1, contradicting "all >1." Do NOT shortcut this to "M>1 because product is large" — P can drop arbitrarily, so size alone doesn't rule out C=0; only the d_p invariant does.
  4. **Stuck ⟺ C≤1 must be stated.** A move needs two numbers >1; C≥2 ⇒ a legal move exists. So the process cannot freeze at C≥2.
  5. **M formula derivation depends only on the terminal state, not on proving exponents concentrate en route.** The lex descent guarantees arrival at C≤1; the d_p invariant at *that* state gives M=∏p^{d_p}. No need to independently prove per-prime Euclidean concentration (the coupling makes that hard) — this is the route's main simplification and should be flagged as the reason the (P,C) global argument is preferred over per-prime.

- **Hard steps this route must settle:** essentially none beyond the five rigor items above. This route solves *both* parts with elementary tools (monovariant + Euclidean identity); no heavy machinery. The only genuinely subtle point is #3 (ruling out C=0 via d_p, not via P's size).
