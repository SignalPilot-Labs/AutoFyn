## imo-2026-03 (recursive / inductive Xiang-strategy route to UPPER BOUND — gap G2)

### Distinct openings (the several angles surfaced — each a different attack the outliner could build into a rival approach)

1. **Engine R-pile — greedy recursive pile-matching on the two largest pieces.** Xiang repeatedly identifies the two largest pieces a_1 ≥ a_2 of the current partition; cuts a piece of size a_2 out of a_1 (one mark), creating the canceling pair (a_2, a_2); the residual piece a_1 − a_2 (≥ a_2 by admissibility, i.e. requires a_1 ≥ 2 a_2) rejoins the partition; recurse with one fewer mark on the residual config. This is the direct generalization of the certified n=1 sliver mode (which matches the small piece x inside the large piece L, giving the pair (x,x)) AND of n=2 Strategy A (match b inside c). Brute-force n=3 confirms Xiang's optimal response on the dominant config (.6,.2,.1,.1) is exactly three pile-matches → three pairs (1/5,1/5) and a singleton, oddsum = 1/2 < 8/15. The recursion is *agnostic* (caps for all P, equality characterization falls out at the dyadic) — exactly the dispatch's preferred shape.

2. **Engine R-dyadic-comparison — agnostic majorization / "the dyadic is the worst case for Xiang."** Conjecture: Xiang's cap is maximized over Liu configs precisely at the dyadic. Probe (n=2, grid 1/84): the dyadic gives cap = 4/7 = f(2); every perturbation, even ±1/84, drops the cap strictly (e.g. to 47/84, 15/28, 23/42). For n=3 the dyadic gives exactly 8/15 and every sampled non-dyadic config gives strictly less. If the cap is Schur-convex (or monotone in the right majorization order) under Xiang's *refinement* freedom, this gives a one-shot agnostic `cap ≤ f(n)` proof with no casework. This is the cleanest "agnostic cap ≤ f(n) for all P" route — it does not need P to be dyadic or even near-dyadic.

3. **Engine R-self-similar — Xiang-controlled M⊎R decomposition.** The certified lower-bound recursion splits the level-(n+1) dyadic as {M} ⊎ R where M = f(n+1) is the largest piece and R is a scaled level-n dyadic, with the load-bearing identity M − total(R) = 1/D(n+1) = α(n+1). For the upper bound, Xiang *chooses* which piece to treat as "M" and how to split it; the hope is to force the rest into a scaled level-(n−1)-dyadic-like sub-structure by spending marks. This is the only engine that directly reuses the certified M⊎R machinery and the per-round value recursion `1/f(n+1) = 1 + 1/(2 f(n))` (a verified algebraic identity, but per-ROUND not per-mark).

4. **Engine R-residual-singleton — strictness via the residual (3,2)-pair excess.** The pair-pile's advantage A = 1/D(n) comes entirely from the single (3,2)-pair (sorted-desc consecutive) — every other consecutive pair cancels. Inductive strictness for non-dyadic configs should come from the residual pair's excess being STRICTLY less than 1/D(n). This reframes G2 as: "Xiang can always arrange the final multiset so that, after canceling consecutive equal pairs, the residual consecutive-pair excess is ≤ 1/D(n), with strictness unless the multiset is the pair-pile." Cleanest framing of where strictness comes from inductively — but it inherits the Hall obstruction (consecutive-rank condition is global).

### Candidate technique(s) — the method(s) the problem points to

- **Strong induction on n with a one-move-per-step recursive reduction** (induction-and-construction knowledge-base entry), BUT the recursion must be GLOBAL on the largest-vs-rest structure (not per-mark — per-mark is a certified dead end). The recursion variable is "number of remaining marks + remaining pieces," not "n itself."
- **Invariants & monovariants** — the advantage A = Σ(−1)^{i+1} p_i is the controlled invariant; the pair-pile is the extremal config of this invariant (A = 1/D(n)). Engine R-pile maintains "consecutive equal pairs cancel" as a recursive invariant.
- **Casework / exhaustion** — the n=2 four-strategy 4-way contradiction generalizes only if a recursive telescoping replaces the (2^n−1)-way flat contradiction. The dispatch explicitly forbids the (2^n−1)-way flat casework; the recursion must replace it.
- **Pile-matching / Hall** (knowledge_base: Hall's marriage theorem) — the round-1 Hall route is DEAD, but the GREEDY recursive pile-match (Engine R-pile) is a different object: it matches the two *current largest* pieces greedily, controlling the global sort at each step, rather than asking for a global perfect matching.

### Where strictness comes from inductively (the dispatch's question)

In the n=1 two-mode, strictness for non-dyadic comes from: the SLIVER mode leaves the largest piece L as a residual singleton at odd rank (Liu = L < 2/3), while the BISECT mode at the dyadic leaves a perfect pair (L/2, L/2) canceling with a small residual x = 1/3. Inductively (Engine R-pile), strictness should come from: after the greedy pile-match creates a canceling pair (a_2, a_2), the residual singleton a_1 − a_2 is STRICTLY larger than the dyadic's residual (3, 2)-pair-excess 1/D(n) UNLESS the config is the dyadic (where a_1 − a_2 telescopes exactly to the pair-pile's residual). Formalizing this needs the recursive pile-match invariant (the open step above).

### Agnostic vs dyadic-aware (the dispatch's question)

The recursion CAN be agnostic — a "cap ≤ f(n) for all P" proof does not need to know P is dyadic. Engine R-dyadic-comparison is fully agnostic (the dyadic-equality characterization is a bonus, not a hypothesis). Engine R-pile can also be made agnostic: it caps for all P, with equality falling out exactly at the dyadic (where the greedy pile-match reproduces the pair-pile). Engine R-self-similar is the LEAST agnostic (it explicitly uses dyadic self-similarity) and is therefore the riskiest for G2. Recommendation: prioritize R-pile and R-dyadic-comparison; treat R-self-similar as a fallback that inherits the k≥2 (G1) obstruction.
