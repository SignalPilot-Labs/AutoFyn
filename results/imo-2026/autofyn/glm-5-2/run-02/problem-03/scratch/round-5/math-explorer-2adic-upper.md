## imo-2026-03

**Lens:** a 2-adic-valuation / halving-step framing of the upper bound G2 — far from the equality-case classification route and the dead R-pile / A-pile engines. The conjectured answer `c(n) = 2^n/(2^{n+1}−1)` is tight ONLY at the dyadic config; this route asks whether the upper bound can be framed as "the dyadic is the unique fixed point of a halving operator, and the cap is uniquely maximized there."

- **Distinct openings (each a different attack the outliner could build into a rival approach):**
  1. **Halving-defect fixed-point framing.** Define Φ(config) = Σ_i |p_i − 2 p_{i+1}| over sorted-desc consecutive pieces (the "halving defect": zero iff every consecutive ratio is exactly 2:1). PROVED: Φ = 0 iff the config IS the order-n dyadic (uniqueness: a=2b=4c=…=2^n p_{n+1}, sum=1 ⟹ p_{n+1}=1/D(n), unique). G2 is then equivalent to "Φ(config)=0 ⟹ cap = α(n) (pair-pile, CERTIFIED); Φ>0 ⟹ cap < α(n) strictly." This is the clean qualitative restatement of regime-N as a fixed-point/uniqueness-of-max theorem. Numerically VERIFIED: 47/47 non-dyadic n=3 configs have cap < α(3); the dyadic has cap = α(3).
  2. **Isolated-strict-max / downward-kink framing.** Near-dyadic perturbation sweep (G=200 grid + targeted midpoint/sliver strategies): perturb ONE halving level by magnitude |e| (e.g. (8+e, 4−e, 2, 1)/15 or (8, 4, 2+e, 1−e)/15). The cap drops STRICTLY below α(3) for every e≠0, linearly: level-1 e>0 gives cap·15 = 1−e; level-1 e<0 gives cap·15 = 1−2|e|; level-3 similarly. The dyadic is an ISOLATED strict global maximum with a downward kink (asymmetric slopes). This corroborates the cell-complex round-4 finding (dyadic = strict local max of Φ with downward kink) and lifts it from local to global: the kink is the signature of the 2-adic "+1 boundary" (D(n+1)=2D(n)+1) being uniquely realizable at the dyadic.
  3. **Halving-level-targeting sliver strategy (existence sketch).** For n=3 with 3 halving levels (level 1: a vs 2b; level 2: b vs 2c; level 3: c vs 2d), if ANY level is "broken" (ratio ≠ 2:1), Xiang targets that level: place a sliver cut that converts the would-be equal pair into (sliver, complement-with-defect), where the defect |p_i − 2 p_{i+1}| is exactly the sliver magnitude that pushes the residual odd-multiplicity leftover strictly below α(3). This gives a FINITE 3-way disjunction (which level is broken) — a cleaner case split than the structural-class classification (balanced / two-dyadic / extreme-dominant / moderate-dominant), because the disjunction is by halving-level index, not by ad-hoc structural class.
  4. **Induction on the halving depth (crux aimo-0093 analog).** The dyadic recursion D(n+1) = 2 D(n) + 1 is the "exact halving + irreducible +1" structure (the +1 is the certified odd-multiplicity leftover at the smallest dyadic piece). Inductive framing: at level n+1, IF the config is dyadic (M = 2·R_largest, R itself dyadic), the pair-pile achieves A = α(n+1) (CERTIFIED). IF the config is non-dyadic, EITHER the top-level ratio M/R_largest ≠ 2 (top-level broken → sliver at the top forces cap < α(n+1) directly) OR M = 2·R_largest but R is non-dyadic (recurse on R, which is a level-n sub-problem). This is a genuine induction on the halving depth — the "rougher" configs (broken at a higher level) admit a strictly smaller cap via a top-level sliver, and "smoother" configs (broken deeper) reduce to a lower-level sub-problem. (Caveat: the per-round value recursion `1/f(n+1)=1+1/(2f(n))` is a verified IDENTITY but was conceded dead as a BYPASS; the 2-adic induction is on the CONFIG STRUCTURE, not on the round-level value — different object.)
  5. **"Dyadic-rounding artifact" warning (negative result for grid evidence).** Coarse-grid search (G=40) reports cap = α(3) for near-dyadic perturbations |e| ≤ 0.1 — this is a GRID ARTIFACT, not a real fact. The grid can't distinguish a near-dyadic config from the dyadic when its resolution exceeds |e|. Fine search (G=200 + midpoint/sliver candidates) shows the real cap drops strictly. IMPLICATION: any grid-only regime-N "confirmation" is weaker than it appears for near-dyadic configs; the real-valued proof must NOT rely on grid evidence near the dyadic. (This sharpens the grid-parity lemma's caveat — the grid gives the BOUNDARY α(n) at the dyadic but is blind to the strict-decrease region just off it.)
