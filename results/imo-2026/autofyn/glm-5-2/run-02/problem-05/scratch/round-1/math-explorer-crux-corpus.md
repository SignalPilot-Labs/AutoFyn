## imo-2026-05 — crux-corpus route terrain report

### Route summary
Problem: determine all `f:R_{>0}→R_{>0}` with `√((x²+f(y)²)/2) ≥ (f(x)+y)/2 ≥ √(x f(y))`. It is a **two-sided QM-AM / AM-GM sandwich over an unknown function**: the outer pair `(x, f(y))` has its QM ≥ (f(x)+y)/2 ≥ GM. Seed (orchestrator) + my probes confirm: **family `f(x)=x+c, c≥0`** works (both inequalities collapse to universal AM-GM / QM-AM on `(x, f(y))`); **any periodic perturbation `x+c+ε·(periodic)` fails both sides** (numerically, violation ≈ ε amplitude — conjectural, not proof). Open gap = **uniqueness**: show `g:=f−id` is constant. Established (conjectural from seed): setting `x=f(y)` forces both inequalities to equality → `f(f(y))=2f(y)−y`, i.e. `g(f(y))=g(y)`, so `g` is constant on each forward orbit and every orbit is the arithmetic progression `y, y+g(y), y+2g(y), …`; positivity of the deep iterate `f^n(y)=y+ng(y)` forces `g≥0`. The **iterate-relation `⇒` translation** step is settled; the missing step is **`g` constant across distinct orbits**.

I mined `past_crux_moves_database.json` (algebra domain; subtopics `functional-equations` + `inequalities-SOS-and-convexity`, 263 + many cruxes). The relevant uniqueness-argument *shapes* are (ranked by adaptability):

### Top adaptable cruxes

1. **aimo-0710** (Belgium) — `f:R_{>0}→R_{>0}`, `x(f(x)+f(y)) ≥ (f(f(x))+y)f(y)`, determine all. **Structurally the closest problem in the corpus**: same domain `R_{>0}→R_{>0}`, same "determine all functions under an inequality involving `f(f(·))`" shape. Cruxes (verbatim `technique`):
   - *"Iterate a one-step substitution along the orbit of the unknown function so a single inequality becomes a shift-invariant relation between consecutive iterate-gaps"* — **direct adaptation**: substitute `(x,y)=(f^n(y), ·)` along the orbit `y, y+g, y+2g, …` to get a shift-invariant gap relation.
   - *"Telescope a chain of iterate-gaps into a bound linear in the step count, then pit it against a fixed bound to force the base gap to vanish"* — **the load-bearing move**: telescope over `m` to get a bound `~ m·δ`; pit against a fixed bound (in aimo-0710: positivity `y > f^{2m}(y)`). In P5 the AP orbit grows, so the "fixed bound" must come instead from the **GM side** of the sandwich at an off-diagonal substitution (the diagonal `x=f^n(y)` is equality — useless); the move *shape* (telescope + pit) is what to borrow, not the literal positivity bound.
   - *"Feed the derived involution back into the original inequality to collapse it into a symmetric two-variable inequality, then swap the variables to force a product to be constant"* — **adaptation**: feed `f(f(y))=2f(y)−y` back; swap `(x,y)` in the squared form of one inequality and equate to force `g(x)=g(y)` (the analogue of "product constant").

2. **aimo-0234** — `f(xy+f(x))=xf(y)+2` over `R_{>0}`; conclusion `f(x)=x+1`. Crux (verbatim): *"Substitute an approximate closed form (exact leading term plus a bounded error) back into the original relation, then send a free variable to infinity so the bounded error is negligible and the coefficient it multiplies is forced to vanish, upgrading the approximation to an exact identity."* **Adaptation**: write `f(x)=x+c(x)` with `c` to be shown constant; plug into the squared form of, say, the right inequality `(x+c(x)+y)² ≥ 4x(y+c(y))`; collect terms; send `x→∞` (or `y→∞`) with the other variable fixed so every term except the one carrying `c(x)−c(∞)` is dominated — the surviving coefficient must vanish, forcing `c(x)` equal to its limiting value. This is the **"kill the bounded-error coefficient by `x→∞`"** move and is the most directly portable uniqueness lever for P5.

3. **aimo-0008** (Bulgaria) — superadditive + multiplicative inequality, `f(a)=a>1 ⇒ f=x`. Cruxes (verbatim): *"Amplify a lossy additive bound `f(x)>x−c` by feeding a power of the argument through the same bound and taking the n-th root, so the constant error becomes negligible as n grows"* and *"Convert a one-sided bound into equality by sandwiching against a known exact value at a large point, splitting that point additively and letting the superadditive inequality force each summand to be tight."* **Adaptation**: "feed a power" → here "feed an iterate" (`f^n(y)=y+ng(y)`); the constant error `g(y)−c` is amplified linearly in `n` along the orbit while any deviation from constancy would force a sub- or super-linear growth that contradicts the GM bound. The "sandwich at a known exact point" move maps to the **diagonal equality case `x=f(y)`** as the anchor.

4. **aimo-0290** (Netherlands, `f:Z→Z`) — `X_v={f(x)=v}` fibres, ≤1 rare. Crux (verbatim): *"Force a function to be constant on a nondegenerate arithmetic progression by squeezing between a matched pair of one-sided inequalities, each obtained by substituting so that a value lands in an extremal (min/max) fibre element."* **Adaptation**: in P5, `g` is constant on each orbit-AP (= "fibre"); to upgrade to global constancy, pick two points `x_1, x_2` where `g` attains extremal values and run the original inequality at `(x_1,x_2)` and `(x_2,x_1)` — two opposite one-sided bounds that collapse to `g(x_1)=g(x_2)`. Also: *"When two extremal choices give opposite one-sided bounds along two arithmetic progressions through the same point, step by a common multiple of both common differences so both bounds apply simultaneously and collapse into exact invariance"* — portable verbatim to the orbit-AP setting of P5.

5. **aimo-0787** (Bulgaria, `f(m+n)≥f(m)+f(f(n))−1`) — crux (verbatim): *"Evaluate the inequality at the argument that maximizes a chosen gap functional so the same maximum reappears amplified, forcing the gap to collapse."* **Adaptation**: maximum-principle route — define gap `h(x)=g(x)−c*` (deviation from a candidate constant); let `x_0` maximize `|h|`; feed `x_0` through `f`'s iterate relation so `|h(f(x_0))|=|h(x_0)|` reappears, but the original inequality at `(x_0, ·)` amplifies it → forces `h≡0`. Works only if a maximizer exists (P5 needs an extremal-value-theorem argument on a bounded interval — non-trivial).

6. **aimo-0331** (Switzerland) — crux (verbatim): *"Introduce an auxiliary product `g(x)=x f(x)` so the equation's iterate becomes a clean self-map power law"* and *"Feed a newly proven regularity property back into the original equation to split a composite argument and solve for the iterate `f(f(x))`."* **Adaptation**: our auxiliary is **`g(x)=f(x)−x`** (additive, not multiplicative); the iterate becomes the clean translation `f^n(y)=y+n g(y)`. Confirms the auxiliary-function reduction is the right first step; the "split composite argument to solve for `f(f(x))`" move is exactly the diagonal substitution `x=f(y)`.

### Relevant knowledge_base entries (named)
- **Standard inequalities: AM-GM, Cauchy-Schwarz, QM-AM, Schur. Equality cases pin down the extremal configuration.** — directly: equality in QM-AM and AM-GM on the pair `(x, f(y))` both at `x=f(y)` → derives the iterate relation `f(f(y))=2f(y)−y`. The equality-case analysis is the engine of the whole problem.
- **Functional equations: test special values, check injectivity/surjectivity.** — gives injectivity of `f` from `f(f(y))=2f(y)−y` (if `f(y_1)=f(y_2)`, apply `f`, use the relation, get `y_1=y_2`). Surjectivity is *not* available — the orbit AP is unbounded above and `f` need not be onto; do not assume it.
- **Sum of squares (SOS) / completing the square.** — both squared inequalities rewrite as SOS / PSD quadratic forms in `(x−f(y))`, `(x−y)`, `g(x)`, `g(y)`. The diagonal-equality reduction and the leftover constraints after substitution are all quadratic-form analysis; this is the algebraic engine of the uniqueness step.

### Borrowable uniqueness-argument shapes (the three serious openings for the outliner)
- **Shape A — telescoped orbit-gap vs. a fixed bound (aimo-0710).** Iterate the substitution along the orbit, telescope the iterate-gaps to a bound linear in `m`, pit against a *fixed* bound coming from the GM side at a carefully chosen off-diagonal substitution. Works if the off-diagonal GM bound is genuinely `O(1)` while the telescoped deviation is `Ω(m)`. The literal aimo-0710 positivity bound does NOT carry over (orbit grows, not shrinks) — the GM side must supply the fixed bound. **Risky but structurally exact.**
- **Shape B — closed-form-with-error + `x→∞` kills the error (aimo-0234).** Write `f(x)=x+c(x)`, substitute into the squared inequality, send one variable to infinity so the `c(x)−c(∞)` term is the only leading coefficient → must vanish. **Most direct; the outliner should make this the primary route.** The crux subtlety: which variable to send to infinity and which squared inequality to use so the deviation survives as the leading term rather than being swallowed by the `x²` term.
- **Shape C — two-extremal-fibres squeeze (aimo-0290 + aimo-0787).** Take two points where `g` attains extremal values; the original inequality at `(x_1,x_2)` and the swapped pair gives opposite one-sided bounds → `g(x_1)=g(x_2)`. Requires a maximizer existence argument (extreme-value theorem on a bounded interval, then transport via orbit-AP) — adds a layer, but the squeeze shape is clean.

### Dead ends / non-adaptable moves
- **aimo-0710's literal involution step (`f²(y)=y`)** does **not** carry over: here `f²(y)=2f(y)−y` is a translation, the orbit is an unbounded AP, so the bound `y > m·(y−f^{2m}(y))` has no analogue. Borrow the **telescope-then-pit shape**, not the positivity bound.
- **aimo-0008's multiplicative-power amplification `f(x)^n ≥ f(x^n)`** relies on multiplicativity of `f`; `f(x)=x+c` is additive, so there is no clean `x^n` route. The "feed an iterate" substitute works; the multiplicative-power lift does **not**.
- **aimo-0255** (BLR existence-of-bad-point) is a *negation/contradiction-by-∞* argument on an arbitrary `f`; its top-level structure (prove ∃ bad `x,y`) is **not** the "determine all" shape of P5. Only its *"drive a free variable to infinity against a linearly-decaying upper bound"* sub-move is portable, and that is already covered (better) by aimo-0234.
- **Surjectivity-based arguments generally**: `f:R_{>0}→R_{>0}` need not be onto (the family `x+c, c>0` has range `(c,∞)`), so any move that assumes `f` is bijective (e.g. aimo-0010's residue-class counting) is unavailable.
- **The diagonal / symmetric substitutions alone reduce to tautologies** (orchestrator seed confirms). The content is genuinely **asymmetric** — do not build an approach on `x↔y` symmetry; it must use an asymmetric substitution (`x=f(y)`, `x=f^n(y)`, or `x→∞` with `y` fixed).

### Small-case / intuition notes (conjectural, not proved)
- Numeric grid check: `f=x+c` for `c=0,1,5` all satisfy (both inequalities tight at `x=f(y)=y+c`, slack elsewhere); `f=2x` fails the left side badly; `f=x+1+ε·sin(kx)` fails both sides for `ε=0.05,0.3`, violation magnitude ≈ `ε`. **Conjecture**: the solution family is exactly `{x+c : c≥0}` and the deviation of `g` from constancy is what the sandwich forbids — consistent with Shape B/C above.
- The iterate relation `f^n(y)=y+ng(y)` (conjectural-from-seed, trivial induction once `f(f(y))=2f(y)−y` is proved) makes the orbit an AP with common difference `g(y)≥0`; this is the structural fact every uniqueness route must exploit.
- The squared-form expansion (computed, not a proof) of the right inequality reads `(x−y)² + 2(x+y)g(x) + g(x)² ≥ 4x·g(y)` after substituting `f(x)=x+g(x)` — a PSD-flavoured quadratic identity that is the natural substrate for Shape B (`x→∞`).
