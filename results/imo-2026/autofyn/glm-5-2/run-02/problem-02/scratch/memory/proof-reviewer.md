# proof-reviewer rules (per-role memory)

ALWAYS: when a builder claims a polynomial "factorisation" of a coefficient of a *reduced* (mod some ideal) polynomial, re-derive the coefficient independently in sympy with the modulus parameters FREE, and check `et2 - claim` as a polynomial. The reduced coefficient has degree bounded by the reduction; if the claimed factorisation has higher degree in the reduction variable, it CANNOT be an exact identity — it holds only on the curve (difference is a multiple of the modulus). (round 1, imo-2026-02: analytic-branch-cert Lemma 3 claimed exact `et2=(b3/2)|C|2(v-ly)|L-C|2`; true relation was `et2 = claim - b^2*D`, valid on D=0 only. Builder's "sympy factor confirms it" was false.)

ALWAYS: verify the *degree in t* of a claimed quotient `G` in a saturation identity `LHS = D*G` — builders may claim "linear in t" when the actual quotient is higher degree. The identity itself may still hold; the degree/form claim about G is a separate check. (round 1, imo-2026-02: G was degree 2 in t, not linear as claimed; identity still verified.)

NEVER: trust a builder's self-report that "sympy `factor(X)` returns Y" without running `factor(X)` yourself. The reproducibility note is the builder's claim, not ground truth. (round 1, imo-2026-02.)

ALWAYS: when a prior round's reviewer verdict contradicts the current builder's claim, adjudicate the contradiction by recomputing the contested quantity from its DEFINING formula yourself — the prior round's "verified FALSE" may itself be the error. Round 2 imo-2026-02: round 1's saturation-identity "FALSE" verdict was an arithmetic slip (Q evaluated as 256 instead of the correct 320/3 at the alleged counterexample); recomputing Q from its defining formula and re-running true field division flipped the verdict back to TRUE. Don't anchor on the prior verdict. (round 2, imo-2026-02)

ALWAYS: for an "ordinary-angle equality implies directed-angle-equality mod π" step, check empirically (least_squares on the ordinary-angle residuals + inside hypotheses, then evaluate the directed encodings) that the theorem's configs lie on the directed-encoding variety — the implication needs the inside hypotheses for branch selection and the proof may hand-wave it. (round 2, imo-2026-02: analytic-branch-cert claimed it was automatic; verified 0/12 mismatches.)

ALWAYS: when a builder claims a "sequential field-division certificate" with `sp.div` over a `QQ.frac_field` domain, the certificate IS the two divisions (remainder zero) — a redundant `sp.simplify` cross-check that times out or hits domain-unification errors is NOT a gap; do not downgrade for it. The zero remainder from exact field division is self-contained. (round 3, imo-2026-02: antipode §8, the simplify cross-check timed out / failed domain unification, but step-2 remainder is_zero=True IS the certificate.)

ALWAYS: to verify a "nonzero counterexample kills an ideal-containment" claim (builder says crux ∉ ideal⟨B,C⟩), check the Γ-locality of the generators first — if (B) depends on B-side vars only and (C) on C-side vars only, then perturbing one side's variable and re-solving from its local relation keeps that relation satisfied but breaks any coupling crux. Reproduce by: perturb `a`, re-solve `γ` from (B) via `brentq`, evaluate crux. (round 3, imo-2026-02: power-secant Step 9b, crux=-0.0366 reproduced exactly from B-side alone, C-side sign convention immaterial to the counterexample.)

NEVER: block an APPROVE on an "independence" check just because the approach mentions the rival certificate's name — grep for whether it CITES the rival's identity to close vs. explicitly DISCLAIMING dependence. The certificate style (field-division-over-frac_field) is a generic technique, not a problem-specific identity; sharing the style is not circularity. (round 3, imo-2026-02: antipode mentions "saturation identity" 5 times, all in disclaimers.)
