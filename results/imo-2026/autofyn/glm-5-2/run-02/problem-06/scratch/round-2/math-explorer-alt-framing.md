Targets the dispatch's explicit ask — break the round-1 collapse where all 4 approaches share the wall "prove the essential-prime set E is finite" (Lemma 4 of essential-monovariant: every pair of terms shares a prime ≤ R := rad(a_1)).

## Why the field stalled

Round 1 produced four approaches (crude-reduced-type, essential-monovariant, covering-system-redundancy, translation-self-similarity) that the outline-reviewer correctly diagnosed as ONE framing in four vocabularies: stabilize reduced types mod L_0 = ∏_{p ≤ R} p, then prove free-rider primes > R never rescue an invalid candidate. The wall is Lemma 4 (every pair shares a prime ≤ R), which is equivalent to E ⊆ Q_R = {primes ≤ R} (finite) and equivalent to free-rider irrelevance. I scouted ~10 distinct framings (direct greedy-structural, explicit-construction with L = k·rad(a_1), sieve/density, backward/self-similar, mod-rad(a_1)-without-E, gap-sequence periodicity, p-adic valuations, shift-equivariance via the rad(a_1) anchor, minimal-counterexample descent, pair-witness, compactness/König). **The honest finding: every framing that produces a_{n+T} = a_n + L via "a_n + L is admissible" or via finite-state-on-residues reduces to Lemma 4 in some form.** The crux is a genuine structural fact about the sequence, not an artifact of one framing — because the greedy decision depends on the full prime-factorization history, the primes appearing are unbounded (proven: a_{8k+6} = 6(6+5k) for a_1=15), and ANY finite state must show only finitely many primes "matter," which IS E-finiteness. So the escape is not a new finite-state framing; it is a different PROOF ROUTE for Lemma 4 itself, or a non-finite-state engine for periodicity (which I could not find).

## Candidate framings

### A. Direct greedy-structural / growth-rate (NO essential primes named)
**Proves:** periodicity directly from the greedy rule + gap bound, without naming E.
**Load-bearing step:** show the greedy decision at step n depends only on a_n mod L_0 (so finite-state). But admissibility of a candidate m ∈ (a_n, a_n+R] depends on whether m shares a prime with each a_i (i ≤ n); the primes dividing m and a_i are ≤ a_n+R (grows with n). The "next multiple of rad(a_1)" anchor (Lemma 2) gives the gap bound but NOT finite state — the rescue primes are unbounded without E-finiteness.
**Escapes the crux?** NO. The greedy decision depends on the full prime history; bounding the relevant primes to a finite set IS the crux. Same wall as crude-reduced-type.
**Where it breaks:** at "the greedy decision depends only on a_n mod L_0," which is exactly the free-rider wall.

### B. Explicit construction of T, L from the seed block + direct induction
**Proves:** a_{n+T} = a_n + L by induction, with L = (multiple of) L_0 and T = |V_0|.
**Load-bearing step:** the inductive step "a_{n+1} + L is admissible against a_i (i ≤ n+T)." Since L ≡ 0 mod every q ≤ R, a_{n+1}+L has the SAME Q_R-type as a_{n+1} (exact: q | a_{n+1}+L iff q | a_{n+1}). So a_{n+1}+L shares a small prime with a_i IFF a_{n+1} does. By Lemma 4 (crux), a_{n+1} shares a small prime with every a_i. So a_{n+1}+L is admissible — BUT this uses Lemma 4. Minimality of a_{n+1}+L (= smallest admissible > a_{n+T}) ALSO uses free-rider irrelevance (no smaller m with non-transversal type rescued by a large prime).
**Escapes the crux?** NO. The induction step is Lemma 4 in disguise. Verified: with L = L_0, the shift preserves small-type exactly, so admissibility of the shifted candidate reduces to pairwise small-prime intersection = Lemma 4. With smaller L (e.g. L = 2·rad(a_1), the value suggested by numerics for many a_1), the shift does NOT preserve small-type and admissibility is even harder. Note L = 2·rad(a_1) works for a_1 ∈ {15,77,91,105,1001} but FAILS for a_1 ∈ {35, 143} (need factor 3); so L is not a clean function of a_1.
**Where it breaks:** the inductive lift requires "a_{n+1} shares a small prime with every a_i" = Lemma 4.

### C. Sieve / density lower bound on admissible candidates
**Proves:** the admissible set A_n = ∩_{i≤n} ∪_{p|a_i} pZ stabilizes mod a fixed L; the residue walk is eventually periodic.
**Load-bearing step:** "late primes are redundant mod L" — a late prime q > R co-occurs with a small essential prime in every term, so its constraint adds nothing. This is the free-rider dichotomy = Lemma 4 = the crux.
**Escapes the crux?** NO. This is the covering-system-redundancy framing, which the reviewer already killed (too close to crude-reduced-type; same wall, different vocabulary).
**Where it breaks:** "late primes are redundant" requires proving late primes always co-occur with small essential primes, which is exactly free-rider irrelevance.

### D. Backward / self-similar (translation symmetry as functional equation)
**Proves:** exhibit T, L with A_{T+n} = L + A_n (allowed sets are translates); induct via min-preservation.
**Load-bearing step:** the lift. The reviewer already showed the LITERAL mechanism (set-translation) is FALSE (concrete counterexample: a_1=15, T=8, L=30, the candidate 51 = 30+21 lies in 30+A_1 but is coprime to a_3=20, so 51 ∉ A_9). The weakened "min-preservation" reduces to "a_{n+1} hits every earlier a_i via an essential prime (free-rider dichotomy), and L = ∏E, so a_{n+1}+L ≡ a_{n+1} mod every essential prime, hence still hits every a_i" — which is the crude/essential wall.
**Escapes the crux?** NO. The reviewer's verdict stands: translation symmetry is a reformulation, not an independent mechanism; the lift reduces to the crux.
**Where it breaks:** min-preservation needs an independent reason the greedy min is preserved under +L; the only viable route is essential-prime preservation = Lemma 4.

### E. mod rad(a_1) finite-state WITHOUT E (the dispatch's framing 5)
**Proves:** reduced types live in the finite powerset of Q_R = {primes ≤ R} (cheap lemma: every a_n has a prime of a_1, ≤ R), so the type family F and transversal family H stabilize FOR FREE (finite lattice), giving a fixed valid-residue set V_0 mod L_0; the residue walk is eventually periodic.
**Load-bearing step:** the free-rider wall — a_{n+1} = min{m > a_n : m mod L_0 ∈ V_0}, i.e. NO smaller m with non-transversal type is rescued by a free-rider prime > R. This is EXACTLY Lemma 4 / E-finiteness.
**Escapes the crux?** NO — and this is the key honest finding. **This framing IS essential-monovariant, not a new framing.** essential-monovariant already uses Q_R = primes ≤ R (finite for free), already has F, H stabilize for free, already has V_0 fixed for free, and already identifies the free-rider wall as Lemma 4. The "without E" branding is misleading: free-rider irrelevance IS E-finiteness in disguise (E ⊆ Q_R iff free-riders > R never rescue iff Lemma 4). **The reviewer should CUT any approach labeled "mod rad(a_1) without E" as a relabel of essential-monovariant.** I verified computationally: the stabilized type family for a_1=15 includes ALL primes ≤ 15 ({2,3,5,7,11,13}), not just the essential {2,3,5} — so the crude modulus L_0 is unavoidable for the finite-state argument, and the wall is unavoidable.

### F. Minimal-counterexample descent + CRT-coprime-shift (a DIFFERENT PROOF OF THE CRUX, not a new whole-problem framing)
**Proves:** Lemma 4 (and hence the whole theorem, since essential-monovariant's Theorem is conditional on Lemma 4).
**Load-bearing step:** Suppose Lemma 4 fails; let (i_0, j_0) be the lexicographically minimal failure (smallest j_0, then i_0). Then i_0 ≤ j_0−2 (consecutive pairs are Lemma 3). By minimality, a_{i_0} shares a small prime with every a_k (k < j_0). Consider a_{i_0} + k·L_0: (a) small-type = τ(a_{i_0}) EXACTLY (q | a_{i_0}+k·L_0 iff q | a_{i_0} for q ≤ R, since L_0 ≡ 0 mod q); (b) admissible at stage j_0−1 (shares a small prime with every a_k, k < j_0, by minimality); (c) shares NO small prime with a_{j_0} (since τ(a_{i_0}) ∩ τ(a_{j_0}) = ∅ by failure — small primes AUTOMATICALLY avoided); (d) the large primes of a_{j_0} are a finite set P_large; for each p ∈ P_large, p | a_{i_0}+k·L_0 forbids ONE residue class of k mod p (since gcd(L_0, p)=1 as p > R). By pigeonhole, a good k ≤ |P_large|+1 ≤ O(log a_{j_0}) exists avoiding all large primes. For such k, a_{i_0}+k·L_0 is admissible at stage j_0−1, coprime to a_{j_0}, and > a_{j_0−1} (since L_0 is huge). Greedy minimality forces a_{j_0} < a_{i_0}+k·L_0.
**Escapes the crux?** It does NOT escape the crux (it IS a proof of the crux), but it is a GENUINELY DIFFERENT PROOF ROUTE from min-of-set monovariant (essential-monovariant) and frozen-prefix bound (crude-reduced-type). It attacks Lemma 4 by descent + CRT, not by monovariant or by transversal-family nesting.
**Where it breaks:** the bound a_{j_0} < a_{i_0} + O(log a_{j_0})·L_0, with a_{i_0} < a_{j_0}, gives a_{j_0} = O(L_0 log L_0) — a FINITE bound on where the first failure can occur (j_0 ≤ O(L_0 log L_0 / R)), reducing Lemma 4 to a finite (astronomical, ~10^43 for a_1=105) computation. **This is a finite reduction, NOT a contradiction.** It does not close Lemma 4 by itself. To close, it needs a SECOND independent constraint conflicting with the bound, or a structural argument for small cases. I could not find the second constraint in this round. But the descent + CRT-coprime-shift is a load-bearing mechanism the other approaches do NOT use, and it verifies cleanly on a synthetic failure (a_i = 2·3·107, a_j = 5·7·107: k=1 makes a_i+L_0 coprime to a_j with small-type exactly preserved).
**Conjecture (labeled as such):** the bound can likely be tightened by replacing L_0 with a smaller modulus M ⊇ τ(a_{i_0}) ∪ τ(a_{j_0}) (still preserving the automatic small-prime avoidance), shrinking the finite search. This is a builder's task.

### G. Pair-witness / witness construction (refined)
**Proves:** Lemma 4 by exhibiting, for each pair of essential primes (p,q), a term with small-type exactly {p,q}, forcing candidates to hit {p,q}.
**Escapes the crux?** NO. I verified the pair-witness structure EXISTS (for a_1=15: pair-witnesses for {2,3}, {2,5}, {3,5}; for a_1=35: {2,5}, {3,5}, {5,7}; for a_1=77: {2,7}, {2,11}, {7,11}). BUT a candidate missing both p and q could still be rescued by a large prime — the pair-witness doesn't forbid that. So pair-witnesses alone don't prove Lemma 4.
**Where it breaks:** large-prime rescue is not ruled out by pair-witnesses. Combined with framing F's coprime-shift it might help, but not standalone.

### H. Witness (SINGLETON-type) construction — DEAD
The experimental explorer's opening D assumed witnesses with singleton small-type {p}. I verified computationally: **NO term ever has singleton small-type** (for a_1=105 over 400 terms, every term carries ≥2 small primes, always from {{2,3}, {2,5}, {2,7}}). Reason: a term with singleton small-type {p} would be m = p·(large); for m to be admissible against the odd a_1=105 (=3·5·7), m must share a prime with 105, but p·(large>105) is coprime to 105 unless large | 105 (impossible since large > R ≥ 105). So singleton-type terms are INADMISSIBLE. **Do not field this.**

## Which (if any) genuinely escape the crux

**None fully escape.** Every finite-state or translation-induction framing reduces to Lemma 4 (free-rider irrelevance = E ⊆ Q_R). The crux is a real structural fact, not an artifact of one framing. The escape the dispatch hoped for — a framing that reaches periodicity WITHOUT proving E-finiteness — does not exist in the finite-state paradigm, because the greedy decision depends on the full prime history and the primes are unbounded; ANY finite state must prove only finitely many primes matter, which IS E-finiteness.

What I CAN offer is **framing F** (minimal-counterexample descent + CRT-coprime-shift): a genuinely different PROOF ROUTE for the crux itself, distinct from min-of-set monovariant (essential-monovariant) and frozen-prefix bound (crude-reduced-type). It does not escape the crux but attacks it by a new mechanism (descent + CRT-coprime-shift + finite-bound reduction). It is the only framing I found that is NOT a relabel of "stabilize reduced types mod ∏E + free-rider dichotomy."
