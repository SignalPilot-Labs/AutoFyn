### 1. `aimo-0030` — IMO-SL 2013 N5 (Ana–Banana coprime-move game) — **the strongest shape-match for Lemma 4**
- Domain/subtopic: `number_theory` / `divisibility-and-gcd` (+ `size-bounding-and-descent`).
- Statement (abbreviated): two players alternate replacing m by a coprime m′ with k ≤ m′ < m; an integer is "good" iff Banana (player 2) wins from it. Prove two numbers with the same prime signature below k are both good or both bad.
- **Crux move (the load-bearing one):**
  > "Strengthen a 'two special objects share some forbidden-class prime' statement to a 'they share an allowed-class prime' statement by minimal-counterexample descent: take the smallest violating pair and use a legal move out of an auxiliary blocked object to manufacture a strictly smaller violating pair."
- **how_used (the descent skeleton):** Good positions are pairwise non-coprime (no legal move connects two P-positions; legal moves require coprimality), so any two good numbers share *some* prime. To show they share a *small* prime ≤ k: assume a minimal violating pair (b, b′), b′ ≥ b, sharing only big primes > k. Since k is good and shares a prime with b, and k has only small primes, that shared prime is small — call it p. Construct the **stripped auxiliary** x = (product of b's small-prime factors) × p^n with least n ≥ 0 making x ≥ k; then x ≤ b (via the comparison "x < p·k ≤ a·q ≤ b" where a is b's small part and q is b's big prime), x has exactly b's small primes and no big primes, so x is coprime to b′ (b′ shares only big primes with b). x coprime-to-good ⇒ x is bad ⇒ x has a legal move to some good b* < x. Now b* is good and shares no small prime with b (b's small primes all divide x, hence miss the coprime-to-x b*); so (b*, b) is a smaller violating pair — contradiction.
- **Adaptation to Lemma 4:** Replace "good position" with "term a_i of the greedy sequence." Two terms share a prime (greedy). Want: they share a small prime ≤ R. Take a minimal violating pair (a_i, a_j), i < j, sharing only primes > R; the "stripped auxiliary" is x = (rad of a_i's small part) × (least power of one small prime) ≥ a_1 with x ≤ a_i and x carrying exactly a_i's small primes (no big primes), so x is coprime to a_j. The non-direct step: in our problem "coprime to a_j" does NOT immediately make x "bad" (≠ "is not a term") the way it does in aimo-0030 — our greedy is a forward-generation rule, not a two-player impartial game with a "bad ⇒ move to good" property. **The crux to port is the descent skeleton, not the game rule.** The right adaptation: use the greedy minimality of a_j itself — a_j is the *least* integer > a_{j-1} hitting every earlier a_k. The stripped x (≤ a_i ≤ a_{j-1}, hitting a_i via a small prime but coprime to a_j) lies in the candidate range of some earlier greedy step; if x were admissible at step j-1 it would have been chosen before a_j (it is < a_j), contradicting a_j's minimality — provided x is admissible at step j-1. The load-bearing sub-question: is the stripped x (carrying a_i's small-prime signature, no big primes) admissible against *all* a_1, …, a_{j-1}? It hits a_i via a small prime, but does it hit a_k for k ≠ i? Not automatic. **This is exactly the "free-rider co-occurrence" obstruction** recognized in round 1. aimo-0030's hint is to make x's small-prime signature a *transversal* of the seen small-prime types — i.e. arm x with enough small primes to hit every earlier term, then x < a_j and admissible ⇒ a_j not minimal ⇒ contradiction. That transversal construction is the lever to try.
- **Sibling shape:** the "minimal violating pair + stripped auxiliary + produce a smaller violating pair" descent is the move; aimo-0030 is its cleanest instance in the corpus.

### 2. `aimo-0678` — IMO-SL 2015 N4 (gcd/lcm recurrence) — **the direct periodicity sibling; already adapted in round 1**
- Domain/subtopic: `number_theory` / `divisibility-and-gcd` (+ `size-bounding-and-descent`, `modular-arithmetic-and-CRT`).
- Statement: a_{n+1}=gcd(a_n,b_n)+1, b_{n+1}=lcm(a_n,b_n)−1; prove (a_n) eventually periodic.
- **Three crux moves** (the field already adapted (b) and (c); the unexploited one is (a)):
  - **(a)** *Regime isolation + invariant sum.* "Form the sum of the two coupled sequences as a candidate invariant, and isolate the regime where the recurrence simplifies so the sum is exactly conserved there." s_n = a_n + b_n is frozen during the divisibility regime a_n | b_n, giving a fixed target to compare a_n against.
  - **(b)** *Min-of-set monovariant.* "Construct a min-of-a-set integer monovariant — the least integer ≥ the current value that fails to divide the frozen invariant — and prove it never increases, forcing boundedness." (Adapted → Lemma 2: gap bound a_{n+1}−a_n ≤ rad(a_1), via the next-multiple-of-rad(a_1) candidate.)
  - **(c)** *Finite-state mod lcm.* "Once one coordinate is bounded, reduce the other modulo the lcm of the bounded coordinate's attainable values." (Adapted → Section 5 of essential-monovariant: residue walk mod L_0 = ∏_{p≤R} p.)
- **Adaptation of the unexploited crux (a):** aimo-0678's (a) finds a regime where the recurrence *simplifies enough to conserve an invariant exactly*. For us, the analogous regime is "after every essential prime has appeared, the residue a_n mod L_0 is constrained to the transversal set V, and the greedy becomes the next-V-residue map." But that *is* the conclusion of Lemma 4, so this regime-invariant route is somewhat circular as a route to Lemma 4. **The genuine unexploited lever from (a) is the "frozen invariant target" trick:** in aimo-0678 the frozen s_n = a_n + b_n is the *fixed quantity* that the descent monovariant compares against. In our problem the analog is the fixed R = rad(a_1) — already the comparison target of Lemma 2. **The new move to try:** find a *second* frozen invariant (beyond R) that the descent can compare against — e.g. the product C of all primes that have appeared in the first K terms (a frozen, finite quantity), and bound the next-term residue against it. This is essentially the crude-reduced-type "frozen-prefix bound" route — so the (a) lever, ported faithfully, points back to the frozen-prefix argument the outline-reviewer flagged as the cleaner sub-route of crude.

### 3. `aimo-0648` — USA_TSTST 2011 8 (floor-average recurrence → eventually constant) — **the "extremal forces arguments equal" move**
- Domain/subtopic: `algebra` / `sequences-and-recurrences` (+ `invariants-and-monovariants`).
- Statement: x_n = ⌊(x_{n−d_1}+…+x_{n−d_k})/k⌋ with gcd(d_i)=1; prove eventually constant.
- **Three crux moves:**
  - **(i) Order-statistic preserved → bounded → eventually periodic.** "Show an order statistic (max/min) of the terms is preserved by the recurrence to confine the sequence to a bounded interval, forcing eventual periodicity of an integer sequence." A bounded integer sequence whose next term is a deterministic function of finitely many predecessors takes finitely many state-vectors ⇒ eventually periodic. — **Already adapted** (gap bound ⇒ bounded growth ⇒ finite-state mod L_0).
  - **(ii) Equality case of floored mean = max ⇒ every argument equals the extreme.** "If x_n = ⌊mean of predecessors⌋ = M (the maximum), then the sum of the k predecessors is ≥ kM while each predecessor is ≤ M, so every predecessor equals M." — The *unexploited* move.
  - **(iii) Bezout combination of step sizes mod period propagates "all terms = M" to every index.** "Choosing integers c_i with Σ c_i d_i ≡ 1 (mod T) composes index-shifts by d_i into a shift by 1, so x_n = M ⇒ x_{n−1} = M, forcing M everywhere."
- **Adaptation to Lemma 4:** move (ii) is the "extremal-forces-equality" pattern. In our setting, the extremal object is the *minimal* admissible integer a_{n+1}. The analogous lever: if a_{n+1} is the minimum admissible and it hits a_i only via a large prime p > R, then... every "cheaper" candidate hitting a_i via a small prime must fail admissibility against some other term. The aimo-0648 (ii) move would say: the *reason* all cheaper candidates fail must force *every* term to be hit by a_{n+1} via a large prime — but that's impossible by Lemma 3 (consecutive terms share only small primes). So at least one term (a_{j-1}) is hit by a small prime, giving a "seed" of small-prime sharing; then aimo-0648 (iii)'s Bezout-style propagation could try to spread this seed to non-consecutive pairs. **This is a genuinely different route** from the descent of aimo-0030 — it's a "propagation from a consecutive seed" rather than a "minimal-counterexample descent." Worth a builder slot.
- **For the periodicity target:** moves (i)+(iii) directly realize the "cyclic permutation ⇒ no-transient" structure the outline-reviewer asked for; already implicit in essential-monovariant Section 5.

### 4. `aimo-0447` — USAMO 2014 6 (gcd>1 for every pair of shifts ⇒ min{a,b} > (cn)^{n/2}) — **the grid-covering counting route**
- Domain/subtopic: `number_theory` / `divisibility-and-gcd` (+ `size-bounding-and-descent`).
- Statement: if gcd(a+i, b+j) > 1 for all 0 ≤ i, j ≤ n then min{a,b} > (cn)^{n/2}.
- **Crux moves:**
  - Form an (n+1)×(n+1) grid; in cell (i,j) place a prime dividing gcd(a+i, b+j); the whole grid is covered by primes.
  - **Bound cells per prime by a rectangle:** a prime p covers ≤ ⌈N/p⌉² cells (it divides ≤ ⌈N/p⌉ values of a+i and ≤ ⌈N/p⌉ values of b+j). Sum over small primes: N²Σ(1/p²) + 2NΣ(1/p) + Σ1 < ½N² (via Σ1/p² < ½ + PNT for lower-order terms), so small primes fill < ½ the grid; > ½N² cells hold large primes.
  - **Large prime > interval length divides at most one element of the interval** (it divides their difference, which is < p in absolute value). So along a single column, the large primes are distinct; their product divides a+i, forcing a+i ≥ (large-prime)^{N/2} > (cn)^{n/2}.
- **Adaptation to Lemma 4:** form the (i, j) grid of *shared* primes P(a_i) ∩ P(a_j). The bound on cells-per-prime ports directly: a prime p divides a_i for ≤ ⌈(span)/p⌉ + 1 of the first N terms (the values a_1, …, a_N lie in an interval of length ≤ (N−1)·R by the gap bound). So p covers ≤ (⌈(N−1)R/p⌉ + 1)² cells. **Key lemma "large prime > span divides at most one term":** if p > a_N − a_1 then p divides at most one of a_1, …, a_N, so p covers at most 1 cell in *every* row — i.e. p is the unique shared prime of at most... (count pairs: a large prime dividing one term is the shared prime of pairs (that term, a partner also divisible by p); but p divides only one term in the window, so p covers ZERO cells). So for the first N terms, *every* essential prime p satisfies p ≤ (N−1)·R. This does not give E finite (N is unbounded), but it gives a *counting scaffold*: among pairs of the first N terms, the cells uniquely covered by large primes are bounded in number, and small primes must cover the rest. **This is the counting-combinatorics route to Lemma 4** — distinct from the descent of aimo-0030 and the propagation of aimo-0648. It's closest in spirit to aimo-0857's "every length-m window contains a blue number" density lemma. Worth flagging as a third framing the outliner can field.

### 5. `aimo-0857` — RMM 2012 3 (every length-m window contains a blue number) — **density / Jacobsthal-style covering**
- Domain/subtopic: `combinatorics` / `coloring-and-parity` (+ `invariants-and-monovariants`, `extremal-principle`, `double-counting`).
- Crux move: "Force a fixed color into every window of a chosen length by expanding one additive quantity two different ways and equating" — assume a whole length-m = xy segment is monochromatic red; expand f(k+xy) along steps of x and along steps of y; equality forces y·f(x) = x·f(y), i.e. f(x)/x = f(y)/y, contradicting the choice of two reds with distinct ratios. Hence every length-m window contains a blue number. Then a straddle + monotonicity gives the uniform gap bound.
- **Adaptation to Lemma 4 / the gap bound (already partly used):** the "every length-m window contains [admissible]" is the gap bound's dual. For Lemma 4, the analogous move: among any window of K consecutive *candidate* integers in (a_n, a_n + K·R), at least one is admissible (the next multiple of R). Already exploited. **New angle:** the two-way expansion trick (expand f(k+xy) along coprime step lengths x, y and equate) is the move that *forces* density without naming Jacobsthal — it could prove a stronger density statement: "among any K consecutive integers above a_n, the admissible ones are not just present but *frequent enough* to force small-prime sharing." Speculative; flag as a possible lever, not a ready-made adaptation.

### 6. `aimo-0421` — Germany TST 2022 1 (pairwise gcd structure on infinite set) — **the sparse/non-sparse prime dichotomy**
- Domain/subtopic: `number_theory` / `divisibility-and-gcd`.
- Crux moves: (i) "gcd of a fixed element with a varying one is always a divisor of that fixed element, so over an infinite family pigeonhole gives an infinite subfamily with the same gcd"; (ii) "shared-prime-not-in-third: b, c share prime p, a avoids p ⇒ gcd(b,c) ≠ gcd(a,b)"; (iii) "When every prime divides only finitely many elements of an infinite set, only finitely many share a factor with a fixed pair, so a third element coprime to both can be chosen."
- **Adaptation to Lemma 4:** the dichotomy of crux (iii) — "either some prime divides infinitely many elements, OR every prime divides only finitely many" — applies to our sequence: by Lemma 1, every term is divisible by *some* prime of a_1, so among the finitely many primes of a_1, at least one (call it p_*) divides infinitely many terms. But Lemma 4 needs *every pair* to share a small prime, not just that one small prime is recurrent. The aimo-0421 dichotomy alone is too weak for Lemma 4. **However**, crux (ii)'s "shared-prime-not-in-third" is a useful *obstruction* move: if a_i, a_j share only a big prime p > R, and a_k is a term coprime to p, then a_k must hit both a_i and a_j via *different* primes — this multi-prime demand on a_k is a structural tension the greedy must resolve. Worth flagging as an obstruction-understanding tool, not a proof lever.

### 7. `aimo-0628` — USA_TSTST 2015 3 (M = P closure) — **sparse-prime premultiplication**
- Domain/subtopic: `number_theory` / `modular-arithmetic-and-CRT` (+ `divisibility-and-gcd`, `orders-and-primitive-roots`).
- Crux moves: "Partition the set by residue mod a forbidden modulus and flag the classes that contain only finitely many members (sparse primes); premultiply the sliding term by the product of every 'excluded' prime so that the +1 expression is automatically coprime to all of them, forcing its prime factors into the freely-substitutable class; read off the affine recurrence's closed form as a geometric sum mod p and invoke Fermat to pick an index forcing 0."
- **Adaptation to Lemma 4:** "sparse prime" = a prime dividing only finitely many terms of our sequence. The primes of a_1 are non-sparse (each divides infinitely many terms — actually Lemma 1 only guarantees *some* prime of a_1 is non-sparse; but in fact, since the next multiple of rad(a_1) is always a candidate admissible integer, *every* prime of a_1 divides infinitely many terms). So the sparse/non-sparse split in our problem is: small primes (≤ R) are all non-sparse; large primes (> R) may be sparse or not. aimo-0628's "premultiply by the product of sparse primes" trick could be adapted: construct a candidate = (product of all sparse primes seen so far) × (something) + offset, forcing the candidate's prime factors into the non-sparse (hence small) class. **Speculative** but genuinely different from the descent of aimo-0030; flag as a possible fourth framing.

## Recommendation to outliner

This is a concrete, named, load-bearing move the builder can attempt this round.
