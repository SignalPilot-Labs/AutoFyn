# proof-builder rules (carried across rounds)

ALWAYS: parametrize symmetric configs SYMMETRICALLY. For imo-2026-02, choosing K=BK∩MK (B-vertex + M-midpoint) AND L=CL∩NL (C-vertex + N-midpoint) made both K and L homogeneous degree-1 in a single side length (c and b resp.), which made the incidence constraints LINEAR in (b,c) and collapsed the proof to a single trigonometric divisibility. The outliner's asymmetric choice (L=BL∩CL) gave a messier, all-(b,c)-dependent L. Symmetry of parametrisation is load-bearing. (round 1)

ALWAYS: before launching a multi-minute Groebner/polynomial-division run, FIRST check the degree structure. tan-half-angle polynomialisation inflates degrees (a trig expression of sin(A+α+γ) becomes degree up to 6 in the four tans); `expand_trig` on a ~7000-op expression can hang for minutes. Prefer building expressions directly in half-angle rational form (sin(a+b) via the sum formula on S,C) rather than `expand_trig`+`subs`, and prefer univariate division (one variable at a time) over full multivariate Groebner. (round 1)

NEVER: trust a reviewer claim that a constraint is "linear in (sin γ, cos γ)" without checking — for imo-2026-02 the incidence constraints were degree 4 in tan(γ/2) (degree 2 in sinγ,cosγ after clearing sin(α+γ)). The "linear" intuition held only for the (b,c)-degree, not the angle-degree. (round 1)

NEVER: present a CAS-timed-out identity as solved. For imo-2026-02 the reduction + structure is rigorous but the final trig divisibility H≡0 mod C was only numerically verified; mark partial honestly. (round 1)

ALWAYS: when a configuration has a free-ring ideal-membership "certificate" F = L1·conK + L2·conL for two incidence constraints conK, conL that are LINEAR in (b,c), check whether conK, conL are linearly independent (det ≠ 0) in the free ring. If yes, the membership is VACUOUS — any homogeneous polynomial lies in the ideal of two independent linear forms in 2 variables, and their common zero is only the origin; the multipliers have poles at the consistency locus. This fooled me for ~15 min on imo-2026-02 (round 3): sympy confirmed D=0 in the free ring, but it proved nothing because the real config has the determinant C=0. (round 3, imo-2026-02)
ALWAYS: before fitting "angle X is linear in params (p1,...,pn)", check whether X is actually a function of FEWER params, and whether it's the DIRECTED angle (linear) vs the UNSIGNED angle (could be |linear| = a V-shape, which looks nonlinear). For imo-2026-02 the base angle ∠A'BC = |90°−A−α| (V-shape, nonlinear in α) but the directed ∡(BC,BA') = 90°−A−α (linear, only A,α). Round 2's "large least-squares residual → nonlinear" conclusion was an artifact of over-parameterising with B,C,β,γ (which are tied to A,α by the consistency) plus the absolute-value. (round 3, imo-2026-02)
ALWAYS: to discover the target angle formula in a geometry problem, build a numerical scanner that prints ALL equal-angle clusters (directed, |.|<180) across several configs, then look for clusters that persist and involve the auxiliary point. This immediately surfaced ∠A'BC=∠BCA'=|90−A−α| for imo-2026-02 (round 3), which the pure angle-chase of round 2 had missed.

ALWAYS: for "point lies on perpendicular bisector" goals, prefer proving a DIRECTION/angle identity (a ratio/tangent, hence rational) over a DISTANCE-SQUARED identity (quadratic). For imo-2026-02 the direction identity ∡(BC,BA')=90−A−α gave a clean polynomial divisibility g=C·T with ZERO pseudodivision remainder in the half-angle ring (no radical, no Pythagorean relations for the free angles), while the distance-squared |A'B|²−|A'C|² lived only in the radical ideal and Symbolic Groebner never terminated. Rational identities are far more likely to admit polynomial certificates than quadratic ones. (round 4, imo-2026-02)
ALWAYS: when building the A'=perp(K)∩perp(L) point via "P·K=|K|², P·L=|L|²" (A at origin), the formula is Ax=(Ly·K2−Ky·L2)/det, Ay=(Kx·L2−Lx·K2)/det with det=Kx·Ly−Ky·Lx. Do NOT use (L2·K−K2·L)/det — that is the wrong vector and produces a bogus A' (cost me ~20 min of confused exploration in round 4). (round 4, imo-2026-02)
NEVER: trust equilateral/isosceles configs for discovering synthetic structure — KL∥BC, |AK|=|AL|, BA'⊥AL all appeared as symmetric coincidences and vanished for scalene triangles. Always explore with at least one scalene triangle (b≠c). (round 4, imo-2026-02)
