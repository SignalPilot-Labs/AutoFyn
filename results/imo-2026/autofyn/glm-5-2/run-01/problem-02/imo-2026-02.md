## Current best
The theorem is proved. The reduction (§1) plus the Direction Lemma (§2, proved by an explicit algebraic certificate) plus the B↔C symmetry (§3) give A'∈perp-bis(BC), hence |A'B|=|A'C|, hence OM=ON.

## Full proof

We prove OM = ON.

**Notation.** ∡(ℓ₁,ℓ₂) denotes the directed angle from line ℓ₁ to line ℓ₂ modulo π. Let A=∠BAC, and set
  α := ∠KBA = ∠ACL,   β := ∠LBK = ∠LNC,   γ := ∠LCK = ∠BMK.
All angles are in radians; sines/cosines of angle-arguments are written sin(·), cos(·).

---------------------------------------------------------------------
### §1.  Reduction to |A'B| = |A'C|

Let O be the circumcentre of △AKL and let A' := 2O − A. Since O is the centre of the circle (AKL), A' is the antipode of A on (AKL), so AA' is a diameter; by Thales' theorem
  ∠AKA' = ∠ALA' = 90°,   i.e.   A'K ⊥ AK  and  A'L ⊥ AL.                (R1)
Equivalently A' is the intersection of the line through K perpendicular to AK and the line through L perpendicular to AL. (This characterization uses only the right angles and is how we shall compute A'.)

The homothety h centred at A with ratio 1/2 sends B ↦ M and C ↦ N (M,N are the midpoints of AB,AC). It scales all distances by 1/2 and preserves perpendicularity, hence it maps the perpendicular bisector of BC bijectively onto the perpendicular bisector of MN. Since O ∈ perp-bis(MN) ⇔ OM = ON and h is invertible with h⁻¹(O) = 2O − A = A', we have
  OM = ON  ⇔  A' ∈ perp-bis(BC)  ⇔  |A'B| = |A'C|.                        (R2)
Thus it suffices to prove **|A'B| = |A'C|**.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
### §2.  Direction Lemma:  ∡(BC, BA') = 90° − A − α

We prove the load-bearing identity. The proof is a coordinate computation closed by an explicit polynomial certificate (an exact algebraic identity, not a numerical check).

**Frame and parametrisation.** Place A at the origin, B = (1, 0) (we scale so |AB| = 1), and C = (b cos A, b sin A) with b = |AC|/|AB| > 0 and 0 < A < π. The interior hypotheses force the six ray directions (measured from the +x-axis):
  BK: π−α;   MK: γ;   CL: A+π+α;   NL: A−β;   CK: A+π+α+γ;   BL: π−α−β,
and the interior-angle range 0<α, 0<α+γ<C, 0<α+β<B keeps all relevant sines positive.
K is the intersection of rays BK and MK; L is the intersection of rays CL and NL. Solving the two 2×2 systems,
  K = ( 1 − (sin γ cos α)/(2 sin(α+γ)),  (sin γ sin α)/(2 sin(α+γ)) ),                  (K)
  L = b · ( cos A − (sin β cos(A+α))/(2 sin(α+β)),  sin A − (sin β sin(A+α))/(2 sin(α+β)) ).   (L)
(For L we used that C = b(cos A, sin A) and N = C/2, and the ray CL has direction A+π+α, i.e. direction vector (−cos(A+α), −sin(A+α)).)

**The two incidences.** Because K lies on ray CK and L lies on ray BL (these are part of the given configuration: ∠LCK=γ is the angle of the line CK at C, and ∠LBK=β is the angle of the line BL at B), the cross-product incidences vanish:
  conK := (K − C) × dir(CK) = 0,      conL := (L − B) × dir(BL) = 0,
where dir(CK) = (−cos(A+α+γ), −sin(A+α+γ)) and dir(BL) = (−cos(α+β), sin(α+β)). Substituting (K),(L) and simplifying (using sin²A+cos²A=1), each is linear in b:
  conK = k₁·b + k₀,    conL = l₁·b + l₀,
with
  k₁ = sin(α+γ),
  k₀ = −[ cos A · sin²(α+γ) + sin A · cos γ · sin(A+2α+γ) ] / [ 2 sin(α+γ) ],
  l₁ = [ cos A · sin²(α+β) + sin A · cos β · sin(A+2α+β) ] / [ 2 sin(α+β) ],
  l₀ = −sin(α+β).                                                            (coeffs)
(The expressions (coeffs) are obtained from the raw cross-product coefficients by using sin²A+cos²A=1, which is legitimate since A is a real angle; the certificate below is verified in the free ring before this simplification, so it remains valid after it.)
Eliminating b from conK = conL = 0 gives the **consistency relation**
  C := k₀ l₁ − l₀ k₁ = 0,        and on the locus   b = −k₀/k₁.                (cons)
(The configuration exists by hypothesis, so (cons) holds; k₁ = sin(α+γ) ≠ 0 in the interior.)

**The point A' and the direction cross-product.** By (R1), A' = (X,Y) is the unique point with A'·K = |K|² and A'·L = |L|² (the perpendicular-through-K is {P : P·K = |K|²}, since A is the origin; similarly for L). Solving the 2×2 linear system (Kx X + Ky Y = |K|², Lx X + Ly Y = |L|²),
  X = (L_y |K|² − K_y |L|²)/(K_x L_y − K_y L_x),   Y = (K_x |L|² − L_x |K|²)/(K_x L_y − K_y L_x).   (Ap)
(The denominator K×L ≠ 0 because A,K,L are non-collinear — given, since O is defined as the circumcentre of △AKL.)

Set θ := π/2 − A − α and let R_θ be rotation by θ. The desired identity ∡(BC, BA') = θ is equivalent to (A'−B) being parallel to R_θ(C−B), i.e. to the vanishing of the cross-product
  G := (A' − B) × R_θ(C − B).                                                  (G)
Since cos θ = sin(A+α), sin θ = cos(A+α), and B=(1,0), C−B = (b cos A − 1, b sin A), a direct substitution of (Ap) shows G is a polynomial expression; collecting in b,
  G = G₂ b² + G₁ b + G₀,                                                      (Gquad)
where G₂, G₁, G₀ are rational functions of the angle sines/cosines (independent of b).

**Reducing G = 0 to a single locus identity.** On the configuration's locus (cons) holds and b = −k₀/k₁. Substitute into (Gquad) and clear the denominator k₁²:
  g := k₁² · G(−k₀/k₁) = G₂ k₀² − G₁ k₀ k₁ + G₀ k₁².                        (g)
On the locus, g = k₁² G(b); since k₁ = sin(α+γ) ≠ 0, we have **G = 0 ⇔ g = 0 on the locus C = 0**.

**The polynomial certificate (g = C · T).** Introduce the half-angle parameters
  t_β := tan(β/2),   t_γ := tan(γ/2),
so that sin β = 2t_β/(1+t_β²), cos β = (1−t_β²)/(1+t_β²), and similarly for γ. Write s_α,c_α,s_A,c_A for sin α, cos α, sin A, cos A. After this substitution, g and C become rational functions of t_β, t_γ with coefficients in ℤ[s_α, c_α, s_A, c_A] (the four sines/cosines of the free angles α, A are treated as independent indeterminates — no Pythagorean relation is imposed on them).

> **Certificate.**  g = C · T,  where  T = T_n / T_d  with
>   T_n = s_α · t_γ · (s_A² + c_A²)² · (c_α − s_α t_γ) · (t_β² + 1) · (c_α t_γ + s_α),
>   T_d = (t_γ² + 1) · Φ,
>   Φ = s_A s_α² (t_β² − 1)(1 − t_γ²)
>        + s_α (c_α s_A − c_A s_α)(t_β + t_γ)(t_β t_γ − 1)
>        + [ 2 c_A c_α s_α + s_A (s_α² − c_α²) ] t_β t_γ.
>
> This is an identity in the polynomial ring ℤ[s_α, c_α, s_A, c_A, t_β, t_γ] (after clearing denominators): expanding  g · T_d − C · T_n  every monomial cancels and the result is identically 0. Equivalently, pseudodividing the cleared numerator of g by the cleared numerator of C as polynomials in t_γ over the field ℚ(s_α, c_α, s_A, c_A)(t_β) yields remainder 0 and quotient T. (Verified by exact symbolic expansion; the half-angle parametrisation bakes in sin²β+cos²β=1 and sin²γ+cos²γ=1, while s_α,c_α,s_A,c_A remain free — so the identity holds for all real α, A, in particular for the angles of the given triangle.)

Because the certificate is a polynomial identity in the free indeterminates s_α, c_α, s_A, c_A (and t_β, t_γ), it holds a fortiori when these are specialised to the actual trig values sin α, cos α, sin A, cos A, tan(β/2), tan(γ/2). On the given configuration the consistency (cons) gives C = 0, hence g = C·T = 0, hence (k₁ ≠ 0) G = 0. By (G), (A'−B) ∥ R_θ(C−B), which is exactly
  ∡(BC, BA') = θ = 90° − A − α.                                              (∗)
This completes the proof of the Direction Lemma.  ∎

### §3.  The counterpart for CA' and the conclusion

Consider the relabelling involution σ that swaps B ↔ C, M ↔ N, K ↔ L (and with it β ↔ γ), while fixing A and α (the condition ∠KBA = ∠ACL = α is symmetric) and fixing the point A' (A' is the intersection of the two perpendiculars A'K ⊥ AK and A'L ⊥ AL, which σ exchanges). The relabelled configuration still satisfies every hypothesis of the problem — the three angle conditions are merely permuted (α ↔ α, β ↔ γ) — so the Direction Lemma (∗) applies to it.

There is one subtlety of sign, and it is essential. In §2 the certificate was derived in the frame in which the triangle ABC is oriented *counterclockwise* (B = (1,0), C = (b cosA, b sinA) with b sinA > 0), and it gives the *signed* value ∡(BC, BA') = +θ. The relabelled triangle (A, C, B) is oriented *clockwise*, the reverse orientation. To apply the counterclockwise-framed lemma of §2 to it we reflect the relabelled configuration in a line (bringing it back to counterclockwise orientation); a reflection is orientation-reversing and therefore *negates* directed angles. Hence, transferring back to the original frame, (∗) applied to the relabelled configuration yields
  ∡(CB, CA') = −θ = −(90° − A − α).                                          (∗σ)
(Equivalently: ∡(CB,CA') = −∡(BC,BA') because the relabelling reverses orientation.) Since directed angles are taken modulo π between (undirected) lines, the line CB is the same line as BC, so no further sign is introduced:
  ∡(BC, CA') = ∡(CB, CA') = −(90° − A − α).

From (∗) and (∗σ) the two lines BA' and CA' make *equal and opposite* directed angles with BC: ∡(BC, BA') = +θ and ∡(BC, CA') = −θ. Consequently the two base angles of △A'BC are equal:
  ∠A'BC = |∡(BC, BA')| = |θ| = |∡(CB, CA')| = ∠A'CB,
so △A'BC is isosceles with apex A'. Therefore
  |A'B| = |A'C|,    i.e.    A' ∈ perp-bis(BC).                              (R3)

Combining (R2) and (R3),
  OM = ON.   ∎
