## imo-2026-02

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Formal swap symmetry of the three angle conditions.** Since M lies on segment AB and N on segment AC, condition 1 (`∠KBA=∠ACL`) is literally `∠KBM=∠LCN` (rays BA=BM, CA=CN). Writing out all three conditions and applying the relabeling σ: B↔C, K↔L, M↔N, A fixed, shows the *whole system of three conditions is invariant under σ* — condition 1 maps to itself (LHS/RHS swap), and conditions 2 and 3 map to each other (`∠LBK=∠LNC` ↔ `∠LCK=∠BMK` under B↔C,K↔L,N↔M). This is a purely combinatorial/labeling symmetry (not a geometric symmetry of the actual triangle, since B,C are generally not interchangeable), but it strongly suggests the "K-relative-to-(B,M)" data and "L-relative-to-(C,N)" data play *dual* roles, and OM=ON should be provable as the geometric shadow of this formal duality — e.g. by finding a common invariant (a length or angle) attached symmetrically to (B,M,K) and (C,N,L) that feeds directly into the OM vs ON computation. Worth having the outliner build one approach that tries to name and exploit this σ-symmetry directly (e.g. via a linear identity for OM²−ON² that vanishes by the symmetry of the constraint set), rather than chasing individual angles.
  2. **Vector/linear-algebra reduction of the target.** OM² − ON² = 2·O·(N−M) + |M|² − |N|² is *linear* in O (standard perpendicular-bisector fact). Since O is the circumcenter of AKL (so OA=OK=OL=R), O's coordinates are an explicit rational function of A, K, L (the standard circumcenter formula). The real content of the problem is then: express K and L via the 3 angle conditions (as functions of a hidden parameter — see small-case findings below, the conditions do NOT pin down K, L uniquely, only up to a 1-parameter family) and show the resulting O always satisfies the linear condition. This is a legitimate route even without full synthetic insight — a coordinate/vector bash instantiating the angle conditions as dot-product/cross-product equations. Flagged as "cheap but not synthetic"; probably what a different (analytic) explorer route should own, but noted here since it clarifies exactly what must be shown.
  3. **Spiral similarity / isogonal search (my assigned focus) — mostly negative result.** I checked directly whether BK, CL are isogonal w.r.t. angle B (they are not — no natural isogonal-conjugate line through B appears from the given data alone) and whether triangles BMK and CNL are related by an obvious spiral similarity (checked numerically below — they are NOT congruent or similar in general: angles ∠BMK ≠ ∠CNL, ratios BM/CN ≠ BK/CL ≠ MK/NL). So a *literal* spiral similarity taking B-data to C-data does not exist as a rigid map of the picture; the symmetry that does exist is the formal relabeling σ above, not a spiral similarity of the actual figure. Do not force a spiral-similarity lemma linking BMK and CNL directly — numerically refuted (see below).

- Small-case / intuition notes (numerical, NOT proof): Built a concrete scalene triangle A=(0,3), B=(-2,0), C=(3,0), M=(-1,1.5), N=(1.5,1.5), and treated θ=∠KBA=∠ACL as a free parameter; for each θ solved the remaining 2 angle equations (`∠LBK=∠LNC`, `∠LCK=∠BMK`) numerically for the two ray-distances (rK, rL) via `scipy.optimize.fsolve`. Findings:
  - **The three angle conditions do NOT pin down (K,L) uniquely** — they leave a genuine 1-parameter family, confirmed for θ ∈ {10°,...,40°} all giving valid interior points with convergent solutions.
  - **Along the whole family, O = circumcenter(A,K,L) has a CONSTANT x-coordinate = 0.25**, exactly the x-coordinate of the midpoint of MN (M=(-1,1.5), N=(1.5,1.5), midpoint=(0.25,1.5)), while O's y-coordinate varies freely (1.53 → 0.82 as θ goes 10°→40°). Since MN is horizontal here, "constant x = midpoint's x" means **O moves along the perpendicular bisector of MN as the free parameter varies** — a much stronger fact than a single instance of OM=ON. Numerically OM=ON held to ~1e-10 in every trial, consistent with O literally lying on that fixed line for the entire family. This is a strong (though still only numerical/conjectural) clue: the real theorem is likely "O always lies on the perpendicular bisector of MN," and the family of valid (K,L) sweeps out a curve while O sweeps the fixed perpendicular bisector line — suggesting a clean linear-algebraic proof (opening 2) or a fixed auxiliary point/circle (opening 1) is the right target, rather than pinning down K,L explicitly.
  - Checked BMK vs CNL for θ=25°: BM=1.803, CN=2.121 (unequal), BK=0.728, CL=1.181 (unequal), MK=1.184, NL=1.164 (close but not equal), ∠BMK=15.06°, ∠CNL=25.39° (unequal), ∠BKM=139.94°, ∠CLN=129.61° (unequal) — confirms no direct congruence/similarity between the two "half-triangles," so the σ-duality (opening 1) is a labeling symmetry of the *equations*, not a rigid symmetry of the *figure*.
