ALWAYS: For geometry problems, verify the problem statement's angle conditions in directed-angle (complex number) form — this often reveals the exact algebraic structure faster than synthetic chasing. (imo-2026-02, round 1)

ALWAYS: When conditions equate two angles, check whether the "equal directed angles" condition translates to "imaginary part of a complex ratio = 0" — these are exact and computationally verifiable. (imo-2026-02, round 1)

ALWAYS: For "prove OM = ON" problems where O is a circumcenter, the target is equivalent to pow(M, circumcircle) = pow(N, circumcircle), which reduces to a metric identity. Try to derive metric relations from the sub-triangles forced by the conditions via the sine rule. (imo-2026-02, round 1)

NEVER: Assume a clean 4-point concyclicity exists without numerical verification — the angle conditions here force many "almost concyclic" configurations that are NOT exactly concyclic. (imo-2026-02, round 1)

ALWAYS: When the problem involves midpoints M, N of two sides AB, AC, note that M and N lie on the nine-point circle of ABC, so the nine-point center lies on the perpendicular bisector of MN. This is the target line for the circumcenter in OM=ON problems. (imo-2026-02, round 1)
