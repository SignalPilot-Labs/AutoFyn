ALWAYS: For functional inequality problems on R+, try substituting x=f(y) first — it often forces an exact derived functional equation by squeezing both sides of an inequality to equality (because QM(f(y),f(y))=AM(f(y),f(y))=GM(f(y),f(y))). (round 1, imo-2026-05)

ALWAYS: Test f(x)=x+c (constant additive shift) as a candidate before anything else for FE problems on R+ — it's a natural family that often turns mean inequalities into trivially-satisfied expressions. (round 1, imo-2026-05)

ALWAYS: When a derived functional equation f(f(y))=2f(y)-y is found, set h=f-id; then h(f(y))=h(y) means h is constant on orbits (arithmetic progressions). Show h>=0 from codomain, then force h constant from the original inequalities. (round 1, imo-2026-05)

ALWAYS: In functional inequality problems, re-derive the ineq (after squaring both sides) as (u-v)^2 >= c(c+2(u+v)) — this is the key form that uses orbit growth to force contradictions when h is non-constant. (round 1, imo-2026-05)

NEVER: Assume f(x)=x is the only solution to a double-inequality problem like QM>=AM_twisted>=GM — check the full f(x)=x+c family first (it often works for all c>=0). (round 1, imo-2026-05)
