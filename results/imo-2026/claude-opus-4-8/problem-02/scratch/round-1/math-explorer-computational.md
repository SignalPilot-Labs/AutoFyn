## imo-2026-02 (computational/analytic lens)

- Distinct openings:
  1. **Radical-axis / equal-power framing.** Numerically confirmed `pow(M, ⊙AKL) = pow(N, ⊙AKL)` identically (this is literally equivalent to `OM=ON` since `OM²-R²=pow(M)`, `ON²-R²=pow(N)`, same `R`). The real content to prove is this power equality — likely via expressing each power as a signed product along a line through `M` (resp. `N`) meeting `⊙AKL` at two known/constructible points, then showing the two products coincide using the angle conditions and `M,N` being midpoints (so `MA=MB`, `NA=NC`). This is the most promising analytic-to-synthetic bridge: reduce OM=ON to a power-of-a-point identity rather than direct distance computation.
  2. **1-parameter family / degrees-of-freedom argument.** Since the configuration is NOT rigid (see DOF analysis below), a valid proof strategy is to fix one convenient member of the family (e.g. take the limiting/degenerate position where the construction becomes tractable, or a position with extra symmetry) and show OM=ON there, THEN separately argue the equality is preserved as the free parameter varies — e.g. by showing `d(OM)/dt = d(ON)/dt` structurally, or (cleaner) by finding the invariant of opening 1 directly, which sidesteps needing continuity arguments. Recommend NOT chasing continuity/algebraic-family arguments as the main proof route (fragile for a synthetic olympiad writeup) — use it only as a sanity framing.
  3. **Coordinate/complex bash as fallback.** Full symbolic (sympy/complex-number) computation of K, L from the 3 angle equations is likely intractable in closed form (transcendental system, non-polynomial after clearing radicals) — not recommended as the primary route; use trig identities (Law of Sines in the various sub-triangles) to convert the angle equalities into ratio conditions on segments (`BK/sin·, CL/sin·` etc.), which is the standard olympiad way to make such angle-conditions tractable synthetically, rather than raw coordinate bash.

- **Critical DOF finding (numerically verified):** unknowns `K=(kx,ky)`, `L=(lx,ly)` — 4 real DOF. Constraints `∠KBA=∠ACL`, `∠LBK=∠LNC`, `∠LCK=∠BMK` — only 3 scalar equations. **The system is under-determined: K, L are NOT uniquely determined by the problem's angle conditions — there is a genuine 1-parameter family of valid (K,L) pairs** satisfying all three angle equalities and the "inside the triangle / inside the angle" containment constraints. This was checked by solving via `scipy.optimize.least_squares` from many random starts (barycentric-in-triangle parametrization to respect containment) on 4 distinct generic scalene triangles; each yielded dozens of genuinely different solutions along a smooth curve (confirmed via SVD of the solution point cloud in barycentric space — motion along ~1 direction). **This DOF-count observation should be highlighted to the outliner**: the proof must NOT implicitly assume K,L are pinned down by the 3 conditions alone; the "inside" containment conditions only cut down to an open sub-arc of the 1-parameter family (they don't add rigidity), so any approach that tries to "solve for K,L explicitly" is solving for an underdetermined system and must instead produce an identity valid along the whole family (matching the radical-axis framing in opening 1).

- **Ground truth established (numerical, high precision):**
  - For 4 independent generic scalene triangles (both acute- and obtuse-flavored, no special symmetry), and for ~50–100 family members per triangle (sampled via least-squares from randomized starts, filtered to satisfy `K` inside ∠LBA, `L` inside ∠ACK, and inside the required sub-triangles), `|OM − ON|` was `< 5×10⁻⁸` in every case (machine-precision-level agreement) — strongly confirms `OM=ON` as an identity that holds **for every member of the family**, not just a single configuration.
  - Stronger structural fact: **the locus of `O` as the free parameter varies is exactly the perpendicular bisector of `MN`** (confirmed via SVD collinearity check on the `O`-point cloud: second singular value ~`10⁻⁷`–`10⁻⁸` vs. first ~`1`–`3`; and the fitted line passes through the midpoint of `MN` to ~`10⁻⁹` and is perpendicular to `BC`/`MN` to ~`10⁻⁸`). `O` itself is **not** fixed — its distance from `M`(`=`from `N`) varies substantially across the family (e.g. from `1.41` to `1.61` in one test triangle) — so there is no fixed point or fixed circle characterizing `O`; the invariant is purely "equidistant from `M,N`," i.e. `O` lies on line `MN`'s perpendicular bisector, which (incidentally, but not specially) also contains the nine-point center of `ABC` (true of any two points on the nine-point circle, not a special new fact — the nine-point center is equidistant from all of `M, N`, feet of altitudes, etc.; this is *not* itself a novel invariant of this problem, just a sanity check that the fitted line was identified correctly).
  - The circumradius `R` of `⊙AKL` varies substantially across the family (not constant), and quadrilateral `BKLC` is **not** concyclic in general (checked via the `4×4` determinant concyclicity test — nonzero and varying) — rules out a naive "BKLC cyclic ⇒ done" shortcut.
  - Ratios `AK/BK` and `AL/CL` are **not** equal in general (checked numerically) — rules out a simple direct spiral-similarity ratio `B↦K, C↦L` centered at `A` sending the whole configuration onto itself; the relationship between `K` and `L` is more subtle than a single spiral similarity with fixed ratio (though a spiral similarity taking `BK↦CL` as directed angles, i.e. matching the given angle condition `∠LBK=∠LNC`, `∠LCK=∠BMK` structurally, may still be a useful *local* lemma — worth the outliner checking whether these two conditions jointly assert `K` is determined from `L` by a spiral similarity at some center, since `∠LBK` (angle at B) `=∠LNC` (angle at N) and `∠LCK` (angle at C) `=∠BMK` (angle at M) have a suggestive M↔N, B↔C, K↔L swap symmetry pattern — this symmetry-under-swap is worth flagging to the outliner as a structural clue, though I did not verify a specific transformation numerically beyond the angle equalities themselves).
