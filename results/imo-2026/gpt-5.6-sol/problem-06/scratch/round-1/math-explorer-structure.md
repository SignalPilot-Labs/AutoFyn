## imo-2026-06
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Block dynamics anchored at multiples of the initial term.** Put `A=a_1`. Every later term has gcd greater than 1 with `A`; consequently every multiple of `A` has gcd greater than 1 with every term already chosen. Hence the next term is never beyond the next multiple of `A`, every gap is at most `A`, and in fact every multiple `kA` occurs in the sequence (the sequence cannot cross an admissible integer). This partitions the sequence into finite blocks inside `[kA,(k+1)A]`, with at most `A` possible offsets per block. The natural dynamical object is the word of selected offsets in successive `A`-blocks. A full route could seek eventual periodicity of this block word, yielding `L` a multiple of `A` and `T` equal to the number of selected offsets in one period. Likely hard lemma: despite gcd constraints from the entire past, only a finite state attached to a block is needed, or old constraints become translation-redundant.
  2. **Stabilization of prime-support constraints.** Regard each integer as its finite set of prime divisors. The selected terms form an increasing pairwise-intersecting family of finite sets, and `P=Supp(A)` is a fixed finite transversal: every selected support meets `P`. Eligibility is the intersection of the periodic conditions “share a prime with `a_i`.” If one can show that after finitely many terms all later gcd conditions are implied by finitely many prime/residue conditions, then the admissible set becomes periodic modulo the product/lcm of those finitely many primes and greedy enumeration of a periodic set is translation-periodic. Likely hard lemma: prove finite constraint stabilization; it is not enough merely that `P` is a transversal, because two terms supported on disjoint subsets of `P` may meet through a newly appearing external prime.
  3. **Finite refinement by `P`-types.** Assign to a term the nonempty type `Supp(a_n)∩P`; only finitely many types exist. Terms of intersecting types are automatically compatible. Nontrivial information lies only between disjoint types, where their external supports must be cross-intersecting. One possible full route is to refine each `P`-type by intersections with supports of finitely many representatives until all disjoint-type compatibility is witnessed by a finite prime alphabet, then invoke periodic residues. The hard point is termination of refinement; arbitrary finite-set systems warn that it cannot simply be asserted.
  4. **Translation/shadowing of a finite block.** For a finite initial segment choose a modulus divisible by the relevant terms or witness primes. Translation by that modulus preserves all gcd witnesses inside the finite segment. A rival route could compare the greedy sequence to its translated copy and use minimality to force coincidence once a complete block is repeated. The load-bearing issue is showing that constraints contributed by terms after the copied segment cannot distinguish the original and translated candidates. This is genuinely different from proving a global finite prime alphabet: it seeks a repeatable local block/certificate.
  5. **Bounded-gap symbolic dynamics.** Since `1≤a_{n+1}-a_n≤A`, the gap sequence has a finite alphabet. A route could try to show the next gap is determined by a bounded residue state (residues modulo finitely many small primes, plus finitely many active obstruction classes), so eventual state repetition gives eventual periodic gaps and hence eventual translation. The hard lemma is an “aging-out” statement for large-prime obstructions: a large prime can couple distant terms even though it cannot divide two terms whose numerical difference is smaller than that prime.
- Candidate technique(s): Prime-support/set-system encoding; greedy enumeration of an eventually periodic subset of the integers; finite-state reduction modulo an lcm; block decomposition using the forced anchors `k a_1`; invariants/monovariants for the set of active gcd constraints; pigeonhole on finitely many offset patterns. These are pointers rather than a proof plan.
- Cheap-kill candidates: The strongest immediate pruning is the anchor observation: **all positive multiples of `a_1` occur**, and therefore all consecutive gaps are at most `a_1`. Also every term has a prime factor from the fixed finite set `P=Supp(a_1)`, so there are only `2^{|P|}-1` base-support types. If two terms differ by less than a prime `q`, then `q` cannot divide both; in particular any common-prime witness for consecutive terms is at most `a_1`. These facts should be established before heavier state machinery. Parity is decisive when `2|a_1`: after the first term, every even integer is immediately admissible and the sequence is simply consecutive evens (as confirmed computationally), but odd `a_1` is the substantive case.
