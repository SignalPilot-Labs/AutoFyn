## imo-2026-06

small-prime-mask-compression: new
Target: Prove that for the given greedy sequence there exist positive integers `T,L` such that `a_{n+T}=a_n+L` for every positive integer `n`.
Technique: Direct greedy-set reformulation, minimal-counterexample induction, prime-factor stripping, and periodic residue masks modulo a primorial. This adapts the exact crux of `aimo-0030` (its second solution and Comments 2–3) from scratch, without relying on the game as a citation.
Skeleton:
  1. Put `k=a_1` and recursively color every integer `m>=k` good if it is non-coprime to every smaller good integer, and bad otherwise — this is a well-defined induction on `m`; equivalently, a bad integer has a smaller good integer coprime to it.
  2. Show that the increasing list of good integers is exactly `a_1,a_2,...` — by induction, after the already listed good integers, the next good integer is precisely the least larger integer having gcd greater than `1` with each of them.
  3. Establish the basic kernel property: any two good integers are non-coprime, and `k` is good — otherwise the larger good integer would have a smaller good coprime predecessor.
  4. Call primes `p<=k` small and call two integers similar when they have exactly the same small prime divisors. Prove the compression lemma: every `b>=k` having a small prime factor has a similar `x` with `k<=x<=b` and with no large prime factors — multiply the distinct small prime divisors of `b` and, if necessary, the least power of one of them that raises the product to `k`; comparison with one large prime divisor of `b` proves the size bound.
  5. Prove by a minimal counterexample that similar integers always have the same color. If similar `a,b` have opposite colors with `max(a,b)` minimal, label `a` bad and `b` good; choose a smaller good `r` coprime to `a`. Since `r` and `k` are good, `r` has a small prime factor, so compress it to `r'<=r`. Minimality makes `r'` good. The two good integers `r'` and `b` share a prime; because `r'` has only small prime factors, similarity transfers that prime first from `b` to `a` and then from `r'` to `r`, contradicting `gcd(a,r)=1`.
  6. Let `L=prod_{p<=k} p`. The small-prime mask of `m` equals that of `m+L`, so Step 5 gives `m` good iff `m+L` good for every `m>=k` — by modular arithmetic and the definition of similarity.
  7. Let `T` be the number of good integers in `[k,k+L-1]`; it is positive because `k` is good. Every interval of `L` consecutive integers has exactly `T` good positions, and translation by `L` bijects good positions to good positions. Therefore the increasing enumeration satisfies `a_{n+T}=a_n+L` for every `n>=1` — by counting the marked residues between `a_n` and `a_n+L`.
Key lemmas (claim + the one-line mechanism that makes it true):
  - Greedy sequence = increasing good-set enumeration — because the recursive definition “no smaller good coprime integer” is exactly the test “non-coprime to every previously enumerated term.”
  - Small-prime compression — because if `A` is the product of the small prime divisors and `p^e A` is the least such product reaching `k`, then `p^eA<pk<Aq<=b` for any large prime divisor `q>k` (with the no-large-prime case taken directly).
  - Similarity invariance — because a least oppositely colored similar pair can be contradicted by replacing its smaller good witness with a no-large-prime representative, after which a common prime must transfer through both similarities.
  - Periodic membership implies translated enumeration from the first term — because a length-`L` block contains a constant positive number `T` of marked residues, not merely eventually but for every starting residue.
Open gaps: The builder must give the full inductive justification of the good/bad recursion and enumeration; handle all branches of the compression lemma (`b` with or without a large prime, and the least exponent equal to zero or positive); formulate the minimal-counterexample argument so both possible numerical orders of `a,b` are covered; and rigorously count indices in Step 7.
Cases to cover: Compression with no large prime versus at least one large prime; least exponent `e=0` versus `e>0`; either member of a similar opposite-color pair may be numerically larger.
Watch out for: The finite set is the primes `<=k`, not merely primes dividing `k`, and not all primes ever appearing. Do not infer periodicity merely from bounded gaps. Explicitly prove a law valid from `n=1`; an eventual finite-state argument would not suffice. The cheap anchor fact that every multiple of `k` is selected gives bounded gaps and positivity, but by itself does not remove the whole-history dependence, so it is not the proof spine.

multiplicative-color-descent: new
Target: Prove that for the given greedy sequence there exist positive integers `T,L` such that `a_{n+T}=a_n+L` for every positive integer `n`.
Technique: P-position/kernel reformulation followed by multiplicative closure and minimal descent on a similar multiple, then CRT-periodic enumeration. This adapts the first solution and Comments 2–3 of `aimo-0030`; its load-bearing route is multiplicative badness propagation rather than prime-factor compression.
Skeleton:
  1. For fixed `k=a_1`, consider the terminating game in which one may move from `m>=k` to a smaller `m'>=k` coprime to `m`. Define good positions as losing positions. By standard P-position recursion, `m` is bad iff it has a move to a smaller good position, while `m` is good iff it has no such move.
  2. Deduce that any two good positions are non-coprime and that the increasing good positions are exactly the given greedy sequence — by the P-position recursion and induction through the integers.
  3. Prove three multiplicative propagation rules: (i) a multiple of a good integer is good; (ii) if `rs` is bad then `r^2s` is bad; (iii) if `q>k` is prime and `n` is bad, then `nq` is bad. Rules (i) and (ii) follow immediately by transferring a good coprime witness; rule (iii) follows by choosing the least counterexample `n`, factoring the forced good witness as `q^e y`, and using the least power of `y` reaching `k` to descend below `n`.
  4. Call integers similar if they have the same prime divisors `<=k`. Prove that if `c` is similar to a multiple `d` of `c`, then `c,d` have the same color. Choose a counterexample with minimal `d`; rule (i) forces `c` bad and `d` good. For a prime `p|d/c`, if `p<=k`, similarity gives `p|c`, so the contrapositive of rule (ii) makes `d/p` good; if `p>k`, the contrapositive of rule (iii) does the same. In both cases `(c,d/p)` contradicts minimality.
  5. For arbitrary similar `a,b`, note that `ab` is similar to each and is a multiple of each; Step 4 therefore shows that `a,b,ab` all have the same color. Thus color depends only on the divisibility mask of primes `<=k`.
  6. Set `L=prod_{p<=k}p`. Congruence modulo `L` preserves every small-prime divisibility bit, hence good/bad membership on `[k,infinity)` is `L`-periodic — by modular arithmetic/CRT.
  7. Let `T` be the positive number of good residues in any full length-`L` block (positive since all multiples of the good integer `k` are good by rule (i)). Ordered enumeration of the good positions then gives `a_{n+T}=a_n+L` for all `n`.
Key lemmas (claim + the one-line mechanism that makes it true):
  - Multiples preserve goodness — because a hypothetical smaller good integer coprime to the multiple would also be coprime to its good divisor.
  - Squaring a factor preserves badness — because a good witness coprime to `rs` remains coprime to `r^2s`.
  - Multiplication by a prime `q>k` preserves badness — because a least counterexample forces every good witness to contain `q`, and a least threshold-crossing power of its `q`-free part produces a strictly smaller counterexample chain.
  - Similar-multiple descent — because removing one quotient prime from a minimal good multiple preserves goodness by the appropriate contrapositive propagation rule.
  - Primorial periodicity — because divisibility by each prime `p<=k` depends only on the residue modulo their product.
Open gaps: The substantial gap is the complete proof of propagation rule (iii), including the factorization `x=q^e y`, exclusion of `y=1`, the strict threshold bound, and the iteration of minimality needed to show the auxiliary chain bad. The builder must also rigorously derive the P-position recursion and the exact greedy enumeration, and spell out the final marked-residue count.
Cases to cover: In Step 4, quotient prime `p<=k` and `p>k`; in rule (iii), exponent and threshold-power boundary cases; arbitrary similar integers reduced through their common multiple.
Watch out for: Rule (iii) is not licensed by a vague “large primes are irrelevant” claim; every descent inequality is load-bearing. The game is only a reformulation and all P-position facts must be proved. As in the first approach, periodicity must start at `k`, not after an unspecified transient.

small-witness-kernel: new
Target: Prove that for the given greedy sequence there exist positive integers `T,L` such that `a_{n+T}=a_n+L` for every positive integer `n`.
Technique: Extremal proof that every pair in the greedy kernel has a common prime at most `k`, followed by direct color classification and periodic-set enumeration. This adapts Claim 5 in Comment 1 of `aimo-0030`; unlike `small-prime-mask-compression`, it first proves the stronger global small-witness theorem and then uses it as a reusable invariant.
Skeleton:
  1. Define good/bad integers from `k=a_1` by the direct recursion: good means no smaller good integer is coprime to it. Prove that the original sequence is the increasing enumeration of the good integers and that any two good integers are non-coprime.
  2. Prove the same compression lemma as a preparatory tool: a number `b>=k` with a small prime factor has a similar `x` in `[k,b]` supported only on primes `<=k` — by the least threshold-crossing power construction.
  3. Prove the small-witness theorem: any two good integers share a prime `p<=k`. If not, choose a violating pair `b<=b'` with `b'` minimal. Since `b` and `k` are good, `b` has a small prime; compress `b` to `x<=b`. Then `x` and `b'` are coprime, so `x` is bad and has a smaller good witness `b*` coprime to `x`. Every small prime of `b` divides `x`, so `b*` and `b` have no common small prime, yielding a violating good pair with maximum at most `b<b'`, contradiction.
  4. Prove similarity invariance using the small-witness theorem. If similar `a,b` had opposite colors, label `a` bad and `b` good and take a smaller good `r` coprime to `a`. The good pair `b,r` shares a small prime, which similarity transfers from `b` to `a`, contradicting coprimality.
  5. With `L=prod_{p<=k}p`, similarity invariance makes the good-set indicator exactly `L`-periodic on all integers `>=k`. Let `T>0` be its number of marked positions per period; the increasing enumeration then obeys `a_{n+T}=a_n+L` for every `n`.
Key lemmas (claim + the one-line mechanism that makes it true):
  - Every two good integers have a small common prime — because a least pair whose intersections use only large primes can have its smaller member compressed to a small-prime-supported number, producing an even smaller violating good pair.
  - Color is determined by the small-prime mask — because any bad member has a smaller good coprime witness, while the small-witness theorem forces that witness to meet a similar good member through a prime that also divides the bad member.
  - Exact translation of the enumeration — because the good indicator is periodic on the entire half-line beginning at `k`, with a nonzero fixed count per period.
Open gaps: The builder must carefully verify that the compressed `x` in Step 3 is strictly below `b'`, that `x` is bad for the stated reason, and that the replacement pair `(b*,b)` has genuinely smaller extremal parameter. Full compression inequalities and the endpoint/index count remain to be written.
Cases to cover: The compression construction's boundary cases; ordering of the alleged violating good pair; opposite-color similar pair in either numerical order.
Watch out for: The theorem is only “some common prime `<=k` for each pair,” not one universal prime and not necessarily a prime dividing `k`. External primes may occur in individual terms and may witness some pairs, so do not claim their support disappears. The immediate anchor observations (all multiples of `k`, gaps at most `k`; parity when `2|k`) are useful sanity checks but do not establish the small-witness theorem.
