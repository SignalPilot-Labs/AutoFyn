- Distinct openings: (1) **Sorted multiset / discrepancy transformation.** Once all cuts are fixed, strategic play is no longer game-theoretically complicated: optimal play takes pieces in nonincreasing order $a_1\ge\cdots\ge a_m$, so Liu’s value is $a_1+a_3+\cdots=\frac12+\frac12(a_1-a_2+a_3-a_4+\cdots)$ (with a terminal $+a_m$ when $m$ is odd). This turns the whole problem into a sequential partition-refinement minimax problem for the alternating discrepancy of the sorted piece multiset. (2) **Geometric/superincreasing initial intervals.** The strong candidate Liu configuration is the normalized geometric partition with interval lengths $1,2,4,\ldots,2^n$ divided by $2^{n+1}-1$. Numerical optimization and random refinement tests indicate that splitting these $n+1$ bins with at most $n$ extra cuts never drives Liu below the largest original interval, suggesting the answer $c_n=2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)$. The relevant structural feature is not physical order but a scale hierarchy: every level equals one plus the total mass of all lower levels. This invites a rank-pairing, binary-carry, or majorization interpretation of arbitrary refinements. (3) **Threshold/layer-cake transformation.** For sorted pieces, the alternating sum can be represented by integrating the parity of the number of pieces exceeding a threshold. Refinement changes that count only when a threshold crosses daughter pieces; each Xiang cut adds one daughter and hence toggles threshold parity on a controlled interval. This translates the payoff into a parity-measure functional of at most $n$ toggles, potentially exposing why $n+1$ dyadic scales are extremal. (4) **Majorization/polyhedral extremality.** Fix Liu’s $n+1$ initial interval lengths. Xiang chooses a refinement with at most $n$ cuts, while the payoff is a symmetric piecewise-linear function of the resulting lengths (linear on each chamber of fixed sorted order). Candidate extremizers should occur when daughter-piece lengths collide, cuts hit endpoints in a limiting sense, or a cut budget is concentrated among a few parent intervals. This is an opening for an exchange/smoothing characterization of the worst refinement, and separately for optimizing Liu’s parent vector. (5) **Dual upper-bound / balancing response.** For arbitrary Liu intervals, Xiang’s target is to make the sorted alternating discrepancy at most $1/(2^{n+1}-1)$, equivalent to Liu’s total at most $2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)$. The dyadic denominator suggests a binary decision tree, repeated halving, or subset-sum balancing certificate rather than equal cuts. This is structurally different from proving robustness of the geometric construction and could give the matching universal upper bound. (6) **Recursive minimax normalization.** Removing or isolating one extremal parent interval and scaling the remainder suggests a recurrence with fixed point relation $c_n=(1+c_{n-1})/2$ if the conjectured formula is correct (indeed $c_n=2^n/(2^{n+1}-1)$ does not satisfy that simple relation exactly, so the natural recurrence must retain a denominator/state parameter). A two-state recursion tracking total residual mass and one unmatched ranked piece may be more faithful than a scalar recursion. (7) **Matching interpretation.** Pair the sorted pieces as $(a_1,a_2),(a_3,a_4),\ldots$; Liu receives $1/2$ plus half the total within-pair excess. The problem becomes controlling the cost of an adjacent matching in sorted length order under at most $n$ splits. A scale-respecting injection from lower-scale fragments to upper-scale fragments is a plausible structural language, while Xiang’s side seeks an almost-equal adjacent matching.
- Candidate technique(s): Rearrangement/greedy drafting reduction; adjacent pairing in sorted order; majorization and exchange/smoothing; threshold (“layer cake”) representation of an alternating order statistic; dyadic or superincreasing weights; binary-tree encoding of successive refinements; extremal principle on a worst refinement; induction with a strengthened state rather than only the claimed scalar bound. These are pointers only; the lower and upper bounds should be treated as rival whole-problem routes until each is validated.
- Cheap-kill candidates: First establish the deterministic drafting identity above, eliminating all later-play strategy. Structural bounds: Liu always gets at least $1/2$ because each odd-ranked term dominates the following even-ranked term; he also gets the globally largest piece. Use exactly $2n+1$ pieces after both players use all marks (endpoint/unused-mark limits can be handled by zero-length limiting fragments, but distinctness must eventually be justified). Check whether an exchange can assume every Xiang cut is interior and whether concentrating two cuts in one Liu interval can be replaced by cuts in two intervals without worsening Liu; this pruning is not yet proved. Parity of the number of pieces above each threshold is the simplest invariant worth testing before any heavy optimization.

Physical left-to-right order appears irrelevant after Liu chooses parent lengths: Xiang’s feasible refinements depend only on the multiset of parent lengths, since cuts act independently inside each parent interval.
