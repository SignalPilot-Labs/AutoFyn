## imo-2026-05
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Displacement/equality-locus formulation.** Because every quantity is positive, both displayed inequalities can be squared without changing direction. Put \(g(t)=f(t)-t\), \(a=g(y)\), \(d=g(x)-g(y)\). Direct expansion shows that the two squared requirements are exactly the pair
     \[
     (x-y-a)^2+d\bigl(2(x+y+a)+d\bigr)\ge0,\qquad
     (x-y-a)^2-d\bigl(2(x+y+a)+d\bigr)\ge0.
     \]
     Equivalently,
     \[
     \left|[g(x)-g(y)]\,[2(x+y+g(y))+g(x)-g(y)]\right|\le (x-y-g(y))^2. \tag{E}
     \]
     This is an exact reformulation, not numerical evidence. It makes translations distinguished: the left side is zero if \(g\) is constant, and the two original squared slacks coincide. The zero locus \(x=y+g(y)\) (when positive) forces a sharp dichotomy \(g(x)=g(y)\) or \(g(x)=-4y-3g(y)\). This supplies a potentially different route from raw radical manipulation: analyze the second, exceptional branch against positivity/global instances.
  2. **Translation family suggested by simultaneous equality.** For \(f(t)=t+c\), symbolic expansion gives, for *both* squared inequalities, the identical slack
     \[
     (x-y-c)^2.
     \]
     Hence every \(c\ge0\) really is a solution (positive codomain requires \(c\ge0\)); the equality locus is \(x=y+c\). This is a verified construction, not merely a fit. It strongly suggests the final characterization is \(\boxed{f(x)=x+c\ (c\ge0)}\).
  3. **Affine/scaling falsification diagnostic.** In the restricted scaling class \(f(t)=ct\), the lower squared condition for all \(x,y\), after \(t=x/y\), has quadratic \((2c^2-1)t^2-2ct+(2-c^2)\ge0\). Its discriminant is \(8(c-1)^2(c+1)^2\); the global condition forces \(c=1\). Thus non-unit dilations are not hidden candidates, while the nonnegative translation direction is genuine. A numerical/symbolic affine sweep consequently points to slope one and nonnegative intercept; this is conjectural only as a classification beyond the verified affine candidates.
  4. **Orbit/iteration diagnostic (not developed into a proof).** If an orbit accidentally has a translation form \(f(t)=t+a\) along consecutive values, substituting \(x=y+na\), \(f(y)=y+a\), \(f(x)=x+a\) makes each squared slack \(a^2(n-1)^2\). Thus the inequality is compatible with, and has equality at, the one-step orbit relation; no monotonicity or fixed-point heuristic should be assumed. This warns against trying to force \(f^2=f\) or \(f^2=\mathrm{id}\): the actual candidate has \(f^n(x)=x+nc\).
