## imo-2026-05
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Forced equality / orbit translation (strongest structural route).** At the legal specialisation \(x=f(y)\), the two endpoint bounds are both \(f(y)\): the upper endpoint is \(\sqrt{(f(y)^2+f(y)^2)/2}=f(y)\), and the lower is \(\sqrt{f(y)f(y)}=f(y)\). Thus the middle must be equal, giving the exact iterate relation \(f(f(y))+y=2f(y)\). Writing the displacement \(d(y)=f(y)-y\), this says \(d(f(y))=d(y)\), so each forward orbit is the arithmetic chain \(f^n(y)=y+n d(y)\). Positivity of every iterate rules out \(d(y)<0\). The key remaining global issue is to show different chains have the same displacement. Cross-substitution of two long chains, chosen with their arguments within a bounded distance of one another, should make the two squared bounds asymptotically pinch and force \(d(a)=d(b)\). This is an end-to-end classification route rather than a local identity alone.
  2. **Interval pinching / variable separation.** After squaring, the hypothesis is exactly
     \[
     4x f(y)\le (f(x)+y)^2\le2x^2+2f(y)^2.
     \]
     For fixed \(x,f(y)\), the allowed interval for \(f(x)+y\) has coincident endpoints precisely when \(x=f(y)\), because the difference between the endpoint squares is \(2(x-f(y))^2\). This is a geometric mean/RMS pinching formulation. Combining it with the orbit formula and arranging \(x\) close to \(f(y)\) across two orbits is a conceptually separate presentation of the same likely rigidity; it may avoid any need to prove continuity, monotonicity, or surjectivity.
  3. **Chain comparison through squared-gap expansions.** If \(X=f^n(a)=a+nA\), \(Y=f^m(b)=b+mB\), where \(A=d(a),B=d(b)\), then \(f(X)=X+A\), \(f(Y)=Y+B\). Substitute \((x,y)=(X,Y)\) into the two squared inequalities. Select \(m\) for each large \(n\) so \(X-f(Y)\) stays bounded (ordinary floor approximation suffices, no irrational-density theorem). The leading \(Y\)-terms in both gap inequalities then have opposite sign unless \(A=B\); dividing by the diverging common scale should be the clean technical hard step. This is worth retaining as a rival algebraic implementation of Opening 1.
  4. **Candidate-first SOS verification.** The structural calculation points to translations \(f_c(t)=t+c\), \(c\ge0\). For these, both squared gaps are the same SOS:
     \[
     (f_c(x)+y)^2-4x f_c(y)=2x^2+2f_c(y)^2-(f_c(x)+y)^2=(x-y-c)^2.
     \]
     This supplies exceptionally clean sufficiency once necessity establishes that the displacement is global. It also suggests the problem was engineered around equality chaining rather than a nonlinear family.
