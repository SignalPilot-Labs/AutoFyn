## imo-2026-01
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Prime-valuation Euclidean invariant.** For each prime `p`, encode the board by the vector of exponents `(v_p(a_1),…,v_p(a_2026))`. A move on two coordinates `x,y` replaces them by `min(x,y)` and `|x-y|`. The gcd of all coordinates is therefore invariant by the Euclidean identity `gcd(x,y)=gcd(min(x,y),|x-y|)`. At a terminal board `(1,…,1,M)`, this invariant is exactly `v_p(M)`. This identifies the choice-independent terminal value explicitly as
     `M = ∏_p p^{gcd(v_p(a_1),…,v_p(a_2026))}`,
     where zeros are included in the gcd in the usual way. This is the cleanest route for part (b), and it also certifies that the terminal nonunit exists.
  2. **Strict scalar monovariant from prime multiplicity plus support.** Let `Ω(n)` count prime factors with multiplicity and let `r` be the number of board entries greater than `1`. For a selected pair with `g=gcd(m,n)`, the pair's total `Ω` changes from `Ω(m)+Ω(n)` to `Ω(m)+Ω(n)-Ω(g)`. Thus a noncoprime move strictly lowers total `Ω`, while a coprime move leaves total `Ω` fixed but lowers `r` by one. Consequently `Φ=ΣΩ(a_i)+r` strictly decreases under every legal move. This gives termination independently of any confluence argument. Terminality itself means at most one nonunit; preservation of the union of prime supports, or the valuation-gcd invariant above, means at least one nonunit remains.
  3. **Multiset-of-prime-atoms formulation.** Regard an integer as a multiset of colored atoms, one color per prime. On a pair, common atoms become the gcd pile and unmatched multiplicities become the quotient pile. Per color this is the subtractive Euclidean operation. This offers an elementary presentation avoiding formal `p`-adic language while retaining both the strictly decreasing common-atom count and the terminal exponent-gcd invariant. The hard presentation point is to state carefully that different primes evolve simultaneously at the same two board positions, so one must not claim they can be normalized independently by chosen moves.
  4. **Terminating rewrite system / normal form viewpoint.** The move is a terminating rewrite by `Φ`; the vector of exponent gcds labels a unique possible one-nonunit normal form. This bypasses a direct local-confluence or diamond check: uniqueness of the invariant-compatible terminal state is enough. A direct confluence approach is a rival viewpoint but appears needlessly harder because moves on overlapping pairs need not visibly commute.

- Dead ends (do not retry):
  - **Product of all board entries as an invariant:** false; a move changes `mn` to `lcm(m,n)=mn/gcd(m,n)`.
  - **Sum of all valuation exponents alone as a strict monovariant:** insufficient; for a coprime chosen pair it is unchanged, so it does not by itself rule out an infinite sequence.
  - **Number of entries greater than `1` alone:** only nonincreasing; a noncoprime pair can produce two nonunits, so it need not drop.
  - **Maximum `p`-adic exponent:** not invariant; `(5,3)` becomes `(3,2)` for that prime.
  - **Primewise independent execution:** invalid as a move argument, because the same two board positions are chosen simultaneously for every prime. Primewise analysis is safe only for invariants and endpoint identification.
  - **Direct local-confluence/diamond verification:** no evidence it fails, but it introduces overlapping-pair casework that the complete valuation invariant makes unnecessary.
