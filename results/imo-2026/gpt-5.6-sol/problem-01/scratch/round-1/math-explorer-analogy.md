## imo-2026-01
- Distinct openings:
  - **Prime-valuation / subtractive-Euclidean route.** For each prime separately, replace the two selected exponents \((a,b)\) by \((\min(a,b),|a-b|)\). This is a two-pile subtractive Euclidean operation with zero acting as a transfer/swap. The natural per-prime datum is the gcd of the **positive** exponents (zeros must be ignored); it predicts that the terminal exponent of \(p\) is \(\gcd\{v_p(a_i):v_p(a_i)>0\}\). Thus the likely terminal value is \(M=\prod_p p^{g_p}\), where \(g_p\) is that gcd. This is the cleanest end-to-end interpretation.
  - **Chip-firing / colored-token route.** Regard every prime as a color and \(v_p(a_i)\) as the size of the color-\(p\) pile at place \(i\). A move performs the same chosen edge operation on all colors, but each color evolves independently by “smaller pile survives; excess becomes the other pile.” This emphasizes the only subtlety: the choices of places are coupled across primes, even though the invariants are coordinatewise. It may give a very intuitive independence-of-choices solution, but it should not claim that each prime can be processed according to its own sequence of moves.
  - **GCD/LCM lattice route.** Embed each integer as its finite-support exponent vector in \(\mathbb N^{(\mathcal P)}\). Gcd is coordinatewise meet, while \(\mathrm{lcm}/\gcd\) is coordinatewise absolute difference, so the operation is not the standard meet/join sorting map; it is a meet-plus-Euclidean-remainder map. The terminal vector should be the coordinatewise gcd of the nonzero coordinate entries. This route packages all primes simultaneously and makes the formula for \(M\) canonical.
  - **Global descent route for termination.** The product of all board entries is nonincreasing because a selected pair with product \(mn\) is replaced by a pair with product \(\operatorname{lcm}(m,n)\). Product alone stalls on coprime pairs. A sharper cheap monovariant is the lexicographic pair consisting of total prime-factor multiplicity \(\sum_i\Omega(a_i)\) and the number of nonunit entries: a move on a pair with gcd \(>1\) lowers the first coordinate, while a coprime-pair move leaves it fixed and lowers the second. This appears to settle arbitrary-choice termination without fairness assumptions.
  - **Symmetric-polynomial/invariant route (mostly a diagnostic rival).** For a fixed prime, the exponent multiset is symmetric under place permutation, so one can ask for symmetric invariants. Ordinary candidates fail: the sum changes from \(a+b\) to \(\max(a,b)\), the maximum may fall (e.g. \((3,2)\mapsto(2,1)\)), and elementary symmetric polynomials are not preserved. The robust symmetric arithmetic invariant is instead the gcd of the nonzero exponents. Symmetric-polynomial machinery therefore looks heavier and less natural than valuation gcds, unless used only to encode a canonical terminal vector.
- Candidate technique(s): Prime-by-prime valuation vectors; subtractive Euclidean algorithm; invariant/monovariant; lexicographic descent; finite-support gcd/lcm lattice encoding. A useful one-line identification is \((v_p(m),v_p(n))\mapsto(\min,|\text{difference}|)\).
- Cheap-kill candidates: Use the lexicographic pair \((\sum_i\Omega(a_i),\#\{i:a_i>1\})\) before seeking any elaborate termination argument. For choice independence, immediately test the gcd of positive \(p\)-adic valuations; do not use the gcd including zeros, since that would incorrectly erase any prime absent from one initial entry. Product alone is insufficient because it is constant on coprime-pair moves.

- Small-case / intuition notes: Conjecturally, and consistently across random simulations, \(M=\prod_p p^{\gcd\{v_p(a_i):v_p(a_i)>0\}}\). Examples: \((12,18,50)\) always ended at \(150\); \((4,8,16)\) at \(2\); \((6,10,15)\) at \(30\); \((72,108,200,45)\) at \(30\); pairwise-coprime \((2,3,5,7)\) at \(210\). Different random choices changed the number of moves but not the observed terminal value. These computations are evidence only, not proof.
