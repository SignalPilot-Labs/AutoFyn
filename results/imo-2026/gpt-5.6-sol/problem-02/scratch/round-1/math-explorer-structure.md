## imo-2026-02
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Reduce the circumcentre claim to one exact cyclicity.** Put directed angles
     \(x=\angle KBA=\angle ACL\), \(y=\angle LBK=\angle LNC\), and
     \(z=\angle LCK=\angle BMK\). Since \(M,A,B\) and \(N,A,C\) are collinear,
     \[
       \angle AKM=x+z,\qquad \angle ALN=x+y.
     \]
     Hence, by the sine rule in \(AKM\) and \(ALN\),
     \[
       AK=\frac{AB}{2}\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)},\qquad
       AL=\frac{AC}{2}\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)}. \tag{1}
     \]
     The sine rule in \(BKL\) and \(CKL\) gives
     \[
       KL=BK\frac{\sin y}{\sin(x+y)},\qquad
       KL=CL\frac{\sin z}{\sin(x+z)}. \tag{2}
     \]
     Therefore
     \[
       AB\cdot CL=AC\cdot BK, \tag{3}
     \]
     and, using \(BM=AB/2, CN=AC/2\),
     \[
       AK\cdot CL=AL\cdot BK. \tag{4}
     \]
     Equation (4) is precisely the converse power criterion for \(B,C,K,L\) after the next angle observation: \(\angle BKC=\angle BLC\). Indeed, if \(\alpha=\angle BAC\), then the ray orders forced by the stated interior conditions give
     \[
       \angle BKC=\pi-(\alpha+2x+y+z)=\angle BLC.
     \]
     Thus **\(B,C,K,L\) are concyclic**. Now (4), together with power of \(A\) in that circle,
     \(AK\cdot AL=AB\cdot AC\), yields the especially strong metric identities
     \[
       AK^2=AB\cdot BK,\qquad AL^2=AC\cdot CL. \tag{5}
     \]
     These imply \(\triangle ABK\sim\triangle AKL\) (SAS: \(AB/AK=AK/BK\) and included angle \(x\)), and symmetrically \(\triangle ACL\sim\triangle ALK\). A promising end-to-end synthetic opening is to convert those two spiral similarities into the target cyclicity below.

     For any point \(X\), because \(O\) is the circumcentre of \(AKL\),
     \[
       \operatorname{Pow}_{(AKL)}(X)=OX^2-OA^2.
     \]
     Hence \(OM=ON\) is equivalent to equal powers of \(M,N\) to \((AKL)\). As \(M\in AB\), \(N\in AC\),
     \[
       OM=ON\iff MA\cdot MP=NA\cdot NQ,
     \]
     where \(P\ne A\) and \(Q\ne A\) are the second intersections of \(AB,AC\) with \((AKL)\). Since \(MA=AB/2, NA=AC/2\), this becomes \(AB\cdot MP=AC\cdot NQ\). Equivalently, reflecting \(P\) in \(M\) and \(Q\) in \(N\), it asks for a matching affine relation along the two sides. This is a clean synthetic target and avoids computing \(O\).

  2. **Circle \((BCKL)\) plus spiral similarities.** The newly detected circle is likely the main hidden object. The equal-angle conditions become arcs/angles on \(\Gamma=(BCKL)\), while (5) gives two centered spiral similarities:
     \[
       \triangle ABK\sim\triangle AKL,
       \qquad \triangle ACL\sim\triangle ALK.
     \]
     Numerically and by the angle labels, the first similarity has centre \(A\), rotation \(x\), and maps \(AB\mapsto AK\mapsto AL\) at the level of rays/ratios; the second supplies the conjugate chain from \(AC\). This suggests looking for a direct image of the midpoints \(M,N\), or for the intersections \(P,Q\) above, under these spiral similarities. The useful exact invariant already available is that the successive scale factors are \(AK/AB=BK/AK\) and \(AL/AC=CL/AL\).

  3. **Orientation-reversing similarity exchanging the sides.** There is a unique direct formula in polar coordinates for the anti-similarity \(T\) centred at \(A\) with \(T(B)=C\): if a ray makes angle \(t\) with \(AB\), its image ray makes angle \(\alpha-t\), and lengths are multiplied by \(AC/AB\). Because \(\angle BAK=x+z\) and \(\angle CAL=x+y\), the angle conditions nearly pair \(K,L\) under such an isogonal anti-similarity, but not literally (the extra \(y,z\) differ). A viable rival opening is to apply \(T\) to one of the spiral-similarity chains rather than assert \(T(K)=L\). Since \(T(M)=N\), the desired equality would follow from a suitably paired description of the circumcircle/circumcentre. The exact obstruction is the mismatch \(y-z\), so this line needs a compensating rotation coming from \(\Gamma=(BCKL)\).

  4. **Inversion/power packaging.** Invert about \(A\). The circumcircle \((AKL)\) becomes the line through the inverses of \(K,L\), and equal powers at the two side midpoints can become a linear distance/intercept statement. Identities (5) are unusually inversion-friendly: \(AK^2=AB\cdot BK\) and \(AL^2=AC\cdot CL\). They suggest choosing inversion radii tied to \(AB\) or \(AC\), or using the two spiral similarities to identify the inverse images. This is structurally distinct from chasing the centre and may turn the final target into Menelaus or a ratio on two rays.
