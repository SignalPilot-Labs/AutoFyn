## imo-2026-02
- Distinct openings:
  1. **Antipode/Thales transformation (strongest synthetic opening).** Let \(X\) be the antipode of \(A\) on \((AKL)\), so \(X=2O-A\), \(XK\perp AK\), and \(XL\perp AL\). Because the homothety of center \(A\) and factor \(2\) sends \((M,N,O)\) to \((B,C,X)\), the desired \(OM=ON\) is *exactly equivalent* to \(XB=XC\). Thus the original circumcenter statement becomes an isosceles-triangle statement about the antipode. The three given angle equalities should be translated through the two right angles at \(K,L\); this is the most natural place to seek a rotation/spiral-similarity chain ending at \(XB=XC\).
  2. **Paired direct similarities.** The equality \(\angle KBA=\angle ACL\) suggests the direct spiral similarities relating the ordered ray-pairs \((BK,BA)\) and \((CA,CL)\). The other two angle equalities relate \((BL,BK)\) to \((NL,NC)\), and \((CL,CK)\) to \((MB,MK)\). Because \(MB=MA\) and \(NC=NA\), the half-turn/dilation data at the two midpoints may make the scale factors telescope. Candidate end target: the resulting composite similarity carries \(B\) to \(C\) while fixing the antipode \(X\), forcing equal moduli \(XB=XC\). This is structurally different from proving a hidden circle.
  3. **Quarter-turn/vector version of the antipode route.** Use the Thales relations to represent \(X-K\) as a scalar quarter-turn of \(K-A\), and \(X-L\) as a scalar quarter-turn of \(L-A\). Encode each directed-angle hypothesis as “one quotient is positive real.” The desired \(XB=XC\) is then one real-part identity. This is an end-to-end complex/rotation route and a useful fallback if the synthetic spiral centers are hard to locate; it retains the geometry instead of expanding raw circumcenter coordinates.
  4. **Circumcenter-locus reformulation.** The target says that the circumcenter of \(AKL\) lies on the perpendicular bisector of \(MN\). Since \(MN\parallel BC\), this line is the inverse image under the factor-2 homothety at \(A\) of the perpendicular bisector of \(BC\). One can therefore target the locus of antipodes \(X\) directly, rather than compute \(O\). A Reim/Miquel implementation would need an auxiliary circle whose radical-axis or equal-power condition is precisely \(XB=XC\); no such circle was numerically evident, so this is less concrete than openings 1–3.
  5. **Inversion is available but not currently privileged.** Inversion centered at \(A\) turns \((AKL)\) into a line and can encode its center/antipode, but the midpoint conditions become awkward metric relations. It is genuinely distinct, yet presently inferior to the factor-2 antipode transformation, which uses the midpoints for free.
- Candidate technique(s): Homothety at \(A\) with factor \(2\); antipode on a circumcircle and Thales right angles; directed-angle spiral similarities; composition of direct similarities/rotations; complex numbers with “quotient is real” angle encoding; circumcenter locus via a perpendicular bisector. Miquel/Reim or inversion should be used only if a concrete auxiliary circle appears, not introduced speculatively.
- Cheap-kill candidates: Apply the factor-2 homothety immediately: \(M\mapsto B\), \(N\mapsto C\), \(O\mapsto X\), where \(X\) is the \(A\)-antipode on \((AKL)\). This prunes the problem to \(XB=XC\) before any heavy coordinate or inversion work. Also exploit \(AM=BM\), \(AN=CN\), and \(MN\parallel BC\). There is no parity/counting-style kill in this geometry problem.

- Small-case / intuition notes: **Conjectural structural picture.** The admissible \((K,L)\) seem to form a one-parameter family for a fixed triangle (four point coordinates subject to three angle equations), while the antipode \(X\) always moves on the fixed perpendicular bisector of \(BC\). In a tested triangle \(A=(0,0),B=(2,0),C=(0.7,1.8)\), several solutions produced distinct \(X\) but always \(XB=XC\). This strongly favors proving a locus/equal-distance invariant rather than searching for one rigid hidden configuration. Symmetric isosceles triangles may conceal the mechanism, so a generic scalene model is the better sanity check.
