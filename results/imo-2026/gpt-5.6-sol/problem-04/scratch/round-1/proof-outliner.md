## imo-2026-04

dyadic-multiples-and-thinness: new
Target: Prove that Mulan can force a finite victory from every initial triangle exactly for \(\theta=180^\circ/n\) with an integer \(n\ge2\), including both strategies, finite termination, and verification that no other angle works.
Technique: Normalize to the angle simplex; use strong induction on integer multiples for sufficiency, and finite-rank AND/OR attractors plus affine-elimination/Baire-category thinness for necessity. This adapts the arithmetic state encoding of `aimo-0225` and the discrete finite-termination lesson of `aimo-0236`/`aimo-0060`, but proves the needed statements anew.
Skeleton:
  1. Normalize all angles by \(180^\circ\), writing a state as \((a,b,c)>0\), \(a+b+c=1\), and \(t=\theta/180^\circ\); derive that a cut splitting \(a=x+(a-x)\) has possible children \((x,b,1-b-x)\) and \((a-x,c,b+x)\) — by angle sums in the two child triangles.
  2. For \(t=1/n\), prove that any state containing an angle \(kt\), \(1\le k\le n-1\), is won within \(\lceil\log_2 k\rceil\) further cuts — split \(kt\) into \(\lfloor k/2\rfloor t+\lceil k/2\rceil t\), after which either child retains one smaller positive multiple, and induct on \(k\).
  3. If no angle is already a multiple of \(t\), place the marks \(0,t,2t,\ldots,nt=1\) around the cumulative-angle interval partition. Some interior mark lies strictly inside one of the three angle intervals; cutting the corresponding vertex so that \(b+x=kt\) gives supplementary cut-point angles \(kt\) and \((n-k)t\), one in each possible child — by pigeonhole/interval partition and the transition formula.
  4. Combine Steps 2–3 to give a strategy with at most \(1+\lceil\log_2(n-1)\rceil\) cuts (and fewer if initially terminal), thereby proving finite termination against every sequence of Shan-Yu choices.
  5. For necessity define \(W_0\) as states containing \(t\), and \(W_{r+1}\) as states admitting a cut whose two children lie in \(W_r\). Prove that a fixed Mulan strategy guarantees finite victory from a state iff the state belongs to some finite \(W_r\) — the nontrivial reverse implication uses König's lemma on the finitely branching binary response tree to turn pointwise finite termination into a uniform depth for that initial state.
  6. Prove the affine-thinness lemma: unless \(mt=1\) for some positive integer \(m\), every \(W_r\) is contained in a finite union of proper affine lines in the two-dimensional open simplex. Start from the three target lines; at a predecessor step pair one affine condition for each child, substitute the two child formulas, and eliminate \(x\). The projection is again a proper affine line unless the two equations become dependent, and tracing the integer coefficients in that exceptional dependence yields exactly \(mt=1\).
  7. Since a countable union of finite unions of proper affine lines cannot cover the open simplex (Baire category, or an explicit line avoiding countably many intersection points), choose a state outside \(\bigcup_r W_r\). By Step 5 Shan-Yu can prevent finite victory there. Thus universal victory forces \(t=1/n\); the range \(0<t<1\) forces \(n\ge2\).
  8. Translate back and explicitly verify the characterized set: \(t=1/n\) is \(\theta=180^\circ/n\); Steps 2–4 prove sufficiency for every \(n\ge2\), while Steps 5–7 exclude every other real \(\theta\).
Key lemmas (claim + the one-line mechanism that makes it true):
  - Multiple-angle descent: an angle \(kt\) has finite rank at most \(\lceil\log_2 k\rceil\) — because splitting it into two nearly equal positive integer multiples leaves one such multiple in either retained child.
  - Grid-mark fork: if \(1=nt\), every non-multiple state admits one cut producing a positive multiple of \(t\) at the cut point in each child — because an interior mark \(kt\) of the cumulative partition gives the supplementary pair \(kt,(n-k)t\).
  - Finite-strategy/rank equivalence — because a binary strategy tree with arbitrarily long branches has an infinite branch by König's lemma.
  - Affine-thinness lemma — because two child hyperplane equations are linear in the split variable; elimination gives a parent line, and a two-dimensional projection can occur only when coefficient dependence collapses to \(1=mt\).
Open gaps: Step 6 is the principal hard gap: the builder must formulate an inductively closed class of integer-affine lines, perform all pairings (including unchanged-angle target conditions and cyclic choices), and prove rigorously that every dependence relation has positive integer form \(1=mt\). Step 5 must handle strategy dependence and the exact rank indexing carefully. Step 3 needs a clean boundary-mark case showing that a mark on a partition boundary already makes an original angle a positive multiple.
Cases to cover: initially containing \(t\); containing another multiple \(kt\); containing no multiple; \(n=2\); rational non-unit \(t\); irrational \(t\); cuts at each of the three vertices; limiting split values are illegal and must not enter the predecessor calculation.
Watch out for: A merely strict real-valued decrease does not prove finite victory. Do not infer the continuous theorem from rational-grid experiments. In Step 6, existential projection may create a two-dimensional region if the equations are dependent; that is the crux, not a routine assertion. Proper affine lines must be shown nowhere dense relative to the open simplex.

binary-tree-integer-certificate: new
Target: Prove the exact characterization \(\theta=180^\circ/n\), \(n\ge2\), by giving a finite Mulan strategy in the reciprocal cases and proving that any purported universal finite strategy algebraically certifies an integer relation \(180^\circ=n\theta\).
Technique: Adversarial binary strategy trees and induction-loaded algebraic certificates, modeled on the “strengthen so either reply satisfies the induction” move of `aimo-0369` and the branch-decomposition idea of `aimo-0917`; constructive balanced splitting supplies the winning direction.
Skeleton:
  1. Establish the normalized transition formula for angle triples exactly as in the first approach.
  2. Define a depth-\(r\) forcing certificate recursively: depth 0 is an angle equal to \(t\); a depth-\(r+1\) certificate consists of one legal split together with certificates for both children. Prove by induction that this is equivalent to winning within \(r+1\) cuts.
  3. Prove a certificate-extraction lemma: if a nonempty open family of parent states admits depth-\(r\) forcing certificates, then the binary certificate identities, after eliminating every chosen split variable, yield \(1=mt\) for some integer \(m\) with \(2\le m\le B_r\). The induction must classify whether a leaf target is an inherited old angle, a split angle, or one of the supplementary cut-point angles; when the two response branches are combined, split variables cancel and positive leaf multiplicities add.
  4. If Mulan wins finitely from every initial state, every state has some finite-depth certificate by König's lemma. Apply Baire category to the countable cover by depth sets to obtain a depth whose certificate set is nonmeagre/has an open cell; invoke Step 3 to deduce \(t=1/m\).
  5. Conversely, when \(t=1/n\), give the explicit integer-certificate strategy: first create complementary multiples \(kt,(n-k)t\) using an interior \(t\)-grid mark, then repeatedly split any surviving coefficient \(m\) into \(\lfloor m/2\rfloor+\lceil m/2\rceil\) until coefficient 1 appears.
  6. Bound the tree depth by \(1+\lceil\log_2(n-1)\rceil\), verify legality/positivity at every split, and translate to \(\theta=180^\circ/n\), \(n\ge2\).
Key lemmas (claim + the one-line mechanism that makes it true):
  - Branch-certificate cancellation — because the two children use \(x,a-x\) and supplementary angles \(b+x,1-b-x\), so adding the appropriate positive leaf identities cancels \(x\) and ultimately expresses the total angle 1 as an integer number of targets.
  - Open-family certificate extraction — because finitely many combinatorial leaf types occur at fixed depth, so one type persists on a nonempty open subfamily and its linear identities can be eliminated uniformly.
  - Reciprocal certificate construction — because the \(t\)-grid partitions total angle 1 and balanced coefficient splitting works under either binary response.
Open gaps: The load-bearing gap is Step 3: positivity of coefficients and uniformization of certificate types must be proved, despite Mulan's split being an arbitrary function of the state. Step 4 needs a precise regularity statement for the depth sets before invoking Baire; if they are not closed, replace “open cell” by the finite affine-type thinness conclusion proved directly. The builder must not assume a single uniform global strategy or continuity of Mulan's choices.
Cases to cover: all three leaf-angle types; inherited target angles; both child choices at every node; variable depth by initial state; \(n=2\); illegal endpoint cuts.
Watch out for: This route is genuinely a certificate proof only if the algebra explains why coefficients are nonnegative integers. Arbitrary subtraction could produce \(1=q t\) with the wrong sign or a vacuous identity. König gives a depth bound for one fixed strategy tree at one initial state, not automatically one bound over all initial triangles.

backward-affine-attractor: new
Target: Determine all \(\theta\) for which the finite-rank attractor of the target-angle set equals the entire angle simplex, and prove this happens exactly for \(\theta=180^\circ/n\), \(n\ge2\).
Technique: Exact backward reachability in the continuous angle simplex, with dimension-jump analysis of affine predecessor sets; this follows the state-encoding/backward-classification spirit of `aimo-0225` but replaces its finite state space by explicit affine geometry.
Skeleton:
  1. Normalize and derive the cut transition. Define exact finite layers \(A_0,A_1,\ldots\), where \(A_0\) is the three target segments and \(A_{r+1}\) adds parents having a legal cut with both children in \(A_r\).
  2. Compute the first predecessor exactly as a sanity/base case: absent an old target, a one-cut win occurs from an angle \(2t\), or from every state when \(2t=1\). This displays the first affine-line propagation and first dimension jump.
  3. Develop a finite symbolic description of each layer by affine cells labeled with integer coefficient data. Under one predecessor, intersect one pulled-back cell condition from each child in \((a,b,x)\)-space and project to \((a,b)\); record legality inequalities \(0<x<a\) rather than discarding them.
  4. Prove the dimension-jump theorem: a two-dimensional cell can first appear only if \(1=nt\) for a positive integer \(n\); before that, all cells have dimension at most one. The mechanism is rank drop in the two linear child constraints, whose constant terms count target leaves and whose supplementary-angle identities contribute the sole constant 1.
  5. If no reciprocal relation holds, \(\bigcup_r A_r\) is a countable union of one-dimensional affine cells and misses some triangle, so Shan-Yu can keep play outside earlier layers forever; justify the response by the defining complement of the attractor and justify failure of finite victory through König's lemma.
  6. If \(1=nt\), show constructive full coverage rather than relying only on dimension: the grid-mark fork puts every state into a layer containing complementary multiples, and balanced splitting assigns each multiple a finite layer. Give the explicit logarithmic depth bound.
  7. Verify the final characterization and endpoint restrictions.
Key lemmas (claim + the one-line mechanism that makes it true):
  - Exact predecessor-cell lemma — because both child maps are affine in \((a,b,x)\), so pullback, intersection, and projection preserve polyhedral/affine-cell structure.
  - First-full-dimension lemma — because projection has dimension two only when the two branch constraints lose rank, and coefficient tracing converts that dependence into \(1=nt\).
  - Complement strategy lemma — because if a state is outside \(A_{r+1}\), every legal Mulan cut has at least one child outside \(A_r\); the finite-rank union plus König separates finite forced play from an infinite evasion branch.
Open gaps: Steps 3–4 require a complete cell-label formalism and proof that fixed layers have only finitely many cells; projection with strict legality inequalities and cyclic vertex choices must be handled. Step 5 must avoid incorrectly concluding that “outside every fixed layer” immediately supplies one coherent infinite play without the tree argument. Sufficiency in Step 6 is expected to close cleanly.
Cases to cover: the four pairings of immediate target equations; unchanged target coordinates; rank-deficient versus full-rank constraint pairs; strict boundary inequalities; reciprocal \(n=2\); nonreciprocal rational and irrational targets.
Watch out for: Iterated predecessors need not be merely unions of lines once a rank drop occurs, so dimension jumps must be classified rather than assumed absent. A fixed-point closure without finite layer membership does not prove finite termination.

quotient-residue-safe-kernel: new
Target: Prove the same exact reciprocal characterization, using an explicit Shan-Yu safe family for every nonreciprocal target and an integer-multiple descent strategy for every reciprocal target.
Technique: Additive quotient/coset invariant for necessity, paired with constructive grid marking for sufficiency; this is the direct invariant alternative suggested by computation and the self-reproducing-family pattern of `aimo-0196`.
Skeleton:
  1. Normalize and encode cuts by the two affine child triples.
  2. For \(t=1/n\), use the marked cumulative interval and balanced integer-multiple splitting to force victory in at most \(1+\lceil\log_2(n-1)\rceil\) cuts.
  3. For \(t\) with \(1\notin t\mathbb Z\), construct a nonempty target-free safe set \(\mathcal S_t\) of angle triples described by their residues in \(G=\mathbb R/t\mathbb Z\), refined by representatives so that “residue zero” does not confuse \(t\) with other multiples. The intended invariant should contain a symmetric seed and satisfy: for every state in \(\mathcal S_t\), every vertex, and every real legal split \(x\), at least one child is again in \(\mathcal S_t\).
  4. Prove that Shan-Yu starts at the seed and always retains the safe child, so no retained triangle ever has angle exactly \(t\); this directly excludes finite victory without category arguments.
  5. Treat rational \(t=p/q\), \(p>1\), by a finite residue kernel modulo \(p\), and irrational \(t\) by the same quotient statement (or a separate affine-coset kernel if order/density differs); then verify these cover every nonreciprocal \(t\).
  6. Translate back and check all reciprocal and nonreciprocal cases.
Key lemmas (claim + the one-line mechanism that makes it true):
  - Reciprocal finite strategy — because total angle is exactly \(nt\), a grid mark makes a complementary-multiple fork and binary splitting decreases an integer coefficient.
  - Safe-kernel response lemma — because the two child residue triples contain complementary affine expressions in \(x\), so a suitable forbidden-residue pattern should make simultaneous escape of both children impossible.
  - Rational/irrational coverage — because \(1\notin t\mathbb Z\) is precisely the failure of the claimed reciprocal relation, but the invariant must distinguish finite-order from infinite-order classes.
Open gaps: Step 3 is wholly load-bearing and presently conjectural: an explicit \(\mathcal S_t\) and a response proof for every real \(x\) are required. Grid parity patterns are evidence only and do not handle off-grid cuts. Step 5 may need two genuinely different kernels for rational and irrational \(t\). The builder should abandon this slug rather than substitute the affine-thinness proof if no explicit safe family emerges, since that would collapse it into the first approach.
Cases to cover: reduced rational \(p/q\) with \(p>1\) (including even/odd \(p\)); irrational \(t\); \(t>1/2\); equilateral seed accidentally containing \(t\); all real split parameters and all selected vertices.
Watch out for: Avoiding \(t\) in the current triangle is not itself invariant: Mulan wins when both children hit \(t\). Equality modulo \(t\) is weaker than equality to \(t\), so representatives/order data are indispensable. Finite-grid kernels do not prove a continuous response strategy.
