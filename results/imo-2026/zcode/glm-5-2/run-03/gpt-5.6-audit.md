## Problem 1 — 7/7

For each prime, the valuation operation

\[
(a,b)\mapsto(\min(a,b),|a-b|)
\]

preserves the gcd of the complete valuation list. Total prime-factor
multiplicity decreases for a noncoprime chosen pair; for a coprime pair it is
unchanged while the number of nonunits decreases. This proves termination.
The invariant then excludes an all-ones terminal board and determines the
unique survivor prime by prime.

Two drafting defects are harmless. An early lemma says every labeled
valuation-game sequence terminates, although `(a,0)` can merely swap; that
lemma is not used for termination in the final proof. The final text says
`(Phi,-C)` decreases when `C` decreases; the correct pair is `(Phi,C)`, and
the surrounding stabilization argument independently states the right logic.

**Verdict: complete after local corrections, 7/7.**

## Problem 2 — 0/7

The retained algebra itself is exact. I read the README and ran
`code/final_check.py`; it reconstructs the coordinate expressions and obtains
an exact zero remainder. The Gröbner scripts reproducibly prove that the target
polynomial belongs to the ideal generated by the two displayed constraint
polynomials.

The fatal issue is that the geometric reduction does not establish that every
permitted configuration lies in this affine tangent chart. It declares

\[
a=\tan\alpha,\quad b=\tan\beta,\quad g=\tan\gamma
\]

positive without proving `beta` and `gamma` acute. Obtuse angles have negative
tangent, and configurations with `beta=90°` or `gamma=90°` are omitted because
the parameters are undefined. No argument proves those cases impossible, and
no cotangent/projective chart or justified limiting continuation handles them.

The normalization `A=(2u,2v)` also assumes `u,v>0`; after placing `B=(0,0)` and
`C=(2,0)`, only `v>0` is automatic. Although the formal polynomial happens to
allow arbitrary `u`, the geometric derivation and sign analysis are written
under the unjustified restriction. Several divisions in the intersection and
circumcentre formulas likewise need nonvanishing checks.

Thus the exact certificate proves the target only after an unproved
parametrization/branch reduction. Covering the missing right-angle cases would
require a new branch analysis or homogeneous trigonometric derivation, not a
one-word repair.

**Verdict: incomplete domain coverage, 0/7.**

## Problem 3 — 0/7

The proposed value may be correct, and the Strong Merge Lemma is useful, but
neither required global bound follows.

For the upper bound, the solution treats only Liu Bang's special dyadic
partition. Xiang Yu must respond to every initial placement of at most `n`
marks; a matching response to the lower-bound construction proves sharpness
only for that one configuration and reverses the minimax quantifiers.

For the lower bound, the induction reaches merge inequalities such as

\[
\operatorname{Li}(A\cup B)
\ge \operatorname{Li}(A)+\operatorname{Xi}(B),
\]

then says that “super-increasing compensation” and “careful accounting” give
the desired recurrence. The accounting is never supplied, and the displayed
inequalities do not automatically yield the claimed bound. The cut-count
observation for the smaller block does not resolve the missing interaction
between the refined large segment and the remaining pieces.

**Verdict: incomplete in both directions, 0/7.**

## Problem 4 — 0/7

The claimed answer `theta<=60°` is false. For example, `90°` is a winning
value, whereas a generic value such as `50°` is not. The correct family is

\[
\theta=\frac{180^\circ}{n},\qquad n\ge2.
\]

The proposed immediate-win step also misunderstands Shan-Yu's choice. Splitting
one parent angle into `theta` and `alpha-theta` places `theta` in only one
child; Shan-Yu may discard it and retain the other. A forcing cut must make
both possible retained children winning, either immediately or through a
proved descent. The submission later explicitly concedes that its claimed
invariant for the converse is unproved.

**Verdict: wrong answer and invalid strategy, 0/7.**

## Problem 5 — 7/7

The proof correctly derives

\[
f(f(y))=2f(y)-y.
\]

For `u=f-id`, the forward orbits are arithmetic and positivity forces `u>=0`.
The reformulated inequality

\[
4(xu(y)-yu(x))\le(x-y+u(x))^2
\]

is correct. If two positive displacement values existed, sufficiently large
points on their invariant arithmetic progressions could be chosen within
bounded distance; the left side would grow linearly while the right stayed
bounded. Hence every positive value is the same.

If a fixed point exists, the inequality gives an open zero-neighborhood around
each fixed point. The recursively chosen fixed points
`a_{n+1}=a_n+sqrt(a_n d)` are unbounded. The draft omits the sentence that the
successive zero-neighborhoods overlap and their right endpoints tend to
infinity, which gives `(a,infinity)` contained in the fixed set and contradicts
a positive-displacement orbit. This is a tiny local completion.

Finally `f(x)=x+c`, `c>=0`, is verified by QM-AM-GM.

**Verdict: complete after a tiny local detail, 7/7.**

## Problem 6 — 0/7

The submission itself retracts the key Small Sufficiency lemma. Its candidate
`c=a_i+p` need not be admissible, as its own computations observe. The proposed
divisibility check is invalid: a prime dividing `a_{i+1}` and
`a_{i+1}=c+(k-1)p` need not divide `c`. The final size argument also treats
`q>a_1` as though it implied `q>2a_1`, which is false.

The bounded-gap preliminaries are useful but do not prove stabilization of the
constraint family, a uniform finite prime witness, or periodicity of the good
set. The statement that the sequence enumerates all of `S` also ignores
possible members below `a_1`; at most it enumerates the tail from `a_1`.

These missing results are exactly the heart of the problem.

**Verdict: incomplete, 0/7.**
