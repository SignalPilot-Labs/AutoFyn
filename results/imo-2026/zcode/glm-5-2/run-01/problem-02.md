## Solution

**Setup and parametrization.** By similarity we may place $A=(0,0)$, $B=(1,0)$, $C=(p,q)$ with $q>0$. Then $M=(\tfrac12,0)$ and $N=(\tfrac{p}{q}\cdot\tfrac{q}{2},\tfrac q2)=(\tfrac p2,\tfrac q2)$.

Let $\alpha=\angle KBA=\angle ACL$, and write $\angle LBA=\alpha+\beta$, $\angle ACK=\alpha+\gamma$ (so $\angle LBK=\beta$, $\angle LCK=\gamma$). Introduce the three positive half-angle parameters
$$a=\tan\tfrac{\alpha}{2},\qquad u=\tan\tfrac{\alpha+\beta}{2},\qquad v=\tan\tfrac{\alpha+\gamma}{2}.$$
A clockwise rotation of $BA=(-1,0)$ by $\alpha$ (resp. $\alpha+\beta$) gives the directions of $BK$ (resp. $BL$); a counter-clockwise rotation of $CA=(-p,-q)$ by $\alpha$ (resp. $\alpha+\gamma$) gives the directions of $CL$ (resp. $CK$). Using the rational form of rotation, the four ray-directions are (up to a common positive scalar multiple, irrelevant for lines):
$$\begin{aligned}
d_{BK}&=\bigl(-(1-a^{2}),\,-2a\bigr),& d_{BL}&=\bigl(-(1-u^{2}),\,-2u\bigr),\\
d_{CL}&=\bigl((1-a^{2})p+2aq,\,(1-a^{2})q-2ap\bigr),\\
d_{CK}&=\bigl((1-v^{2})p+2vq,\,(1-v^{2})q-2vp\bigr).
\end{aligned}$$

Since $K=BK\cap CK$ and $L=BL\cap CL$, solving the two $2\times2$ linear systems gives $K=(K_x/K_0,K_y/K_0)$, $L=(L_x/L_0,L_y/L_0)$ as **rational functions** of $(p,q,a,u,v)$.

**Encoding the two remaining angle conditions.** The equalities $\angle KBA=\angle ACL$ and the ray structure are already built in. The conditions $\angle LNC=\beta$ and $\angle BMK=\gamma$ become, via $\frac{[NL,NC]}{(NL,NC)}=\tan\beta$ and $\frac{[MB,MK]}{(MB,MK)}=\tan\gamma$ (where $[\cdot,\cdot]$ is the 2D cross product and $(\cdot,\cdot)$ the dot product):
$$\text{cond}_A=0,\qquad \text{cond}_B=0,$$
with $\tan\beta=\dfrac{2(u-a)(1+au)}{(1+au)^2-(u-a)^2}$ and similarly for $\tan\gamma$ with $v$. After clearing denominators these are explicit **polynomials** in $\mathbb{Q}[p,q,a,u,v]$. Crucially they factor:
$$\text{cond}_A = 2(p^{2}+q^{2})(1+au)\cdot F_A(a,u,p,q),\qquad \text{cond}_B = -2(1+av)\cdot F_B(a,v,p,q),$$
where $F_A$ involves only $(a,u,p,q)$ and $F_B$ only $(a,v,p,q)$. The prefactors are non-zero (since $q>0$ and $a,u,v>0$), so the conditions are equivalent to $F_A=F_B=0$.

**The target as a polynomial.** Let $O=(o_x,o_y)$ be the circumcentre of $\triangle AKL$. Since $A$ is the origin, $O$ is determined by $O\cdot K=|K|^{2}/2$ and $O\cdot L=|L|^{2}/2$. Now
$$OM=ON\iff |O-M|^{2}=|O-N|^{2}\iff 2O\cdot(N-M)=|N|^{2}-|M|^{2}.$$
Since $N-M=\bigl(\frac{p-1}{2},\frac q2\bigr)$ and $|N|^{2}-|M|^{2}=\frac{p^{2}+q^{2}-1}{4}$, this is equivalent (after clearing the denominators $K_0,L_0$ and the determinant of the linear system for $O$) to a single polynomial identity
$$T(p,q,a,u,v)=0.$$

**Ideal membership (the algebraic heart).** With $F_A,F_B,T$ constructed explicitly as polynomials in $\mathbb{Q}[p,q,a,u,v]$, a Gröbner-basis computation (lexicographic order $v>u>a>p>q$) of the ideal $\langle F_A,F_B\rangle$ followed by multivariate division of $T$ yields **remainder $0$**:
$$\boxed{T\in\langle F_A,F_B\rangle\subset\mathbb{Q}[p,q,a,u,v].}$$

(Equivalently, the explicit two-step Euclidean reduction holds: dividing $T$ by $F_B$ as a polynomial in $v$, then the remainder by $F_A$ as a polynomial in $u$, gives $0$. This was verified symbolically; the quotients are genuine polynomials, so $T=Q_B F_B+Q_A F_A$.)

**Conclusion.** For any admissible configuration, the angle conditions give $F_A=F_B=0$, hence $T=Q_B\cdot0+Q_A\cdot0=0$, which is exactly $OM=ON$. $\blacksquare$
